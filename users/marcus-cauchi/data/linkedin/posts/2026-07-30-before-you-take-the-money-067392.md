Before you take the money, understand the company you will be left running

You have found an investor. The valuation looks attractive. The money could accelerate growth. The advisers are moving the deal forward.

But have you diligenced the capital as thoroughly as the investor has diligenced your company?

Most founders examine the valuation, dilution and headline terms. Far fewer examine what changes after the deal closes.

What will you still control?
Which results will you remain accountable for?
Who decides what happens if the plan misses?
What gets paid before you?
What information are you giving away before the investor has made a binding commitment?
And how does this investor behave when supporting the company becomes difficult, expensive or uncertain?

A good deal can still leave you with most of the responsibility, less authority and far less financial upside than the ownership percentage suggests.

That does not mean you should reject the funding.

It means you should understand the company, role and operating conditions you are agreeing to before the decision becomes difficult to reverse.
