Deals don’t fall apart because someone missed a step.

They start slipping much earlier, when the buyer decides it’s safer to hold back than to say what’s really going on.

Most buyers aren’t avoiding your salespeople. They’re avoiding the pace of the conversation.

They’re worried it’ll move faster than they’re ready for, and they’ll get pushed into something they can’t justify later.

Their day’s already packed before you show up. Someone’s asking for answers. Someone’s demanding a forecast. They’re carrying the weight of decisions that could backfire if they’re wrong. So they play it safe.

That’s why they do their own research. Why they share just the surface, not the deeper stuff. Why decisions take longer than you think they should.

Silence feels safer than committing when they’re unsure.

And that’s the real issue. They’re not slow - they’re unsure.

The way your sales process runs, even with good intentions, can make that uncertainty feel riskier.

But when the seller feels honest, not steering, not rushing, the whole tone changes. The buyer relaxes. They stop guarding every word. You hear the real situation, not the polished version. The pressure fades.

This isn’t complicated, and it doesn’t add work. If anything, it removes risk. It strips away friction. It clears the noise that clogs your pipeline.

It gives the buyer a moment where they don’t have to brace themselves. And that’s when they trust you with the part they won’t put in writing.

Once that trust is there, deals move straighter, with less strain on everyone.

When it’s missing, you feel it long before the numbers tell the story.

You feel it in the drag, the silence, the deals that look fine on paper but never close.
