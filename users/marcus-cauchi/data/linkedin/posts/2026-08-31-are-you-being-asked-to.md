Are you being asked to approve, fund or keep backing something important when the commitment is getting bigger but the case for it isn’t getting stronger?

It might be more money against a recovery or growth plan, more headcount behind a forecast, a capital raise or acquisition, or an AI or supplier commitment that will be difficult to unwind. Each part may have been checked properly by good people. What can still be missing is whether the whole decision hangs together strongly enough to justify what you’re about to commit.

I’ve been developing a way of testing decisions like these before the next difficult-to-reverse step, and I now want to put it against live cases. The decision needs to be real, already in motion and important enough that getting it wrong would cost meaningful money, time, capacity, control or future options. It also needs to be open enough that better evidence could still change what you do.

We’ll work through what has to be true for the decision to work, what you actually know, what is still assumption, what contradicts the case and whether you have the authority and conditions needed to deliver what you’re being held accountable for. We’ll also look at what happens if one of the important assumptions fails. The purpose isn’t to find reasons to stop, it’s to establish the largest commitment the evidence currently justifies.

Sometimes that will be exactly what you were already planning to do. Sometimes it may mean changing a condition, committing less, staging the commitment, getting one important answer first or deciding that the case isn’t strong enough yet. Either answer is useful if it prevents you committing further than the evidence warrants.

This will suit someone carrying real responsibility for the outcome and prepared to discover that the evidence may not support the answer they currently prefer. It won’t suit someone looking for reassurance, a hypothetical discussion or a tour of the method. If we uncover something larger that deserves more work, we can decide separately whether there’s anything useful to do together.

If you have a live decision that fits, DM me three things:
What are you deciding?
What becomes difficult to undo once you say yes?
When does the next meaningful commitment happen?
I’ll tell you whether I think this is the right kind of decision for us to work on.
