I’ve been teaching “cards-face-up” negotiating for years…which may just sound like, “Ok already…we should be nice in negotiations. Got it,” at this point. 

But it's ain’t just me - and it's not ALL about being a nice, good human, either.

I sucked at traditional negotiating. When I accidentally played my cards face up for a customer during a large transaction, negotiating stopped being a battle. We got value for every concession, and the LTV of the customer relationship went through the roof. 

Oh, and my negotiating anxiety disappeared, and our late-stage sales cycle shrank dramatically.

Six years ago, I was skimming research articles and found this one in the Journal of Marketing. (I immediately stalked the researchers… connected with one of them, and ended up guest lecturing a UT Austin class on it 😬) 

The study looked at the impact of what they called “open negotiation”. Five studies spanning 400 buyer-seller negotiations at auto dealerships, of all places.

→ When sellers disclosed cost/pricing structures early in a conversation, trust increased.

→ But, more importantly, the buyers bought more, and it resulted in higher long-term value, 

The old-school way taught me, at least, to hide the price as long as possible. 

I’m not 1927 old, but Herbert C. Casson wrote it in his ‘Tips for Traveling Salesmen’: “Never mention the price until the customer thinks it is more.” 🤢 

That’s not only gross, but also bad advice. 

When we buy something, our brain is trying to predict…”Is the juice worth the squeeze?” If we don’t know the squeeze, we can’t process the goodness of the juice. 

Lead with the structure - the Four Levers!

▪️ The timing matters: Set a range early, along with the levers…not after the buyer is feeling like they’re being managed.
▪️ Verifiability matters: Peers connect, pricing information is proliferating everything, AI is exposing pricing models. Pretending like your price is some state secret is…hilarious? 🤔 

It’s not just me out teaching and writing about it - the research continually supports it.
