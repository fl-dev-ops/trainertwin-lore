Most organisations don’t fail because they make bad decisions.

They fail because they keep running with decisions that no longer stand up, long after the moment has passed.

Nothing dramatic happens at the time. The plan gets approved. The meeting ends.  Everyone nods and moves on.

At first, everything still works. Then the small shifts start.

People stop pushing back. Exceptions creep in. Work gets done, but it feels fragile. Months later, leaders start asking why delivery isn’t landing.

It looks like a people problem. It isn’t. Nor is it a process problem. Nor is it an execution problem.  It’s a sign that the decision can’t be changed anymore, even by the people who are meant to own it.

That’s how organisations end up delivering results no one would knowingly choose again.
