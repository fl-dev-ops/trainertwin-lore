Buyers don’t hate being sold to. They hate being sold to by someone who sounds like he found their name in a spreadsheet 11 seconds ago.

I spoke with Tom Stearns and Peter Cleary on *The Inquisitor* about Graphic Sales: How to Build a Prospecting Playbook. It’s a graphic novel about sales, which sounds daft until you remember most sales training already has fictional characters, exaggerated conflict, and a villain called Procurement.

That’s why the format works. It doesn’t lecture salespeople. It lets them recognise themselves before they defend themselves.

Sometimes the mirror says, “You didn’t prepare.” Sometimes it says, “That ICP is just a wish list wearing a company logo.” Sometimes it says, “You opened the relationship with a lie and now you’re surprised trust is low.”

Humour lowers the guard. Tell a rep, “You need better pre-call planning,” and they nod like a hostage in enablement training. Show them a comic of a rep winging a call after 400 touches got the meeting, and everyone knows exactly who that is.

Sometimes it is them.

Peter called sales the easiest hard job. Know who you’re selling to. Understand why they might care. Prepare. Listen. Tell the truth. Learn from losses. Don’t make the buyer regret letting you into their calendar.

Then the quarter gets weird and someone decides the answer is 900 automated emails beginning with “Hope you’re well.”

Nobody is well after receiving those.

Tom made a point every sales manager should hear: the first call is too expensive to wing. Someone spent time, money and reputation to create that meeting. Then the AE turns up with three tabs open, no theory, no plan, and the confidence of a man who has mistaken charm for preparation.

That isn’t selling. That’s commercial fly-tipping.

AI came up too. AI is useful. So is a chainsaw. That doesn’t mean you give one to someone who hasn’t learned where their legs are.

The robot didn’t need replacing. The humans needed to stop outsourcing their judgement to it.

That’s what I liked most about the book. It helps salespeople feel less alone without letting them off the hook. The goal is not slicker sellers. It is better ones. The kind who prepare properly. The kind who tell the truth. The kind who understand the buyer’s world before pitching their own.

The kind buyers are relieved to meet.

Buyers don’t hate being sold to. They hate being sold to badly.
