Pipeline Truth: The Lies That Roll Uphill

Founders lie to investors all the time.
 Not because they want to.
 Because they’re scared.

Scared of losing confidence.
 Scared of looking out of their depth.
 Scared the truth will cost them the next round.

So they polish the story.
 They “smooth” the numbers.
 They call a soft maybe a strong commit.

And that’s where the rot begins.

The lie doesn’t stay small.
 It rolls uphill.

A rep overstates their confidence to keep their manager off their back.
* The manager passes it to the VP, not wanting to look like their patch is underperforming.
* The VP rounds it up for the CRO, who needs a clean narrative for the CFO.
* The CFO wraps it for the board, and the board presents it to investors as progress.

Everyone’s lying.
 No one’s malicious.
 They’re just scared.

Fear travels faster than truth.

This isn’t a forecasting issue.
 It’s a cultural one.
 A system built on pressure, fear, and fragile ego will always breed fantasy.
 A system built on fiction always breeds CYA.

If you want truth in your forecast, you need safety in your culture.
 That’s what kill criteria create.

They’re not about quitting early.
- They’re about deciding what’s real before emotion, fear, and sunk cost start running the numbers.

Kill criteria don’t just protect your time.
 They protect your integrity.

Here’s what that looks like:

Segmentation:
 If there’s no urgency or pain in six months, it’s TAM (total addressable market i.e. anyone with a pulse), not SOM (serviceable obtainable market i.e. people who can buy and you can deliver what you promised excellently). Stop talking shite. Tell the truth. ALWAYS.

Pipeline:
 If it’s been pushed twice, it’s gone.
 If there’s no sponsor, you’re selling into a void.
 If procurement’s leading before value’s agreed, it’s not a deal - it’s at best a delay. Most likely, you are column fodder.

Forecast:
 If you can’t prove it with data or behaviour, it doesn’t belong in commit.
 “Optimism and Hope” are not good strategy.

Kill criteria are where courage meets process.
 They stop the small lies before they become company culture.

Because once you start reporting hope as fact, you lose the one thing investors can’t buy back - trust.

When you strip the ghosts from your pipeline, it stings.
 The numbers shrink. Often by more than 80%. Sometimes to a trickle.
 But so does the bullshit.
And what’s left - the truth - is something you can build on.

Forecast integrity isn’t a reporting metric.
 It’s a reflection of your leadership.

It says: We’d rather tell the truth than look good.
That’s when the fear fades. (Unless you have investors who only care about extraction and the valuation number in which case you are running a financial instrument, not a business).
 -That’s when trust can grow
 - And that’s when the real numbers start compounding

Lies roll uphill. They stink
 So does courage. It doesn't
 Be the one who stops the lie before it starts moving

Protect your people and your business
