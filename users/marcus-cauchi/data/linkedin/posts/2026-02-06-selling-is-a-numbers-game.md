Selling is a numbers game.

That belief explains a lot.

It explains why volume is rewarded over timing.
Why activity stands in for judgment.
Why pressure feels justified.

Numbers make selling feel controllable.
If one deal does not move, add more.
If response drops, escalate.

Silence stops being a signal.
It becomes a problem to manage.

CROs weigh in.
Managers push.
Reps are told to message their champion again.

More people appear on the seller side.
More urgency is applied.
More insistence follows.

What becomes clear to the buyer is not importance.
It is priority.

Not their priority.
The vendor’s.

Pressure does not reduce uncertainty.
It increases anxiety.

Anxiety narrows options.
The safest move becomes delay.
Or disengagement.

Silence deepens.

That silence is then treated as resistance.
Or incompetence.
Or poor execution.

It is neither.

It is a rational response to being pushed into a decision that does not yet fit.

Pressure does not disappear when a deal moves.
It relocates.

What could not be absorbed before close
shows up later as friction no one traces back.

The numbers improve.
The dashboards relax.

And the organisation lives with what the escalation never solved.
