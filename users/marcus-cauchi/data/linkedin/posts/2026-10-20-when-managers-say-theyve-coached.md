When managers say they’ve coached someone, what actually happened? 

I’m researching how managers spend their time and whether their interventions help people get better at their jobs. Some of that time goes into teaching, asking questions and helping people solve problems. Some goes into checking, chasing, correcting mistakes and taking over. All of these can be useful. 

Sometimes clear instructions are exactly what’s needed. Sometimes stepping in prevents a costly mistake. 

But how do we know whether the manager chose the right approach? 
And what happens the next time that person faces a similar problem? 
Do they handle it better? Do they still need the same help? Or has the manager become the person everyone depends on to get things done? 

I’d value your vote, but I’d learn more from a real example. What was happening? What did the manager do? What happened afterwards? 

I’m particularly interested in examples that challenge my assumptions. Please share this with people whose experience you trust.
