Most managers I know aren’t short of wisdom.
They’re short of minutes.
The job keeps taking from the very place people grow — the small moments where real coaching actually happens.

The best coaching isn’t a meeting.
It isn’t a slot on the calendar.
It’s what happens in the middle of the work, when someone hesitates, or gets stuck, or is about to choose a path they’re not quite sure about.
That’s when they come to you.
Not for content.
Not for a tool.
For a person who won’t rush them or make them feel foolish.

Those ten minutes, the ones you squeeze in between the noise, often do more good than anything the company buys.
They steady people.
They help them think again.
They get them back on their feet.

And of course, those ten minutes are the first to disappear.
Forecast here, fire drill there, meetings that sprout like weeds.
By Friday, coaching becomes the thing you meant to do, not the thing you managed to do.

So the company buys a system to fill the gap.
Everyone nods politely.
Nothing changes.

Here’s the part we don’t say out loud.
People grow when someone sits beside them, not above them.
When you ask the question that sharpens their thinking instead of giving the answer that dulls it.
Because once you give the answer, they stop thinking.
And once they stop thinking, they stop growing.
Then they lean on you for things they could do themselves, and everyone gets slower, not better.

Operational coaching keeps responsibility where it belongs — with the person doing the work.
You guide.
You nudge.
You hold the space long enough for them to see what they already know but haven’t reached yet.

It’s slower in the moment, yes.
But everything moves faster because of it.
And you end up with people who can stand on their own two feet long after you’ve stepped away.

If you’re a manager trying to keep that space alive in the middle of all the noise, I’d love to hear how you do it.
What helps?
What gets in the way?
What do you wish someone had told you sooner?

Share it if you can.
Your answer might give another manager a little more room to breathe next week.
