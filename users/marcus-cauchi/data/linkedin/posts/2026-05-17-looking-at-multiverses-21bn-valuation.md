Looking at Multiverse’s $2.1bn valuation it's legitmate to ask whether it’s overhyped? That’s the wrong question.

The real question is, what happens when AI adoption, public policy, workforce transformation, state-backed capital and private infrastructure all begin converging around the same operating model?

Because once you view the latest Multiverse raise through the backdrop of the King’s Speech, the picture changes completely.

The King’s Speech repeatedly reinforced:
AI-led productivity
digital transformation
cyber resilience
public-sector modernisation
strategic investment
economic competitiveness
public-private coordination

That matters enormously.

Why?

Because Multiverse is no longer being valued like a “training business.”
If it were, the numbers would look difficult to justify.

Revenue: ~£79.6m
EBITDA loss: ~£59.7m
Cash reserves down from ~£135m to ~£82m
Pre-tax losses widened again
Approximate EBITDA margin: around -75%

For a normal training company, that would raise serious concerns. But that’s not how investors are viewing this. They’re not buying “online apprenticeships.”

They’re buying the possibility that Multiverse becomes the workforce AI adoption layer for Europe. And that distinction changes everything. Every technological gold rush creates secondary industries.

When everyone rushes to buy shovels (Nvidia, compute, AI infrastructure), the next wave emerges:
integration
governance
implementation
optimisation
compliance
workforce adaptation

That’s where Multiverse sits.

Not as the AI itself. But as the system teaching organisations how to absorb it.
Now add the wider architecture around it.

The Tony Blair Institute frames AI as a national productivity and public-sector transformation imperative. Palantir and similar firms provide the infrastructure layer: data integration, analytics, operational systems and institutional dependency. The British Business Bank and National Wealth Fund increasingly direct state-backed capital toward “strategic” sectors like AI, cyber, defence and digital infrastructure.

Digital ID systems become the authentication layer connecting citizens to services and platforms.

And companies like Multiverse become the behavioural adaptation layer, reskilling workers so institutions can actually implement the transition.
This is what convergent power architecture looks like.
