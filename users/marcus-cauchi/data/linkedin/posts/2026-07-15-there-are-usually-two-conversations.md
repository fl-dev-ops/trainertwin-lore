There are usually two conversations happening in a partner relationship.
The first happens with the vendor in the meeting. The market opportunity sounds good. The margins look attractive. The support offer is explained. 

Everyone agrees that there is potential.

The second conversation happens after the vendor has left.

That's when the partner asks the questions that decide what happens next. Will our customers trust this? Can our sellers recognise a real opportunity? Will the margin survive the cost of winning and delivering the work? Will the vendor help when something goes wrong? Will their direct team compete with us? Could this damage our reputation?

These are not signs that the partner lacks ambition. They are signs that the partner is making a proper commercial judgement.

Vendors often mistake politeness for commitment. A partner can attend training, accept content and join calls without deciding to put real weight behind the relationship.

Commitment starts when the partner risks something scarce. That may be customer access, skilled people, reputation, management attention or money.
Most partner programmes explain why the vendor wants the partner.

Far fewer make it clear why the partner should choose the vendor over every other demand on their business.

The real decision is rarely made in the meeting.

It's made in the conversation that happens after you leave.
