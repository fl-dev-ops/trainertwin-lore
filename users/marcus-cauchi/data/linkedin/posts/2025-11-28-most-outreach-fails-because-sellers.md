Most Outreach Fails Because Sellers Won’t Slow Down

I posted a rant the other day about pitch-slaps.
It got more traction than anything thoughtful I’ve written in months.
That told me something uncomfortable.

Maybe the problem isn’t lazy outreach.
Maybe it’s the pressure we put on ourselves to “cut through the noise.”

Here’s the calmer, more honest version:

Most outreach fails for one simple reason:
The seller rushes past the human moment.

When someone gets thirty messages a day, your research doesn’t matter.
Your intent does.
Your timing does.
Your relevance does.
Your restraint does.

Last week someone sent me a nine-paragraph pitch.
I didn’t even make it to paragraph two.
Not because I’m rude.
Because I’m already drowning in decisions I didn’t choose.

So here’s the filter I use before I contact anyone, founders, operators, whoever:

If they replied honestly, would it help them or just help me?

If it helps me?
I stop.

If it helps them?
I slow down.

Then I ask a real question.
One that shows I’ve actually seen their world.
One they can answer without pressure.

Try this instead of a pitch:

“Before I ask for anything, what part of your world is getting harder faster than expected?”

No hook.
No angle.
No trap.
Just curiosity.

People respond to that because it creates safety.
And safety is the doorway to relevance.

If your outreach is getting ignored, don’t get louder.
Get clearer.
Get calmer.
Get closer to the truth of someone’s day.

I’m practicing this too, not perfectly, but deliberately.

What’s the one message this year that made you feel genuinely seen?
I’m curious to learn from it.
