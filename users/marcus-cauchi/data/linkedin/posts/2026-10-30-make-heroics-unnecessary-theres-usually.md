Make Heroics Unnecessary

There’s usually someone everyone calls when a big customer is threatening to leave. They know the people involved, understand what matters and can sometimes get it sorted by lunchtime.

That person may deserve every bit of the thanks they receive. It’s worth noticing, though, how often the same kind of problem reaches them, and what happened before it did.
Perhaps someone made a reasonable decision with the evidence available. Perhaps an important commitment was assumed rather than agreed. Sometimes the situation changed and nobody recognised it until another team had to deal with the consequences.

None of those possibilities proves that the original decision was wrong. Nor does it mean a senior person should have stayed out of it.

Over the next few weeks I’ll publish thirteen short fictional stories about these situations. The people, conversations and events are invented. They’re meant to be recognisable, not accounts of clients.

They involve people trying to do their jobs well. The customers have their own priorities, managers have responsibilities they can’t simply delegate, and people sometimes disagree for good reasons.

Some decisions work. Others leave something unresolved. In several stories, we don’t learn what happens next.

The first story begins with a promotion that seemed obvious to everyone involved.
