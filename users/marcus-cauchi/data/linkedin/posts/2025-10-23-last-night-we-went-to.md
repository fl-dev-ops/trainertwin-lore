Last night we went to see "I Swear", a powerful and poignant film about John Davidson, who has a condition you may or may not have heard of called Tourette's Syndrome. Wow! Highly recommended.

Reviewers describe it as “funny, fierce and full of heart,” but what stayed with me was how honest it was about lack of control.
 It’s not about choice.
 It’s wiring.

I see the same thing in sales - different symptom, similar reflex.

The buyer doesn’t even mention price yet.
 They just pause, or tilt their head, or ask, “So how does this work?”
 And the rep jumps in with,
“Of course, there’s some flexibility on price…”

No one asked.
 No self generated pressure.
 Just an internal tic firing off before the buyer even blinks.

They can’t help it.
 They’ve learned to avoid tension, to chase approval, to believe they're buying safety in their own heads by giving something away. 

But every unprompted discount tells the buyer,
“I don’t believe in the value I just described.”
Discounting isn’t generosity.
 It’s a nervous reflex dressed up as goodwill.
 And like many reflexes, it can be rewired, but only once you stop pretending it’s a deliberate choice.

You can’t stop it with willpower.
 You fix it by retraining the loop that fires under pressure, the one that links discomfort to price cuts.

That starts with how you think about money, rejection, and your own worth.
Because until you do, every “I swear I won’t discount this time” ends the same way.

John Davidson doesn't have a choice. You do.
