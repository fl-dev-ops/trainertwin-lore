I worked 17 to 21 hours a day at times. I thought that made me a good manager. It cost me years with my kids that I’ll never get back.

In my first ten years of managing people and running a business, I was forever sorting things out. Someone had a problem, I knew the answer, and I stepped in. Customers got looked after, work got done, and I felt I’d earned my keep. Then the same problems came back, and I’d get frustrated that people couldn’t sort things out for themselves.

I was so busy fixing what was wrong that I rarely stopped to ask why it kept going wrong. Worse, I couldn’t tell whether my help was making people more capable or more dependent on me. I could see the problems I’d solved. I couldn’t see the decisions people might have made if I’d given them the chance, or what was stopping them.

Meanwhile, I was missing my kids growing up. Even when I got home, I was often too tired to be present. I can’t get those years back.

Here’s something I wish I’d understood sooner. A problem can be solved today and still be waiting for you tomorrow.

Think about the next five problems people bring you. 
* What made your help necessary? 
* What changed because you stepped in? 
* And what would have to change for them to handle something similar safely next time?

Two people can ask you exactly the same question and need completely different help. Getting that wrong can keep you busy for years without making your team any stronger.

I’ve explored why in my latest newsletter, including the things I missed when I was too busy being useful to notice.

Read the newsletter. Save those three questions for the next time someone needs you.
