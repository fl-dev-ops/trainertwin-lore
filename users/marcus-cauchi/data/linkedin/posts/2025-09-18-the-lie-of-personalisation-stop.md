The Lie of Personalisation

Stop sending trivia, start sending help

You’ve seen these emails:
 “Hi Sarah, I saw you went to XYZ University. I grew up nearby.”

It looks personal. It isn’t. It’s trivia.

Buyers don’t reply because you noticed something.
 They reply because you noticed what matters.

Let’s compare:
 – Trivia: “Loved your podcast episode.”
 – Relevance: “You said finance kills late-stage deals. Here’s how three teams got CFO sign-off in week 2, not week 10.”

One proves you can Google. The other proves you can help.

This is where most new SDRs fall. They confuse familiarity with relevance. They scrape LinkedIn for facts instead of signals. They try to sound warm instead of being useful.

Relevance means this:
 – Naming the real pressure your buyer is under.
 – Showing you see the gap between effort and outcome.
 – Offering one sharp, timely way forward.

That’s it. Not charm. Not trivia. Not fake warmth.

Before you send your next message, ask: does this opener tell them I know a fact about them, or does it show I understand their world?
If it’s the first, delete it. If it’s the second, send it.

Stop proving you can research. Start proving you can help.

Is your INTERRUPTION:
* Timely?
* Relevant?
* Personally Valuable?
