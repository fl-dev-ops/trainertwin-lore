At the sharp end, certainty doesn’t feel confident.
It feels like the decision can no longer be questioned. 

Once a decision is treated as settled, the risk changes.
Not the risk of being wrong, the risk of speaking up. 

You stop naming edge cases.
You translate concern into progress language.
You adjust the work so it still functions, even when the decision no longer stands.

Certainty gets rewarded.
So contact with reality gets edited.

What actually keeps things working is something practical.
Someone noticing what no longer stands up.
Pressure treated as information, not resistance.
Workarounds named early, before they harden into habit.

That isn’t indecision.
It’s maintenance.

From the sharp end, the safest organisations aren’t the most certain ones.
They’re the ones where reality is allowed to interrupt certainty before it breaks the work.
