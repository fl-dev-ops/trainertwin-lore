The Number Can Be Right and the Decision Still Be Too Big

Roshaan Mahbubani makes a simple point here that matters. Cash gives a family room to wait, say no and act when the right opportunity appears.

What his post made me think about is what happens next. A family can have plenty of liquidity and still put too much behind a decision because the evidence is being asked to do more than it can.

Imagine a family office considering a direct investment in a recurring-revenue business. Customer retention is strong, the data is clean and nobody is arguing about whether the number is accurate.

So that number starts getting used to support other things. Confidence in pricing. Expansion. The next cohort. The value creation plan.

That's where trouble can start. Strong retention tells you customers tend to stay. It doesn't automatically tell you they will accept higher prices, buy more, behave the same in a weaker market or support the growth assumed in the plan.

The number hasn't failed. The job we gave it has become too big.

A lot of important decisions are built from facts that are perfectly credible on their own. The problem appears when one of those facts gets stretched further than it deserves.

Nothing necessarily looks wrong. The spreadsheet works. The source is credible. People can agree on the facts.

The useful question isn't only, “Is this true?” It's, “What does this actually justify us believing?”

And from there, “How much commitment does that justify?”

Sometimes the answer is the full investment. Sometimes it is a smaller one, staged capital, waiting for something else to become clear, or doing nothing yet.

Having the money gives you the choice.
It doesn't tell you how far the evidence supports going.
