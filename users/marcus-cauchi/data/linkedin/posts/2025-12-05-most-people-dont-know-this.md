Most people don’t know this (you probably guessed), but: the average sales manager gets their first real training after about 12 years in the job. Twelve years. Some never get any. In not so many words I got told, "Congratulations, now you're a manager" (I have to apologise to those I managed. I am SO SORRY for what I did to you, please forgive me!)

Which… explains quite a lot, doesn’t it?

Because somewhere along the way, sales stopped being taught and started being white-knuckled. The apprenticeship didn’t blow up dramatically - it just slipped out the back door one Tuesday and never came back.
And then you got promoted. 

“No big deal, you’ll figure it out.” Except that means nobody showed you how to actually manage eight people, three fires, and a pipeline with trust issues.

So your week becomes the usual circus: Slack ping. Teams ping. “Quick one?” A rep freezing mid-sentence. You winging back answers like breadcrumbs to a flock of mildly panicked pigeons.

Seems harmless, until you notice it drains the one thing a team needs: judgement. Every rescue quietly trains them not to think. And suddenly you’re the only adult in the room, which is a terrible room to be in.

None of this is your fault. But you end up carrying it anyway: their panic, your quota, their mistakes, your calendar. Hands weren’t built for that kind of double-load.

Here’s the part people don’t say out loud: there’s nothing wrong with you - you were promoted without the craft. The craft is teachable. And it’s teachable quickly.

The video I made walks you through three tiny coaching moves that shift you from rescuer → teacher, the apprenticeship you should’ve had in the first place.
Use them Monday. Or in the next “Oh god, what do I do with this?” moment — which, let’s be honest, might be later today.

Watch it when you get ten quiet minutes. (If you just snorted, good. Same.)

And look, everyone has that one moment they’d rather not admit: the deal that imploded, the rep who froze, the call where your brain melted and you rescued without thinking.

If a message from a rep makes you sigh this week, screenshot it and send it to me, privately. I’ll show you how to turn it into a coaching moment instead of another rescue.

One less thing for you to carry on your own.
