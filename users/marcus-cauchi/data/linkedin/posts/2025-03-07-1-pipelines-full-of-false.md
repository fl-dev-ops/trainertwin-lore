1. Pipelines Full of False Hope
Sales teams are drowning in deals that were never real. Unqualified opportunities, polite interest misread as intent, and prospects who have no urgency or authority, all clogging up forecasts and wasting precious time. Benjamin D. Justin Michael 🏄🏼‍♂️ Scott Leese 

2. Decision Drift
Deals that seem to be progressing mysteriously stall, slip, or die quietly without explanation. No clear no, no clear yes, just silence. Sales teams are left chasing shadows because they never knew where they really stood. Avner Baruch (B.Eng') #ProjectMoneyball

3. Happy Ears and False Positives
Salespeople hear what they want to hear, not what is actually said. “Sounds promising” gets confused with “this will close,” creating a forecast built on wishful thinking rather than reality. Roderick Jefferson #SalesEnablement3point0

4. Stakeholder Soup
Sellers rarely deal with just one decision-maker. They face shifting committees, internal politics, and influence chains they cannot see, let alone manage. They pitch to one person and lose to ten. Dan Pfister has a fix you will never have thought of.

5. Trust Deficit from Day One
Buyers start from a position of suspicion. They assume sellers will push, spin, and hide the uncomfortable bits. Most sales teams have no structured way to build trust fast enough to break through that defensive wall. Charles H. Green offers outstanding antidotes as do Darryl Stickel, PhD and Simon Bowen

6. Risk Blindness
Sales teams are trained to sell benefits, not surface risks, which means buyers’ fears, doubts, and internal blockers stay hidden until they quietly kill the deal. Sellers think they are managing objections, but they are only hearing the easy ones. Matt Dixon wrote about this in #TheJOLTEffect

7. Value Misalignment
Sellers talk about product features. Buyers care about outcomes and risks. The gap between what is sold and what matters is often so wide that even strong products fail to land because the story is wrong. Patrick Boucousis

8. Too Many Tools, Not Enough Truth
The average sales team has over a dozen tools in their tech stack, but still lacks a single source of truth. Data is scattered, conflicting, or distorted to look good in reports, meaning reality is always one step removed. Gerry Hill 🏌🚀🤖☎️

9. Activity Obsession Over Outcome Discipline
Leaders chase metrics like dials, emails, and meetings, mistaking activity for progress. The result is burned-out sellers who do more and more but achieve less and less. Evelyne Brink Kyle Gray

10. Emotional Suppression
Sellers are expected to be confident, upbeat, and resilient at all times, leaving no room to address their own fears, doubts, and uncertainties. Those emotions do not disappear. They just leak out in hesitation, discounting, or avoidance of tough conversations. Moeed Amin
