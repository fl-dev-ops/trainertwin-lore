What Suzanne surfaced here in our audit data is one of those truths everyone in sales bumps into eventually. The difference between good sellers and elite ones isn’t hustle. It’s how they read the room when things get tense.

Elite sellers don’t orbit the buyer’s needs. They drop into the buyer’s world. They notice who’s scared of looking foolish, who’s avoiding internal politics, who’s carrying the risk if this goes wrong. Once you see the pressure someone’s under, their decisions stop being frustrating and start being human.

And trust… it’s never built by keeping everything agreeable. It’s built when you’re willing to define and state the line that protects the buyer’s judgement, even if it makes the moment uncomfortable.

Co-building matters for the same reason. When you work the solution out together, the people in the room relax. People stop performing and start thinking clearly again.

The discipline point hits home too. It’s the quiet habits that sharpen judgement, not the activity noise most teams celebrate.

But the real sting’s in the system. Even brilliant sellers shrink inside cultures that reward panic. Incentives shape behaviour long before talent does.
