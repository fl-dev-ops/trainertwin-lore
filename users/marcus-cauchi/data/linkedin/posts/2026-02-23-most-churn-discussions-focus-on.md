Most churn discussions focus on analytics. Rates, cohorts, retention curves and expansion metrics are all useful, but none of them are decisive on their own.

The harder conversation is governance.

Every company carries customers it tolerates but should not. Not because they fail a spreadsheet test, but because they slowly redefine what the company is willing to accept. They introduce exceptions, stretch positioning and normalise work that does not fit the model.

The damage rarely shows up immediately. It appears later in diluted focus, confused messaging and teams unsure what good actually looks like.

Strategy is not only about who you win. It is about who you choose not to build around.

Letting the wrong customer go is rarely about revenue. It is about protecting the standard.

Standards, once lowered, are difficult to restore.

Resharing Avner Baruch (B.Eng') point because churn is not just a growth metric. It is a test of leadership discipline.
