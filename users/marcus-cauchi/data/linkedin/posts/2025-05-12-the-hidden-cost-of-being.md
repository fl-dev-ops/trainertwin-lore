The Hidden Cost of Being the Hero

Let’s name it:
 So many managers are praised for being the hero.
"Bob always rolls up his sleeves and jumps in", "If ever I have a problems I go to Bob, he is a problem solver", or "Bob jumped in and saved the day ... again"

Bob sounds like a stand up boss.

Or is Bob:
 The rescuer. The fixer. The one who jumps in, solves the problem, and saves the quarter. 

Impact over time?
 That “heroic” behaviour silently becomes Bob's biggest bottleneck.

It stops Bob's team from growing.
 It stops Bob from scaling.
 And it builds a system that only works as long as Bob is exhausted and feeling the burnout.

***

Real leadership starts when you stop saving, and start designing for strength without you.
When you coach instead of control.
 When you teach your team to think, not just obey.
 When your presence becomes a multiplier, not a dependency.

You don’t need to micromanage to protect your numbers.
 Instead, you can build a team that can own the number, and make excellent decisions without you holding the steering wheel every second.

The trade-off isn’t perfection.
 It’s capacity. It’s culture. It’s a life where you still lead… without losing yourself.

Drop the cape.
 Build something that can hold more than just you.

Reprogramme your thinking.

If you struggle to make time, you are always juggling chainsaws, and you know what you're doing is making your life harder than it could be, ask yourself this, what have you done to make yourself redundant today? How have you created succession?
