Borrowed capability is not transformation

The fractional CRO debate is focused on the wrong distinction.
The issue is not whether the leader is fractional, interim or permanent.
It is this. Did they improve the system, or did they become the system?

Contract type is a weak proxy.

The more important variables are authority, capability transfer, dependency reduction and exit design.
A business can show every sign of progress: Pipeline rises. Forecasting improves. The board deck becomes clearer.  Fewer fires reach the CEO.

But that progress may still be borrowed.

If the judgement, relationships, operating rhythm and decision-making disappear when the leader leaves, the organisation has not built capability.

It has rented it. That is the Borrowed Capability Trap.

The true measure of a revenue leader is not only what improves while they are present. It is what the organisation can now do without them.

Can the team make better decisions?
Can functions resolve conflict without escalation?
Can the forecast be trusted without heroic intervention?
Can performance continue after the handover?

Temporary leadership creates real value when it reduces dependency. Otherwise, it may be effective support. But it is not transformation.

Of course, no CRO can transfer capability into a system whose CEO or board will not transfer authority.

The best engagement should make itself unnecessary.

CROs: when you leave, what should still work without you, and what usually does not?
