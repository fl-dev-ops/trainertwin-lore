Something I’ve noticed after watching this play out a few times.

Once teams make execution faster, there’s an assumption that everything else should speed up too. Decisions. Alignment. Confidence. It feels logical. If the system moves quicker, the organisation should as well.

In practice, the opposite often happens.

The decision gets made earlier and more quietly. The output shows up clean and on time. Then, later on, someone asks a basic question about why it happened.

That’s when things slow down.

People aren’t being cautious. They’re trying to work backwards. They’re looking at settings, signals, and outcomes and trying to piece together what the decision actually was.

I’ve seen this stall rooms that otherwise move fast.

Not because anyone disagrees with the outcome. Because no one feels fully comfortable owning it without reconstructing the logic.

At a certain point, speed stops helping. It starts creating risk.

That’s usually when teams make a small but deliberate change. They slow a few decisions back down. Not everything. Just the ones that need to be explained to someone else later.

It’s not about control. It’s about being able to say, simply, why something was done.

When that goes missing, the system isn’t scaling judgement. It’s separating people from it.

That gap doesn’t show up in the metrics.
It shows up in how long conversations take once someone asks a real question.

And that’s where things tend to stick.
