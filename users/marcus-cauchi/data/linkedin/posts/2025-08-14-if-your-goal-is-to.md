If your goal is to “convince,” “close,” or “handle” your customer, you’ve already crossed the line.

I’ve been in those rooms. I’ve been that seller. And I’ve seen the damage it does.
When your focus is your quota, the customer becomes a means to an end. Every question is a setup. Every answer is a hook. The conversation stops being about their truth and starts being about your target.

It’s irresponsible because you’re playing with someone else’s capital, career, and credibility.

It’s unethical because you’re taking away their ability to decide freely.
Our job is not to wrestle someone into a “yes.”

Our job is to serve the customer so they can make the best decision for them, even if that decision isn’t us.

That means:
* Listening without steering.
* Naming the risks without inflating them.
* Offering a path forward without cornering them.

You might lose a deal today. But you’ll keep the trust tomorrow.
And #trust is the only quota worth chasing.

Question: When was the last time you walked away from a deal because it wasn’t right for the customer, and what happened next?
