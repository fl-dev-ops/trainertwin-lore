The Four Equations Boards Can Trust”:

Boards often pride themselves on being data-driven. The danger is being loyal to the wrong data. Boeing worshipped EPS. Nordea clung to NPS. Both collapsed, not because of fate, but because of flawed metrics. EPS and NPS look neat on a slide deck. Neither predicts resilience. Neither measures trust. 
Neither protects lives, customers, or shareholder value.

The lesson is stark. When you subordinate safety and loyalty to optics, you build castles on sand. Boeing went from “if it costs more to build it safe, we build it safe” to 346 lives lost and $60B erased. Nordea declared loyalty “mission critical” while losing 200,000 customers, reporting NPS of -28, and mistaking sentiment for substance. Both were victims of false North Stars.

There is a better way. The Mathematics of Trust™ reveals four equations every board must understand: the Trust Equation, Perceived Risk, the Confidence Index, and the Compounding Value Radar™. Each is grounded in behavioural science, finance, and risk logic. Together they form a compass that links trust to compounding financial value.

The new Value Compounding Index™(VCI™) is the capstone. Unlike NPS, it measures realised outcomes, stickiness, advocacy, resource costs, and risk drag. High VCI™ predicts resilience, renewals, and future cash flows, hard metrics investors can bank on.

Boards don’t need loyalty theatre or safety theatre. They need metrics that track trust, risk, and confidence in real time. VCI™ is that metric. It tells you where value is compounding, where risk is rotting, and where to double down.

The question isn’t whether your customers are loyal. 
It’s whether you are loyal to the right metric.
