Maybe it's me!

We'd been talking for about twenty minutes. She'd walked me through the role, the extra responsibility, another restructure and another promise that things would settle down once the business got through this latest change. She'd also explained why her manager was under pressure, why budgets were tight and why everyone was having to do more with less.

Then she stopped, looked at me and said, "Maybe it's me."

There wasn't any self-pity in it. She sounded tired. What struck me wasn't the sentence itself. It was everything that had happened before she said it. She'd spent twenty minutes explaining everyone else's situation before she'd allowed herself to wonder whether she was simply expecting too much.

I've noticed that pattern hundreds of times. The people who ask whether they're being unreasonable are rarely the ones making unreasonable demands. More often, they're the people who've worked hardest to understand everyone else's position before giving themselves permission to trust what they're seeing.

After a while, something shifts. Promises start carrying more weight than patterns. Hope begins winning arguments that the evidence has already lost. Another month feels fair. Another conversation deserves a chance. Another restructure might finally change things.

We stopped talking about what her employer might do next and laid out what had actually happened. She wrote down what had happened, in order, without trying to explain anyone’s intentions. About halfway through she became very still. Then she looked back at the page, smiled and said, "I don't think I've been questioning them at all. I think I've been questioning myself."

I've thought about that conversation many times since. I wonder how many capable people spend months trying to solve themselves when the evidence has been pointing somewhere else all along.
