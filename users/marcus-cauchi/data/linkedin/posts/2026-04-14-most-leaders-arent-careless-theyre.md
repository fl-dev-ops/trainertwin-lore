Most leaders aren’t careless. They’re incentivised.

The system rewards hitting this quarter, not owning what breaks next quarter. So the work starts to lean before anyone calls it a decision.

The number gets defended early. The timeline gets protected before the work can bear it. The risk gets noted, but not owned. Nothing has been decided yet. Except it has.

Once momentum starts, stopping becomes expensive. And expensive truths get delayed, softened, or passed on.

That’s how risk moves. Not through bad intent, but through a system that rewards progress over judgement and certainty over honesty.

By the time the deal is signed, the decision is already load-bearing. By the time delivery starts, reversibility is already gone.

And when it breaks, it gets called execution. It isn’t. It’s the cost finally landing after a sequence that made it inevitable.

Decision safety fails long before the decision is named. It fails when the downside has no owner. It fails when silence is treated as agreement. It fails when momentum replaces choice.

So the only question that matters is this:

If this works less well than promised, who pays first?

If you don’t force that into the room early, the system will answer it for you later.
