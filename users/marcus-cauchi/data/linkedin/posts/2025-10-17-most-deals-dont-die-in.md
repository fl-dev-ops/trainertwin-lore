Most deals don’t die in the room.
 They die in the corridor. Quietly. Predictably. Avoidably.

The sponsor nods. The team leans in. The logic? Flawless.
But when your champion walks it upstairs, the sentence tweaks, the risk gets softened, the exit vanishes.
 By Room 2, your “safe step” sounds like a leap.
 By Room 3, it’s dead. No flags. Just silence.

That’s not a pipeline problem.
 It’s a pressure problem, and your value cracked under load.

This isn’t about better storytelling.
 It’s about engineering structural trust:
 
A sentence that travels.
 Risk that’s named.
 An exit that’s real.
 A pitch that survives without you in the room.

This post maps the unseen crucible your deal walks through, and why most don’t make it.
Don’t just pitch well.
 Design conviction that carries.
