Most firms don’t remove risk. They move it to someone with less power.

The board keeps the upside. The customer gets the workaround. Delivery inherits promises it didn’t make. Middle management carries pressure for numbers they didn’t believe in to begin with. That isn’t bad luck. That’s process.

A bad process rewards confidence over honesty, treats challenge as disloyalty, and makes it easier to pass risk on than to name it. So the deal gets signed, the target gets praised, and the truth turns up later wearing the badge of “execution failure”.

Usually it isn’t an execution failure. It’s the moment the original distortion becomes too expensive to hide.

The real question is simple: if this works less well than promised, who pays first?

If the answer is the customer, delivery, or the next layer down, then the risk was never managed. It was moved.
