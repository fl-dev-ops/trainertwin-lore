This happens all the time.

A company buys a new CRM. Sales liked the demo, the business case made sense, the board approved it and procurement got it over the line.

Then the real work starts. Nobody has cleaned the data. Managers still run pipeline from spreadsheets. Reps see the system as admin. Marketing and CS use different fields. The sponsor is busy with another project.

Three months later, everyone is annoyed and the supplier gets blamed. The software may not be the problem. The conditions were.

That is the link between ghosting and buyer’s remorse. Ghosting is often the buyer seeing the risk before they sign. Buyer’s remorse is the buyer discovering it after they sign.

That is why sales has to test more than interest, pain and budget. Can the buyer actually make this work? Who owns it? What has to change? Who will resist it? What will get in the way? What happens after the excitement of signing has gone?

If those questions are not tested before the deal is won, Customer Success inherits the mess.

The better sales test is simple. Can this customer succeed from this decision, with the conditions they actually have? If not, the deal may still close. It just may not hold.
