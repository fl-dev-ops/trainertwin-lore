Most forecast risk is approved before it is noticed.

It gets approved when weak evidence is allowed to travel upward as commit confidence.

The seller says the deal is committed. The manager accepts the confidence. The forecast absorbs the number. The board sees a cleaner picture than the buyer evidence underneath it deserves.

Nobody must lie for this to happen. The proof standard is just too low.

That matters because commit confidence does not stay in the forecast call. It travels into hiring, spend, cash planning, board commitments, and value creation plans.

A commit deal should need more than seller belief, a good meeting, a close date, a positive sponsor, or a stage in the CRM. Those signals may be useful, but they do not prove the buyer can carry the decision inside their own business.

The board question is simple. What buyer-owned evidence must exist before a deal is treated as commit?

That means the buyer has named the problem, accepted the consequence, understood the decision path, found the funding route, involved the right people, and shown a reason to act.

If that evidence is missing, the deal may still happen. But it should not carry commit confidence yet.

That is Decision Safety in practice. Don't ask the board to trust a deal the buyer has not made safe to defend.

Which signal gets promoted into commit too easily? A positive sponsor, a close date, procurement activity, verbal intent, or CRM stage?
