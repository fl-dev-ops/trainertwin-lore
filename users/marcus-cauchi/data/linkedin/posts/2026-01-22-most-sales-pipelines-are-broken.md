Most sales pipelines are broken before the first call.
Not because sellers lack skill.
Because the system turns interest into pressure the moment someone enters.

Qualification is where this starts.
Budget. Authority. Need. Timing. Fit.
These don’t protect buyers. They protect seller motion.
They answer one question:
Is this deal good for us?

Once that question runs the system, buyers get pushed by default. Deadlines appear. Movement is rewarded. Hesitation is treated as something to overcome.
That’s not a behaviour issue.
It’s a structural one.

Most leaders only see this after carrying a forecast that looked fine and failed late.

There is a stricter question most systems avoid:
Is it safe for them to decide, and am I permitted to participate?
When that question governs movement, qualification stops making sense. Buyers self-qualify through ownership. Momentum only exists if stopping is safe. Sellers earn permission instead of advancing deals.
Pipeline shrinks at first.
Then churn collapses.
Reversals disappear.
Forecasts get boring.
Revenue gets harder to stop.

You don’t qualify deals.
You license participation.
If the licence is revoked, you stop.

I break down what replaces qualification, and why this produces better pipeline, in today’s newsletter.

You don’t have to agree.
But if qualification is still your control point, this will challenge it.
