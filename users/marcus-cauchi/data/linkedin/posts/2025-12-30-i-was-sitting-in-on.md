I was sitting in on a GTM review a few weeks back.

The numbers looked fine. Pipeline was up. Activity was steady. No one was stressed and no one was celebrating. It felt like a meeting where things were mostly under control.

Halfway through, someone asked a simple question.

“Why are we seeing more movement in this segment, but the team doesn’t seem confident selling into it?”

No one jumped in. A couple of people glanced at their screens. One person said, “Out system’s prioritising those accounts.” Someone else added, “That’s what it’s set up to do.” Both were true. And that’s where it stopped.

The conversation drifted back to volume and coverage. The meeting carried on. It ended on time.

What stayed with me wasn’t that anything had broken. It hadn’t. The system was doing its job. The issue was that when the question came, no one could clearly say what the system was optimising for, or what it was giving up to get there.

Afterwards, one of the managers said to me, “I trust the output. I just don’t know how to defend it.”

That was the moment.

Not because the system failed. Because it worked well enough that the gap only showed up when someone had to own the decision.
