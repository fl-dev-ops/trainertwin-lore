The forecast looks fine… until it doesn’t.
You know that sinking feeling.
 Monday feels steady.
 By Friday, half the “done deals” have wandered off.
 Not because anyone’s hiding things, but because no one feels safe enough to say, “This might fall apart.”

Most forecast meetings don’t invite the truth.
 They reward the loudest voice.
 They punish doubt.
 So people tidy their numbers instead of telling you what’s actually going on.

And then we act surprised when the forecast blows up.
The problem isn’t data.
 It’s pressure.
 When people feel squeezed, they start guessing what you want to hear.
 When they feel safe, they tell you what you need to hear.

I watched a CRO at a £40m ARR SaaS firm get beaten up by a 42 percent miss.
 Six weeks later, she was within 8 percent.
 That gap was £9m of stress, shame, and board meetings no one enjoys.

We didn’t run a big project.
 We just stopped grilling the team.
 We added a 10-minute truth check.
 One simple question:
 “What feels at risk this week, and what help do you need?”

The whole mood changed.
 People dropped their shoulders.
 The Friday scramble went quiet.
 By the next quarter, her team were upfront on Monday instead of confessing on Thursday.

If your forecast still runs on hope and heroics, start here:
 “What would make it easier for you to share the truth earlier?”

Accuracy doesn’t come from pressure.
 It comes from safety.
 Lower the fear, and the truth shows up.
