“Intellectual resting points”: why people get angry when they’re wrong, and rarely change their minds.

Being certain of things feels great. Something we no longer need to think about.

We can now function on autopilot as the world feels safe.

So when someone points out that we missed something, how does it feel?

Scary? Annoying? Stressful?

They are pushing us from a state of comfort into a state of discomfort. And the fact they appear right simply means we’ll need to employ MORE effort in ignoring them.

So we direct our anger towards them.

Or people have directed their anger towards you, as you scratch your head thinking “but I demonstrated you were wrong.”

With prospects and clients, identify their “intellectual resting points”.

Tread very, very carefully.

Read up about persuasion.

And no, persuasion is not about “getting someone to agree they are wrong or believe we are right”. 

It’s about getting them to be open to making fewer mistakes in their reasoning.

The more we practice, the higher the probability we can avoid conversations derailing.

Whether about finances, markets, wealth, banking, or even… politics.
