Your sales process is not neutral.

It either reduces customer risk or transfers that risk downstream.

If your team is rewarded for customer commitment before the customer is ready to execute change, you are not just driving growth.

You are converting customer risk into reported growth.

It looks clean in quarter.
It comes back later as weak onboarding.
Slow adoption.
Fragile champions.
Buyer regret.
Churn.
Discounted renewals.
Expansion that never happens.
Forecasts the board stops trusting.

This is where many CROs misread the signal.

They treat hesitation as a rep problem.
They treat stage movement as deal health.
They treat signature as proof of value.

But the customer is not only deciding whether your solution is better.

They are deciding whether they can defend the decision internally, absorb the change operationally, and realize value without taking unacceptable career, financial, or execution risk.

What shows up as pipeline friction is often unaddressed customer risk.

This is not a rep problem first.
It is a system design problem first.
Reps usually follow what the system rewards.

Your business is perfectly designed to deliver exactly the results you are generating.
Not necessarily the results you want. The results your system rewards, tolerates, and reinforces.

And when that risk is ignored, the deal may still close.
The cost just moves.

Customer success inherits it.
Finance sees it in retention.
The board sees it in forecast credibility.
The business feels it in slower, more expensive growth.

That is how growth quality breaks.

This is not only a market problem.
It is a leadership choice.

The job of the CRO is not only to increase bookings.
It is to decide what kind of revenue the business is willing to create.

You cannot remove all risk from buying.
But you can stop adding avoidable risk through weak qualification, stage pressure, incentive design, forecast pressure, and handoffs that reward signature before customer readiness.

Those choices still belong to you.
Hard quarters do not remove them.
They expose them.

Question for CROs, CEOs, and operating partners:

Where in your revenue engine are you still rewarding customer commitment before customer readiness to execute?
