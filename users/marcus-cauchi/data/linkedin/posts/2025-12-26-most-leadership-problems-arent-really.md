Most leadership problems aren’t really people problems.
They’re decision problems that no one feels able to touch anymore.

A plan gets approved and everyone moves on. At the time, it feels settled. Then reality shifts. Pressure turns up. Assumptions stop holding. Trade-offs surface.

Going back to the decision would mean reopening politics, so people don’t. They work around it instead.

That’s when things start to slow. Not because people don’t care, and not because they’re incapable. Judgement becomes cautious. Questions get softened. Risks get parked. Work continues, but it no longer lines up in the same way.

From the outside, it looks like misalignment, resistance, or poor follow-through. From the inside, it feels like choosing words carefully.

The organisations that cope best under pressure aren’t the ones with perfect plans. They’re the ones where decisions can still be questioned after they’ve been made. That’s what keeps authority intact. And when authority holds, people speak plainly and act with confidence.

If your team is busy but progress feels fragile, there’s usually a decision underneath it that no one feels able to reopen.
Most leaders know exactly which one that is.
