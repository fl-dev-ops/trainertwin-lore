How to rebuild trust when the rot’s already set in ...

By the time you notice the smell, it’s already in the walls.
 You can’t talk your way out of rot - you have to cut and clean.

When I realised our culture was running on fear and discounts, I tried everything except the truth.
 More all-hands.
 More spin about “shared goals.”
 More workshops.
 ... and threatened the dreaded PIP to scapegoat the victims not the perps.

None of it worked, because everyone was still watching what I’d do next, not what I’d say.

So I started smaller.
 One act at a time.

I told my reps I’d overpromised on a target and asked them to help me fix it.
 I stopped rewriting people’s emails before they sent them.
 I made one sincere apology in front of the team.

At first, nobody believed me.
 They waited for the old me to show up.
 And fair enough - I’d trained them to.

But trust rebuilds the same way it decayed: slowly, predictably, through repetition.
 Every time you prove you can face discomfort without hiding, the room breathes a bit easier.
That’s when you know you’re starting to win it back, when silence feels like listening, not fear.

If you’ve let the rot set in, start there:
Admit what you’ve done, not what “we’ve” done.
Fix one small thing fast.
Let people see you do it.

You can’t delegate healing.
 But you can lead it.

Before your next meeting, pick one uncomfortable truth you’ve been avoiding, own it, and put it on the table first.
 Watch how quickly the room follows.
