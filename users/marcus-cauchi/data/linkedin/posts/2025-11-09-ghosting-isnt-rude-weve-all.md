Ghosting Isn’t Rude

We’ve all been on both sides of the silence.
 One week you’re chasing replies.
 The next, you’re the one avoiding them.

Ghosting isn’t rude.
 It’s human.

When someone stops replying, their nervous system is saying, “This feels unsafe for me right now.”
They might like you.
 They might even want what you offer.
 But when they feel rushed, confused, or exposed, silence feels safer.

That isn’t disrespect.
 That’s biology.

And in sales, we trigger it all the time.
 We talk over people.
 We fill the quiet.
 We make our need louder than their comfort.

Here’s how to handle the quiet better.
Name the reality. “You’ve probably got a lot on. It happens to all of us.”
Remove the pressure. “No rush. I just didn’t want to leave things hanging.”
Leave a soft door open. “If priorities have shifted, that’s fine. I’ll be here when it’s right.”
That’s not manipulation. It’s safety. Because ghosting isn’t the end of a conversation.

It’s a pause.

It’s someone waiting for proof that you’re still safe to talk to.
