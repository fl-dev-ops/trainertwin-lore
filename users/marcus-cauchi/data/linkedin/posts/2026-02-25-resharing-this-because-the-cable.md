Resharing this because the cable story is useful, but not for the obvious reason.

Cables breaking isn’t new. They’ve always been cut, dragged, snagged. The network was designed assuming that would happen, so traffic reroutes and the system carries on. If global connectivity were genuinely one incident away from collapse, we would already know.

What’s more interesting is how much attention we give the physical layer once it becomes visible. The real exposure in most systems isn’t that something can break. It’s how much quietly depends on a small number of paths, owners or regions without anyone mapping it clearly.

That pattern shows up inside companies all the time. Performance looks stable. Forecasts hold shape. Reporting stays coherent. Underneath that surface, dependency concentrates. Revenue sits with one champion. Delivery hinges on two or three people. A major account threads through a single relationship. Nothing appears broken until something routine forces a reroute.

When that happens, the issue isn’t failure. It’s concentration. Too much was running through one path and no one treated it as exposure. By the time it reaches the numbers, the correction window is narrow and political.

The internet works because it assumes failure and builds routing around it. Most operating systems assume continuity and only discover concentration when it is exposed. That is usually where the real fragility sits.
