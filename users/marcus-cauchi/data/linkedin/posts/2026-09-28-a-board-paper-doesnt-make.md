A board paper doesn’t make an assumption true. Neither does putting it in the operating plan. Or the finance model.

But something interesting can happen once it appears in all three.

People start making decisions from it.
Money gets committed. People get hired. Capacity gets reserved. Plans become harder to change.

The original assumption may be perfectly sound. That’s not the point.

The interesting question is whether anything happened to the evidence while the consequences of being wrong got larger. That’s what I examine in this edition of Commercial Reality Notes.

If you’re responsible for decisions that become expensive to reverse, there’s one commercial commitment worth having in mind when you read it.

What Are You Relying On?
