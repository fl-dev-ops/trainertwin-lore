I wasn't expecting Schrödinger's cat to teach me anything useful about capital allocation.

I was trying to work out whether the cat could escape.

So we built the maths, gave it choices, allowed it to wait for better information, gave it several ways out and then ran 500,000 simulated attempts.

Something interesting happened.

The obvious answers weren't reliably the best ones.
More information could make things worse. More options could turn out to be the same option wearing different clothes. Moving faster wasn't necessarily safer. And sometimes the best answer wasn't to make a smaller decision.

That got my attention.

Because Family Offices and PE firms face a more consequential version of the same problem whenever they put serious capital, reputation or relationships behind a decision.

The interesting question isn't simply whether the opportunity is good.

It's what happens when you're wrong.
And whether you've left yourself somewhere useful to go next.

Turns out the cat has something to say about that.
