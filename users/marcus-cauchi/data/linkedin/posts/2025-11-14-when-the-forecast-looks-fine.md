When the Forecast Looks Fine... Until It Doesn't
As a sales leader, you know the feeling all too well.
The week starts off calm and collected. But by Friday, half of those "committed" deals have mysteriously vanished. No one's outright lying - they just don't feel safe enough to be fully transparent about the risks and challenges they're facing.

It's Not About the Data, It's About the Pressure
Most sales forecast meetings aren't actually designed for uncovering the truth. They're set up for survival. The loudest voice in the room wins, doubt and uncertainty get punished, and everyone learns to polish their numbers instead of naming the real risks. It's a vicious cycle that drains the steady, open clarity right out of the system.

Fix the Safety, Fix the Accuracy
The problem isn't bad data - it's pressure. Forecasts fail because tension crowds out the truth that's essential for accurate projections. But once people feel unpressured enough to openly name the challenges they're facing, the accuracy starts to fix itself.

What Does This Look Like in Practice?
One CRO at a £40m ARR SaaS business was missing her forecast by a staggering 42%. Six weeks later, that gap had shrunk down to just 8% - that meant £9m in revenue variance recovered and board confidence restored. The secret? We ditched the traditional interrogation-style forecast meeting and added a simple 10-minute "truth check." No slides, no spin - just one question: "What feels at risk this week, and what help do you need?"

This subtle shift in tone and approach flipped the dynamic from defensive to open. The Friday panic stopped. Forecast calls got shorter, quieter, and accurate. By the next quarter, her team was telling the truth on Monday, not Thursday.

Steal This Question for Your Next Meeting
If your forecast process still relies more on bravery than clarity, start small. Ask your team a single question: "What would make it easier for you to share the truth earlier?"

That's where forecast accuracy starts - when truth feels safe enough to share. When pressure drops, data quality rises, and confidence returns.
