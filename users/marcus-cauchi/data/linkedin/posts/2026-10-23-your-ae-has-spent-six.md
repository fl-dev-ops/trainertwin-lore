  This is one of the questions behind The Price of Pipeline.

  Most of the attention goes into generating meetings and opportunities. I’m interested in what happens to the investment afterwards.

  What does it cost to pursue an opportunity? When does continuing make commercial sense? And how much time and money gets committed because nobody has established whether the evidence still justifies it?

  What happened to the pipeline you already paid for?

Your AE has spent six months pursuing a deal.

The buyer has missed three agreed dates. The decision-maker hasn’t been involved. Nobody can say what needs to happen for the customer to proceed. But the opportunity is still in the forecast, and your AE is preparing for another meeting.

Who decided that was a good use of their time?
The deal might still close. There may be good reasons to keep pursuing it.
But what evidence supports that decision?

You’ve already paid for the prospecting, meetings, demonstrations, proposals and management reviews. You won’t recover that money by spending more. The decision now is whether the next hour, meeting or proposal is worth what it will cost, including the work your AE could have done instead.

If the AE closes the opportunity, their pipeline shrinks and their manager’s forecast may get worse. Someone may have to explain the gap to target.

Leave it open, and those conversations can wait. Meanwhile, the business keeps paying for the work. Now add volume.

Your SDR manager improves the rate at which meetings become opportunities by 30%.
More opportunities. More pipeline. That could be excellent news.

But if you haven’t established which opportunities deserve continued investment, what happens when you increase the number entering the pipeline?

You might create more worthwhile revenue. You might also increase the time and money spent pursuing opportunities that no longer justify the effort.

Your SDR dashboard won’t tell you which.
Neither will a bigger pipeline.

Before you pay to generate more opportunities, can you show that the ones you already paid for still deserve the resources they’re getting?
