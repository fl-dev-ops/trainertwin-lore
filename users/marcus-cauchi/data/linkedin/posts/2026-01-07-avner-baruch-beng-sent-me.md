I like your thinking, Avner. It doesn’t blame SDRs. It doesn’t default to effort or discipline. It looks at the system instead of the people caught inside it. That alone makes it worth engaging with

Where I’d add something is not as a correction, as a continuation of the thought. Because in most teams, qualification isn’t where things start to go wrong. It’s where earlier choices finally show themselves

Long before an SDR sees a lead, someone usually notices something that doesn’t quite line up. A lead that never should have entered the system. A buyer who sounds interested but can’t really decide. An opportunity that looks fine in a report but feels off in conversation

Saying that out loud has consequences. It affects forecasts. It affects targets. It affects how safe people feel being direct with each other. It carries reputational and political risk

So people adapt inside systems that make honesty costly. They keep things moving instead of stopping them. They track instead of choosing. They record instead of tying up loose ends. Stages advance. Fields get filled. Activity stays high

Very little actually closes, one way or the other

From the outside, it looks like a funnel issue. From the inside, it’s more about what the system allows people to say without fallout

There’s also the buyer side, which rarely fits neatly into qualification rules. By the time someone is being worked, many buyers are already careful. They respond. They attend meetings. They stay polite. And they avoid putting their name on a decision that could come back on them later

That isn’t bad behaviour. It’s how buyers, who are human beings and not corporate abstractions, protect themselves

When qualification is built on surface signals, it can’t tell the difference. Engagement looks like intent. Caution looks like momentum

So teams stay busy. Pipelines stay full. Results lag behind both

No villains. No laziness. Just incentives doing what they always do

None of this contradicts what you’re pointing at. If anything, it helps explain why the problem keeps appearing in the same place

For qualification to actually narrow anything, the organisation has to allow things to end early. Cleanly. Without rushing to replace them. Without softening the story about why they ended

When that’s allowed, qualification works. When it isn’t, qualification becomes the place where unfinished decisions collect

I read this as a marker, not a final destination. It shows where the strain becomes obvious

The next step is deciding whether the system is ready to deal with what’s been feeding that strain all along. That’s the harder conversation

It’s also the one that makes everything downstream actually work

I wonder what would happen if we deepen the conversation by looking at the ICP and the list marketing is building the funnel from?
