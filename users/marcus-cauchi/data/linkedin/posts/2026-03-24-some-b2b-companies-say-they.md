Some B2B companies say they are customer-centric while paying as if the job ends at signature.

That is a strange place to stop rewarding judgment.

Because that is exactly where the customer’s exposure begins.

Not before the contract.  
After it.

In implementation.  
In adoption.  
In first use.  
In scrutiny.  
In renewal.  
In the moment the promised outcome meets operating reality.

And yet many revenue systems still celebrate the earlier moment most heavily:

pipeline  
stage progression  
bookings  
closed revenue  
quarter attainment

Useful signals.  
But still early signals.

They show that something was sold.  
Not that it will work.  
Not that the customer made a good decision.  
Not that the value will survive contact with reality.

So I keep coming back to this:

many companies reward commercial commitment as if it were proof.

When often the real proof comes later.

And that may be one reason so many “wins” become someone else’s problem after the deal is signed.

I am curious where this is wrong.

What is the earliest moment your company celebrates a deal that later turns out not to have been a good customer decision?
