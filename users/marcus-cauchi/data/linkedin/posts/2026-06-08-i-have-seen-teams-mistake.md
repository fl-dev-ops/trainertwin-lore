I have seen teams mistake activity for progress. I have done it myself. More calls. More sequences. More “pipeline”. 3-5 x coverage. Everyone busy, everyone reporting green, and still not enough real conversations with the people who can actually move the decision forward to a conclusion.

That failure usually starts before the first call.

Not with the script. Not with objection handling. With poor account judgement. A loose ICP. Thin research. A handoff that drops everything the SDR learned. A forecast that looks comforting because nobody has asked the harder question: what evidence do we actually have that this will convert?

The gold in this conversation is the cost of speed without understanding. AI can help. So can process. But if they make sellers quicker and less curious, you have not improved prospecting. You have industrialised guesswork.

I liked this because Peter Cleary and Tom Stearns do not treat prospecting as a hustle problem. They treat it as an operating problem. Who are we going after? Why them? What risk is the buyer carrying internally? What did we learn? Where did that knowledge go? What did the manager inspect before letting the number into the forecast?

Graphic Sales uses comics to show sales failures we have all seen, and some of us have rewarded. Funny afterwards. Expensive at the time.

Worth listening to if your team has plenty of pipeline, but too little of it survives contact with reality.
