First Time We Meet, I’m Already Bleeding

The fact is when we meet for the first time, 
I’m not a blank slate.
I’m carrying scars.

I’ve been feature-f***ed by sellers who used product dumps as a smokescreen for not listening.
I’ve been sold a pup, promised the moon, delivered a paperweight.

And worst of all?
I’ve survived the “hunter” drive-by, 
A quota-chaser who blasts through my inbox, bags the deal, then vanishes, leaving a mess for Customer Success to sweep up.

The handover?
“It’s your problem now. See ya!”

Every time, it’s the same:
My team has to start over, retell our story, fight to get what was sold.

I get to explain, again, why we trusted someone who treated us like a transaction, not a partner.

So When We Sit Down, Here’s What’s Unsaid:
	•	I’m expecting another sales ambush.
	•	I’m waiting for you to prove you’re not another ghost.
	•	I’m protecting myself from making the same mistake, because if I do, I pay for it.

If you’re in sales, here’s the only question that matters:

Can you be the one who breaks the cycle?
	•	Listen like my trust isn’t free (because it isn’t).
	•	Show me you’ll own my experience, end-to-end, not just until the paperwork is signed.
	•	Make it safer for me to be honest than to be guarded. 

If you’ve ever had to clean up the mess after a drive-by sale, what did you wish someone had done differently?

Drop your answer, or tag a seller or CS pro who actually stood up and stayed in the room when things got hard.

Buyers remember the pain. Sellers have a choice: add to it, or heal it.
