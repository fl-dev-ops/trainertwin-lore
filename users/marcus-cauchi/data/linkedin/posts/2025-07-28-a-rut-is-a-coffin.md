A rut is a coffin with both ends kicked out.
You weren’t lazy.
 You were loyal.
 To a system that praised your endurance… but never protected your aliveness.

You keep showing up.
 Keep adapting.
 Keep delivering on outcomes that weren’t yours to own.

Until something inside you whispers:
“This isn’t what I said yes to.”
But by now the rut has walls.
 And the walls have rituals:
The calendar that rewards attendance not attention.
The leadership that celebrates resilience, not resonance.
The KPIs that track speed, but never soul.

And here’s the dangerous part:
The longer you stay in the rut, 
 the more it starts to feel like a calling.
Until the dissonance becomes data.
 Until your energy can’t lie anymore.
 Until the “success” you built starts to taste like grief.

So let’s name it.
You’re not stuck.
 You’re spinning.
 You’re scaling.
 But the direction?
 That’s what hurts.

If your calendar is full but your spirit is bankrupt,
 it’s not a workload problem.
 It’s a worldview problem.

Ask yourself gently:
Where have I confused repetition with purpose?
What truth have I delayed because it didn’t fit the metrics?
What’s the cost of looking competent while feeling completely misaligned?

You don’t need to burn it all down.
 But you might need to design a way out.
 A ladder. A lens. A new logic.

Because this?
 This isn’t leadership.
 It’s survival in a costume.

Your turn:
No performance. No proof.
 Just honesty:

What’s one rut you’re ready to outgrow?
 And what helped you remember your way out?
