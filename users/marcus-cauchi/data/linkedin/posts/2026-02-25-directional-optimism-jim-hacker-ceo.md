Directional Optimism

Jim Hacker – CEO
Humphrey Appleby – CRO
Frank Gordon – CFO
Bernard Woolley – Head of Sales Operations

CEO OFFICE – LATE AFTERNOON
Jim stands at the window. Forecast deck open on the screen.
Humphrey sits composed.
Frank has a thin finance folder.
Bernard holds a tablet.
JIM
Our win rate is ten percent.

HUMPHREY
Which means we are vigorously competing in ninety percent of opportunities.

FRANK
It also means ninety percent of cost of sale produces no revenue.
Silence.

JIM
Margin is down three points. Forecast variance widened again this quarter.

HUMPHREY
The environment is challenging. Buyers are cautious.

FRANK
Cash collection is late because half the “commits” were not ready to close.

JIM
Why were they commits?

HUMPHREY
To maintain executive confidence.

JIM
Confidence in what?
Bernard glances at his tablet.

BERNARD
Three regional managers upgraded deals after the forecast call, sir.

JIM
Based on what?

BERNARD
Revised optimism.

FRANK
Optimism does not reconcile to revenue.
Humphrey shifts slightly.

HUMPHREY
The CRM reflects internal alignment.

FRANK
The CRM reflects internal pressure.

JIM
They say “commit” because we require certainty.

HUMPHREY
Certainty reassures the board.

FRANK
Accuracy reassures the bank.
Beat.

JIM
The team looks exhausted.

HUMPHREY
High performance environments generate visible effort.

BERNARD
They are spending most of their time on deals we will lose.

FRANK
We are forecasting hope, not cash.
Jim turns from the window.

JIM
If airline pilots landed ten percent of flights, we would ground them.

HUMPHREY
Sales is more complex.

FRANK
Sales is more tolerated.
Silence hangs.

JIM
What happens if we allow one room where probability is reported honestly? No punishment. No theatre.

HUMPHREY
That would destabilise the system.

FRANK
Or stabilise the numbers.
Jim looks between them.

JIM
We built this system.
Humphrey holds his gaze.

JIM
We can change it.
Beat.

FRANK
Shall I inform the board we are replacing optimism with accuracy?
Jim nods.
Humphrey exhales, almost imperceptibly.

CUT TO BLACK.
