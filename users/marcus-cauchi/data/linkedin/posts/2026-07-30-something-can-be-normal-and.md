Something can be normal and still be wrong for you

Most institutional investors are not villains. Most advisers are not asleep at the wheel. The problem is more ordinary and more dangerous than that.
Each person is looking at a legitimate part of the deal.

The lawyers focus on legal rights and obligations. The accountants focus on the financial information. The investors focus on returns and protection. Your management team focuses on getting the money and moving forward.

The combined consequence can still remain invisible.

You may discover after signing that you are responsible for delivering the plan but no longer control the budget, hiring or pricing needed to deliver it. You may still own a large percentage of the company but sit behind debt, preferences and future dilution that radically change what your shares are worth.

You may be promised a meaningful “second bite” without being shown the class of equity, who sits ahead of it, what happens if more money is needed or how your position changes if you leave.

You may give an investor access to customers, contracts, staff information, pipeline data and intellectual property before they have made a commitment that justifies the exposure.

None of these problems requires anyone to be dishonest.
They only require nobody to join the whole decision together.
