One of your partners has stopped recommending you. 

They’re still signed up. Still in your reports. They might even turn up to the odd meeting. But when a customer asks who to use, your name doesn’t come up. And you might never know. They learned your products, trained their people and put their name behind your promises. 

Then they found out how long it takes to get a straight answer from you. 

Their people chase yours. Customers wait. The partner pays for that time whether the deal closes or not. So they stop. You call them inactive. They call you an expensive supplier. 

Meanwhile your dashboard looks lovely. 

Someone’s probably preparing a slide on ecosystem momentum! How much of the extra work your partners do because of decisions made inside your company ever gets measured? And when partner revenue disappoints? Someone suggests recruiting more partners. 

What if that’s the last thing you should be spending money on?  There’s a question that comes first: are we actually worth partnering with? 

I wrote Partnerable because the people expected to make partnerships work often can’t change the decisions making them difficult. And the people making those decisions may never see what they cost. 

The book helps leaders examine whether their company is worth partnering with, which partnerships deserve investment, and when it’s time to reconsider. It’s for anyone wondering why the numbers look better than the results feel. 

Try this at your next leadership meeting: how many of your partners would invest in you again, knowing what they know now? And how many have already decided they wouldn’t?
