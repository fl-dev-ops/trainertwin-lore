CAPITALISM PORN: The Mirage of Prosperity and the Price We Pretend Not to See

We’re surrounded by a new kind of economic propaganda. Towering skylines, “run-like-a-business” states, headlines about growth and surplus, glossy stories that sell us a dream and keep us distracted from what’s really happening on the ground. Let’s call it what it is: capitalism porn.

It’s seductive. Fast. Full of spectacle. It wants us to believe that if the cranes are moving, the system must be working. But it’s all surface. The scaffolding, the lives and costs hidden behind the lights, never make the highlight reel.

When government tries to be a venture fund, citizens become numbers on a dashboard. Value gets measured in quarterly returns, not in dignity, health, or the freedom to dissent. Invisible workers, migrants, cleaners, “non-contributors”, get erased, exploited, or treated as an afterthought. The winners stay in the spotlight, everyone else is kept off stage.

The pitch? You too can win, if you play the game. But opportunity is doled out by gatekeepers. The system sorts us by utility. If you’re not valuable, you’re replaceable. That’s the blueprint being sold and copied, city by city, country by country.

We trade democracy for “efficiency,” rights for “prosperity,” justice for “growth.” The cost? Human lives turned into line items. Public trust hollowed out. And a generation told to measure progress by the size of the skyline, not by who gets to stand in its shadow. Are you OK being a cash crop for the ultra-wealthy 0.0001%?

We need to ask harder questions. Not just “who benefits?” but “who pays?” Not just “what’s growing?” but “what’s being ground down?” If we don’t, we end up living in someone else’s fantasy and calling it freedom.

Before we buy the next glossy story, let’s get real:
What kind of society do we want our kids to grow up and live in?
