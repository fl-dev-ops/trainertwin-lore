It’s ten o’clock on Monday morning and the work you planned to do is already blown. 

Someone needs you to sort out a customer problem. Two people can’t agree what to do. A decision you delegated last week has landed back on your desk. You step in because something needs sorting. The customer gets an answer, the team gets moving and the immediate problem is dealt with. Until something similar happens again. 

By Friday, you’ve spent another week helping people make decisions, resolve disagreements and deal with problems you hoped they’d be able to handle themselves. You’ve delegated. You’ve encouraged people to take responsibility. You’ve told them they can make decisions without asking you. 

So why do so many decisions still come back? 

Sometimes people genuinely need your help. Sometimes they don’t have the authority, information, experience or support to act without it. And sometimes the way you’ve been helping means they haven’t had the opportunity to develop their own judgement. 

When you step in to solve a problem, what changes that makes it less likely they’ll need you to solve the same problem next time? 

That’s why I wrote Make Heroics Unnecessary: How Managers Build Teams That Perform Without Rescue. 

It’s for managers who want to develop people who can think, make sound decisions and take responsibility, while knowing when they genuinely need help. Because solving today’s problem matters. So does making sure your people are better equipped to handle tomorrow’s.
