When a decision closes, not everything ends with it.

The moment passes.
The forecast updates.
Everyone moves on.

That is where most attention stops.

What follows is less visible.

Someone has to make the decision work in practice.
Someone has to deal with what wasn’t fully settled.
Someone has to handle what only appears once things are in use.

That work doesn’t arrive as a failure.
It shows up as coordination.
As follow-ups.
As exceptions that need handling.

Nothing about the decision feels wrong.

But the effort required to sustain it spreads.

From the decision into execution.
From agreement into daily work.
From one team into several others.

No one assigns this work.
It appears where things are expected to continue.

The people who receive it don’t question the decision.
They make it work.

Months later, the organisation feels more loaded than expected.

More effort is required just to maintain what was agreed.

People ask what went wrong.

They rarely ask a different question.

What carried on
after the decision was considered finished?
