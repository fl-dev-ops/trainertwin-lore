Antonio Garrido’s post made me think about how often companies treat recurring friction as something to eliminate instead of something to understand.

You see it everywhere: teams building covert workarounds instead of using the official process, customers repeatedly asking for things that sit outside the roadmap, or someone in meetings raising the same uncomfortable point until people start visibly losing patience with it.

And you definitely see it in forecasting.

I’d laugh at some of the stories if I wasn’t hearing versions of them every day from clients.

One Head of Sales running a multibillion P&L gets the same reaction from their CEO every time they talk honestly about pipeline:

“This will/won’t land well with the Board.”

A very experienced CRO told me another one recently. Proper pipeline review. Reality lands. Pipeline is down 65.9%. The CEO’s immediate response:

“I can’t take that number upstairs.”

So the CRO said, “No problem. What number do you want? I’ll move a few big deals up a stage and we’ll fix it.”

The CEO said, “You can’t do that.”

Exactly.

But that’s what had been happening for years. Just less obviously. That’s where the conversation usually goes wrong. The business gets shown a signal, but leadership reacts to the discomfort of the signal instead of the reason for it.

So the real questions get pushed aside. Is the pipeline real? Has coverage actually collapsed? Are deals sitting in the wrong stages? Is creation, conversion, qualification, or deal strategy the actual problem?

Instead, the pressure becomes: make the number survivable.

The uncomfortable signal isn’t being studied. It’s being managed for optics. Weak pipeline isn’t the thing to hide. It’s the thing to understand.

And yes, sometimes the data is wrong. But often the uncomfortable number is telling the truth before the business is ready to hear it.

There’s usually a reason people keep walking across the grass instead of using the path you built for them.
