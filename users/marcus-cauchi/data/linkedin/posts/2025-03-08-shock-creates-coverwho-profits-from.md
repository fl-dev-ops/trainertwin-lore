Shock Creates Cover—Who Profits from Panic?”

	How disaster capital distraction begins: Use crisis as a pretext for rapid, unchecked decisions that benefit the few while the many are too disoriented to push back.

Red Flag Clusters:
	1.	Sudden urgency: “This must happen now, no time for debate.”
	2.	Narrative control: “If you question it, you don’t understand the crisis.”
	3.	Opportunity hoarding: Those in power consolidate control under the guise of “stability.”

3 llusions
	1.	Speed: “We have no choice but to move fast.”
	2.	Consensus: “Everyone agrees this is necessary.”
	3.“Trust us, we’re protecting you.”

Historical Spotlight:
   •   2008 Financial Crisis: Bailouts framed as urgent rescues, but the system reset in favour of those who created the collapse.
   •   Post-9/11 Legislation: Sweeping surveillance laws justified by fear, with no sunset clause.
   •   COVID-19 Recovery Funds: Rapid capital injections disproportionately benefitted corporations over small businesses.
