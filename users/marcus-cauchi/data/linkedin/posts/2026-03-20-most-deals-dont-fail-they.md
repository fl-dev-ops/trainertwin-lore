Most deals don’t fail. They decay.

They start strong. The conversations are good. There is clear interest. People nod. It feels like momentum is building and that this one will move.

Then something changes.

Replies take longer. Next steps drift. Energy drops out of the process. No one says no, but nothing moves forward either. From the outside, it still looks alive. Inside, it’s already breaking down.

Most sellers respond the same way. They push harder. More follow-ups. More urgency. More pressure to get it over the line.

It doesn’t work.

Because the problem isn’t in the conversation. It’s what happens after the conversation, inside the business, when the buyer has to explain the decision to other people.

That’s where most deals fail.

Not because the solution is wrong, but because the decision isn’t safe enough. The risks aren’t clear. The proof isn’t strong enough. Ownership isn’t defined. The story doesn’t hold up under scrutiny.

So the deal stalls. Not lost, just not survivable.

And in this market, that’s expensive.

Because bad decisions don’t hide anymore. They show up later in slow adoption, missed forecasts, weak renewals, and lost expansion.

That’s not a sales problem.

It’s a decision problem.

If your buyer can’t defend it internally, you don’t have a deal. You have future regret.
