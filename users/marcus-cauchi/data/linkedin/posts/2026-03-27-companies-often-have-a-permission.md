Companies often have a permission problem disguised as a revenue problem.

The people who can see the damage coming usually cannot stop the deal.

Delivery sees the timeline is fantasy.
CS sees the customer is a poor fit.
Finance sees the margin is fiction.
Product sees the promise is ahead of reality.

They raise it. Carefully. Professionally. Early enough to matter.

And the deal closes anyway.

Not because anyone shouted them down.
Because their challenge had no power to change the outcome.

That is what makes this expensive.

The business calls it alignment.
The process calls it collaboration.
The forecast calls it progress.

But what is really happening is simpler:

Some people are being paid to create revenue.
Other people are being left to survive it.

If the people who inherit the consequences cannot stop the deal before signature, the business is not qualifying risk.

It is staging optimism.

And if that feels uncomfortably familiar, this piece is for you.
