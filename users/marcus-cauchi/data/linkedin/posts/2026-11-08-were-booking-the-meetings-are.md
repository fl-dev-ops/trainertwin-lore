We’re booking the meetings. Are we winning the right business?

Your SDRs can hit their targets and your AEs can close deals, yet you can still end up with customers you wish you hadn’t won.

It’s easy to call that a meeting-quality problem.

Sometimes it is. But sometimes the meeting was reasonable and things went wrong later.

Maybe the account was never a good fit. Maybe sales misunderstood what the customer needed. Maybe the discount wiped out the margin. Or delivery was left trying to keep promises it couldn’t reasonably fulfil.

You won’t fix all of those problems by telling SDRs to book better meetings.

So before changing targets or qualification rules, wouldn’t you want to know where things actually went wrong?

Take the meetings booked during a period at least two or three quarters ago. Go further back if your sales or renewal cycles require it. Don’t cherry-pick the successes or failures, and be clear about what you still don’t know.

Follow them through.

Which meetings were held and accepted by AEs? Which became real opportunities? Which customers bought? And, knowing what happened after the sale, which would you be happy to win again?

Now go back to what people knew at the time.

Was the account a poor fit from the start? Could the SDR reasonably have known?

Did important information get lost between the SDR and the AE?

Did something go wrong later, during selling, pricing, contracting or delivery?

Or did someone make a perfectly sensible decision that simply didn’t work out?

Each answer points to a different fix. But there’s a bigger question.

What did the AEs learn about those customers? What did delivery discover? What did finance find out about the cost of winning and serving them? What did renewal reveal?

How much of that information ever got back to the people finding and booking the next customer?

You might need more meetings. But you could also be paying people to repeat decisions the business already has good reason to question.

What have the people booking tomorrow’s meetings learned from the customers you won last year?
