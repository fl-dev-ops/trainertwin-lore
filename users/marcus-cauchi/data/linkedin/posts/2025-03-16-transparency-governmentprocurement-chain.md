#Transparency #GovernmentProcurement #Chainsaw

If a private contractor’s failure led to the wrongful conviction of 700+ people, what specific action should disqualify them from future government contracts?

What formal criteria does the government use to assess whether a company’s past misconduct makes them unfit for further contracts?

If this company remains essential despite its failures, does this prove that government procurement is incapable of fostering competitive alternatives?

If government contracts are meant to serve the public interest, how does awarding £6.8bn to a company responsible for a national scandal uphold that principle?

If the system is committed to reform, what specific policies have changed to prevent another Horizon-style disaster?

What prevents other government contractors from seeing this case as proof that reputational damage is temporary, and accountability is optional?

If this exact scenario happened again with a different company in five years, what would be different in the government’s response?
