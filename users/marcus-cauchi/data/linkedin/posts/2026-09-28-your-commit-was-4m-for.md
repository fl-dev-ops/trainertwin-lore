Your Commit was £4m. For the sake of the example, suppose the quarter closes at £3m.
The £1m didn’t arrive. The question is what the business does next.

Decisions were made during the quarter on the strength of that forecast. That doesn’t mean they were wrong when they were made. But things have happened since.
Some commitments haven’t been made yet, so you can still stop.
Some have been made but can still be changed, at a cost. A supplier agreement might be cancellable, but with a charge.
Others have already had consequences you can’t undo. Someone has left another job and joined you. Capacity has been committed and other work turned away.

And time matters. Something you can stop cheaply now may cost considerably more a few weeks later.

Other decisions can follow too. Bring someone in early and you may need equipment, onboarding and work ready for them. Commit capacity to expected work and you may turn other work away. Spend cash you expected to replace from the quarter and the shortfall may force another decision about what gets delayed or how the gap is funded.

Then comes another decision: what to do because the number missed.
Freeze recruitment. Delay spending. Change the forecast process.

Any of those might be sensible. But the miss doesn’t prove that any of them is.
The miss is new evidence. It’s evidence of a shortfall, not of a cause. By itself, it doesn’t tell you what to do next.

So ask:
What has actually changed?
What can still be stopped or changed, and at what cost?
What evidence does the next decision need?
Some things can still be stopped. Some will cost you to change. Some have already had consequences you can’t undo.

Before you make the next decision, work out which is which.
