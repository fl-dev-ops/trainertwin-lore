Badly applied AI doesn't make better decisions. It makes weak evidence look safe enough to act on. A scraped fact becomes "personalisation". A click becomes "intent" A polite reply becomes "pipeline". A meeting becomes "momentum".

The needle on the dashboard moves and everyone convince themselves the machine is working.

It isn't. It's just shoving the cost somewhere else.

Buyers pay with wasted attention and irrelevant interruption. Your reps pay by chasing and building a funnel of dead work and non-customers. Managers pay with hours of inspection. RevOps and CS pay by cleaning up the mess. Finance pays when your forecast turns out to be fiction.

What's really broken?

Not the copy. The cadences may piss people off but it's not them either. Nor is the tool at fault. The problem begins much earlier.

Weak evidence is being allowed into the system and AI makes it travel faster.

The worst question is, "What can AI help us produce faster?" That's how teams scale idiocy.

Ask, "What should never reach the buyer?". 
Then ask, "What should never enter our system?" 

That's where badly applied AI breaks your business, your team's morale and your customers' trust in your brand.

Stop weak evidence becoming outreach. 
Prevent curiosity becoming "demand". 
And stop interrupting your buyers with seller activity dressed up as "buyer progress".

AI shouldn't make the machine louder. 
It should stop your GTM lying to you.
