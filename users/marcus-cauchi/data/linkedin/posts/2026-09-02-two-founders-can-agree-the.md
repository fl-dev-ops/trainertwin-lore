Two founders can agree the same £50m valuation and end up making very different decisions.

One keeps broad authority over budget, hiring, pricing and future funding. Another needs approval for decisions they are still expected to deliver.
One has relatively simple economics if the plan misses. Another discovers that debt, preferences, dilution or rollover behave very differently once the outcome disappoints.

The valuation hasn’t changed.
The decision has.

That is why I wouldn’t ask only, “Is this a good deal?”

I’d want three other questions answered before the transaction becomes difficult to change:
What happens to my economics if the plan misses?
Which decisions do I still have the authority to make?
Which options will still be available if the next few years don’t unfold as expected?

None of this assumes the investor is bad. It assumes the decision is bigger than the headline number.

Before you take institutional capital, find out what comes with it.
