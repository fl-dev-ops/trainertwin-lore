When Does an Approved Decision Stop Being Approved?

Getting a big decision through an investment committee can take weeks or months. The evidence gets tested, assumptions get challenged and risks get discussed. Eventually there is enough confidence to say yes.

Then the world moves on. Revenue misses. A key hire fails. Financing changes. A customer disappears.

Sometimes nothing dramatic happens. The decision itself just gets bigger, harder to reverse or more dependent on things that still have to go right.
The cat problem gets harder once you've already gone through one door and committed to the route.

This is where we ask the wrong question.
"Is the original thesis still right?"

That can turn into months of argument.

I'd ask something more useful: "If this decision came back today, knowing what we know now, would we approve the same commitment on the same terms?"

That matters for PE. It also matters for Family Offices making follow-on investments into businesses they already know well.

Yesterday's approval should not automatically become today's permission.

If the evidence still supports the commitment, carry on. If it supports a different size, pace or structure, that matters. If the evidence no longer supports the commitment, the fact that you approved it six months ago does not make today's decision yes.

And you don't need bad news to justify reopening it.

£10m becomes £20m. A minority position becomes control. A reversible move becomes difficult to unwind.
The next £10m has not been spent yet.

At that point, you're no longer making the decision you originally approved.

The real question is not whether yesterday's decision was right. It is whether today's commitment is still justified by what you know today.
