Before recruiting more partners, find out whether your company is ready to be relied on.

The case for partners is easy to make. They can extend reach, bring local knowledge, open customer doors, provide implementation capacity and create growth without equivalent headcount.

The spreadsheet likes that story because it looks like leverage.

The partner sees something else. They see the risk of taking your offer to their customer, using their people, putting their reputation behind your claims and depending on your company after the contract is signed.

That is why partner strategy has to begin before recruitment.

You need to know why partners are necessary, what job they are expected to perform, what the vendor must continue to own, why a credible partner should choose the relationship and what evidence shows that both sides can rely on each other.

You also need clear ownership after the sale. Someone must know who is responsible for delivery, adoption, failure and recovery.

A small group of committed partners can help you learn while the model is still being built. They can show you where the friction is and where the promise does not survive contact with the customer.

Scaling is different. Scaling multiplies every weak claim, unclear handoff, poor incentive and unresolved ownership gap.

Do not scale the number of partners before you have earned the right to scale reliance.

The question to ask before approving the next recruitment target is simple.
Are we worth partnering with?
