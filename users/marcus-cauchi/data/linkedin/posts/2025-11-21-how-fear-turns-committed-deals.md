How fear turns committed deals into ghosts
The first time I saw fear break a forecast, it wasn’t a rep.

It was a manager.
We walked out of a team meeting.
 People were chatting.
 Energy was good.
 Numbers looked fine.

She leaned in and said, quiet,
 “I can tell when a deal is slipping.
 The rep freezes for a second,
 then says it’s fine.”

That tiny pause is when a deal becomes a ghost.

Not when the buyer backs out.
 Not when the date moves.
 It happens in that moment where truth gets swapped for whatever feels safer.

Reps don’t overcommit because they want to impress you.
They do it because they’re scared.
 Scared of looking unsure.
 Scared of slowing the room.
 Scared of what you’ll think if they name the wobble.

So they smooth the update.
 They hide the doubt.
 They tuck away the early signs.
 And the deal drifts while everyone is still smiling.

The real cost isn’t the miss.
It’s the silence after it.
 Managers start guessing.
 Reps stop asking for help.
 Founders fly half blind.
 And the whole place tightens a little each week.

Here is the fix.
Stop asking, Is this committed?.
 That question makes people perform.

Ask, What one difference would make you more confident than you are right now? (say nothing, wait for their answer)
 That question makes people honest.
The room softens.

People breathe.
 They stop acting.
 They say what’s real.
 And ghosts turn back into deals you can actually coach.

Try that question once this week.
 One small pocket of safety.
You’ll see the ghosts before they disappear.
