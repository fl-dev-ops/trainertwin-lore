Forecasts fail long before the number misses.

They fail when seller confidence gets treated like buyer evidence. A good meeting is not progress. Pipeline coverage is not forecast quality. CRM movement is not buyer movement.

Weak evidence is expensive.

It drives hiring plans, spend decisions, and board confidence from assumptions that have not earned the weight.

The question that changes forecast reviews, “Which part of this forecast is buyer-owned evidence, and which part is still seller confidence?”

That question exposes a lot.
