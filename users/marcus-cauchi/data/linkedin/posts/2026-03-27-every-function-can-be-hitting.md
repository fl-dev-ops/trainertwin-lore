Every function can be hitting its number while the business gets harder to run.

That is not healthy growth.
That is revenue debt.

Marketing generates demand.
Sales closes deals.
Product ships.
Customer Success rescues accounts.
Delivery absorbs strain.
Partners keep volume moving.

Everyone can point to a clean local result.

And still the company gets weaker.

That is the dangerous part. This pattern rarely arrives looking like failure. It arrives looking like performance.

The wrong customer enters as pipeline, becomes a celebrated win, turns into a difficult implementation, then a rescue plan, then a churn explanation. The label changes at each handoff. The cost does not.

By the time the problem becomes visible in Delivery, Customer Success, or Finance, the original error has already been renamed and pushed downstream. What should have been stopped at qualification is now being managed as complexity. What should have been challenged before signature is now being carried by people who never had the power to refuse it.

This is why bad revenue looks good at first.
It flatters the pipeline.
It helps the quarter.
It supports the story.

Then the bill arrives as rework, concessions, slower time to value, thinner margin, noisier forecasts, tired teams, and customers who never become references.

I wrote a piece on why this keeps happening, and why the issue is usually not execution.

It is incentive design.

If your teams are winning locally while the business is paying globally, this is for you.
