Resharing CJ Gustafson's excellent post. Compression hits smaller operators before they realise it.

When capital tightens, lenders and investors stop rewarding growth narratives. They start measuring cash flow durability and cost of growth.

In the 5M to 20M range, expansion often runs ahead of governance. Headcount grows. Tools multiply. Working capital stretches. Capital allocation feels flexible.

By the time efficiency becomes urgent, the cost structure and customer mix are already embedded.

Markets do not create discipline. They expose where it was missing.

If win rates stall, sales cycles extend and cost per dollar of growth increases at the same time, that is not a market problem. It is a system design problem.

Durability is built before pressure arrives.

If capital tightened tomorrow, where would the fragility show first?
