How long do you give a big change before you decide whether it’s working? 

Sometimes the answer is longer than you’d like. People need time to learn a new way of working. Your customers don’t suddenly change how they buy because you’ve changed how you sell. New products need enough exposure to real customers before the numbers mean much. 

So waiting can make perfect sense. The question is, what are you expecting to learn while you wait? If the result you want won’t show up for six months, what should you be seeing after one month? What should be different after three? What would give you good reason to keep going, and what would make you change your mind? 

Say you’ve changed the way you sell. Revenue may take months to come through, but you can start learning much earlier. 

Are you reaching the customers you expected? Do they care about the problems you thought they would? You can see whether the right people are getting involved and whether the opportunities you’re creating are behaving the way you expected. None of that proves you’ve got it right. It does give you something better than, “We need to give it more time.” 

There’s another question worth asking before you start: what would you have to see to decide that carrying on unchanged no longer made sense? 

That matters because once we’ve spent money on something, put people behind it and told everyone this is the plan, changing our minds gets harder. Another month can easily become another quarter. By then you may know more, but you’ve also spent another quarter on the assumption that you’re heading in the right direction. 

So perhaps the question isn’t, “Have we given this enough time?” Perhaps it’s, “What have we learned that tells us what to do next?” 

Sometimes the answer will be to keep going and give it more time. Fine. At least you know why you’re waiting, what you’re looking for, and what would make you change your mind.
