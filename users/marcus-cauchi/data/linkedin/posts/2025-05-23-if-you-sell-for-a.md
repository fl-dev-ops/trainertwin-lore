  William Lopez Leah Borges. You are both speaking from truth, but from different sides of the same fault line.

  William, you named the structural flaw: too many roles, not enough responsibility. The customer is passed around like a parcel. Context is lost. Costs spiral. No one owns the outcome.

  Leah, you brought the emotional intelligence. You called out the quiet erosion. That sense that it is not your talent or effort failing, but a system that was never designed to hold real trust.

  Taiichi Ohno of the Toyota Way fame, said that they system must empower the human operator to perform to standard> If they aren't, it's the system. Culture, business model, metrics tracked, compensation, organisational structure are usually built for the convenience of shareholders and the business ... not the convenience of customers, and not to serve their people to execute excellently.

  Here is the missing link:
  The problem with silos isn’t the walls. It’s the drops in the gaps.

  Every time a handover happens without care, someone gets dropped in the muck and blamed, usually the rep, or the customer. That’s when trust bleeds out. That is when deals die without a sound.
  It is not just an issue of process. It is an issue of coherence.
