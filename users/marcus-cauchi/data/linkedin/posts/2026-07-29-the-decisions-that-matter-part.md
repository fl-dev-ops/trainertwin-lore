The Decisions That Matter
Part 9: Should We Believe Ourselves?

The easiest person to persuade is often the one looking back from the mirror.

Pressure changes how we interpret evidence. We explain away warning signs because the opportunity matters. We mistake silence for agreement because the forecast needs good news. We remember information that supports our view and quietly overlook the information that challenges it.

The strongest decision-makers don’t trust themselves less. They test themselves more. They actively search for evidence that would prove them wrong because weak assumptions are much cheaper to lose than expensive decisions.

Confidence is valuable, but only when it has survived the hardest questions you can ask yourself.
