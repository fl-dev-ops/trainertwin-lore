This came up in a conversation about lead prioritisation.

The team had tightened routing and scoring months earlier. Accounts were ranked. Reps knew where to focus. It had been running that way long enough that it felt normal. It felt efficient. It felt settled.

Then someone asked why these accounts were at the top and not the others.

There was a short pause.

Someone said, “This is where the data’s been pointing us.”

That was true. The signals all made sense. Size. Behaviour. Timing. Past wins. Everything lined up with how they’d been ranking accounts before.

What no one could say was whether those signals still matched how buyers were actually behaving now.

As long as the list produced activity, it didn’t really come up. Reps worked it. Managers reported on it. Leadership looked at the numbers and moved on.

The gap only showed itself when someone tried to explain the focus without leaning on the data. That’s when it became clear the decision hadn’t been revisited in a while. It hadn’t been challenged. It had just kept running.

No one had chosen these accounts recently. They’d just kept following them. And by the time that felt uncomfortable, the pipeline and the motion were already shaped around it.
