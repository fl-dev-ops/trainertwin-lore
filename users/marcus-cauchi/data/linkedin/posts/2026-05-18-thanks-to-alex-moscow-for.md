My take is this is useful, but manager engagement isn’t the real issue. It’s the signal.

A lot of managers haven’t stopped caring. They’re stuck in the compression layer between board ambition and frontline reality.

Strategy changes. Budgets tighten. AI gets dropped in. Headcount gets questioned. Customers still expect delivery. Teams still need reassurance. The number still has to be hit.

Then managers are expected to turn all of that into calm, coherent execution.
That’s not a motivation problem. It’s a system design problem.

The bit that gets missed is what happens next.

Managers start carrying the load for the contradictions the business hasn’t resolved. They smooth the message upward. They soften the risk downward. They keep the machine moving because stopping to tell the truth feels more dangerous than carrying on.

That’s how disengagement becomes commercial risk.

Not because managers suddenly became weak. But because the business trained them to preserve confidence after contextual and commercial reality changed.

If managers are the multiplier of performance, stop treating them like shock absorbers. Give them fewer priorities, clearer trade-offs, real decision rights, better coaching, and permission to say “this won’t work” before the evidence becomes expensive.

Disengagement isn’t the disease. It’s where the system is telling you the truth. Heed the warning or pay the consequences.
