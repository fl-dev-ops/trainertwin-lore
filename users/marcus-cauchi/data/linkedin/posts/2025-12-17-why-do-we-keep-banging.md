Why do we keep banging our heads against the wall, then blame the wall for the headache?

I keep seeing the same story play out.

Hundreds of calls. Nothing lands.
And the response is applause. APPLAUSE for creating dead work, waste and interrupting busy people with badly timed irrelevances.

That’s the activity trap fuelled by metrics porn and zero thinking. It's compliance and idiocy at scale. It looks like effort. It feels like discipline. Yet the pipeline is empty or full of the wrong prospects.

If someone makes 500 calls and nothing moves, the takeaway isn’t grit.
It’s a warning siren with flashing red lights.

Most of the time the issue isn’t rejection. It’s that nothing in the call gives the other person a reason to stay.

They aren’t deciding no. They’re deciding this doesn’t help me. It’s badly timed. It’s irrelevant. It’s an unwelcome interruption.

Sales stalls now because decision confidence collapses. Karl Schmidt and I were talking about this only last night.

People are flooded with information. Getting agreement internally is fragile. The personal cost of choosing the wrong option is real. In that context, doing nothing feels safer than acting.

So when an SDR or AE manager’s response is “just push through”, that isn’t leadership. It’s anxiety passed down the chain. It’s abdication and mismanagement.

The buyer fears loss of control. The seller becomes an interruption. The market gets worn down call by call. The waste of time, money, resource and viable leads borders on criminal.

If nothing changes between call ten and call four hundred, that isn’t learning.
It’s blind repetition.

The same miss, practised until it feels normal. My golf game doesn’t improve if I keep shanking and make it habit. Note to self, book some lessons.

Real progress doesn’t look dramatic

It looks like knowing who not to call. Filtering out non ICP and anti ICP leads often recovers most dial time. It looks like a reason for this person, now.
CS and product teams already know who, when, and why. Map to the buyer's journey not your quota. It looks like a call that earns a pause instead of a hang-up. It looks like something the buyer can explain without you present

If your message can’t travel through the second and third rooms unedited, it’s dead. What works only in the first conversation dies in the second

If the buyer can’t defend the decision internally, the deal ends quietly.
The average deal involves 16 stakeholders. I had one client report 42

Pressure doesn’t speed this up
It shuts it down

This isn’t purely an SDR issue. It sits higher

Leaders decide what counts as good work
They choose what gets praised and rewarded
They shape whether teams think or just dial

Reward activity and you get noise.
Reward judgment and improvement becomes possible.

Sales isn’t about enduring no’s.
It’s about earning yeses that still make sense tomorrow.

If all that’s happening is more calls and louder encouragement, the wall hasn’t changed.
Only the headache has.
