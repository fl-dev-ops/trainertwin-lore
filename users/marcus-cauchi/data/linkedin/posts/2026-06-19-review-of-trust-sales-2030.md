Review of Trust: Sales 2030 by Richard Spanier

Trust: Sales 2030 is a timely critique of modern sales. Richard Spanier argues that sales does not have a technology, productivity or lead generation problem first. It has a trust problem.

https://lnkd.in/eCGqaYvV

His central point is simple. Buyers have changed faster than sales systems. 

They research independently, compare options, ask peers, avoid interruption and engage sellers later. Yet many sales organisations still rely on volume, urgency, funnel movement and seller-controlled process.

Spanier’s answer is Frictionless By Design. FBD aims to reduce unnecessary friction so buyers can learn, evaluate and engage with less pressure and more clarity.

The book is strongest in its diagnosis. Spanier’s critique of the funnel is sharp. 

The funnel may help sellers report activity, but buyers do not move through it neatly. They loop, stall, return, compare and decide in their own way.
The pipeline chapter is also strong. Spanier shows how pipeline can become emotional theatre. Weak evidence becomes “good conversations.” Silence becomes “continued interest.” Forecasts become stories people tell under pressure.

The practical core is the landing page as a decision environment. In FBD, the landing page is not a campaign asset. It helps a specific buyer understand a specific problem, risk and next step before speaking to sales.

The BRAVE content framework also works. Content should be business-oriented, relevant, authoritative, valuable and evidence-based. Its job is not to fill channels. Its job is to help buyers think.

The book shows why the old model is failing and what a better direction looks like. It is less detailed on how leaders change incentives, evidence standards, management behaviour and governance.

FBD helps buyers move with less friction. The next question is whether the buyer can defend, implement and live with the decision after the sale.

Both of these are topics we will explore when Richard is my guest on TheInquisitor Podcast. Out in July.

Trust: Sales 2030 is a serious and useful contribution. It is not another book about better scripts or sharper cadences. It asks a better question: what if the sales model is creating the resistance it is trying to overcome?

Spanier’s best idea is that buyers are not difficult. They are behaving reasonably. They avoid sales experiences that create pressure, confusion, wasted effort or risk.

The future will not belong to the companies that automate the most outreach. It will belong to the companies that make themselves easiest to trust.
