One of the things I hear all the time from agency owners is, “My account managers aren’t commercial enough.”

Before concluding the account manager is the problem, it’s useful to take a deeper look at whether they are set up for success to do it:

- Are they expected to grow the account while spending most of their time delivering projects in a hybrid AM/PM role? 

- Are they running weekly status calls and expected to turn those into strategic conversations? 

- Do they understand enough about the client’s business to have those conversations? 

- Do they have the time and support to do it?

I was talking about this with Marcus Cauchi on TheInquisitor Podcast and the work I’ve been doing with my clients on an account growth strategy prior to any training.

Account managers can be seen by clients as  “delivery reliable” but not “business relevant” in their interactions.

The work gets done and the client isn’t necessarily unhappy. 

But you’re not bringing ideas anymore. 

You’re not asking particularly useful questions. 

You don’t really know what’s changing in their world.

Then meetings start getting cancelled. 

Emails take longer to come back. 

The client starts speaking to somebody more senior. And the agency owner suddenly thinks, what the hell happened?

So when an important account stops growing, my first question is: why?

Has something changed in the client’s business? 

Has their budget gone somewhere else? 

Are we talking to the wrong people? 

Have we dropped the ball? 

Is the account manager genuinely out of their depth? 

Or have we put them in a role where they were never properly enabled to do what we’re now blaming them for not doing?

Quite often there’s more than one thing going on.

That’s why I look at commercial clarity, capability and cultural continuity together. 

You can train somebody, but if the role, the management around them or the client relationship is working against them, the training isn’t going to solve very much.

And sometimes the most useful thing you can do is much simpler.

Go and talk properly to the client.

Find out what has changed, what matters now, who else matters inside their business and whether you’re still as useful to them as you think you were.
