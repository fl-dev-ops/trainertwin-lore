Carole’s right, and this is where a lot of sales hiring goes wrong.

Companies say they want reps who can handle pressure, think clearly, and adapt in the moment. Then they hire for polish, confidence, and answers that sound good in a friendly room. So they end up selecting for interview performance, not selling.

That misses the real job. Buyers push back. They go quiet. They change the rules. They introduce doubt, delay, politics, and risk. The real question is not who comes across well when the conversation is easy. It is who stays clear, curious, and composed when it stops going their way.

Part of the problem is that too many hiring managers have never been taught how to assess for selling, only how to run an interview.

If you don't test for the work, you'll keep hiring for theatre.
