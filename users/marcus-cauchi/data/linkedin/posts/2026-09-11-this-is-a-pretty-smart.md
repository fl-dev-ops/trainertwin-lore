This is a pretty smart picture of where AI in sales is heading. I think it also reveals a problem with how we are defining maturity.
 
More autonomy does not automatically mean more maturity.
 
Going from 50 activities to 500 may be a huge improvement. So might cutting research from 30 minutes to two, automating routine follow-up, qualifying accounts faster and responding while the buyer is still paying attention. AI can remove enormous amounts of waste and reduce plenty of human error.
 
The question is what happens when the judgement underneath the automation is wrong.
 
A seller might make one poor call. A system can repeat the same judgement across hundreds or thousands of accounts before anyone realises there is a problem. Faster execution increases the value of being right, and it can also increase the reach of being wrong.
 
That's why I struggle with “autonomous revenue” as the destination. We can automate more and more of the seller's work, but revenue still requires another party to decide. The buyer still has to judge the evidence, weigh competing interests, deal with risk, get other people comfortable and decide whether they are willing to live with the consequences.
 
There is another risk as we remove more work from humans. If people stop practising research, qualification, judgement and exception handling for long enough, the person supposedly “in the loop” may no longer be able to recognise when the system has got something important wrong.
 
That doesn't mean we should preserve work for its own sake. It means capability and authority are different things.
 
A system may be capable of researching, recommending, writing, qualifying, pricing or acting. Before giving it greater authority, I want to know what evidence supports that decision, what consequences it can create, whether somebody competent can challenge it, and whether we can recover if we're wrong.
 
For me, that's the more useful definition of AI maturity.
 
The question isn't how much work the AI can perform. It's how much consequence we are justified in letting it create.
