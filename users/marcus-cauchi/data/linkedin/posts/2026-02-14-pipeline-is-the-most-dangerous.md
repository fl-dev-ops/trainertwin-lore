Pipeline Is the Most Dangerous Vanity Metric in Sales

Be honest. You don’t actually believe your pipeline. You believe the feeling it gives you. Big number. Big coverage. Temporary relief. You’ve sat in that forecast meeting. The slide goes up. The total looks healthy. You relax. Not because it’s real. Because it’s large.

You don’t ask: Who actually owns this decision? Who carries the risk if it fails? What would have to be true for this to collapse? Why hasn’t procurement surfaced yet?

You just move on. Because questioning it would shrink it. And shrinking it would feel dangerous. So instead, you add more. It’s easier to create three new Stage 2 deals than to downgrade one shaky Stage 4.

So the pipeline grows. And forecast integrity declines in tiny increments. Then the quarter slips.

And the instruction is automatic: “Build more pipeline.”

Which really means: Lower ICP. Increase cold outreach. Advance hope. Push marginal deals.

Next year:
Support load rises. Churn rises. Hiring rises. Attrition rises. GRR slips. Forecast variance widens. You call it growth pressure. It’s structural debt. Pipeline inflation doesn’t protect revenue. It postpones truth.

Here’s the tricky trade:
If your pipeline fell by 40% tomorrow but your GRR rose to 95% and your forecast variance halved, your company would be worth more. But you would feel exposed. That’s why bloated pipelines survive. They comfort ego.
Clean pipelines expose reality. Coverage feels like control. Integrity is control.

Which one are you optimising?
