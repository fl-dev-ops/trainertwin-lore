Discovery isn't about finding pain. Pain is already there.

If pain created change, buyers wouldn't need you. They would've changed before you arrived.

The real mistake is believing pain explains why nothing moves. It doesn't.

Pain is a feeling. Constraints are facts.

Pain can be absorbed. Constraints determine what can and can't continue.

When discovery hunts for pain, buyers defend themselves. They protect credibility. They offer problems that are safe to admit. You get cooperation, not clarity.

Real discovery exposes Decision Mechanics.

It shows where movement breaks, what must give way for change, and why staying the same is already a decision.

Most teams never see this.

Their discovery notes are detailed. Their deals still stall.

Not because buyers don't feel pain. But because no one ever mapped the constraints.

Sellers talk about pain. Operators talk about what can't continue.

Here's the diagnostic.

If your calls feel productive but decisions keep slipping, you're not missing pain. You're missing Decision Mechanics.

You don't earn leverage by exposing discomfort. You earn it by revealing what has to change.

If a buyer leaves your call clearer about what hurts but no clearer about why nothing moves, you didn't run discovery. You ran an interview.
