There comes a point in a job where trying harder stops being the obvious answer. Something's changed, and you can feel it, even if you can’t quite put your finger on what it is yet.

Maybe the role's changed. Maybe your boss has changed. Maybe the deal you thought you had isn’t the deal you’re working under any more. Or maybe you’ve changed enough that something which used to fit now feels wrong in ways you can’t ignore.

This is where people can make very expensive decisions too quickly. They decide that because something has changed, they already know what it means.
If the problem is repairable and you leave, you may be walking away from money, reputation, relationships, influence, options and work you still care about. If the problem is structural and you keep treating it like a rough patch, you can burn another year trying to fix something that was never going to get better.

And “should I stay or should I leave?” can be a lousy question to start with. 

There may be a much better deal to negotiate. The role may be fixable. The reporting line may be the problem. You may be able to protect the parts that still work, change the parts that don’t, test what the market thinks you’re worth, or build a sensible route out before you need one.

Sometimes leaving is absolutely the right answer. Sometimes staying is. And sometimes there’s another answer you couldn’t see because you were forcing yourself to choose between two options too early.

You don’t need some grand five-year career plan. You need to work out what actually changed, what is still worth protecting, what choices are genuinely open to you, and what you’d need to see before risking something valuable.

That’s what Career Pathfinder is for.

One career decision can close a surprising number of doors if you make it before you know which ones you still want open.
