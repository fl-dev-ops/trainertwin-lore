Resharing this from Tom Stearns because it gets something right that most teams underestimate.

Most ICP discussions stay at the definition level. This one stays at the behavioural level. Where I see teams get surprised is not in who they target, but in what that targeting decision sets in motion.

An aspirational ICP does not fail immediately. It fails downstream.

It shows up later as longer onboarding, slower value realisation, higher support load, and internal tension between sales, delivery, and success. Everyone feels the drag, but the original decision is already forgotten. It's seen in CS as more open or unresolved tickets, and churn.

These are the real cost of false positives. Not that they close. That they reshape the system after they do.

This is why exclusions, medians over averages, and saying no early matter so much. They are not sales hygiene. They are decision hygiene.

Strong post, Tom. This is the kind of thinking that protects teams from paying tomorrow for choices that look reasonable today.
