No one needs to hear you claim you’re credible.

Yet listen to how many salespeople open a call.
They introduce their company, list their customers, explain their experience and tell the prospect why they’re worth listening to.
Nobody asked.

You’ve interrupted someone. Filling the first few minutes with reasons they should take you seriously doesn’t make the interruption more welcome.
You can behave credibly in the first conversation. That doesn’t mean you’ve established credibility.

Reliability isn’t something you promise. You demonstrate it over time.

Trust isn’t yours to demand. The other person decides whether you’ve earned it.
They judge you by whether you keep your commitments and how you behave when their interests conflict with yours.

A prospect agreeing to another meeting doesn’t prove they trust you.
A decision-maker saying they understand doesn’t mean the concern they raised has been resolved.

Next time someone objects or questions your judgement, notice what happens.

Do you start defending your credibility, or do you find out what they need to know?
