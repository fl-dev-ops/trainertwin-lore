Reposting this because Brad Wolfe and Scott Schoenfeld are having the debate every RevOps, CRO and CFO should be having, and most companies never do.

The real question underneath their disagreement: what is a CRM actually for?
Not tracking the pipeline. Not managing the relationship. Those are just the visible tasks.

The real job is this: let someone who wasn't in the room trust a decision, without having to trust the person who was.

A forecast call. A board update. Someone taking over a territory. A buyer in diligence. None of them were there for the real conversation. All they've got is what got written down.

Here's why most CRMs fail that job.

They're built for the person filling them in, not the person reading it later. Every field is designed to satisfy today's manager, not next year's sceptical buyer.
They record activity, not commitment. A stage change and a logged call prove the seller did something. They don't prove the buyer decided anything.

Nobody checks whether the rep believes the field matters. A rep ticking a box because they're told to writes different data to a rep who knows a buyer or colleague will one day rely on it. Same system, same process, completely different evidence, because the belief behind the keystroke is different.

Optimism gets rewarded by design. Deal stages are usually self-reported by the person with every reason to look good, with nothing forcing counter-evidence before it moves to committed.

Old data looks as certain as new data. A note from three months ago carries the same weight as one from this morning, with nothing marking how stale it's become.

History gets erased, not kept. When a deal gets re-staged, most systems just show where it is now, not the story of how it got there, or who changed it.

Nobody owns the definitions. "Qualified." "Committed." "Expansion." They mean whatever the person typing today decided they mean, because no one with the authority to enforce consistency owns the record.

And heroics insideously corrupt the whole thing. When someone works extra hard to rescue a shaky deal, the system just says "closed won." It never shows the number only held because someone was compensating for a gap nobody fixed.

None of this is a tooling problem. It's a belief problem, wearing a technology costume.

So here's the real question, and I'd genuinely like to know how people answer it:

If your CRM disappeared tomorrow and a sceptical outsider had to trust your numbers from memory alone, whose memory would they be trusting, and would it hold up?
