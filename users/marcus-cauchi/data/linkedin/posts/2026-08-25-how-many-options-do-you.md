How Many Options Do You Really Have?

I had a conversation yesterday that made me think again about Schrödinger's cat.

Three escape routes sound better than one. Unless they all use the same lock. Then you haven't got three options. You've got one point of failure dressed up as three options.

That gets expensive when there's real money on the table.

A PE firm can have three growth plans that all depend on the same thing going right. A family can spread capital across different managers who all own the same crowded trade. An IC can have several exit routes that all need the same valuation environment.

On paper, that looks like choice. In practice, it can still be one point of failure.
More options ≠ more safety.

The bit I care about is what happens when Plan A starts to fail. Do B, C and D still work, or do they disappear with it?

That's the question worth asking before you commit serious capital.

Not, "What are our alternatives?"
Ask: What could go wrong that would kill several of our alternatives at the same time?

You may still make exactly the same investment. You may still write the same cheque. The difference is that you know whether you genuinely have somewhere else to go if you're wrong.

Because three doors with the same lock are still one door.
