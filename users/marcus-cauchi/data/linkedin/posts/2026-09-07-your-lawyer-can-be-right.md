  title: Your advisors may be right but ...

Your lawyer can be right. Your accountant can be right. Your corporate finance adviser can be right. And the decision can still be unclear.

Your lawyer can explain exactly what the documents mean. Your accountant can explain the numbers. Your adviser can help structure and execute the transaction. Someone still has to take all of those answers and ask a different question: given everything we now know, what should I actually agree to?

A term may be legally normal but affect your practical authority. The model may work but depend on another round arriving on acceptable terms. The valuation may be attractive while the miss case changes what eventually reaches you.

None of that means one of your advisers has failed. It means the founder decision is bigger than any single specialist question. That is the gap I'd want closed before signing.
