MOST OF THE BUYING JOURNEY HAPPENS WHEN YOUR SELLER ISN'T THERE

Most of the buying journey happens when your seller isn't there. That's where deals are won, lost, slowed, or killed. Not in the demo. Not in the proposal review. Not in the CRM update. In the corridor conversations your team never hears.

Deals don't stall randomly. They stall where the buyer can't defend them or explain them safely.

If your proposition can't travel from the first room into the second and third rooms, it doesn't matter how good the call felt. It'll stall in the middle of the funnel, your rep will get ghosted, and another commit will miss the forecast.

That's the risk most teams don't inspect.

They ask what stage it's at. They don't ask if the buyer can defend it when they're not there. They ask for the close date. They don't ask who inside the buyer's business is fighting for it. They ask for the next step. They don't ask whether the buyer is mobilising change, or being pushed through CRM gates.

That's the difference between pipeline activity and buyer commitment. One makes your forecast look healthy. The other makes revenue happen.

Before you trust the number, ask what's really driving the deal.

Is it your pressure, or the buyer's urgency?

Can your champion explain the risk of doing nothing?
***
Follow Marcus Cauchi. I help revenue leaders decide what’s safe to trust before they commit the number.

Do they know who will resist this internally?

Can they defend the cost, timing, trade-offs, and disruption?

Have they created movement without you in the room?

If the answer is no, you don't have a committed deal. You have a conversation your seller is still carrying.

This is how forecasts look credible right up until they aren't.

Most teams don't see it until it's already in the number.
