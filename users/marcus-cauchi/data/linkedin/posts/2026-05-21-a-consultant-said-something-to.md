A consultant said something to me that should make a lot of independent operators since.

"I like the strategic work more."

Five minutes later, he admitted half his business had become repetative training. And that's the trap.

Not failure. Not incompetence. Comfort and boredom. The curse of being good at what you preventing you from evolving.

This often happens to experienced consultants, fractional leaders, RevOps and sales operators. They leave corporate life to do the work they were known and trusted for in their old world, not the work they can scrape up when they fly solo. They build decent businesses, get referred and earn genuine trust.

When they look up they realise they have recreated a job.

Only now they own the printer, the pipeline, chasing payment, and low level existential dread.

Customers buy what's easy to understand: training, workshops, new-hire onboarding, "can you deliver a refresher on sales / discovery / prospecting?" and the occasional objection handling séance where every pretends the real problem is the wording.

The diary fills because the money is tangible. And the traps snaps shut.

Your business rewards your lowest-leverage competence, not your judgement, nor your ability to see patterns your customers are blind to. Your diagnostic skills to get to the heart of what is really broken are glossed over. And your decade(s) of experience and ability to speak truth to leadership where you say, "Training your reps is pointless while the system is chewing them up. Fix the system before wasting penny on training."

They buy only what they already know how to buy.

So you take on the same kind of work, over and over. Groundhog day, until our consultancy become a coffin with both ends kicked out. Technically open, still moving, not quite alive. A rut.

Here's the kicker question.

What would disappear from your calendar if you were only doing your best work and doing what you are capable of doing, do excellently and love doing? And if you knew that was what you could be doing for the next 5-10 years instead fo what you are doing today, what would you stop selling? What work would refuse to take on? Or outsource? What would you turn into an asset? And what would you finally charge for properly?

Who would you be willing to disappoint by no longer being available for the old work?

There comes a point where being useful becomes the problem! Useful gets booked, referred, even thanked. But useful also gets trapped.

The old work fills the diary and trains your market to misunderstand you. Month after month you do work you've outgrown, you struggle to scale, your business becomes harder to sell, and you lose self-respect.

The next stage isn't do more or add capacity. It's design. Design your business around who you've become and the work you should be doing.

Everything else is expensive admin wearing a clever hat.
