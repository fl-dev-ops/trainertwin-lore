If the problem is that we celebrate revenue too early, the answer is not to stop rewarding sales.

It is to change what we treat as evidence.

Because in complex B2B, a signed deal is not proof.

It is a commitment.

The proof comes later:

in implementation  
in adoption  
in whether the promised use case actually works  
in whether the customer would make the same decision again

So a better system separates those moments.

Reward commitment at signature.  
Reward judgment at proof.

That means:

pay at close  
but reserve a portion for outcomes the original sale should have made more likely

Clean implementation.  
Time to value.  
Adoption.  
Retention.  
Expansion after success.

Not every variable.  
Just enough to distinguish good decisions from fragile ones.

Then change how revenue is evaluated.

Not as a point-in-time event.  
As a sequence:

commitment → implementation → adoption → durability

That reveals something most dashboards hide:

not all revenue is equal.

Some compounds.  
Some decays.  
Some gets quietly carried by other teams.

The system decides which one you produce.

So the real question is not about compensation mechanics.

It is about definition.

What do we count as proof?

Because that is what behaviour will optimise for.

So I am curious:

what is one change you have seen that improved revenue quality without killing pace?
