Let me ask you something plainly.

Have you ever committed a number you did not believe in? Filled a pipeline because you were told to, not because it was real? Pushed a deal forward knowing the buyer would carry the operational risk long after you were paid? Walked out of a forecast call with that quiet knot in your stomach because you knew the story was cleaner than the truth?

If you have, you are not alone. But you are also participating in the system that creates the problem.

You know how this works because you live inside it. Pressure rolls downhill. Targets compress time. CRM fills up. Language gets louder. Discovery gets thinner. Trust gets weaker. What starts as professional selling slowly turns into managed theatre. Not because you lack standards, but because the system rewards speed over truth and compliance over judgment.

The real question is not whether you see it. The real question is whether you are prepared to challenge it, even when it is uncomfortable.

Tonight on Sellers Anonymous we are hosting Andrew Hough. Andy has carried a bag, led enterprise teams, co founded the Institute of Sales Professionals, lectures at Cranfield University, and researches adaptability and trust in modern sales. He has worked inside the system and now studies how it shapes behaviour and performance.

We're going to examine what most organisations avoid. Why sales is distrusted and how our own incentives contribute to that outcome. Why CRM often reflects compliance rather than commercial reality. Why psychological safety for sellers matters as much as safety for buyers. Why most claims of customer centricity collapse under quarterly pressure. And what sustainable, demand side, trust based selling actually looks like when political risk is real and decisions must survive rooms you are not in.

This is not anti growth and it is not anti performance. It is about building performance that does not corrode trust. It is about revenue that survives internal scrutiny, operational reality, and time. It is about treating sales as a profession rather than a pressure system.

If you're selling complex decisions into rooms you cannot enter, where your champion is fighting internal battles you cannot see, you already understand how fragile trust can be. If you are tired of defending numbers that you know are soft. If you want to win without compromising who you are. If you suspect that the way we sell today is creating downstream cost that no one is measuring.

You belong in this room.
If you are looking for applause, shortcuts, or scripts, you do not.

Because if we do not fix this, buyers will. They will fix it by excluding us earlier, trusting us less, compressing our margins, and reducing us to a commodity.
No slides. No theatre. Just serious sellers having a serious conversation.
