Pressure changes what people agree to, not what they are ready to live with.

When pressure is in the room, decisions start to move. Alignment forms more quickly. Timelines tighten. Meetings end with clear next steps. From the outside, it looks like progress. It looks like momentum.

Inside the organisation, something else is happening.

Questions that still matter get set aside because now is not the right time. Trade offs are acknowledged, but not really absorbed. Risks do not disappear. They are carried forward, often by people who were not part of the original conversation and will not have the authority to change it later.

There is nothing unethical about this. Nothing manipulative. Most of the time it is done by people who are genuinely trying to be helpful. No one sets out to pass the burden forward. The system does that on their behalf.

Sales usually feels the pressure first. Movement becomes the goal. Relief starts to substitute for readiness. Getting to yes feels better than sitting with what is unresolved.

Buyers feel it next. Not as a clear objection they can articulate, and not as something they want to escalate. It shows up as hesitation. As a delay in replying. As a sense that committing now will create work they will have to explain, defend, or absorb later.

So they protect themselves.

Sometimes the deal slows down. Sometimes it goes quiet. Sometimes it moves forward anyway and returns months later heavier than expected, full of friction that no one can quite trace back to a single moment.

What usually gets missed is that this is not a failure of intent, and it is not a lack of skill.

It is that decisions made under pressure tend to relocate cost instead of resolving it.

Movement reduces discomfort in the moment. Safety reduces regret over time.

The relief arrives at approval. The work arrives later. During onboarding. At renewal. In the small exceptions and accommodations that pile up quietly, long after everyone has forgotten what was actually agreed.

When sellers are equipped to manage pressure, buyers brace themselves. When sellers are equipped to make safer decisions while the pressure is on, buyers find it easier to commit. Not because they have been convinced, but because they are not being asked to live with something they are not ready for.

Most teams are very good at optimising for agreement. Far fewer pay attention to what happens after.

And that difference is where most of the cost hides.
