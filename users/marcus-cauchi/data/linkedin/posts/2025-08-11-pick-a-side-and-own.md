Pick a Side. And Own It.

For decades, a toxic idea has been drip-fed into our culture: if government focuses on the public good and taxes the wealthiest among us, it will inevitably turn into a corrupt special-interest racket.

Milton Friedman sold it. The ultra-wealthy amplified it. And the gullible, whether greedy or just not thinking, repeated it until it sounded like truth.

It isn’t. It’s a self-serving lie.

The logic is absurd: because something might be mismanaged, we shouldn’t even try. Because a bridge could need repairs, we shouldn’t build bridges. Convenient reasoning, if your aim is to keep all the resources in private hands and let public infrastructure rot.

Here’s the reality: decade after decade, the same small clique has gutted public institutions, pointed to the predictable failings, and declared this “proof” that collective action doesn’t work. They’ve engineered tax codes, bankrolled politicians, and carved loopholes wide enough to sail a super-yacht through, all to make sure the richest pay the least and keep the most.

Meanwhile:
Billionaires pay lower tax rates than nurses.
Schools crumble while luxury developments get public subsidies.
Hospitals close in poor areas while private jets get write-offs.

And every time someone suggests the wealthiest should pay their fair share, the accusatory bleating starts: Government can’t be trusted. Funny how that concern never seems to apply to corporations that buy laws, to private equity firms that gut jobs, or to dynasties that inherit fortunes and influence without earning either.

If you’ve ever benefitted from clean water, public roads, disease control, emergency services, or public education, you’ve benefitted from the very thing they want you to distrust. The very thing they weaken, so they can point to the weakness as proof it should be dismantled.

Here’s the line in the sand:
If you believe those with more money than they could spend in a thousand lifetimes should contribute fairly to the society that made their wealth possible, you are on one side.

If you believe the richest should hoard while schools crumble, roads collapse, and children go hungry, you are on the other.

There’s no neutral ground here. This is the price of civilisation: fair taxation is not charity, and it’s not punishment. It’s the entry fee for living in a functioning society.

You can’t sit this one out. Pick a side. And own it.
