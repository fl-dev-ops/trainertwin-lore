2 hours from now Andrew Hough FF.ISP,Cranfield Lecturer will be our guest speaker at #SellersAnonymous (AA for recovering traditional transactional sellers). 

Answer something honestly.

Have you ever said “commit” out loud while mentally adding caveats you did not voice? Reassured your manager while recalculating risk in your head? Watched a deal show green in CRM while knowing it was politically fragile? Pushed momentum because the quarter was closing, even though the buyer was not ready?

Most experienced sellers have. Not because they lack integrity. But because they operate inside systems that reward certainty and compress time.

You did not design that system. But every forecast call, every update, every quiet compromise keeps it running.

You care about doing this job properly. That is why the tension bothers you.
If you sell complex decisions into rooms you cannot enter, you already know how fragile alignment is. Your champion is fighting internal battles you cannot see. Procurement has its own incentives. Risk is assessed in conversations you are not in. And you are still expected to commit.

That pressure shapes our behaviour. It shapes our language, and it shapes what gets said internally and what stays unsaid.

Tonight we are joined by Andrew Hough. He has carried a bag, led enterprise teams, co founded the Institute of Sales Professionals, lectures at Cranfield University, and researches trust and adaptability in modern sales. He understands the commercial reality and the system behind it.

This conversation is not about blame. It is about clarity.

We will examine how incentives shape behaviour. Why CRM often reflects pressure rather than reality. How psychological safety determines what sellers feel safe to say. Why trust erodes long before deals are officially lost. And what demand side selling requires when political and career risk is real.

If you've defended a forecast you privately doubted, you understand the cost. If you care about long term relationships as much as this quarter’s number, you have felt the strain.

If we don't examine this honestly, trust erodes slowly. When trust erodes, margin, influence, and access go with it.

This is a space to look at that tension directly. Not to criticise, moralise, or perform. But to raise the standard together. If you want scripts or shortcuts, this will frustrate you.

If you want an honest conversation about trust and performance in complex sales, join us.

No theatre. Just serious sellers examining the system they operate inside.
Tonight.
