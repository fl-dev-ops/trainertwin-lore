What Has to Be True Before the Next Tranche?

The first commitment has been made.
Now the business wants more capital.
That can feel like the second part of a decision you already made. Same company. Same management team. Same thesis. Everyone knows the history.

But something important has changed.

You now have evidence you did not have when the first cheque was written.

Some assumptions have survived contact with reality. Some have not. Some questions have been answered. New ones may have appeared.

So before the next tranche goes in, I'd want to know: What had to become true before we committed again?
And then: Did it?

For PE, that may be follow-on equity, an acquisition or another round of investment behind the value creation plan.
For a Family Office, it may be a direct investment asking for more capital after everyone has become familiar with the business and management team.

That familiarity can make the next decision feel easier.

It should never make the evidence less important.

The first commitment may have bought you something valuable: the chance to replace assumptions with evidence.
Use it.

If what you have learned strengthens the case, good.
If it supports continuing on different terms, that matters.
If something the original decision depended on has not happened, the next cheque should not arrive simply because it was contemplated when the first one was written.

The first commitment can buy you evidence.
It should not automatically authorise the next one.
