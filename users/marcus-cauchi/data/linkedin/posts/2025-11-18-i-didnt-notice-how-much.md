I didn’t notice how much I was carrying until that afternoon.

The morning felt slow.
Not because of the work.
Because people kept stopping in front of me with small questions.
Simple things.
Things they could do on their own.

But they looked at me first.
And I stepped in out of habit.
Again and again.
Trying to keep the day smooth.

By lunchtime I wasn’t leading.
I was holding everything together with quick answers.

Then something small opened my eyes.

A customer asked one of my team a basic question.
I watched them pause.
Eyes turn to me.
Waiting to see my reaction before they said a word.

I moved to jump in.
But I caught the customer’s expression.
Not upset.
Just unsure.
Like they could sense the hesitation.

And it hit me:
“They’re waiting because of the way I lead.”
Not because they’re unsure.
Because they don’t want to get it wrong in front of me.

That stayed with me.

After lunch, I tried something different.
No speech.
Just a pause.

When someone brought me a question,
I didn’t give the answer.

I asked:
“What have you tried?”
“What do you already know?”
“What would you do if I wasn’t here right now?”

The first time, they hesitated.
The second time, they thought it through.
The third time, they answered for themselves.

And something shifted.
They stood straighter.
Their voice steadied.
They moved with more certainty.

The whole room changed with them.
Less checking.
Less waiting.
More small decisions made on the spot.

Nothing risky.
Just everyday work done with a bit more trust.

And I realised something simple:

When I jump in, I take the moment away from them.
When I coach instead of tell, they grow.
And the day gets easier for both of us.

By the end of the shift, the place felt clearer.
More sure of itself.
Customers felt it too.
You could see it in their faces,
the quiet confidence that the whole team could handle things.

One small change.
Big ripple.

Where does this happen in your team?
