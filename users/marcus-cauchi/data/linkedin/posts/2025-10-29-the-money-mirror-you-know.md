The Money Mirror

You know the moment.
You say your price ... and something in you shifts.

Your voice tightens.
You fill the silence.
You start adding extras that no one asked for.

I’ve done it.
Most of us have, especially the ones who care about being fair.

But that isn’t fairness.
That’s fear wearing good manners.

At some point, money stopped measuring value.
It started measuring worth.
And that’s when every deal turned personal.
Because once your price becomes a mirror, you’re not negotiating numbers, you’re negotiating yourself.

Here’s the line I hold onto when that feeling creeps in:

The least you’ll accept is the most you’ll ever get. 

Pricing isn’t ego.
It’s composure.
It’s trusting the silence long enough for respect to grow in it.

You don’t need new lines.
You need peace with fairness.

This isn’t about charging more.
It’s about ending the quiet discount on yourself.

Before your next price call, say the number - then count to five.
Let the silence tell you who’s really uncomfortable.
