The people who feel the work aren’t the ones who can change it.

The friction shows up first in the work itself.

In follow-ups that weren’t planned.
In handoffs that need extra explanation.
In small adjustments that keep things moving.

The people closest to it learn where things bend.
They learn what needs compensating for.
They learn how to make it hold.

They don’t experience this as a problem.
They experience it as doing their job properly.

From further away, the picture looks different.

The outputs arrive.
The numbers stay steady.
Nothing appears unstable.

The effort that keeps everything together resolves before it travels far enough to matter.
It gets absorbed where it appears.

So the people with authority see stability.
And the people without authority carry the work of maintaining it.

Neither side is careless.
Neither side is missing something obvious.

They are looking from different distances.

By the time effort could reach someone who might question the original call,
it has already been normalised by the people who had to live with it.

That’s how work persists without review.

Not because no one cares.
Because responsibility and authority don’t sit in the same place.
