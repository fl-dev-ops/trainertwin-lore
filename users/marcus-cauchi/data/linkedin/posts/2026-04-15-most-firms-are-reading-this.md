Most firms are reading this as an engagement problem.

It isn’t. It’s a conversion problem.

Companies are now spending heavily on three things at once: people, management layers, and AI. When output per employee doesn’t move, the issue isn't effort and it’s not access to tools. It’s that management isn’t turning that spend into real work getting done.

Clarity is loose. Coaching is thin. Teams don’t line up cleanly across the business. So the tool gets rolled out, but the way work is done doesn’t really change. That’s how AI ends up as cost, not lift. This is where most firms get it wrong. They add more tools. They add more pressure. They ask for more activity.

But they don’t fix the point where decisions are made, checked, and owned.
That’s where output is won or lost.

The question for founders, CROs, and operating partners isn’t whether the team is engaged. It’s this:

Where is management failing to turn spend into output, and how quickly does that show up in your numbers?

Because if growth is not showing up as cleaner execution and higher output per head, it is usually creating drag somewhere else. And that cost always lands later.
