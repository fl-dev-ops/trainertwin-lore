Is The Job You Said Yes To, The Job You Got?

A client called me about three months into a new role. Good business. Growing fast. Real ambition. The brief had been clear enough. Join as CRO, build something predictable, reduce founder dependency, and create a revenue system that could scale without every deal, exception and decision running through 1 or 2 people

They said yes. It looked like a strong move. Then the real job became clear

Marketing sat with another leader. Customer Success sat with Operations. RevOps was being built from scratch. Product owned some of the most important growth levers, but had no real reason to coordinate with commercial. There was also a major event expected within a year, which meant the role would almost certainly change shape again

The title was CRO. The job was Head of Sales. Accountability was for outcomes the role did not fully control. They were not calling because they had made a stupid decision. They were calling because they were starting to understand the decision they had actually made

This is one of the traps in senior hiring. The yes can be perfectly reasonable & still not be safe enough. Good company. Serious brief. Strong title. Decent people. Real upside. Nothing obviously broken

Then you get inside and discover the gaps. The outcome sits with one person. The authority sits with five others. The politics were never named. The timing is moving. The number is real, but the levers that produce the number are scattered across the business

That's where a lot of senior roles start to go wrong. Not because the person can't do the job, but because the job has not been built properly

You write a brief that says transformation and hire someone capable of leading it. Then you hand them a target, spread the real control across Marketing, Product, Finance, Operations and Customer Success, and wonder why the first year feels like trench warfare

The executive isn't always failing. Sometimes they are surviving a role that was difficult to win before they arrived

Accountability without authority is not a leadership test. It's bad design

Before you accept a senior role, slow the yes down. Ask what outcomes you are really accountable for and what authority you actually have and which parts of the result sit outside your control. Ask what assumptions are baked into the brief and what changes to prep for in the next 12 months. Ask who decides how the role changes when reality strikes

If you're designing the role, ask the question most companies avoid - what would have to be true for a strong person to fail here?

That question will tell you more than the job description. A clean no is not the worst outcome. A messy yes is. A clean no saves both sides before damage is done. A messy yes gives everyone three months of optimism, then a year of friction, disappointment & blame

Most senior hiring failures aren't capability failures. They're role design failures

Make sure the job you say yes to is the job you get
