If the plan misses, what happens to your economics, authority and options?

That is the question I would want answered before taking institutional capital. A strong valuation can make a deal look attractive. So can a respected investor, positive advisers and a plan everyone believes in. But the headline number does not tell you what happens if revenue lands late, another round is needed, debt is added, the exit takes longer or the investor loses confidence in the plan.

You can still own a large percentage of the company and discover that much less of the eventual proceeds reaches you. You can still be CEO and discover that you no longer control the budget, hiring or pricing decisions needed to deliver the number you are accountable for. You can still have meaningful equity and discover that debt, preferences, dilution or the terms of your rollover put other people ahead of you when the outcome is disappointing.

None of that requires anyone to be dishonest. It only requires the individual parts of the transaction to make sense while nobody joins them back together into the decision you will actually have to live with.

Your lawyer will tell you what the documents mean. Your accountant will tell you what the numbers mean. Your corporate finance adviser will help you structure and execute the transaction.

What's often missing is someone asking: given everything we now know, what does this capital actually justify us agreeing to?

That is the job of Founder Capital Decision Safety. This isn’t a verdict on the investor and it isn’t a replacement for your advisers. It joins the consequences together so you can see what the proposed capital does to your economics, authority and future options before the next step becomes difficult to reverse.

If you have a live term sheet, debt proposal, PE offer, rollover or partial sale in front of you, bring me the transaction and the part that doesn't quite sit right.
Before you take the money, find out what comes with it.
