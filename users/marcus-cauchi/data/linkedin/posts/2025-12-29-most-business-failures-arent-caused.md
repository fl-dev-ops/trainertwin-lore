Most business failures aren't caused by incompetence.
They happen because ordinary people do what the system rewards.

Power in organisations rarely shows up as abuse.
It shows up as distance.
Distance from consequences.
Distance from customers, operators, and risk.

Over time, decisions harden.
They stop feeling like choices and start feeling fixed.
Reality changes.
The decision does not.
So people work around it.

That is how capable organisations drift into bad outcomes.
Not because of bad intent.
Because momentum takes over.

Most systems reward certainty and punish hesitation.
They favour speed, clarity, and confidence.
They make doubt, reflection, and pause feel risky.

Under pressure, people do not become reckless.
They narrow their focus.

They optimise what is visible.
They avoid what is uncomfortable to say.
They try not to be the person who slows things down.

That is not a culture issue.
It is a design issue.

This is decision stewardship.
The responsibility to protect a decision after it has been approved, not just before.

Most value is not destroyed by bad strategy.
It is destroyed when decisions keep running after the conditions that justified them have changed.

Advantage accumulates.
Responsibility spreads thin.
Outcomes emerge that no one would choose directly
and no one feels able to stop.

Power becomes dangerous when it hides in process.
When harm appears as a side effect no one owns.

The real test of an organisation is not its strategy.
It is what happens when a decision no longer holds.

Can someone say,
this decision is no longer safe,
and be heard?

Interruption in business is usually quiet.
A forecast gets reopened.
A delivery date is questioned.
A plan slows down without someone needing to take the blame.

In serious organisations, stopping a decision when conditions change
is not escalation.
It is part of the job.

That only works if it is designed in from the start.

If a decision is failing,
how long is the system allowed to hide that fact
before the agreed kill criteria are met
and a pause is required?

When people can question decisions after they are made,
power stays grounded.
When they cannot, power drifts.

The point of leadership power is not confidence.
It is managing consequences.

That does not require heroes.
It requires adults.

People willing to stay in contact with reality
when everything around them says keep going.

So the question is not,
who is in charge?

It's this:

When this no longer stands up,
who is allowed to say so,
how quickly must it be heard,
and what changes as a result?
