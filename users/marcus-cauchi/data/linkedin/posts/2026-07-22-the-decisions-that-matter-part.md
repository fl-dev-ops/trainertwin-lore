The Decisions That Matter
Part 4: Should We Discount?

Most discounts don’t begin with price.

They begin much earlier, when value wasn’t fully understood, the right stakeholders weren’t involved or the opportunity stayed alive long after the warning signs appeared. By the time commercial discussions begin, the decision has often narrowed to one uncomfortable question: reduce the price or lose the deal.

Discounting can create immediate relief, but it rarely removes the original problem. It moves the cost somewhere else through lower margins, greater delivery pressure, weaker expectations and customers who begin the relationship by learning that value is negotiable.

Price tells a story. Sometimes it reflects competitive pressure. More often it reveals something the business missed much earlier in the buying process.

Before approving the discount, ask what the customer still doesn’t believe.
