A good company outcome does not settle whether the capital decision worked for the founder.

The company can grow and sell well while the founder spends years with less authority than expected. A rollover can leave a large part of their wealth dependent on a second exit. They may be unable to leave, realise liquidity when expected, or exercise choices they thought they had retained.

None of that proves the original deal was bad. Nor does a successful exit prove it was right. Before signing, I'd want to know what a good outcome has to look like for the company and for the founder making the commitment.

Company success is one outcome. The founder's economics, authority, liquidity and options are others.
