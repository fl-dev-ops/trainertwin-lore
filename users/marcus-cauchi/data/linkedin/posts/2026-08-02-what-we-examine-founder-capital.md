What we examine
Founder Capital Decision Safety begins with five questions.

First, what is the capital being hired to do? A founder can spend months negotiating valuation before deciding whether this is the right form of capital for the outcome they want.

Second, what will you still control after the transaction? We map who decides, who can block, who can force and who remains responsible for the result.

Third, what happens when the plan misses? We examine debt, preferences, covenants, dilution, rollover, removal and proceeds under several plausible outcomes.

Fourth, who gets paid before the company improves? We trace transaction costs, recurring fees, related-party arrangements, financing charges and other ways value may leave the business.

Fifth, what evidence supports your trust in the capital provider? The best reference is not always the founder whose company smashed the plan. It may be the founder who missed, needed more money, breached a covenant or disagreed with the investor.

These are simple questions.

The answers are rarely simple because they are spread across documents, models, promises and conversations that were never designed to be read as one decision.
