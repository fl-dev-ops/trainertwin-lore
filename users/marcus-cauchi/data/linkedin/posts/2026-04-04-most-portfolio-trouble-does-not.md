Most portfolio trouble does not begin with a miss.

It begins with a meeting that feels broadly reassuring.

The pack is tidy.
The quarter looks close enough.
The caveats sound manageable.
The pressure points get described as normal.
Nobody feels enough discomfort to force the harder question.

That is usually the moment the real risk gets protected.

Not because anyone is lying.
Because each person in the room is carrying a different version of the truth, and no one has yet forced those fragments into one ugly, board-level picture.

That is where weak revenue survives.
That is where bad assumptions stay alive.
That is where companies mistake smoothing for governance and optimism for evidence.

The article below is about that earlier moment.

Not the miss itself.
The quieter point before it, when the signal is already there, the language is still too soft, and the wrong people still believe they have time.

Read it for the part most board packs never show:
who sees what first, who fails to force it into the room, and why that delay gets so expensive.
