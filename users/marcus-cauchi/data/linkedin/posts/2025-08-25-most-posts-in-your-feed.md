Most posts in your feed will tell you how to “drive growth.” Few will tell you the truth: growth is impossible if you’re bleeding trust and clarity.

That’s why this post matters.

Chaos in portfolios doesn’t come from markets or products, it comes from broken systems. Renewals left to chance, forecasts padded with theatre, logos chased to mask churn. It’s not noise, it’s leakage. And leakage compounds.

Here’s what you’ll get if you read this post:
Clarity on why Protect, Expand, Win isn’t theory, it’s the only sequence that compounds certainty.
Trust that comes from a system proving uplift in 120 days, not slides, not promises, but results.
Alignment between what operators grind through daily and what ICs actually value: EBITDA, multiple expansion, cashflow certainty.

Trust turns effort into outcomes and chaos into opportunity. Without it, your plans fail, your teams crumble, and your clients walk away. With it, everything multiplies.

That’s why you should read on. Because the difference between leakage and lift isn’t strategy, it’s trust, clarity, and alignment, proven in 120 days.

Net Promoter Score (NPS)
A sentiment snapshot.
Measures how likely a customer is to recommend you.
Useful for brand signals, but it’s a lagging, feel-good metric.
Weakness: it doesn’t link to revenue, renewal, or enterprise value. You can have promoters who still churn.

Time to Value (TTV)
A speed measure.
Tracks how quickly a customer reaches their “aha” moment with your product.
Useful for onboarding efficiency and CS teams.
Weakness: it stops at initial adoption, ignores expansion, loyalty, and long-term economic impact.

Net Value Score (NVS)
A truth engine.
Segments every customer into Advocates, Growth, Conversion Opportunities, or Renewal Risks, based on real outcomes, not sentiment.
Directly tied to GRR, NRR, EBITDA, and multiples — the levers ICs and operators care about.

Runs on a 120-day cycle, so you see truth in weeks and uplift in a quarter.
Shows not just “do they like us?” or “did they onboard fast?” but:
Who is compounding value,
Who can expand,
Who will renew,
Who will leave.

In short:
NPS shows how customers feel.
TTV shows how fast they start.

NVS shows where enterprise value is being created or destroyed, and what to do about it.
