What Are You Actually Committing To?

Two investments can have the same cheque size and be completely different decisions.

One gives you room to change course.
The other ties up capital, control, management attention, reputation and future choices at the same time.
On paper, both may say £20m.
In practice, one may be a much bigger commitment.

That is easy to miss because money is the easiest part to count.
Control is harder.
Reversibility is harder.
The cost of unwinding the decision is harder.

So is the value of the options you give up when you say yes.

A Family Office can put the same amount into two direct investments and carry very different levels of exposure.
A PE firm can write the same cheque into two deals and end up with very different amounts of flexibility once the value creation plan starts.

So when the decision is being sized, I wouldn't stop at: How much capital are we committing?

I'd want to know what else is being committed with it.
What becomes harder to reverse?
What future choices disappear?
What starts depending on this decision being right?

Because the cheque tells you how much money went in.
It doesn't tell you how big the decision became.
