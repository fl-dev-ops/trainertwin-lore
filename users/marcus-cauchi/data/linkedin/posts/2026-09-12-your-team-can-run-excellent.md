Your team can run excellent discovery calls, build strong relationships and win deals that should never have got that far.
The problem is what your process does with evidence that weakens an opportunity.
When a customer says the problem is expensive, urgent and getting worse, everyone leans in. But what happens when the evidence says they have bigger priorities, the cause isn’t clear, implementation looks doubtful, or a smaller and cheaper intervention might be enough?
If those answers don’t get tested with the same curiosity, confidence can build faster than evidence. That affects more than conversion. Weak opportunities stay in pipeline, forecasts become harder to trust, management plans around revenue that hasn’t earned that confidence, and expensive problems can appear after the contract is signed.
There’s a simple test.
Take the opportunity your team feels most confident about and ask: what would have to be true for us to downgrade it today?
If nobody can give you a clear, evidence-based answer, that’s worth investigating.
That’s the job of the Reality Check: find out what the evidence actually justifies before you commit more time, money or confidence.
