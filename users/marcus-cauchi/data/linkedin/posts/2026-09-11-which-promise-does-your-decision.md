Which promise does your decision stop working without?

"We're long-term investors." "We'll support the business if it needs more capital." "We want you running the company." "We won't interfere unnecessarily." "We're aligned on the exit." Any of those statements may be made sincerely.

The useful question isn't whether you trust the person saying them. It is whether your decision depends on the promise remaining true. If the deal only works for you while one of those things holds, what evidence do you have that it is safe to rely on? Who controls whether it remains true? What happens if the people, circumstances or incentives change?

A promise can be genuine and still be too fragile to carry an irreversible decision.
