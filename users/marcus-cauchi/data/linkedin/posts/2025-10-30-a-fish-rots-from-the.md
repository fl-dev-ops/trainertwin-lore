A Fish Rots from the Head Down

I once overheard my CEO tell a consultant,
“My VP Sales is the problem.”

He was right.
 I was.
 And it wasn’t a secret.
 I knew it the moment he said it –
 and my team knew it too.

Who promoted me?
 Who set the targets that made discounting look heroic?
 Who built a culture where everyone smiled at the numbers but flinched at the customers?

You can’t outsource tone.
 It leaks from the corner office first.

Every time a leader says,
“We just need a closer,”
 the culture learns that trust is optional.

Every time the CFO says,
"We need to bring forward £40k from next quarter,"
 the workload for the team just bumped
 and morale sank further.

Every time you say,
“We’ll fix culture later,”
 you trade respect for speed.

You don’t get performance.
 You get fear with a quota.

I’ve been that VP, promoted out of my depth, smiling harder than I should’ve.
 I’ve made compromise hires to appease my boss,
 because I wanted results faster than I’d earned them.
 And I’ve been fired for doing exactly what I was told.

Numbers-first built a team that went quiet when the boss walked in.
Every discount. Every half-truth. Every rushed deal.
 They’re just symptoms of the operating system you designed.

And when a team starts doing the bare minimum just to avoid helping their boss succeed,
 you don’t have a sales problem 
 you have a trust vacuum.
That’s how rot starts: quietly, in the spreadsheets,
 while everyone’s still faking commitment.

And then one day, you wake up surrounded by salespeople doing the bare minimum,
 not because they’re lazy,
 but because they’d rather fail than make you look good.
That’s not a performance issue.
That’s mutiny with manners.

Culture doesn’t break.
 It copies you.
The good news?
 If you built it, you can rebuild it.

Before your next pipeline review, close the laptop.
 Look around the table.
 Then ask yourself:
“What have I done that made trust optional here?”
 “Where do my fingerprints still show?”

That silence?
 It’s where leadership starts again.
What’s one habit you’ve built to stop trust leaking from the top?
