What happens between the evidence changing and the forecast changing?

Imagine a £750,000 deal in the commit forecast.
On 12 March, something changes inside the customer’s business. On 18 March, the salesperson gets evidence of it. It’s material enough that the assumptions behind the forecast no longer look as strong as they did.
The forecast finally changes on 27 May.

I’m interested in what happened between those dates.

Maybe the salesperson didn’t realise how important the new information was. Maybe they wanted another conversation before changing anything. Maybe the buyer reassured them. Maybe their manager disagreed. Maybe everyone knew the deal was weakening but nobody wanted to take £750,000 out until they were certain.
All of those are plausible.

That’s why I want the evidence rather than the explanation afterwards.

What did we know on 18 March? What contradicted what we’d previously believed? What remained uncertain? What did we do next? When did our view actually change?

Then I’d look at the cost of the gap.
How many seller hours went into it? How many management reviews? Did we bring in a solutions engineer? Did an executive get involved? Did Legal do work? Did Finance continue planning against the revenue?

Forecast accuracy tells us where the number eventually ended up.
It doesn’t necessarily tell us how long the organisation continued relying on an old belief after the evidence underneath it had changed.

That’s a number I really need to know.
Not so we can beat salespeople up for being slow to change their minds. Sometimes waiting for more evidence will be completely justified.
I want to know whether we can tell the difference.
