The Decisions That Matter
Part 3: Should We Hire?

Hiring often feels like a vote of confidence in the future. Sometimes it’s simply a forecast that nobody challenged.

A growing pipeline creates optimism, so the business recruits ahead of demand. Salaries begin immediately. Recruitment fees are paid. Managers invest time. Expectations rise. The revenue remains uncertain because the buyers haven’t made the same commitment the business has.

When the forecast slips, attention quickly turns to productivity, onboarding or market conditions. Those things matter, but they may not be where the decision first went wrong. The original mistake may have been treating expected revenue as though it already existed.

Every hire deserves more than optimism. It deserves evidence that demand is real, that management capacity exists and that the business can support the decision if conditions change.

Before asking whether you can afford to hire, ask whether today’s evidence deserves tomorrow’s commitment.
