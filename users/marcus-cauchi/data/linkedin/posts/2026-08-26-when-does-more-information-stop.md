When Does More Information Stop Helping?

The investment has been under review for six weeks.
There's always another question. Another model. Another customer call. Another report. Another adviser. Another scenario.
All of those might produce more information.

But before asking for any of them, I'd want one thing made clear:
What decision could the answer change?

If the answer came back differently from what we expect, would we change the size of the commitment? The structure? The timing? The conditions? Would we walk away?

If yes, the information may matter.
If no, why are we waiting for it?

This matters in PE. It matters just as much in a Family Office looking at a direct investment, where one more reference, one more market view or one more adviser can feel reassuring long after the answer has stopped being capable of changing the decision.

This is not an argument for less diligence. Weak evidence still deserves more work.

But more information and better evidence are not the same thing.

Waiting also has a cost. The opportunity can change. Your negotiating position can change. Markets can change. Other options can disappear. So before asking for one more piece of evidence, ask: If we knew the answer tomorrow, what would we do differently?

If nobody can answer that, more information may not be the thing the decision is missing.
