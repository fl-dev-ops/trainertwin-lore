The decision didn’t fail.
It changed hands.

The contract was signed.
The kickoff happened.
The plan looked complete.

Then the seller moved on.

Not suddenly.
Just on to the next deal.

After that, the work shifted.

Questions appeared that weren’t in scope.
Edge cases surfaced in real use.
Integrations behaved differently than expected.

None of this invalidated the decision.
It just wasn’t carried by the person who framed it.

So the organisation adjusted.

Internal teams translated intent into reality.
They filled gaps.
They made small compensations to keep things working.

What had been sold as a solution
became something that needed managing.

No one called it a problem.
The purchase still made sense.

The extra effort was absorbed.
Into operations.
Into support.
Into “we’ll handle it.”

Over time, the decision stopped feeling external.
It became internal work.
