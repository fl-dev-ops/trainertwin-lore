What you measure taxes your business

Every metric changes behaviour. It tells people what matters, what will be rewarded and what they can safely ignore. That can improve performance, but it always creates a cost somewhere else.

Measure activity and people become busy. Calls get made, emails get sent and meetings fill the calendar, while customer progress may barely move. The business pays the Activity Tax through wasted effort, tired people and dashboards that look healthier than the outcomes.

Measure revenue without measuring revenue quality and the business pays the Discount Tax. Sellers find ways to bring the number forward, but margin falls, weak deals close and delivery inherits promises it never agreed to keep.

Measure new logos and existing customers become less important. Attention shifts towards acquisition because that is what the scoreboard rewards, while poor adoption, weak relationships and missed outcomes build the Churn Tax behind the scenes.

Measure utilisation too tightly and people stop thinking. Every hour must be allocated, explained or billed, so reflection, learning and improvement start to look unproductive. The business saves visible capacity and loses the ideas that would have improved the work.

Measure speed without protecting quality and rework rises. Measure cost without protecting capability and standards fall. Measure forecast accuracy without testing the evidence behind the forecast, and the bill arrives later as surprise, blame and emergency action.

None of these taxes appears as a line in the accounts. They show up as burnout, discounting, poor handovers, weak decisions, customer frustration, margin loss, churn and trust that has to be rebuilt.

People do not only respond to what leaders say. They learn what the business truly values from what gets measured, reviewed and rewarded. If the dashboard celebrates motion, people create motion. If it rewards short-term revenue, someone else absorbs the long-term cost.

The question is not whether a metric creates a tax. It is whether the outcome it produces is worth the cost it shifts elsewhere.

Before adding another target, ask what behaviour it will reward, what people will neglect to hit it and who will eventually receive the bill.

What you measure does not merely report the business. It shapes behaviour and shifts the cost of whatever the measure leaves out onto someone else.
