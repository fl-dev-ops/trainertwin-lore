Don't be a lemon. If you’re job hunting, stop applying. Start connecting.

Most people don’t lose confidence because they’re lazy.
 They lose it because silence wears them down.
You send the CV.
 You wait.
 You hear nothing.
 And after a while, it’s hard not to believe the silence means something about you.

The best salespeople don’t wait for luck.
 They find the right people, learn their world, and start conversations that matter. They establish what makes them timely, relevant and valuable.

You can do the same.
 Treat your next role like a major account: talk to people who already work there.
Read the reviews and the stories underneath. See who you know in common and ask for a quiet intro. Do your resaerch and find a fit between their strategy and you. Build a story that links what you’ve done to what they care about. 

And yes ... be brave, pick up the phone when it feels the timing is right for them (even if it makes you want to curl up a die). The first time’s awkward. The second feels normal. The third it'll feel as easy as falling off a bike.

If that feels too big, start smaller: Find one company you’d genuinely love to work with. Follow their people. Comment thoughtfully on what they share. After a week or two, send one kind, personal message. Be curious. 

That’s prospecting, just done with humanity.

This isn’t about selling yourself.
 It’s about respecting your value enough to sell it properly.
 Work out what the "You-shaped" hole is that only you can fill. What do you do naturally that makes others’ work easier? What are you excpetional at, and how does that support their vision or strategy?

Before you send your next CV, pause and ask:
 If this company were my biggest client, how would I approach them?

You’re not chasing approval.
 You’re building relevance.
 That’s how doors open to the unadvertised  job market (which also has almost zero competition) - quietly, for the right reasons.

Try it this week and tell me what happens.
