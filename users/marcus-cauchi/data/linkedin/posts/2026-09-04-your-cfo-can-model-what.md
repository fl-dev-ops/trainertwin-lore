Your CFO can model what a 30% miss does to the company. The founder still has a different problem.

Suppose revenue is 30% below plan and more money is needed sooner than expected. Cash, runway and covenant headroom can all be modelled. What changes in your decision?
Do your economics change? Do you become dependent on one source of capital? Do approvals that looked minor at signing now control the choices needed to recover? If the exit moves out by two years, do your options still look the same?

The company downside belongs in the model. The founder downside belongs in the decision. I'd want both before signing.
