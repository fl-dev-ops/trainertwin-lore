If your ICP is built around what you can take, it’s not an ICP. It’s a red flag.

Most SaaS teams confuse “ideal” with “extractable”. ARR, headcount, vertical, tech stack, these aren’t signals of trust, they’re signals of greed. What you’ve actually built is a harvest list. It looks sharp in a boardroom and useless in the field.

Here’s the typical supply-side ICP fantasy:
• Industry: Fintech
• Headcount: 500 to 5,000
• Revenue: $100M+
• Stack: Salesforce, Microsoft, Netsuite, Snowflake
• Geography: North America
• Pain Point: “Wants to scale”

Translation: you care that they’re big, funded, and easy to reference. But you haven’t done the hard work to understand who they are, what they’re dealing with, or whether they’re ready to trust you.

That’s why your messaging lands flat:
• “We help fintech teams like yours accelerate digital transformation”
• “At your scale, manual processes are costing you millions”
• “Let’s explore synergies to optimise operational efficiency”

No urgency. No intimacy. No trust.

You’re pitching to a spreadsheet. And buyers can smell it.

The cost? Stalled deals, ghosted follow-ups, and messaging that sounds like every other competitor in your category.

Same language.
Same promises.
Same “schedule a demo”.

Now look at your website. If it’s structured like this:
• What we do
• How we do it
• Why we’re the best
• Book a call so we can sell to you

You’ve built a monument to your own ego. A digital billboard shouting about your product, your process, your greatness. And to the buyer, it all says one thing:

“You are prey. We are here to extract.”

This is what supply-side narcissism looks like in your GTM. You position yourself as the hero, which makes the customer the obstacle. And that erodes trust before the first conversation ever happens.

The fix isn’t a tighter ICP. It’s a trust-calibrated ICP.

Build from trust resonance, not data points.
• Who is already signalling urgency?
• Where do you have asymmetric insight, not just access?
• Who feels light to work with, not labour intensive?

Now apply that lens to your website.
• Lead with their chaos, not your clarity
• Show you understand what’s at stake before you explain how you help
• Speak like a partner, not a predator

Your site should be a trust interface, not a feature-function landfill. If it doesn’t make the buyer feel seen, safe, and supported, you’ve already lost the sale.
