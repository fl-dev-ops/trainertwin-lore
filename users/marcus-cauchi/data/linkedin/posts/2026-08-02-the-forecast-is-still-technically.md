"The forecast is still technically possible."

That attachment has kept a lot of weak decisions alive.

Nobody can point to enough closed-lost deals to kill the quarter. The pipeline is large enough to defend. Several opportunities are described as late rather than weak.

But the buyer evidence is getting thinner as the internal confidence gets louder.
At that point, the company has more than two choices. It can keep the forecast, keep it with explicit conditions, downgrade the unsupported portion, gather the missing buyer evidence or remove opportunities that no longer justify reliance.
Those are commercial decisions, not displays of optimism or pessimism.

Before the board uses the number to approve hiring or spend, I would ask:
What has the buyer actually decided, authorised or funded?
