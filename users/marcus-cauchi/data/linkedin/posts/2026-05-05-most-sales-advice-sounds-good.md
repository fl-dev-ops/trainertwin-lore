Most sales advice sounds good because it’s easy to agree with.

Ask better questions. Build rapport. Show empathy. Of course. But you can do all of that and still walk away from the call with something that looks like progress, but isn’t.

Because the buyer gave you the version of the truth that felt safe to share.

Buyers don’t hold back because you failed to create psychological safety properly. They hold back because they’re running a calculation in real time: if I say what’s actually going on here, what happens next?

Do they lose control of the process, get pushed faster than they want, or expose something internally they can’t defend later?

If the answer isn’t clear, they stay polite. You get a clean conversation built on partial truth, and the deal starts to drift.

It’s not a questioning problem. It’s not a rapport problem nor is it a consequence problem.

Most sellers are focused on how they sound. The better ones are watching where the story tightens up, where answers get too neat, and where certainty shows up without evidence. That’s usually where something real is being protected.

Technique helps, but only up to a point. After that, it can make the conversation smoother without making it more honest.

And smoother can be dangerous.

Because it feels like you’re getting somewhere, when really, you’re just not being told the whole picture.

So here’s the one question I’d want to get answered. In your last few calls, where did things feel on track, but you had a slight sense something didn’t quite add up?

Most people ignore that moment. That’s usually where the deal had already started to go sideways.
