Urgency does NOT come from sellers. It either exists for the buyer, or it does not.

One of the easiest mistakes in sales management is trying to manufacture urgency through pressure instead of finding it in the buyer's world.

End of month pushes.
Expiring discounts.
Quarter end pressure disguised as help.

Managers call this creating momentum.
Buyers feel exposed.

A buyer's real job is to decide without being punished for thinking.

They need room to compare options.
They need time to test regret.
They need to know slowing down will not trigger a penalty.

When sellers compress time, buyers defend themselves.
Managers then misread that defence as lack of interest or weak pipeline.

The damage shows up late.
Deals move quickly at the start and collapse during consensus.
Pipeline feels full and behaves fragile.
Forecasts become theatre.

If urgency belongs anywhere, it belongs in the buyer's world, NOT the team's calendar.

Here is the diagnostic for managers:

If deals accelerate under pressure but stall in consensus checks, urgency was manufactured, not discovered.

When reps feel safe to let buyers slow down, decisions speed up.
When stopping has no penalty, urgency becomes real and deals stick.

If you are seeing late stage fragility or forecast slippage, urgency is almost always upstream.

Where does urgency turn into fragility in your team?
