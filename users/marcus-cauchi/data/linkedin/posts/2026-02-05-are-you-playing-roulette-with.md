Are You Playing Roulette With Your Forecast?

Most boards still ask for 3x to 5x pipeline coverage as if it were doctrine.

Not because it works.
Because it feels safe.

It is ritual, not reality.

The assumption is simple.
If the pipeline is big enough, the outcome will take care of itself.

That assumption is almost always wrong.

Here is what actually happens.

A deal survives in the forecast not because it is real, but because
no one wants to be the one who kills it
removing it would trigger “the conversation”
the optics matter more than the physics

So the deal stays.

Headcount gets approved.
Capacity gets allocated.
Expectations harden.

And when the deal dies, the failure is treated as bad luck, not bad governance.

That is Vatican Roulette.

The wheel spins unseen.
Everyone prays.
No one touches the ball.

Pipeline coverage does not fail because people are dishonest.
It fails because the system rewards comfort over truth.

Forecast confidence becomes detached from execution reality.
Narrative replaces signal.
Late stage fantasy replaces reversibility.

By the time the miss is visible in the numbers
the hires are already made
the spend is already committed
the board is already exposed
the correction is no longer cheap

This is not a sales problem.
It is not a RevOps problem.
It is not even a forecasting problem.

It is an execution exposure problem.

There is a layer between strategy and numbers where value leaks unseen.
Most organisations do not instrument it.
They perform around it.

And the more pressure increases, the more theatrical the performance becomes.

The dangerous part?

The firms with the cleanest decks are often the most fragile.
Because they have optimised for belief, not reality.

If this makes you uncomfortable, good.
It should.

Because the question is not whether your forecast will miss.

It is how late you find out.
