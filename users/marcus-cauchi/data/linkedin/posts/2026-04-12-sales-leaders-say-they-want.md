Sales leaders say they want a repeatable process.

But when performance still varies after rollout, the explanation often shifts from the system to the individual.

The diagnosis gets personal.

More grit.
More resilience.
More effort.
More accountability.

That move is convenient and incomplete.

It protects the method. The training organisation absolves itself. The manager escapes scrutiny. And the blame goes back on the rep. But if a team has a sound process and performance is still inconsistent, the serious question is not who wants it more.

It is where good selling stops becoming visible, coachable, and repeatable across the team.

Top performers can make almost any system work. The harder test is whether the middle gets better in a way managers can actually coach. If that is not happening, grit is not a serious explanation. It is often what leaders reach for when they cannot clearly see what is or is not being coached.

So let’s get specific.
