Management Consulting Can No Longer Rent Trust

What is breaking is not just the old firm moat.

It is the old ability of brand to rent trust in advance.

For years, the logo did more than signal quality. It lowered perceived risk. It made advice feel safer than it often was. The brand acted as a trust proxy, and a lot of the commercial value sat inside that gap.

AI is collapsing that gap fast.

When polish, synthesis, and presentational fluency become abundant, the premium can no longer rest on presentation. It has to rest on something much harder to fake:

Judgment that survives scrutiny,
Thinking that survives first use,
and 
Advice that still holds once other people have to live with the decision.

That is the real transition.

The question is no longer:
Who produced the deck?

It is:
Whose judgment still holds after the meeting, after the handover, and after the consequences arrive?

That is where legacy advantage starts to break.

The next premium will not go to the biggest brand.
It will not go to the cleverest independent with the best tools.

It will go to advisors who can see the decision more clearly than the client sees it themselves, surface what would otherwise stay hidden until later, and leave behind thinking that still holds under pressure.

That is earned credibility.

And it is far rarer than branded credibility ever was.
