Question: When is it ok to lie to a prospect?

People usually ask that when something in them feels a bit shaky.
It’s rarely a question that shows up on good days.
Most of the time it’s when you’re trying to sound steady and hoping the other person can’t hear the small tremor underneath.

The urge to lie doesn’t walk in loudly. It sort of creeps in from the side. A tiny voice saying it might help keep things moving or stop the call sliding into places you don’t feel ready for.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth most salespeople dodge. You don’t think about lying because you lack integrity. You think about it because the moment knocks you off balance. You worry the deal might go south. You worry you’ll look like you don’t know enough. You worry this conversation could confirm a fear you haven’t managed to shake.

Pressure has a way of pulling you out of yourself. It drags your attention out of the room and into imagining problems that haven’t even happened yet. Most of us start catastrophising when that shift happens. It’s not a weakness. It’s just what minds do when they feel cornered.

There’s something simple underlying this. A lie doesn’t break trust straight away. What it breaks is the decision the buyer’s trying to make. Once they’re working from information that’s even slightly off, their whole picture starts to tilt and you can’t control what happens next.

You don’t need to be impressive here. You don’t need to pretend either. You just need to keep the ground level enough that both of you can stand on it safely.

What helps is something like: “Here’s what I know for sure. There are a couple of things I need to check properly. I’d rather be accurate than sound certain.”
It takes the pressure out of the air. People relax when they don’t feel like something’s being hidden.

Stay honest. Always. Never lie. Always tell the truth. They stay oriented. And the conversation stays in a place where real decisions can form.
