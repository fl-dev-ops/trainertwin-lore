Inside the Bitter Lemon Orchard

Trust evaporates.
Nobody shares leads, ideas, or warnings.
Every win is met with suspicion, “Who did they step on to get that?”
Collaboration dies; politics bloom.
A-players check out, or check out competitors.
The best quietly update their CVs.
The rest stay, get resentful, or learn to play the blame game to survive.
Blame culture becomes default.

Every missed target? “It’s marketing’s fault.”
Every customer loss? “Bad leads, not us.”
Ownership is for the next mug in the chair.

Activity theatre ramps up.
Calls, emails, CRM updates, all busywork, no impact.
Lemons cover their backs, not the pipeline.

Customer experience tanks.
Prospects smell the rot, pushy sales, follow-up drops, no accountability.
Churn climbs. Word spreads.

Manager becomes a liability.
No one trusts their feedback. Coaching is a joke, just more “targets” and public beatings.

If they try to "motivate", it’s met with eye-rolls and snark.
Leadership and board get spun.

On paper, the team looks “busy.”
In reality, the only thing compounding is bitterness, not pipeline.
The cycle self-reinforces.

New hires join, see the lemons, and adapt to survive, either leave or turn sour themselves.
The whole sales culture becomes a punchline.

What’s really happening?
A manager who can’t build trust, set standards, or protect the team from system rot,
will always create lemons.
Bitter, costly, hard to shift.

A manager who is failing is a product of a broken system.
 Don't start by raging on the manager
 Look in the mirror at the culture YOU created
 And how you hired or made
 underperformance your culture.

Bottom line:
You don’t get high performance from a basket of bitter lemons.
You get excuses, churn, margin loss, and a pipeline nobody believes in.
