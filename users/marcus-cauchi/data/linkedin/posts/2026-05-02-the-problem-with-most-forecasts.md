The problem with most forecasts isn’t accuracy.

It’s that they’re built on conversations that don’t survive scrutiny.

That’s why deals slip late, “committed” revenue disappears, closed deals turn into delivery problems, and the number looks right… until it doesn’t.

If you’re responsible for the number, you’ve felt this.

And you’ve probably let a few through because stopping them would’ve slowed the quarter down.

I wrote this for leaders who’ve ever defended a number they didn’t fully trust.
