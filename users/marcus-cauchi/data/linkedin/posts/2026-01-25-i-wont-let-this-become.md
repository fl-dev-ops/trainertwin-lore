I won’t let this become insight you agree with and then ignore.

Buyers don’t decide who to trust by how convincing you sound.

They decide by how much exposure you remove when their reality feels like you’ve stopped cooperating.

Trust doesn’t form during explanation. It forms when pressure arrives and something has to give.

Dates slip.
Scope narrows.
Budget stops being hypothetical.

That moment is the test.

Buyers watch whether your view of the deal stays coherent as conditions change.

If it does, you’re relied on.
If your certainty rises while reality degrades, you’re contained.

Every experienced buyer keeps a private ledger. Promises that survived contact with reality. Promises that didn’t.

You are not compared to competitors. You’re compared to that ledger.

If you sell, this isn’t theory.

At least one deal you call stalled is already decided.

You were classified as safe to listen to and unsafe to lean on, so the buyer kept you talking while moving risk elsewhere.

Notice what that implies about your current pipeline.

If you’re still narrating progress instead of revising exposure, you’re already being managed.

Buyers aren’t asking who you are. They’re watching what breaks when you’re wrong. They’re watching whether you notice before they do.

Here’s the part most sellers dodge.

You know which behaviours cost you trust last time, and you repeated them as soon as the next deal felt promising.

Reading this without testing it against a live deal is the same behaviour.

What’s unresolved is this.

If trust is decided before deals stall, then most pipeline reviews are fiction, and the system that replaces them will not be kind to those who needed the fiction.

Stop engaging the moment your words no longer reduce the buyer’s exposure.
