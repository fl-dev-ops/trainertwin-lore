Karrie Sullivan commented on Mark Stouse's post and made the point that a lot of what gets called innovation is really incremental improvement on low-stakes problems.

That makes sense when you look at how most organisations approve work.

Short payback is easier to fund. Clear metrics are easier to measure. Limited downside is easier to live with. The work that could really change something is harder. It takes longer. The outcome is less certain. Some of it will fail. For a while, it can look like a poor use of money.

So nobody has to say they don’t want innovation.

The funding, measures and review process can squeeze most of it out before it gets very far. Then people keep improving what already exists and everyone still gets to call it innovation.

The real test is whether the organisation leaves enough room for hard work that may take time, may fail and may not fit the current model.
