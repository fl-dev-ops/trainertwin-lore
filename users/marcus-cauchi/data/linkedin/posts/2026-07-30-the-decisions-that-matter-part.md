The Decisions That Matter
Part 10: Should We Commit?

Every important decision reaches the point where discussion must become commitment. Budgets are approved, contracts are signed, forecasts are accepted and people begin acting as though the future is now certain.

The quality of that commitment depends entirely on what happened beforehand. Were assumptions tested? Were the right people involved? Were the commercial, operational, political and personal risks made visible? Is the decision still sound if one important assumption turns out to be wrong?

Commitment isn’t the absence of uncertainty. It’s the point where uncertainty has been understood well enough to justify action.

Before committing, ask one final question.

Has our confidence been earned, or have we simply run out of time?
