I will be posting this repetitively. I am
Hiring. My most urgent hire is a second Technical Account Manager.

Melak Anbesse  has broad shoulders but he can’t drive the growth of our customers alone.

Do not apply for this role if:

	•	You’re looking for a support or ticket-driven job. This isn’t that. There is no queue to hide behind.

	•	You prefer clean handoffs and narrow swim lanes. This role owns outcomes — including the first 30 days of technical implementation and everything that follows
.
	•	You need a playbook for every situation. You’ll get principles, not recipes. If you’re uncomfortable operating in ambiguity, you’ll struggle here.

	•	You think “customer happiness” is the goal. It isn’t. Customer results are. Sometimes that means saying no. Often it means pushing back.

	•	You’re uncomfortable telling a VP of Sales or RevOps that their workflow is wrong — and then showing them why.

	•	You haven’t actually lived inside modern outbound systems (CRM, SEP, dialer, data, workflows) and dealt with the messy reality of how sellers really work.

	•	You like roles where someone else owns the number, someone else owns adoption, and someone else owns expansion. Here, you own the outcome.

	•	You prefer theoretical frameworks to operational reality. This is a field role. The system must work on Monday morning.

	•	You don’t naturally close loops, raise standards, and feel personally responsible when something underperforms.

	•	You want to be liked more than you want to be respected as the expert in the room.

	•	You believe your past wins entitle you to comfort. TitanX runs on a Day Zero mindset. Yesterday’s success buys you nothing.

	•	You’re not prepared to:
	•	Empty the tank
	•	Own your shit
	•	Hold your ground with customers
	•	Plan and play to win
	•	Act like it’s Day One, every day

This role is for someone who:

	•	Can run first 30-day implementations
	•	Then own long-term adoption, workflow performance, and expansion readiness

	•	And who takes the personal responsibility for whether the system actually produces revenue outcomes for the customer.

If you want a serious operator’s role in a company that actually cares about standards, outcomes, and doing this craft properly — this one might be for you.
