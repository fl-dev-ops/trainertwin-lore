Your forecast is only as good as the evidence beneath it. “Good call” is not evidence. “Positive feedback” is not evidence. “They seemed keen” is not evidence. A buyer doing decision work is evidence: naming the problem, involving others, confirming risk, agreeing criteria, allocating time, testing value, or changing internal behaviour.
 
Use this standard: “Did” beats “said”. “Said” beats “observed”. “Observed” beats “inferred”. “Inferred” must be labelled. “Unverified” cannot be forecast.
 
That one discipline would clean up most pipeline reviews. It would stop sellers mistaking activity for progress, managers turning hope into commit, and leaders hiring, spending or promising against revenue that only exists in CRM.
 
The question is not, “What stage is it at?” The question is, “What buyer-owned evidence makes this safe to believe?” If there is no evidence, it may still be worth pursuing. But it is not safe to count.
