The decision was approved. That doesn’t mean it’s still safe.

Most commercial failures don’t begin with an obviously bad call. They begin when a reasonable decision outlives the evidence that made it reasonable. The forecast looked solid. The business case worked. The partner committed. The AI project promised savings. Someone credible signed it off.

Then the conditions changed. The evidence weakened. Contradictions appeared. Assumptions expired. Nobody reopened the decision because everyone still treated it as settled.

That’s when the cost starts showing up.

A decision can be well made on Day 1 and unsafe to rely on by Day 187. Approval tells you what was justified then. It doesn’t tell you what’s still justified now.

Before you keep funding it, forecasting against it, hiring around it or reporting it to the board, check what still remains true and trustworthy.

What’s still true? What’s changed? What would you stop relying on if you had to rebuild the decision from the evidence today?

If this has made you think of a decision that’s been running on inherited confidence rather than current evidence, I’m curious, what changed first?
