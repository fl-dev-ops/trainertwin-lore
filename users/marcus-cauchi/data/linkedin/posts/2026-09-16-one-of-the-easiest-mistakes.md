One of the easiest mistakes after something goes wrong is fixing the wrong thing.

You survive it. Tighten a few controls. Change the process. Add another review. Everyone can point to what’s different.

But that doesn’t mean you learned the right lesson.

Maybe the process wasn’t the problem. Maybe the evidence got softened on the way through. Someone saw the contradiction and didn’t push it. People waited for more certainty while their choices got worse.

Then the fix creates its own risk.

You make the organisation better at avoiding the last failure and worse at seeing the next one.

That’s what I took from Dan Blake’s piece on anti-fragility.

Pressure shows you how the organisation actually behaves when the stakes rise. What gets heard. What gets ignored. What people feel able to challenge. What gets decided while the picture is still incomplete.

Then you have another decision to make: what should change, if anything?

That decision deserves just as much scrutiny as the one that got you there.

Think about the last thing that went wrong in your business.

What did you change afterwards, and what evidence do you have that you changed the right thing?
