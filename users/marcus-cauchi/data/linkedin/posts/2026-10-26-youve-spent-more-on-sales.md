You’ve spent more on sales. More tools, more tokens, more activity and more data. You’d expect something to improve.

Torsten Slok, Apollo Global Management, Inc.’s chief economist, has been examining the financial returns from AI investment. Outside the seven biggest US technology companies, revenue per worker and profit margins across the other 493 companies in the S&P 500 have been broadly flat. So far, he’s finding little evidence of AI producing a financial payoff across that group.

https://lnkd.in/eTaTbYY2

The point he makes about judgement interests me even more.
Businesses depend on people understanding their customers, recognising what’s happening, weighing the evidence and making decisions they’re accountable for. Trust, context and knowing how things actually work matter. AI can help with parts of that, but buying more computing power doesn’t automatically improve judgement.

We’ve seen this happen in sales for years. Businesses invest in more leads, technology, training and people without first establishing what’s stopping them getting better results from what they’ve already paid for.

Take pipeline. If the opportunities you’ve already generated aren’t converting, producing more of them could simply make the problem more expensive. Whether you need more opportunities, better opportunities or to fix something else depends on what’s actually getting in the way of customers buying.

AI can help salespeople do more. Whether that produces better commercial results depends on what was getting in their way in the first place.
