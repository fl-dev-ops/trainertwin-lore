Many PE firms are good at reviewing reported GTM performance. Revenue growth, pipeline coverage, CAC payback, retention, forecast accuracy. Those numbers matter. But they often tell you last what is becoming expensive first.

A business can still hit the quarter while the commercial condition underneath it is weakening. Pipeline can grow while fit weakens. Retention can look intact while adoption narrows and executive sponsors go quiet. Forecasts can look clean while stages are being advanced by seller effort rather than buyer conviction.

The cost is not only the original miss. It is the decisions made on top of a weak read of reality: hiring too early, protecting spend for too long, building delivery plans around revenue quality that is not there, and finding out late that what looked like growth carried more fragility than the numbers suggested.

That is the gap I wrote about here: the difference between visible GTM performance and underlying operating condition, and why more investors and operators should inspect revenue quality and operating debt earlier, before the P and L forces the issue.

When GTM Looks Fine and Value Is Already Leaking

For GPs, Operating Partners, founders, CROs, and CFOs, I think the harder question is not whether GTM is performing. It is whether GTM is producing revenue the business can safely build on.
