Late-stage deals rarely stall because buyers suddenly lose interest.
They stall because something changed in how the buyer sees the decision.
You can see it in the pipeline before anyone names it.

* Commit slips
* Forecasts get revised
* Discounting increases

The response inside most sales teams is predictable.

* Add urgency
* Push harder
* Tighten timelines

That often makes the problem worse.

Because the moment a buyer gets close to a decision, their attention shifts.

Early on, they think about value.
Later, they think about consequences.

* What happens if this fails?
* Who carries the risk?
* How exposed am I if this goes wrong?

If anything in the process introduces doubt at that point, the brain does what it always does.

It fills the gap.
It starts running failure scenarios.

The buyer begins imagining not success, but what happens if the decision backfires. They picture explaining it, defending it, living with it if it goes wrong.

Nothing has happened yet.
But the decision already feels different.

This is anticipatory buyer's remorse.

The buyer starts to feel the regret before anything has happened.

And once that happens, behaviour changes.

* Momentum slows
* Engagement drops
* Decisions drift

Not because the solution stopped making sense.
Because the decision stopped feeling safe.

This is where many teams misread the problem.
They see hesitation and add pressure.

But pressure applied at the moment of decision often looks less like leadership and more like risk.

If your late-stage pipeline keeps slipping, you may not have a velocity problem.

You may have a decision safety problem.
