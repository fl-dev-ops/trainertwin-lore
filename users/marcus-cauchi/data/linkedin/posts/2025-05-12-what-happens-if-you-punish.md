What Happens if You Punish Failure?

Many managers burn out not from lack of effort, but from trying to do too much, too often, alone.
 The real breakthrough comes from learning to let go, deliberately, not carelessly.

Developing people isn’t about controlling outcomes.
 It’s about designing an environment where your presence becomes increasingly optional, by design, not default or accident.
 That’s not weakness. That’s what sustainable scale looks like.

 That’s leadership. Have a read of David Brock's latest blog. Pure gold. (link in comments)

Failure isn’t a verdict. It’s feedback.
 It reveals friction, system gaps, and where the design is doing the opposite of what’s needed.

Blame shuts down learning.
 Ignoring it lets small cracks compound into systemic fractures.
 Punishment breaks trust faster than performance ever will.

The best managers stay grounded and curious.
 They ask the questions that uncover cause and intent, not just symptoms.
 They create space for reflection without fear, shame, or theatre.

If your team freezes when something goes wrong, you don’t have a performance issue.

 You have a trust issue. A culture issue. And ... a management issue.
 And that’s something YOU CAN change ... if you want to.
