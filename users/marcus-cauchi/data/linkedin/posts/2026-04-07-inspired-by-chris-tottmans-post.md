Inspired by Chris Tottman’s post.

CAC payback tells you how fast cash comes back.

+DRR by cohort tells you whether the revenue you bought becomes more valuable over time.

That is the more important investor test.

A cohort can recover CAC and still weaken underneath, through pricing support, contract shape, discounting, or service intensity that will not hold.

So the real allocation question is not which cohort pays back fastest.

It is which cohort compounds cleanly after payback, without hidden support.

That is why a slower-payback cohort with clean +DRR can deserve more capital than a faster-payback cohort that starts decaying once the initial lift fades.

Chris, the question I’d add is this:

When you see +DRR in a cohort, what do you look at first to decide whether it reflects real durability rather than a short-term lift from pricing, packaging, or support load?
