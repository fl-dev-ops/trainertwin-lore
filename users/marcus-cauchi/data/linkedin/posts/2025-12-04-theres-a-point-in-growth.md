There’s a point in growth where the company looks fine from the outside, and inside it feels like something’s quietly come loose. Not broken, not burning… but there’s still that instinct to check for smoke.

A deal that should’ve landed last Thursday is still “nearly there”. A renewal’s gone strangely quiet and everyone’s sure someone was on it, though nobody can quite remember who. Bits of work drift round like lost luggage. Nothing dramatic. Just things that used to move on their own now needing a little shove and a muttered prayer.

The team’s not the issue. They’re not perfect but pretty solid. Still turning up, still grafting. It’s the wiring underneath them, built for a smaller company, now asked to carry a pace it never signed up for.

So managers spend half their week sweeping up oddities that fell in the cracks between teams. People redo work because it’s faster than finding the rightful owner. Forecasts behave like weather reports: confident right up until you actually need accuracy.

Everyone trying to keep the wheels turning, everyone a bit tired, everyone pretending this is just “the stage we’re in”.

It isn’t.
It’s the moment the business has outgrown the system it’s running on. Happens to every company in this bracket. Not because of weak people or poor leadership, just because success arrived quicker than the structure meant to support it.
The leak never shows up as one big problem. It arrives as a collection of tiny, polite ones.

A delay here.
A drift there.

Real money and real time disappearing into the spaces between handoffs that don’t fit the pace anymore. Most leaders shrug and blame noise, or the season, or the market being moody again.

It always starts in the work everyone insists “should be simple by now” and yet somehow still needs three people and a small ritual sacrifice.

That’s usually the moment the penny drops.

And once it does, the whole picture shifts, which is exactly what ended up coming through when I produced the video, almost without meaning to. 
The signs are the same every time a company outgrows its own bones.
