Most sales teams don't lose the truth in one big dishonest moment. They lose it by inches.

A polite, positive meeting becomes interest. Interest becomes intent. A next step is recorded as progress. A proposal becomes evidence. A date in the CRM becomes belief or worse, a commit.

Then everyone acts surprised when the deal slips.

In fact, most deals don't slip. They weren't where the forecast said they were.

That's not usually because sellers are bad or dishonest. It's because the process teaches good people to defend bad evidence.

It's time we slaughter this sacred calf! 

MORE ACTIVITY DOESN'T FIX FALSE PROGRESS.

It usually makes it worse. If your process can't tell the difference between seller forced movement and real buyer progress, more activity just creates more places for weak evidence to hide.

The vital question is not ,"What did we do?"
It is, "What changed for the buyer?"
