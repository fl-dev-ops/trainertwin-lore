  Luke Eggerton, the first question is not, “Why has the buyer gone quiet?”
  It is, “What did we do, or fail to do, that made silence the easiest option?”

  Silent deals usually come from one of five places:
  We mistook interest for intent.
  We failed to attach the work to a business consequence.
  We did not earn access to the people who carry the risk.
  We left the buyer with homework but no reason to do it now.
  We avoided a hard conversation because the deal still looked alive in CRM.

  The fix is not more chasing.

  It is a clean reality check:
  What decision are they actually trying to make?
  Who is exposed if they get it wrong?
  What evidence are they still missing?
  What would make doing nothing unsafe?
  If we cannot answer those, the deal is not quiet.
  We are blind.

No bad news is not good news. Sometimes it is just no news.

A quiet deal can feel safe because nothing has contradicted your story. A quiet account can feel safe because no one has complained. A quiet renewal can feel safe because no objection has appeared.

Silence is not proof. It is a gap. Before you treat quiet as safe, test the evidence.
