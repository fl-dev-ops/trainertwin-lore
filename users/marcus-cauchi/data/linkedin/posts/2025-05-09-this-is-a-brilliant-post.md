Your ICP Is Lying to You
 And it's not just wasting money, it’s breaking trust.
We love the phrase product–market fit.
 But most companies have no idea who they're even trying to fit with.

The Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) is supposed to guide marketing, sales, and product.
 Instead, it’s often a fantasy, vague, bloated, and aspirational.

“Mid-market B2B, 50–500 employees.”

 That’s not strategy. That’s astrology.
And the cost?

Marketing generates content no one reads. Or they read it, steal it and buy from your competition.
 Sales chases anyone with a LinkedIn profile and a pulse.
 Product builds features for people who never should’ve been sold in the first place.

Here’s the worst part:
 Every bad-fit deal erodes your team’s confidence.
 They don’t say, “this customer’s a mismatch.”
 They say, “maybe the product needs more features.”
It’s not the product.

 It’s the imaginary customer.

Here’s how to fix it:
Study your REAL best customers.
 Not the biggest. 
The happiest. The ones who renew, refer, and don’t churn at the first bump.

Tell a story, not a spreadsheet.
 Understand the moment of need. What triggered the search?
 What internal conversations were they having before they reached out?

Create a ‘Do Not Sell’ list (your anti-ICP-amber-red false-positives)
 Your worst customers leave clues. Capture them. Stop attracting them.
 And make disqualification a strategic advantage.

Align your teams.
 If marketing is aiming one way, sales is qualifying another, and product is solving for neither, your ICP is just noise.

Make it a living system.
 Revisit it every quarter. Fit is not static.
 Neither is your market.

Final test:
 If a new rep joined tomorrow, could they grasp your ICP in 10 minutes?
 If not, it’s not clear enough. And it’s costing you real money.

The ICP is NOT a persona.
 It’s a point of view.
 And most leaders and managers are too scared to make it narrow. They'd have to let got of the illusion that sales is a numbers' game.

That’s not just inefficient.
 That’s how good teams lose.
