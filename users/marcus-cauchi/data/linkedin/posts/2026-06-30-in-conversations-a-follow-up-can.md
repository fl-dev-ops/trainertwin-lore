In conversations, a follow-up can sometimes amplify the strength of a discussion beyond its original intent, often due to misinterpretations of key phrases. For instance:

- "Agreed" may be taken as a stronger commitment than simply "discussed."
- "Next steps" might imply a clearer path than "possible next steps."
- "Value confirmed" can suggest a stronger endorsement than "they liked the idea."
- "Timeline" may indicate a more concrete schedule than "rough timing."

These misinterpretations can lead to follow-ups that serve as evidence later, even if they exaggerate the actual conversation. 

Before sending your next follow-up, consider testing the evidence:
- What claims are you tempted to make?
- What evidence do you currently have?
- What evidence might be missing?
- What should you not trust just yet?
- What are your options to advance, fix, or exit the situation?
- Take a 3-day reality check to reassess your approach.
