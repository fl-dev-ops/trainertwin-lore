We keep turning things buyers say into things buyers have done

A buyer says, “I’ll speak to the CFO next week.”
That’s useful information. But it’s an intention. They haven’t spoken to the CFO yet.

A week later they tell the salesperson, “I spoke to the CFO and she seems interested.” That’s different. Something may have happened, but we’re still relying on the buyer’s account of it.
Then the CFO joins the next meeting. Now we’ve observed something ourselves.
Then she approves the business case and asks procurement to start its work. That’s different again.

Four pieces of information. Four different levels of evidence.

Now imagine what happens to them inside a busy sales organisation. The AE updates the CRM. Their manager discusses it in the forecast call. The CRO hears the summary. Finance gets the forecast. The board gets the number.

Somewhere along the way, “I’ll speak to the CFO” can become “CFO engaged”.
Nobody needs to be dishonest for that to happen. Nobody even needs to make a particularly stupid decision.

Each person compresses what they received into something shorter and easier to use. The trouble is that the uncertainty tends to disappear during the compression.
Then the next person inherits the confidence without inheriting the uncertainty that came with the original evidence.

This matters because we use these records to make increasingly consequential decisions. We forecast from them. We allocate resources against them. Managers coach from them. Marketing analyses them. Finance plans against them. AI is increasingly being asked to interpret and update them.

We need to know how often the information at the top of that chain still means the same thing as the information that entered at the bottom.

Take ten of your most important opportunities and trace a few important CRM claims back to where they came from. Economic buyer engaged. Procurement underway. Decision expected this month. Budget approved. Champion confirmed.

Don’t ask whether the field has been completed.
Ask what actually happened that earned the field.

I'd wager a pint those are two very different audits.
