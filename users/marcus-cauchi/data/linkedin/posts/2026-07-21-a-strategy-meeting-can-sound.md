A strategy meeting can sound convincing right up to the point where someone asks a simple question: what are we relying on here?

Not what do we hope will happen, and not what is written in the plan. What must be true before we hire against it, invest against it, report it to the board or build an exit story around it?

That is where many strategies become fragile. The assumptions are buried, the evidence is mixed, the trade-offs have not been made, and weak claims are repeated until they sound settled.

Then the business starts depending on them.

A strategy becomes useful when people can say, in plain language: this is what we believe, this is the evidence, this is what remains unproven, this is where the boundaries are, this is what we will not do, and this is what would cause us to review the decision.

That is the real work. Not predicting the future, but deciding what the business can safely rely on before reality tests it.
