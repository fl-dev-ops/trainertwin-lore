The Meeting That Changed Everything

I used to think good selling meant finding the pain and pressing on it until buyers couldn't ignore it anymore. Dig deeper. Remind them of their hurt. Get them to admit how bad things really are. (Hint: They already know)

I was wrong.

That approach killed more deals than I care to admit. I damaged trust when I needed to build it. I pushed buyers into defensive mode right when I needed them to open up.

The breakthrough came when I stopped making buyers feel worse about their problems and started helping them see a way out. Same problems. Same solutions. Completely different emotional state.

The results were immediate: trust jumped, close rates climbed, and buyers became long-term advocates instead of one-time purchases. And referrals skyrocketed.

This isn't about being soft or avoiding difficult conversations. It's about understanding that pain gets attention, but safety and vision drive decisions.

Here's how one small shift changed everything.
