Imagine within 120 days forecasting is within 3% variance. Turnover drops. Fewer new logos but the ones that land, stay and spend more. They open fewer preventable tickets for CS and Ops. Product develops products ideal customers want, buy, use and recommend.

There's so much hype about AI. What nearly all of it seems to miss is that AI is best applied to helping support human judgement.

The fashion for automation is a hangover from the past 20 years' obsession with technologically driven efficiency. How do we automate more? How do we get more with less cost, fewer pesky employees on payroll and drive up efficiency?

But:
Efficiency ≠ Effectiveness
Interest ≠ Commitment
Contract ≠ Activation
Closed Revenue ≠ Good Revenue

Today alone, my inbox has been hit up with a dozen or so automated outreach messages. I've blocked a couple of sellers because they didn't read the previous message responses and simply carried on spamming me regardless of the attempted interaction on my part. My thought when this happens is "Go away! Never bother me again." 

I doubt I'm alone in that feeling of "FFS, leave me alone", the feeling of being a number in the sequencing mentality of "throw enough shit and some of it will stick" and I decide, "No more!"

AI is brilliant when humans use it as their thinking partner, to filter through masses of data and sift out the stuff we miss, and to help support better judgement.

Human judgement in the age of AI is what differentiates those who use it well, and those who compound and amplify what is already shockingly bad. You don't hire AI to make your results worsen so why do you use it that way? Why do you let or encourage your marketing and sales teams to abuse it that way?

If you are CFO, CRO CEO or founder, and your GTM function is using AI to scale stupidity, interrupt more people who could be your customers, your partners or your advocates, ask yourself, "Why are our results tanking, our costs spiralling, new hires turning over, middle management burning out and senior hires failing?"

The mirror is a great place to start. It's almost never pretty and almost always deeply unsettling. When you realise what you thought made sense and what gave you the illusion of being successful (fat pipelines where sales cycles are getting longer, close rates falling, discounting is on the rise, churn is getting worse and you're spending more to keep less in profits and your cost to service is rising), STOP!

I like to ask, "What will happen if you stopped all cold outbound for a month and focused on developing your sellers' judgement?"

Imagine the reactions I get. But when the occasional leader stops long enough to do this, their results flip. 

The real AI question is not “how much can we automate?” It is “which decisions are we making safer, and what evidence proves it?”
