Most stalled deals aren't stuck in sales.
They are stuck inside the customer.

A deal only moves when the buyer can and is willing to make a decision.
If they can't align internally, release funds, and take risk, your stage changes are just activity.

Most deals die at the same point - the buyer can't justify the political risk.
They fear losing credibility if it fails, or they doubt they can make the change happen.

When we forecast based on activity (calls, meetings, proposals), we manufacture problems for ourselves:

false confidence -> bad resourcing decisions -> wasted exec time -> quarter end discounting.

A deal earns forecast weight only when the buyer can evidence five things:
- why it must happen now in their words
- who will drive it internally
- where the money comes from
- what the organisation agrees the problem is
- what gets worse if they do nothing

One test that removes the guesswork:
If the buyer cannot write a 5 line internal summary that names the decision date, approvers, and funding path, THEY are NOT ready to buy.

Watch forecast error and no decision rate. Both should fall as evidence quality rises.
