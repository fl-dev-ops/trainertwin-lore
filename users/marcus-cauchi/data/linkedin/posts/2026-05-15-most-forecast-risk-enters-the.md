Most forecast risk enters the business disguised as confidence. Not dishonesty. Not incompetence. Confidence.

A useful buyer conversation becomes “progress.” A warm buyer becomes “intent.” CRM movement becomes “deal movement.” Pipeline coverage becomes “growth confidence.”

By the time the number reaches the CFO, CEO, board, or PE sponsor, the story can feel safer than the evidence underneath it deserves.

That is where businesses get trapped, because weak evidence does not stay inside sales. It shapes hiring plans, marketing spend, delivery capacity, cash expectations, board commitments, investor confidence, and value creation assumptions.

The business starts spending against revenue that has not been proven.

The problem is not optimism. The problem is allowing seller confidence to travel upward as if it were buyer-owned evidence.

A seller can feel good. A manager can believe the story. The CRM can be current. The forecast can look coherent. None of that proves the buyer has actually moved.

The most useful forecast question I know right now is this: “Which part of this forecast is supported by buyer-owned evidence, and which part is still seller confidence?”

That question changes review quality immediately. It removes theatre, exposes assumption, and improves forecast honesty while there is still time to act.
This is the core idea behind Commercial Reality: do not let confidence rise faster than evidence.
