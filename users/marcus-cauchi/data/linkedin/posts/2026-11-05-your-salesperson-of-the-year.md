Your salesperson of the year has just become your CFO’s biggest headache.

They smashed their target. Brought in the biggest deals. Got the bonus, the applause and the photograph with the CEO.
Six months later, revenue’s up, but profit isn’t keeping pace.

CEO: What’s going on?
CFO: I’ve been looking at what some of these new customers are costing us.
CRO: Sales hit the numbers. We approved the discounts.
CFO: I know. But have you seen what happened after they signed?
Some customers needed more support than expected. Others required changes to what was agreed. A few are barely using what they bought.
CEO: Were these bad deals?
CFO: That’s what I’m trying to establish.
CRO: We checked the margins when we approved them.
CFO: Before we knew what they’d actually cost to serve.
CEO: So what do we know?

And there’s the problem.

Sales knows what was sold. Finance knows what was invoiced. Customer success knows what’s happening after the sale. But nobody’s put it together to establish which customers are worth winning, what they really cost or whether they’re likely to stay.

Perhaps the deals were good decisions when they were made. Perhaps delivery costs changed. Perhaps the warning signs were there and nobody joined the dots.

We don’t know yet.

But before we change the commission plan, blame the salesperson or spend more money generating similar business, wouldn’t it be worth finding out?

And if your best salesperson is judged on revenue, who is checking whether the business is better off because of what they’ve sold?
