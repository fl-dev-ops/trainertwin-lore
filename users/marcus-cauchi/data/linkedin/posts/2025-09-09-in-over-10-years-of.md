In over 10 years of customer-facing roles, one thing has always been clear: people buy from people.

Let’s face it AI isn’t going anywhere and in fact it has its place in customer service. It’s a tool that can be cost-effective, speed up processes, and even deliver some productivity gains. But at what cost to a company’s reputation and long-term loyalty?

I took some time to look deeper into this. A ServiceNow study found that 69% of UK respondents believe AI chatbots fail to understand emotional cues like tone and frustration, and 68% feel AI has under-delivered over the past five years. There is further evidence to back this up. While AI adoption is soaring, the majority of companies (95%) report little to no measurable business return from their AI investments overall. In fact, the biggest wins often come not from replacing people, but from using AI to support them.


Customer service is where this shows most clearly. Bain & Company found financial firms using AI saw about 20% productivity gains in support teams, but crucially, those results came when AI was paired with, but not instead of, human agents. In contrast, when businesses lean too heavily on chatbots or automation, customers often walk away frustrated, which damages trust and reputation.

A wise friend and mentor of mine Marcus Cauchi  likened this to “Dave the doorman” to which I have added a modern take. 

It’s like checking into a hotel. You can have the slickest booking app in the world, but the moment you arrive, it’s Dave the doorman who makes the difference. He remembers your last stay, asks about that thing you had going on, carries your bag, and greets you with a smile that feels personal. That’s the moment you decide you’ll come back, not because the process was efficient, but because the experience was human.

Look at brands like Apple and Lush versus their competitors: both put people at the centre of their customer experience. It’s not just their products that people buy into It’s the trust, empathy, exeperience, and service that surrounds them and keeps people loyal.

AI should empower staff, not replace them. Because in customer service, efficiency may win the moment but humanity wins the customer.
