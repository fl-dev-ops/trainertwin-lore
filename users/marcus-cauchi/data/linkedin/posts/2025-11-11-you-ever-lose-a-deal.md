You ever lose a deal that felt certain, then silence?

Champions don’t sign. Signers don’t champion.

That’s not ghosting.
 It’s a gap between the person who believes and the one who decides.
Your champion believes.
 They see the value, they want to help, they push.
 But when the decision moves upstairs, belief meets risk and that’s where it breaks.

The signer’s job isn’t to believe. It’s to protect.
 They don’t know you yet. They are managing risk, not chasing progress.
 And your champion can’t always carry that story safely across.

That silence you hear isn’t rejection.
 It’s risk without reassurance.
 Deals don’t move because one person believes, they move because the next room does too.

You can’t close that gap with more pressure or persistence.
 You close it by making buying feel safe.
 That starts early, in discovery, in how we talk about risk, and in how managers coach judgment every week.

When champions feel safe explaining why this works,
 signers feel safe saying yes.

Teams that build that bridge cut forecast variance in half within 90 days.
 And when the champion moves on, the story still stands.

That’s when deals move without drama.
 That’s when founders stop rescuing every call.
 That’s when selling starts to feel human again.
