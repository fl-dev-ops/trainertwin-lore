When Growth Feels Hard
 
The café was half-empty, sunlight cutting thin lines across the table.
 Ben stirred his coffee without really drinking it. Sarah watched the steam drift for a moment.
“You ever open a forecast and just stare at it?” she asked. “Everything looks fine, but you know it isn’t.”
Ben gave a dry smile. “Every quarter. We all nod through the board deck, then stay up half the night checking if we’re lying to ourselves.”

Sarah laughed quietly. “Yeah. Everyone’s smiling, but we’re all bluffing.”
He leaned back. “It’s that strange point where things work, but still feel fragile. Like it only moves if you push.”
“The second I stop,” she said, “it slows. It’s like I’m the motor.”
Ben nodded. “You think you’ve built a team, but really, it’s just another version of you.”
“That’s what gets me,” Sarah said. “I thought growing meant freedom. Turns out it’s just more people waiting for my approval.”

He chuckled. “I told myself it was a busy spell. Then I realised it’s always a busy spell.”
“Success,” she said softly, “but heavier.”

He looked down at his cup. “Your judgment’s all in your head. They’re good people, they just can’t see how you think.”
“Maybe I never showed them,” she said. “Half the time I forget to explain, then I get annoyed when they guess wrong.”
“Yeah,” Ben said. “It’s not trust. It’s translation.”
She smiled faintly. “Guess that’s on me.”
“Speed feels like progress,” he said, “but sometimes it’s just panic wearing a nice suit.”

Sarah let out a short laugh. “Calm feels weird now.”
“Because chaos used to mean we were moving,” he said. “Now it just means noise.”
She looked at him. “So what did you do?”

“I stopped trying to pull away,” he said. “Started building things that could work without me hovering. Once I saw it start to hold together, I stopped trying to control every inch.”
“Must’ve been hard.”
“It was. You’ve got to give up being the hero first.”

Sarah nodded slowly. “Maybe that’s what growing up looks like.”
Ben paused. “I don’t think it was ever about control. I just wanted to know it wouldn’t all fall apart.”

They sat for a while, letting the sound of cups and quiet conversation fill the space.
“I used to think busyness meant growth,” she said.
He smiled. “And now?”
“Now I think calm does.”

They both laughed. Not loud, just that small laugh people share when they realise they’ve been fighting the same battle all along.
