It looked settled.

The decision was made.
The meeting ended.
Everyone moved on to the next thing.

Nothing felt unfinished.

But the work did not stop.

It showed up later, in different places.
In follow-ups.
In coordination.
In exceptions that needed handling.
In teams who were not part of the original decision, but now had to make it work.

No one called it rework.
No one escalated it.
It was not framed as a problem.

It just became part of the day.

Over time, that became normal.
Not because anyone chose it.
Because it kept returning.

The decision still made sense.
No one questions that.

We thought the work would end when the decision was made. It didn’t.
It keeps appearing downstream.
Different teams end up absorbing it.
It survives multiple initiatives without ever being revisited.

This is not about bad decisions.
It is about noticing what decisions leave behind once they are no longer discussed.

Most organisations are not stuck.
They are managing around things that once looked settled.
