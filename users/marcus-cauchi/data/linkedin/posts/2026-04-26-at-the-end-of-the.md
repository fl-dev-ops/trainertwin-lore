At the end of the 1990, Carl Sagan asked a simple (and uncomfortable) question:

What if even a fraction of the resources we poured into rivalry had been invested in improving life on Earth?

Sagan was not an activist.
He was a scientist. An astronomer. A teacher.

Someone who spent his life trying to understand our place in the universe... and who concluded that our greatest responsibility might be to take care of our home planet.

At the height of geopolitical tensions, the  USA alone spent hundreds of billions on nuclear weapons and defense systems.

Sagan’s point was not ideological.

It was systemic.

👉 We are extraordinarily good at mobilizing resources when we perceive a threat.
👉 We are far less efficient at doing so when the threat is diffuse, slow, or collective.

Today, the nature of risk has changed.
Or has it?

Health systems.
Energy systems.
Natural systems.

We still don’t have a resource problem.
We have a prioritization problem.

Because the real question is not whether we can afford to invest in resilience.

It’s whether we can afford not to.

If Sagan were speaking today, I suspect his question would remain the same:

What are we choosing not to see?
