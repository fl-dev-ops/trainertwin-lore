Because watching stand-up might be the best sales training out there.
No slides. No AI tools. Just a mic, a spotlight, and a masterclass in human behaviour.

Here’s what salespeople can steal from stand-ups, and why it matters:

1. Open strong
You’ve got seconds to earn attention. Great comics start hot. So should you. Ditch the preamble.

2. Read the room
Comics watch for tiny shifts in energy. Great sellers notice when buying signals dry up, or when someone tunes out. You can't sell if you're not paying attention.

3. Earn the right to challenge
The best comics don’t start with taboo jokes, they build trust first. Same in sales. Build credibility then go deeper.

4. Move on fast
If a joke flops, they don’t explain it, they let it go. Same with prospects who aren't a fit. Don't waste energy trying to revive the dead.

5. Use silence well
Amateurs panic in the quiet. Pros use it to build tension and make people lean in. Learn to shut up at the right moment.

6. Cut the fat
Every word in a set is intentional. Your pitch, email, or deck should be too. Say less. Say it better.

7. Practise in the wild
Comics test material live and tweak it constantly. Sellers should do the same. You don’t get better in your head.

And no, I won’t be ‘switching off’. I’ll be scribbling notes.
