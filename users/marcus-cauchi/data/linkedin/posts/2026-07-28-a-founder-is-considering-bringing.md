A founder is considering bringing in a fractional CRO. The company needs growth, the sales leader is struggling, and the pipeline cannot be trusted. An experienced operator who can take control quickly feels like a sensible answer.

The accepted explanation is that the business needs stronger leadership. The fractional executive brings the judgement, discipline and credibility the internal team currently lacks.

The missing condition is whether the business is building commercial capability or borrowing one person’s ability to rescue it. A highly capable executive can improve meetings, rewrite the forecast, repair deals, challenge managers and create confidence quickly. That early progress may be real, but it may also hide the fact that the system still cannot work without them.

The problem appears later. The executive leaves, the meetings weaken and the forecast deteriorates. The founder discovers that authority, judgement and customer knowledge never transferred into the organisation.
Fractional leadership is not inherently unsafe. It may be the best answer when the company needs experienced help before it can justify a full-time appointment.

The useful test is:
Which decisions will the internal team be able to make reliably by the end of the engagement without the fractional leader present?

If the answer is unclear, the founder is not only buying leadership. They may be creating a new dependency.

That's worth examining before the first month’s progress makes the arrangement look safer than it is.
