How Fear Turns ‘Committed’ Deals Into Ghosts

The first time I saw fear kill a forecast, it wasn’t a rep who showed it.
 It was a manager.

We were walking out of a team meeting.
 Room was busy.
 People were upbeat.
 Numbers looked good.

And she leaned over and said, quietly,
 “I can tell when a deal is in trouble. The rep’s face tells me before the pipeline does. They go still for a moment. Then they say it’s fine. And I don’t know if they’re protecting themselves or protecting me.”

That’s the moment deals turn into ghosts.
 Not when the buyer pulls out.
 Not when the date slips.
 It happens in that half-second pause where the truth gets replaced by what feels safer to say. The tiny stammer.

Reps don’t overcommit because they’re reckless.
 They overcommit because they’re scared.
 Scared of looking weak.
 Scared of being the one who slows the meeting down.
 Scared of the reaction they’ll get if they admit a wobble.

So the risk gets polished.
 The doubt gets hidden.
 The early warning signs get lost.
 And the deal starts drifting away while everyone is smiling.

The cost isn’t the missed number.
 It’s the silence that follows.
 Managers stop trusting updates.
 Reps stop asking for help.
 Founders start working in the dark.
 And the whole system gets a little more tense every week.

Here’s the fix.
 And it’s softer than you think.
Stop asking, “Is this committed?”
 That invites performance.
 Ask, “What would make you more confident than you are right now?”
 That invites honesty.

The whole tone shifts.
 People exhale.
 They stop acting.
 They start thinking.
 And the ghosts show up as real deals with real risks you can actually coach.

Try that question once this week.
 One moment of safety.
 One bit of truth that arrives on time instead of too late.

You’ll see the ghosts long before they disappear.
