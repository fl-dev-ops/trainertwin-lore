She stopped taking notes.

She'd been writing since we started. Dates. Names. Things her manager had said. Promises about the restructure. Reasons to stay. Reasons to wait. Every few minutes she'd underline something she'd already written, as though looking at it again might make it mean something different.

About half an hour into the conversation she stopped writing. She looked down at the page for a while, closed the notebook and slid it to one side. Neither of us said anything. The silence didn't feel awkward. It felt like she'd just noticed something that had been sitting in front of her all morning.

Then she smiled.

It wasn't the smile of someone who'd solved the problem. It was more serene than that. She'd realised she'd stopped looking for an answer and had started looking for permission to ignore one.

Until then, every new conversation at work had felt important. Another promise. Another explanation. Another reason to wait a little longer before deciding. 

She'd treated each one as though it might change the outcome.

When we looked back through her notes, something else became obvious. Everything that mattered had happened weeks earlier. The same promises appeared again and again. The same frustrations appeared again and again. The same explanations appeared again and again. She hadn't been collecting new evidence. She'd been collecting new reasons to doubt the evidence she already had.

We never talked about whether she should leave. By then it didn't seem to matter. The conversation had moved somewhere much more interesting. She'd stopped asking what the business might do next and started noticing what it had already been doing for months.

She picked the notebook up when she left. She tucked it under her arm, thanked me and walked out.

She never opened it again.
