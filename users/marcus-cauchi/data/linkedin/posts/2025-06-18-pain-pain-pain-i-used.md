Pain Pain Pain = ???

I used to teach it like gospel.
Pain = $$$
No Pain, No Sale.
Twelve to one, pain moves people twelve times harder than pleasure.
All true. All incomplete.

Because here’s what actually happens.

Reps get good at finding the wound.
Then they start enjoying it.
They rub in the salt, the chilli, the lime juice (better on crispy prawns!)
They think they’re doing “discovery.”
They’re actually triggering the threat response.

Fight. Flight. Freeze. Not frolic.
And once the buyer’s brain flips, the sale is already lost.
Because it’s not pain that closes deals.
It’s safety in the presence of pain.

Salespeople become interrogators.
They look like a threat.
And most people, 97%, don’t keep threats (sadists) in their lives.

When we fail to offer hope, we leave buyers in a heightened emotional state they will associate with you.
That becomes the memory.
That becomes the brand.
And that’s why they don’t come back.

So under what conditions does pain-pressing actually serve either side?

It doesn’t.

It’s outdated. It’s misused. It’s malpractice.
And it’s showing up in the data.

Fewer than 25% of reps are hitting quota halfway through 2025.
We’ve dropped from 60%+ to below 25% in eight years.
And the playbooks haven’t changed.
They’ve just doubled down.

Sales leadership has normalised failure.
But the problem isn’t the reps.
It’s the structure.

Behavioural science, neuroscience, real-world performance data, they’re all screaming the same thing:

STOP pressing the wound!

Pain isn’t irrelevant. But the way we’ve taught people to weaponise it is.

The alternative?
A demand-side, buyer-centric discovery process that makes space for the buyer to explore, decide, and stay in control.

They chose to spend time with you.
Not your competitors. Not the internet. You.
Do you even know why?

Your job isn’t to convince.
It’s to create clarity.
To create safety.
To help them make the best decision for themselves—with or without you.

That’s not weakness. That’s strength.

Todd Caponi talks about it as transparency.
I’d call it vulnerability in service of trust.

Be your buyer’s ally.
Not their accomplice. Not their adversary.

Charles H. Green taught me:
#Trust = (credibility + Reliability + #INTIMACY) / Self-Orientation

And intimacy? That’s the signal that tells your buyer:
You’re safe here.

That’s what makes the sale.
Not the pain. The absence of it.

#BuyerSafety #TheAllyMethod #PrincipledSelling #DemandSideSales
