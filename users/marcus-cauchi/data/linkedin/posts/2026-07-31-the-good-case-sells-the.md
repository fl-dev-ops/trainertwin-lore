The good case sells the deal. The bad case shows you the deal.

Most capital conversations are built around the plan working.

Revenue grows. Margins improve. The investor helps. The company exits at the expected valuation. Everyone does well.

You need to understand what happens when revenue is late, the market turns, another round is required, a covenant is missed, the exit takes longer or the investor loses confidence in the plan.

Who controls the response?
Can the investor cut the budget while leaving you accountable for the target? Can new debt be added? Can the option pool be increased? Can more senior equity be issued? Can you be removed, and what happens to your retained or incentive equity if you are?
What happens to the money at a low, base, high and distress exit after debt, fees, preferences and dilution?

You should know the answers before you sign, because the bad case is when the documents stop being theoretical.
