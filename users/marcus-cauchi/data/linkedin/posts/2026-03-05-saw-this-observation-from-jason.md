Saw this observation from Jason M. Lemkin and it captures an important shift. Most people are framing this shift as a UX problem.

Manual data entry.
Too many sales calls.
Long contracts.

But the deeper shift is economic. Traditional SaaS sold capability. Tools teams used to operate themselves. 

AI software increasingly sells execution capacity. And when software starts doing the work, buyers evaluate it differently. Not as a tool. As a replacement for labour.

This is where many companies will make an expensive mistake.

If AI is applied purely as a cost cutting tool, it strips out assets that are difficult to rebuild. Institutional knowledge. Relationships. Earned trust.

The real opportunity is not labour removal. It is capability amplification. The companies that win this transition will not simply reduce headcount.

They will redesign how humans and software combine to produce better decisions and better outcomes.
