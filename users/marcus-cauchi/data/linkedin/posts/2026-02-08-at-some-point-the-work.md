At some point, the work stops being intentional.

Not because it is broken.
Not because it is ineffective.

Because it already exists.

People know where the friction is.
They know which parts need watching.
They know what happens if something slips.

That familiarity creates momentum.

Effort flows into keeping things from breaking.
Small adjustments keep everything moving.
The day stays predictable.

Over time, that predictability starts to feel like progress.

Even when the effort stays high.

The work is no longer compared to what it costs.
It is compared to the uncertainty of touching it.

Changing it would not just mean changing the work.
It would mean reintroducing questions people have learned how to avoid.

So evaluation fades.

Not through neglect.
Through continuation.

The organisation keeps functioning.
The work keeps accumulating.

But the choice that once justified it
is no longer being actively made.

That is how things harden.

Not because people fear change.
Because they manage the uncertainty that change would bring.
