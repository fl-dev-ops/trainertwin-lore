In complex deals, decision-makers do not decide.

Believing they do is why deals collapse at the end.

Most buying decisions are shaped by people you never speak to.
Peers managing exposure.
Functions reducing risk.
Quiet vetoes with no calendar invite.

The person you call the decision-maker is usually a representative, not the authority.

Here is the micro stakes most teams miss:
If you build around one voice, you mistake access for power and agreement for alignment.

That is why deals feel healthy early.

Momentum builds.
Signals look positive.
Then consensus arrives and shuts it down.

The buyer didn't change their mind.
The organisation finally responded.

You can see the system working by what disappears when it is done right.
Late reversals drop.
Forecast debates shrink.
Deals do not unravel during clean-up.

Reality check:

If deals stall at consensus or die quietly, the issue is rarely the champion.
It is the set of unseen deciders you never mapped.

Selling to one person is not closing.
It's hoping the rest stay quiet.

Where have invisible stakeholders ended a deal you thought was done?
