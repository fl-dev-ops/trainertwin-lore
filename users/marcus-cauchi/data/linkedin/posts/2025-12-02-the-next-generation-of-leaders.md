The next generation of leaders won’t be defined by ambition, but by their ability to stay steady in a crisis.

Charles H. Green's piece hits a deeper point than most people realise.

We talk a lot about “skills gaps”, “soft skills”, “fragile talent”, “Zoom culture”, and everything the younger generation didn’t get.

But we’re missing the real issue right in front of us. Most leaders were never shown how to pass on judgment in the first place. They learned by taking the knocks. By walking into tough rooms. By getting things wrong and recovering the hard way. And because of that, their judgment is lived, not taught.

So now we’ve got two groups stuck in the same loop. One unsure how to step forward. The other unsure how to explain how they did it.

That’s not a talent problem. It’s a missing handover between generations, and it’s become the real leadership bottleneck inside organisations.

We’re trying to grow new leaders without giving them the thing leadership actually runs on: the ability to stay steady in the moment, see it clearly, and choose well under pressure.

You can’t learn that from slides. You learn it from someone who knows the moment walking you through it, step by step, decision by decision. Until we make judgment transferable, not accidental, we’re going to keep losing good people on both sides.

I’m interested in how others see it. Where does this handover break down in your world? And what has helped you close it when it works?
