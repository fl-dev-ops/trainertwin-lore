When pressure works… then turns

You can feel the moment when pressure behaves itself.
Month’s tight. Targets close enough to smell. You nudge a little. People move with purpose. Updates get neat, almost smug.

And for a minute you let yourself believe, “Alright, fine, we might just pull this off.”

Then your people do that unfortunate human thing. They let fear drive behaviour. Answers get thinner. Someone starts staring at their laptop like it’s trying to communicate in Morse. Energy sags. People speak in sentences that sound pre-washed. Everything’s tidy in a way real life never is. That’s the turn.

Pressure stops helping. It starts bending the truth. And people don’t hide the truth because they’re lazy, or difficult, or need a webinar on “radical candour”.
They hide it because they’re scared. Scared of slowing the team. Scared of being the one who says the awkward thing. Scared of kicking the wobble that everyone can already feel under the floorboards.

So the language gets careful. The numbers get... polite. And the information you actually need leaves by the side door.
Pressure creates motion. Fear removes data. And once the data goes, you’re steering the month by smell and folklore.

Here’s the fix. No theatre. No chanting about accountability. Just the grown up move.

When you feel the urge to push, pause. 
Ask one calm question: “What feels risky to say here, and what would make it easier to put that on the table now?”

Give them room. Let the oxygen back in. The truth shows up early when people aren’t bracing for impact.
