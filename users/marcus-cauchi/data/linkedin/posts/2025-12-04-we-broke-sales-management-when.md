We Broke Sales Management When We Broke Apprenticeship

Somewhere along the way, we decided experience didn’t matter.
We promoted top reps who’d never led.
We gave CRO titles to people barely out of SDR school.
We treated management like an upgrade, not a craft.
And the apprenticeship path - the thing that used to pass down judgment, discipline, and skill - vanished.

When you cut the apprenticeship ladder, you don’t get a new kind of leader.
You get leaders who were never taught the job they now hold.

And that’s the crack running through the entire system.

Managers aren’t stupid.
They’re overloaded, underdeveloped, and drowning in meetings they can’t refuse.
They want to coach but don’t know how.
They want to develop people but spend their time firefighting.
Most weeks they’re holding the team together with tape and caffeine.

So reps don’t get coached.
They get rescued.
They get given the answer instead of being taught how to think.
And every time that happens, their capability shrinks a little more.

Meanwhile the organisation buys tools to cover up the gap, AI this, enablement that, anything to avoid admitting that no one has time to teach anyone anything anymore.

The result?
Teams full of good people who were never shown the basics properly, led by managers who were never shown the basics at all.

It’s not a talent problem.
It’s not a motivation problem.
It’s not even a technology problem.

It feels like an apprenticeship problem.

If you want better sales managers, you don’t need another tool.
You need a way back to teaching the craft, slowly, directly, and in the real work, the way every serious profession develops its people.

That’s the hypothesis.
And if it’s true, almost every problem we’re seeing now is downstream of that one break.
