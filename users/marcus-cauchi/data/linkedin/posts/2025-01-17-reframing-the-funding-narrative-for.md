Reframing the Funding Narrative for Women Founders
"Women don’t get funding because they don’t have good ideas."

We all know that’s not true. The real reasons are systemic and cultural:
Women-centric products are dismissed as “niche.”
Venture capital remains overwhelmingly male-dominated.
Female founders are asked risk-focused “prevention” questions, while men are asked growth-focused “promotion” questions.
Women are held to higher standards of proof for scalability and ROI.
But what if we could reframe the conversation - transforming biases into bridges and objections into opportunities?

💡 Reframing Female-Centric Products as Universal Solutions
 When male investors shy away from products like menstrual health or pelvic care, it’s often due to discomfort or lack of relatability—not the market’s potential. Here’s how we shift the narrative:

🔄 Reframe Objections into Growth Opportunities:
"Too niche." → "This market is underserved and growing at 15% CAGR, representing a $2B+ opportunity."
"Not relatable to non-female users." → "Menstrual health drives workplace productivity, reduces absenteeism, and boosts GDP globally."
"How do you scale this?" → "Our modular design and digital infrastructure enable rapid scaling to new markets and demographics."

🎯 Make It Relatable with Analogies:
Menstrual health products = fitness trackers for reproductive health—real-time data empowering smarter decisions.
Pelvic health solutions = Invisalign for orthopaedics—personalised, innovative, and addressing a neglected need.

✅ Anchor the Conversation in Impact and Data:
"Workplace absenteeism due to menstrual issues costs $XX annually—our solution reduces that by 20%."
"The female health market is projected to grow 2-3x faster than male-dominated health sectors by 2030."
