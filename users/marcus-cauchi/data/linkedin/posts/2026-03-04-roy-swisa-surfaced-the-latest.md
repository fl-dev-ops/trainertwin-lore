Roy Swisa surfaced the latest SaaS Benchmarks Report from High Alpha, and there is a structural implication in the data that is easy to miss.

ARR per employee does not meaningfully inflect until companies move beyond 20M ARR, and it steepens again above 50M.

That pattern suggests leverage is not engineered at scale. It's exposed there.

Below 20M, revenue grows and teams adjust, but the slope remains shallow. Productivity improves incrementally while complexity often grows alongside it. Nothing feels broken.

Above 20M, the curve bends.

Revenue per employee measures output. It does not show whether contribution per employee widened while coordination layers, managerial load, servicing complexity and tooling overhead expanded in parallel.

If contribution per employee does not improve faster than organisational complexity, revenue density can rise while economic density stays flat. At 8M or 12M, that feels manageable. Founder intensity absorbs friction. Expansion revenue masks inefficiency. Cash flow still looks healthy. At 22M, it becomes governance.

The uncomfortable implication in the benchmark data is this. The structural model built between 5M and 15M determines whether the 20M inflection becomes compounding or compression.

Scale does not fix architecture. It amplifies it.

The real question is not whether revenue per employee improved. It is whether contribution per employee improved faster than complexity during the climb.

How many founders are measuring contribution per employee net of coordination cost rather than relying on revenue per head as a proxy for leverage?
