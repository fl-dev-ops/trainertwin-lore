Something happens to good reps long before they burn out.

They start selling in a way that works on paper but wears them down in private.

You see the numbers.
You don't see the cost.

Most teams try to fix this with more pressure, more training, more process.
It never works.
Because the problem isn't skill.
It's the way the job forces people to behave when month end closes in.

If you've ever wondered why someone who should be flying is just holding it together, read this.

It's the story of a rep who looked perfect on the surface and was coming apart underneath.

Nothing dramatic.
Nothing messy.
Just the quiet damage caused by selling in a way that didn't sit right.

If you lead a team, you've got a Julia.
If you sell for a living, you might be her.

Here's what really happens inside a rep long before performance drops.

And what changes when they stop pretending.
