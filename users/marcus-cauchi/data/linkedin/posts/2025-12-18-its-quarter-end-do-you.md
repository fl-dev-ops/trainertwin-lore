It’s quarter end. Do you move the deal forward or protect the decision?

You’re on a call that matters.

Not because the product is complicated.
Because if it goes sideways, it lands on you.

The buyer is cautious. Reasonable. Asking the right questions.

Meanwhile the quarter looms. The number stares back. The Board expects movement.

This's the moment that goes unnamed.

You know the buyer needs time. Or clarity. Or space.

You also know what gets noticed is progress. Momentum. A next step.

You feel it before you justify it. The tightening. The urge to steer. To phrase things more cleanly than they are. To nudge the decision along. That tension is why buyers invite salespeople in.

Not for information. Not for persuasion.

They are already carrying risk they don’t want to carry alone.

They want someone who can think with them while pressure is present. Someone who won’t smooth over trade-offs to make the meeting easier. Someone who can stay in the uncertainty without passing it back.

That costs something.

It can mean a longer cycle. A thin forecast. Explaining why “not yet” was the right call. Sometimes it means watching a deal fade, knowing the decision stayed intact.

Most sales talk skips this. It jumps to techniques. Language. Control. But this is where our real work lives.

At some point, you decide what you’re really willing to be paid for. And what you’re willing to do when no one's watching. I reached a point where I said to myself, I don’t want to lie anymore - not to buyers, not to myself - and I don’t want to lose my livelihood for that choice. Where are you?
