Managers usually get their first leadership role around 30.
 On average, it takes 12 years before they get the help that makes them stop controlling, criticising, and coercing,and start leading with coherence.

That’s 12 years of reactive decisions, trust-eroding habits, and invisible cost.
 Most don’t survive it.

We burn through SDRs, AEs, and managers like talent is infinite.
 As if human beings are disposable.
 As if broken trust is just another line item.

It was already bad when times were good.
 Now? We’re in volatility, constraint, and cognitive overload.
 And our systems still expect high performance from leaders with no structural safety.

So when a manager shows up reactive, unclear, inconsistent, ti’s not incompetence.
 It’s a survival pattern.
 It’s leadership under trauma.
 It’s your culture trying to function without oxygen.

How they show up, what they model, prioritise, tolerate, is your performance.
 It is the pipeline, the yield, the waste.
 It’s the difference between a team you can trust—and a revolving door.

So ask yourself:
 Is the current system producing leaders you’d trust in a storm?
 Or just survivors of one?

If you’ve lived this, on either side, what do you wish someone had said sooner?

And if this landed, pass it to the one person who’s quietly holding more than they should.
 Not to fix them.
 To remind them:
 They’re not the problem.
 The system is.
