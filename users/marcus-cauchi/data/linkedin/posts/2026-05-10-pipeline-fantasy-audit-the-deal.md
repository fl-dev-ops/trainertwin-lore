Pipeline Fantasy Audit

The deal is still in commit. The buyer has stopped moving. Procurement has not appeared. Finance has not accepted the value case. But nobody wants to take it out. So the forecast stays alive.

Not because the evidence supports it.
Because the quarter needs it.

That's how revenue teams drift into fantasy world.
Not through laziness. Through pressure.

The seller wants enough pipeline.
The manager wants predictability.
The CRO wants confidence.
The board wants certainty.
So weak deals survive longer than they should.

One experienced multi-exit CRO described it perfectly: “Everyone becomes too scared to be honest and take stuff out… and off we go into fantasy world.”

Most pipeline reviews are too soft to stop this.

Too many opinions.
Not enough evidence.
Not enough buyer-based questions.
Not enough hard yes/no decisions.

So I built something simple.

The Pipeline Fantasy Audit
12 hard yes/no questions to expose, protected pipeline, false momentum, hidden procurement risk, finance rejection risk, weak buyer sponsorship, forecast theatre, seller confidence dressed up as evidence

Questions like:
Would you still forecast this deal if the quarter did not depend on it?
and
What would we remove from the forecast if nobody was punished for telling the truth?

This is not CRM hygiene.

It's a leadership reality test. Because once forecasts drive hiring, board updates, investment decisions, spend, headcount, cash planning, weak deals stop being a sales problem.

They become a business risk.
