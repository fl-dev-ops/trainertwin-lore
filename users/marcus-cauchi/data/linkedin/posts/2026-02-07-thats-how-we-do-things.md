That’s how we do things around here.

It usually starts with a decision that worked.
At the time, it fit the situation and removed uncertainty.

That success is why it stayed.

But context doesn’t stand still.
Teams change.
Volume changes.
Constraints change.

The decision remains, while the conditions that made it useful move on.

So the organisation adjusts around it.
Not because it’s wrong.
Because it no longer fits as cleanly as it once did.

The extra coordination becomes routine.
The follow-ups blend into the day.
Exceptions stop standing out.

Language shifts.
People stop referring to the decision itself.
They start saying, “That’s how we do things around here.”

What was once relevant becomes familiar.
What was once useful becomes assumed.

That’s how past decisions harden into habit and culture.
Not through neglect.
Through time.

Most organisations aren’t carrying bad choices.
They’re carrying decisions whose context expired before the decision did.

That gap is easy to live with.
And very hard to notice.
