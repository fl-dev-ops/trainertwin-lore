Who gets punished for telling you the truth early?

You tell your salespeople you want an accurate forecast.
Then one of them tells you a £500,000 deal isn’t as strong as everyone thought.

Watch what happens next.
The forecast drops. Coverage drops. Their manager wants an explanation. A recovery plan appears. Someone asks where the replacement £500,000 is coming from.

Now consider the salesperson who has seen the same warning signs but waits another week before changing anything.
For the moment, their numbers look better.
Nobody has lied. Nobody has fiddled the CRM. Both may be acting entirely reasonably.

But one of them has given the company better information earlier, and may look worse because of it.

That’s a problem if you’re using that same data to judge performance, allocate resources, plan revenue and train AI to recognise a good opportunity.

I’ve written about what happens when the people supplying your sales data are also affected by what that data says.

What happens in your business when someone makes the number smaller?
