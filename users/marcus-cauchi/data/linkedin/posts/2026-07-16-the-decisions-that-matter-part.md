The Decisions That Matter
Part 2: Can We Trust This Forecast?

Forecasts don’t normally fail at quarter end. By then, the damage has already been done.

They begin to fail when evidence slowly becomes interpretation and interpretation quietly becomes confidence. A meeting happened. A proposal was sent. Procurement became involved. None of those things proves the buyer will buy, yet they are often treated as though they do.

Once confidence replaces evidence, the forecast begins influencing decisions far beyond Sales. Hiring plans change. Spending increases. Capacity is committed. Board expectations rise. One assumption starts creating several new assumptions, each one further from the original evidence.

The best commercial leaders separate seller activity from buyer movement. They ask what the buyer has actually done that they didn’t have to do, what risk they’ve accepted internally and what evidence would still exist if the salesperson disappeared tomorrow.

Don’t ask whether you believe the forecast. Ask whether the evidence deserves your belief.
