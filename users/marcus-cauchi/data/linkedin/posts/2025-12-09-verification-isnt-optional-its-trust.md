Verification isn’t optional. It’s trust protection.

Happy ears don’t close deals. Honest conversations do.
- Rep: When’s the last time you truly verified the next step? Not assumed, not hoped.
- Manager: Are you checking pipeline hygiene or just nodding along?
- Leader: Is your forecast real or wishful thinking dressed in numbers?

Most teams fail not for lack of effort, but because they stop verifying. Sellers avoid it -it’s uncomfortable. Managers avoid it - it’s confrontational. Leaders avoid it - it forces reflection. Meanwhile the deal clings like a barnacle, waiting for a decision that may never come.

The Framemaking Sale by Brent Adamson and Karl Schmidt covers the verification discipline in depth. Read it to understand the framework, then layer in systems that keep your team and forecast reliable.

Ask yourself:
* Are your reps pretending the deal is further along than it is?
* Are your managers giving clarity or covering uncertainty with activity?
* Are you looking at the forecast you wish for instead of the one that exists?

In the Ally Method™, verification is discipline, not policing. It’s the safety rail for reps, the duty of care for managers, and the truth signal for leaders.

Your blind spots are obvious when you pause: Where are you relying on hope over evidence? Where is trust being assumed instead of measured? Where does inertia masquerade as progress?

Stop ignoring it. Start verifying. Protect the team. Build trust. See the forecast for what it is.
