Ambiguity at the top leads to politics at the bottom ...
 Maybe this scenario is familiar ...
 If it is, what is the mission you and everyone in your organisation serves?

If you can't name it, then you can see the source of a litany of woes.
If you know your team can't name it either, it's time for clarity.
***
I thought I was running a sales team.
 But I was really running interference.
Every forecast call felt like defence.
Not against the market.
 Against my own team’s contradictions.

One AE qualifies on having "a good relationship"
 But the evidence is inaction, not forward movement.
 Another actually used the words 
 ... "Hope, think, try and maybe!" reporting on a live deal!! FFS!

Our CRM? A glorified notepad with stage names that mean something different to everyone.

We marked a deal Green. It turned Red.
 No warning. No clear breakdown. Just silence. And I was the one left explaining it up the chain.

That’s when it hit me.

We don’t have a sales performance issue.
 We have a system trust issue.

And when the system can’t hold clarity, people start protecting themselves:
 - Coaching becomes guessing.
 - Forecasting becomes fiction.
 - High performers quietly detach (and get their CVs out).

It’s not that my team isn’t trying.
 They’re navigating drift disguised as process.
 And then I'm beaten for non-performance.
 And apply the screws to "get things moving",
 And my reps get ghosted
 Or worse, close a deal at a stupid discount,
 That could have yield 50% higher ARR.
How can it be politically safer to do that,
 Than wait and win the entire value, year on year.

Isn't it fiscally irresponsible to give away margin,
 And create preventable work later (if I strip next month to feed this month my guys are playing catchup out of thhe gate)

Here’s what I’ve come to believe:
Motivation doesn’t fix misalignment.
Pressure doesn’t fix trust decay.
The answer isn’t louder goals. It’s better ground.

I’m still in it.
 But I’m committed to leading from a system that backs me, not one I have to compensate for.

***

If you’ve ever been in this spot, carrying clarity for a team stuck in fog, what helped you hold your ground?

 Or if you didn’t? What do you wish someone had said to you?

🙏 Learn once. Teach twice. Tag someone who’s living this quietly.
