We spend a lot of time asking whether a decision was properly approved.

I’m less sure we spend enough time asking what happened to the judgement afterwards. Can we still find it? Can we see what evidence it rested on, what was assumed and what remained uncertain?

Most organisations can tell you who signed something off. Far fewer can show what would have caused the decision to be reopened, or who remained responsible for doing that when the facts changed.

The approval survives. Its basis slowly disappears.

A forecast is still relied on after the evidence beneath it has changed. A system stays in use after its role has grown beyond what was originally reviewed. A project continues after the conditions that justified it have gone.

A supplier, investment or strategy is renewed because stopping now would be awkward, expensive or difficult to explain.

Nobody has to be reckless for this to happen. The decision simply becomes detached from the judgement that once made it reasonable.

We can name an owner, keep an audit trail, require human oversight and measure the result. None of that guarantees that contradictory evidence can get in, that the original judgement can be challenged, or that someone still has the power and duty to change course.

The person accountable for delivery may not control the conditions needed to deliver. The person who approved the decision may no longer be close enough to see what changed.

The people seeing the consequences may have evidence but no standing to force reconsideration.

So the system can remain compliant, owned and measurable while the decision itself becomes steadily less safe to rely on.

That leaves me with a question I can't get past -  Can we still identify, test and change the judgement that moved this decision, and is someone still responsible when the consequences become visible?

Approval should not end when authority is granted. It should create a continuing obligation to notice when the basis for approval no longer holds.

Otherwise accountability begins with the decision and disappears just before the consequences arrive.

What have I missed?
