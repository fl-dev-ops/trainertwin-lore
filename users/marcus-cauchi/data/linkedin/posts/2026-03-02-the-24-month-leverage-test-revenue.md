THE 24-MONTH LEVERAGE TEST

Revenue growth does not create durability. Leverage does.

In the 5M to 20M range, most businesses feel momentum because revenue is rising. Very few stop to test whether leverage actually improved while that growth occurred. The only question that matters is simple: over the last 24 months, did contribution per unit rise faster than cost per unit?

If payroll grew faster than revenue per employee, leverage compressed. If customer count expanded but contribution per customer did not improve, complexity scaled faster than margin. If each incremental dollar of revenue required more cost than it did two years ago, the engine weakened. If working capital intensity increased, exposure embedded. If fixed cost coverage would not comfortably withstand a slowdown, durability is fragile.

When three of those conditions are present, governance is not “tightening soon.” It is already late.

The market does not create discipline. It exposes leverage drift that accumulated while growth looked healthy. By the time valuation pressure appears, the cost structure and customer mix are already embedded.

Before approving the next hire, expanding the stack, or raising the next growth target, run the 24-Month Leverage Test. If leverage has not improved, scale is amplifying fragility.

Growth financed is not growth compounded.
