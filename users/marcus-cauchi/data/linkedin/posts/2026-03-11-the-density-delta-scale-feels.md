THE DENSITY DELTA

Scale feels like progress. Density determines whether it is durable.

Between 5M and 20M in revenue, most leadership teams celebrate expansion in headcount, customer count, and topline. Far fewer measure whether economic density improved as the organisation scaled. That gap is where structural drift embeds.

Density Delta is the difference between the rate of change in contribution per unit and the rate of change in cost per unit. When contribution per employee, per customer, or per project rises faster than cost, leverage compounds. When cost rises faster than contribution, leverage compresses, even if revenue continues to grow.

A negative Density Delta rarely feels dramatic at first. It looks like normal expansion. Over time, it manifests as margin pressure, working capital strain, lower revenue per rep, and eventually valuation compression. The deterioration began long before it was visible.

If your Density Delta has been negative for consecutive years, growth has been financed rather than compounded. Scale amplifies the architecture you built. It does not repair it.

Durability is contribution density.
