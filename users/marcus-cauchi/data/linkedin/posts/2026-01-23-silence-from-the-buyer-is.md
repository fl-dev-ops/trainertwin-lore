Silence from the buyer is not confusion.
It is self protection.

The moment your urgency enters their inbox, silence becomes the rational move.

They didn't forget you.
They didn't get busy.
They did not lose interest.

They ran the calculation.

If I reply, I acknowledge momentum.
If I acknowledge momentum, I move closer to a decision.
If I move closer to a decision, I move closer to owning consequences I did not author.

So they don't reply.

Not because procurement's slow.
Because engaging now increases personal risk.

This is where you are already implicated.

When your next message carries quarter pressure, internal escalation, or urgency they did not create, you stop being useful.
You start exporting anxiety that belongs entirely on your side of the table.

From their position, silence is not avoidance.
It's control.
And it is not accidental.
You trained it.

You trained it the first time urgency showed up without authority.
You trained it when more people appeared instead of ownership.
You trained it when replying stopped feeling safe.

Here is the part you don't get to negotiate.

Buyers respond only when responding lowers their personal risk.

End rule.

If your reply increases their exposure, silence is the correct response.
