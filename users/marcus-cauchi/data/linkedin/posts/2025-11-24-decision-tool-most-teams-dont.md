Decision Tool

Most teams don’t actually need a new vendor.
They need a simple way to decide what to do next.

Here’s the rule that keeps things calm and honest:

Stay while things are stable.
Switch when they aren’t.

What does “stable” mean?

1. The system does the job.
If the platform can do what you need, the problem isn’t the platform.

2. The issues are human, not structural.
Slow replies.
Messy handovers.
Small mistakes.
Annoying, yes.
Switch-worthy, no.

3. You’ve agreed the basics:
• Clear response-time numbers
• A real escalation path
• A short check-in every 4-8 weeks where they show what’s been fixed

If you’ve got these in place, staying is the right call.

So when should you switch?

Only after you’ve set expectations
and the same problems keep coming back.

Three things make switching reasonable:

1. They break the commitments you agreed.
Not once.
Repeatedly.

2. They hide from transparency.
Skip reviews.
Avoid updates.
Downplay issues.
That never gets better.

3. They can’t keep up with your growth.
Your team speeds up.
Their support doesn’t.
Now it’s holding you back.

Here’s the simple test:

“If this issue disappeared tomorrow, would we still switch?”

If the answer is no,
you’re not in a switching moment.
You’re just frustrated.

That happens to everyone.

When things are stable, stay.
When the facts change, switch.
