Founders and CROs are often told the same thing:

“Partnerships are the answer.”

That is incomplete.

Partnerships are not the answer.

Distribution is the answer.

That was one of the strongest themes in my conversation with Alexander J. Buckles.

You don't need the best product in the market first.

You need a way into the accounts that matter, through a partner whose expertise, trust, or installed base gives you leverage you do not have on your own.

But here is where most firms get it wrong:

They hire a traditional partnerships person too early.
They build infrastructure before proving pipeline.
They chase logos before they build a repeatable co-sell playbook.
They confuse “signed partnerships” with distribution.

Alex’s point was brutally simple:

Find a real gap in the market.
Build something that helps the partner solve that gap.
Create a high-value motion around one ICP and one pain.
Then prove repeatability.

That's very different from most partnership strategy decks. And it 's closer to the truth.

Question for founders and CROs:

What would happen if you treated distribution as the first design problem, not the last go-to-market layer?
