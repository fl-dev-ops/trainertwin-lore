Over the last few months, I’ve spent a lot of time asking myself a simple question: what do I actually stand for? I don’t mean what services I offer, what frameworks I’ve built or what books I’ve written. I mean something more basic. If someone met me for the first time in ten years, what would I hope they already understood about my work?

I’ve realised the answer isn’t selling, management, partnerships or AI. Those are all places where I work, but they are not the work itself. The work is making commercial judgement more reliable.

That can sound abstract until you look at what happens when judgement fails. Forecasts become fiction. Managers become rescuers. Customers inherit promises that should never have been made. Partners become busy instead of useful. AI scales mistakes instead of capability. People spend months fixing consequences that should have been prevented.

I’ve made enough of those mistakes myself to know they don’t usually begin with bad people. They begin with systems that reward the wrong things, allow weak assumptions to survive and let responsibility drift away from the people who set the decision in motion.

That's why I wrote the piece below. It is the closest I’ve come to explaining why I’ve spent the last thirty years doing what I do, what ties all my work together and what I want to devote the rest of my career to building.

If you’ve followed my work for a while, I’d genuinely like to know whether this feels like an accurate description of where it is heading. If we’ve never met, this is probably the best place to start.
