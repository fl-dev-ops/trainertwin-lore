“We need more pipeline.” 

Maybe. But before you spend another pound generating leads, hiring salespeople or buying technology, what happened to the pipeline you’ve already paid for? 

The forecast slipped again. Deals that were supposed to close last quarter are still there. Your salespeople are chasing decisions, specialists are preparing another demonstration and managers are reviewing opportunities they’ve discussed a dozen times before. Everyone’s working hard. You’re paying for all of it. And now you’re being asked to spend more. 

Some of those opportunities deserve further investment. Others may be tying up people and money that could produce a better return elsewhere. The problem is knowing which is which. A customer can be genuinely interested. The deal can be real. Your salesperson can be doing everything right. That still doesn’t tell you whether another month of work, another demonstration or another proposal is worth the cost. 

And winning doesn’t settle the question. 

You still have to deliver what you’ve promised, support the customer and earn enough to make the whole investment worthwhile. 

If you had to approve every opportunity in your pipeline as a fresh investment today, knowing what you now know, how many would you fund and resource? 

That’s why I wrote The Price of Pipeline: What Happened to the Pipeline You Already Paid For? 

It’s for CEOs, CFOs, CROs and investors who want to know what their commercial investment is producing and whether the next commitment is justified.
