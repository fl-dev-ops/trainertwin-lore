ICP is usually treated as a marketing definition.

In practice it’s a capital allocation decision.

Every customer admitted into the system pulls resources with them - sales time, onboarding capacity, support load, product attention, sometimes even hiring plans built on the assumption that the revenue will hold.

When the fit is wrong, the economics reveal it first: CAC payback stretches, NRR weakens, and forecasts start relying on narrative instead of signal.

Individually those decisions look reasonable. Across the organisation they redirect time, attention, and capital toward problems the company was never built to solve.

That’s why strong companies treat ICP as a governance mechanism, not a slide in a strategy deck.

Customer admission determines where the organisation’s capital actually flows.
