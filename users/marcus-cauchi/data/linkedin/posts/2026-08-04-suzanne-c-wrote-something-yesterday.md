Suzanne C. wrote something yesterday that stuck with me: “AI won’t replace salespeople. It will expose them.” 

She's right. It exposes weak selling, weak management and the decisions nobody wants to reopen once money, careers and reputations are tied to them.

Her point about silos was the sharpest part. One team makes the decision. Another lives with the consequences. Suzanne's answer was to involve the people closest to those consequences much earlier. She's right about that. The harder question is what happens when the evidence changes after approval.

That matters anywhere an AI decision now sits beneath a value-creation plan, cost case, operating model or board claim. By the time the position reaches the board or investment committee, the doubt, exceptions and rescue may have been compressed out of it.

Which AI-backed value claim or operating decision would be hardest to unwind if the evidence behind it proved weaker than the investment case assumes?
What exact decision are you relying on now?
What value has been established, rather than attributed?
What cost, rescue or dependence has been left outside the claim?
Who carries the downside, and who can change permission?
Can the business still withdraw before the choice becomes expensive or political?

I've written a free paper called The AI Value Evidence Gap. It sets out what boards and delegated decision-makers should be able to answer before widening or continuing material reliance on AI.

I'll send it to anyone responsible for a live material AI decision. Message me and tell me which decision you're examining.
