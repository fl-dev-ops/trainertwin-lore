Some of the most expensive decisions look completely sensible when they’re made. The numbers stack up, the case is credible, everyone can explain why it makes sense.

The problem can be the clock.

Look at the same decision three months, a year or five years from now. If we act and we’re wrong, what happens? If we don’t act and we’re wrong, what do we lose? Which consequences can we undo, and which ones are we stuck with?
What options are we keeping open? What are we closing off? Who actually pays if we get this wrong?

And watch the story you’re telling yourself about the future. What have you started treating as true simply because you can picture it happening? What evidence supports it? What would tell you you’re wrong?

What could you learn before committing more? What would justify a bigger bet, or a smaller one?
And what’s the smallest step that keeps the upside alive without taking more risk than the evidence justifies?

Change the time horizon. Come back to the decision in front of you. Does the evidence still justify the same commitment?
