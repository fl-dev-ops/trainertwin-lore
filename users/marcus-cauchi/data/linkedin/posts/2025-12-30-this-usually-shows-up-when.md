This usually shows up when the conversation moves rooms.

Inside the team, things feel workable. There’s context. People know roughly how decisions are being made and what the system is doing.

Then the discussion goes upstairs. Someone asks, “Why did we do it this way?” Not as a challenge. As a responsibility.

That’s when the answers start to feel thin.

You hear things like, “That’s what the data showed,” or “The system flagged it,” or “It’s been working so far.” All of those might be true. None of them really answer the question.

What they’re actually asking is whether someone is willing to stand behind the decision without leaning on the system. Whether it still makes sense when you have to explain it plainly.

I’ve seen teams realise in that moment that speed covered a lot of gaps. Not mistakes. Gaps in reasoning. After that, things change. A few decisions slow down. Not because anyone demanded it, but because the team wants to be able to explain what they’re doing without pointing at a tool.

That’s when it becomes clear this isn’t about performance. It’s about judgement. And once that clicks, it’s hard to go back.
