You’re on the board. The AI programme is showing an 18% fall in handling time, and finance has a £4m annual saving in the pack. Management wants two approvals next: let the AI make customer refunds up to £500, and remove 60 service roles. I wouldn’t vote yet because I’d want to know whether the evidence supports either decision.

The original approval was for assisted service, with people keeping authority over consequential actions. Today the AI handles most routine work, review has shifted towards exceptions, self-service has expanded, and experienced staff still step in when cases get difficult. Now the board is being asked to approve autonomous financial decisions while reducing the human fallback. That is a materially different decision from the one it approved before.

Start with what we actually know. Handling time fell 18%, which is an observation if the measurement is sound. Saying AI caused the reduction is an inference because the workflow and self-service changed at the same time. Saying AI saves £4m a year is a bigger claim again because we still need the realistic alternative, transferred work, rescue, supplier cost, supervision, correction and fallback. Those are different claims and they need different evidence.

The staffing cut needs its own evidence. Faster routine work does not tell us whether the difficult work disappeared, moved elsewhere, or is still being absorbed by experienced people. If those people leave, we may also be removing the fallback that makes the current result safe enough to use. So I’d split the decisions rather than let one good number answer all of them.

Keep the assisted use that the evidence already supports. Treat autonomous refunds as a separate test: define the customer population, the loss we can afford, who can intervene, how quickly they can intervene, what gets measured, and what stops the test. If we cannot answer those questions, we are not ready to run it. And I would not approve the 60-role reduction on the back of the £4m claim until we know the saving is genuinely incremental and removing those people does not change the risk of the AI decision itself.

Same programme, three decisions. They should not be bundled. I wrote The AI Value Evidence Gap to make that mistake easier to spot before the next approval. What are you being asked to approve now using evidence gathered for a different decision?
