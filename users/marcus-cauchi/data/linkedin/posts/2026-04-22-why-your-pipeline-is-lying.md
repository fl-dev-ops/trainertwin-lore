Why Your Pipeline Is Lying to You

The quarter lands. The deal is signed. The number goes in the board pack. Then the bill shows up later. Success is firefighting. Delivery is fixing bad promises. Finance is carrying margin leak. The sponsor is defending a decision that was easier to sign than sustain.

A month after the close, the handover call happens. Sales says the deal is a big win. Success is already uneasy. Delivery is asking what was actually agreed. The buyer who pushed hardest for the deal is suddenly harder to reach. Procurement is done, but the real users still look half-in, half-out. Nobody says it plainly, but everyone in the room can feel it: this customer bought, but they did not fully decide. From that moment on, the account starts living on subsidy. More calls. More explanation. More senior attention. More work to hold together something that looked finished when it was signed.

Most revenue problems start as decision problems counted too early. A signature is easy to report. It is not proof the buyer did the hard internal work to make the change stick.

That is why pipeline lies. It tracks movement. It does not test strength. Legal, procurement, late-stage activity, none of that proves the decision will hold once the seller steps away.

Not all revenue is equal. Some is booked. Some is usable. Some is durable. Weak revenue looks fine at close, then turns into rescue work, noise, and operating debt.

The better question is not, can we get this over the line? It is, will this still hold when we are gone?

The signature is easy to count. The decision is what remains.
