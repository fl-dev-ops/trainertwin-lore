Your SDR is missing target. You’ve listened to their calls, reviewed their numbers and decided they need coaching. You agree a plan. You give them support. You set a date to review progress. 

Six weeks later, they’re still missing target. 

What does that tell you? That they lack the skills? That they haven’t made enough effort? That they’re unwilling to change? Or could it mean you’ve been coaching the wrong problem? 

Here’s what bothers me. The manager decides what needs fixing. The manager chooses the coaching. The manager decides what improvement should look like. And when the numbers don’t improve, it’s usually the SDR whose future is in question. 

Now imagine you replace them. 

You recruit someone with a stronger track record. You invest in onboarding. You give them the same territory, accounts, targets and management support. Six months later, they’re struggling too. What would you conclude? 

That you’ve hired badly twice? That both SDRs lacked the necessary skills? Or would you finally start questioning the conditions you expected them to succeed in? 

Think about what that sequence has cost. Two people’s time. Coaching. Recruitment. Onboarding. Lost opportunities. Management attention. And possibly two perfectly capable people whose performance was judged against expectations they couldn’t reasonably meet. 

Of course, some SDRs genuinely need coaching. Some won’t improve, even with good support. Sometimes replacing someone is the right decision. But how do you know which situation you’re dealing with? 

A missed target tells you there’s a gap between expectation and result. It doesn’t tell you why that gap exists. And six weeks of unsuccessful coaching doesn’t necessarily tell you either. If you can’t establish why someone is underperforming, what exactly gives you the confidence to decide what happens to them next?
