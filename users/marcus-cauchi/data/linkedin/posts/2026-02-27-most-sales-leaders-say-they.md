Most sales leaders say they want long term capability. They talk about strategic thinking, deeper client relationships and raising the commercial standard of their teams. Yet many operate compensation structures that reward short term survival above all else.

That contradiction is not philosophical. It is structural.

Research consistently shows that managers account for roughly 70 percent of the variance in team engagement. If that finding holds, engagement is not a morale issue or a personality issue. It's the outcome of the system leaders build and fund.

In sales environments, structure always wins. If development time such as coaching, deal reviews, training or call practice reduces this quarter's earnings, the message is clear. This quarter comes first. Learning is secondary. Reps will prioritise deals that close fast, conversations that protect commission and pipeline that converts quickly. That is rational behaviour in the system they are paid to operate in.

If development time is protected, the signal changes. Growth is part of the job. Capability is expected. Over time that compounds. It improves deal quality. It strengthens customer trust. It builds revenue that is harder to displace.

Short term design doesn't just cap performance. It shapes who stays. Over time it selects for short term thinkers and filters out the people capable of principled, strategic selling. It is not neutral. It's directional.

It is hard to sustain buyer centric selling while funding transactional pressure. The system will decide which one survives.

Every incentive system produces the behaviour it pays for. It is that simple.

It's also difficult to see structural flaws when the current structure is producing income. Not because leaders lack integrity, but because incentives shape perspective as well as behaviour.

So the real question for revenue leaders is this. Is your structure building professionals who can raise the standard, or closers who can only survive the month?

I am genuinely interested in examples. What have you changed structurally that improved revenue quality: protected learning time, manager coaching rhythm, compensation design, or something else?
