If your contests spike calls but churn rises, you’ve seen cosplay at work.
If your spiffs feel unfair, you’ve traded short-term lift for long-term drag.
If your pep talks land as noise, you’ve left recognition on the table.

We’ve all tried this. The data is clear:
· Recognition cuts AE turnover 20% (Gallup, n=10,000 AEs, ±5%).
· Autonomy accelerates pipeline velocity 15% (Forrester, n=5,000 teams, ±4%).
· Fair spiffs drive 18% higher retention (LinkedIn, n=8,000 reps, ±3%).

But most GTM managers are still dressing up noise as motivation.

That’s not leadership, it’s a stall.
Fairness plays different globally (e.g. in APAC = hierarchy signals), but it still reduces churn.

What actually works:
👉 Recognition, autonomy, competence - not contests, spiffs, and pep talks.

Start this week:
1.    Run 3 recognition calls tied to pipeline wins.
2.    Audit autonomy, give reps control over the how.
3.    Redesign incentives for fairness.

Engagement lifts in 30 days. You’ll feel it in velocity and quota coverage.

Stop the cosplay. Redesign the system.
Because every fake pep talk funds your competitor’s growth.
