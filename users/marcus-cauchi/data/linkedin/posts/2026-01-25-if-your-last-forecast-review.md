If your last forecast review felt clean but unconvincing, this is about you.

Think back to the most recent number you defended in a room where no one below you was present. You knew it would pass. You also knew what it would not survive.

If you're playing the numbers game, or letting it be played in your name, you're not unclear on what's happening. You're choosing insulation and calling it discipline.

You know the move. Push volume to calm the quarter. Accept numbers that survive the meeting, not the field. Reward activity because it is defendable. Nothing breaks, which is why it keeps working.

What you're actually doing is deciding where consequence is allowed to land.

If you ask for more leads, sales pays in weeks they can't ever recover.
If you accept a forecast you already know is padded, managers pay in credibility they won't regain.
If you praise motion because it is countable, customers pay by arriving with expectations you designed and will fail to meet.

You don't feel this cost. That's the feature.

If your job is to hit a number, you learn which inputs to ignore.
If your job is to explain the number, you learn how to soften it.
If your job is to approve the number, you learn which questions slow things down.

Sales adapts first. Reps build private filters so they can survive your targets. This's not resistance. It's correction for a number you know can't be trusted.

Management adapts next. Pipeline reviews turn into performances. Forecasts stop being predictions and start being explanations prepared in advance. Everyone knows which numbers matter and which ones exist so no one has to say the wrong thing aloud.

Leadership adapts last. Dashboards absorb friction. By the time reality reaches you, it looks abrupt. To anyone closer to the work, it was overdue.

Here is a claim you can test immediately. If a metric only needs defending upward and never needs to be lived inside downward, it's already distorting decisions.

If you manage people, notice who stopped arguing. They didn't get aligned. They learned that disagreement damages them, not the call.

If you lead leaders, notice how quickly good news moves and how carefully bad news is wrapped in cotton wool. That's not culture. That's you teaching the system what you are willing to hear.

This is not a systems problem. It is an authorship problem.

The numbers game survives because it protects your authority longer than it protects the truth. You keep the metric that lets you look reasonable while the damage accumulates elsewhere. You know exactly which one that is.

Stop the moment a number makes it easier for you to keep your standing than to face what it's hiding.
