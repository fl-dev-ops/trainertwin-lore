The Measure of Who You Are
... What they say when you’re gone.

People talk when you’re not in the room.
 What they say then is who you left them feeling you really are.
 Not the title on your card.
 Not the numbers on your board.
 The words said behind your back, that’s your real reputation.

You can’t buy that kind of talk.
 You earn it one fair act at a time.
 You lose it the moment you cut corners or hide from the truth.

The choice every leader faces is simple:
 play for applause, or play for respect.
 Applause is cheap.
 Respect compounds quietly, like trust.
 You build it by being the steady hand when others grab for credit or hide from blame.

Anyone can chase the short term.
 Anyone can sell by pressure, manage by fear, or lead by ego.
 The better person, the better leader, takes the longer road, even when no one’s watching.

Sales exists because customers do.
 Business exists to serve, not to exploit.
 Our people are the business.
 Our partners extend it.
 If they fail, we fail, not in revenue, but in reputation.

Selling is service.
 Management is service.
 Leadership is service.
 The point of power is to use it for someone else’s good, not your own.

A true leader doesn’t collect followers.
 They build more leaders.
 They protect the conditions where people can think clearly, act fairly, and recover fast when things go wrong.
That’s the compounding effect of trust, agency shared, not hoarded.

So ask yourself:
 When you’re gone, what will they say?
 That you played small to look safe?
 Or that you stood for something bigger than yourself?

Because that’s the real measure.
 Not the quarter.
 Not the applause.
 But whether the people you led are now strong enough to lead others.
If that’s what they say behind your back, you did it right.
