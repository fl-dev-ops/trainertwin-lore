When you let an AI agent query systems, trigger workflows or take action, you’ve given it authority. Calling it software doesn’t change what you’ve done.

Dr. Lisa Palmer gives five questions to ask about what the agent may decide, when a person takes over, what performance drift should cost it its autonomy, who can stop it and who owns the outcome. Those controls are necessary. I want another question answered before giving it that authority: what evidence says we can safely rely on it to do this?

I also want that question asked again later, because the answer can change. An agent can still be doing exactly what it was supposed to do when the original reasons for trusting it no longer hold. You can know exactly who can stop it and still find that switching it off breaks something else that now depends on it.

Lisa’s question about performance drift raises a further problem. It assumes the performance evidence can be trusted. That’s the assumption I want to test.

If an agent triages customer problems and decides which ones get escalated, the escalation rate you later use to judge it is partly a product of its own decisions. A falling rate might mean the agent is resolving more problems, or simply that it is escalating fewer of them. The rate alone can’t tell you which.

That matters because an agent can look like it’s performing well while making the evidence that it isn’t harder to find. The question is no longer simply whether the controls work. It’s whether the evidence still justifies the authority you’ve given it.

For a CEO, CFO, CRO or board, this gets practical quite quickly. What are we allowing the AI to decide or do, what evidence justified that authority, and does that evidence still hold? If it doesn’t, can we reduce or remove the authority without breaking something else?

This is the problem I’ve been working on in my AI Decision Reliability white paper. If AI is already making or acting on decisions in your business, take one real case and ask: does the evidence still justify the authority we’ve given it?
