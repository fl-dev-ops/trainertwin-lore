When is it OK to lie to your customer?

Never. I'd rather be fired than lie to them.

But many companies drift into self-deception themselves gradually through optimism, internal pressure, and weak commercial discipline.

The founder reassures the customer delivery is under control when the team already knows capacity is stretched. Sales calls a deal committed before the buyer has secured funding, mapped approvals, or worked out how to defend the decision internally. Management protects the forecast long after the underlying evidence has weakened.

Not because everyone is dishonest.

Because the system starts rewarding confidence more than truth.

That is where Decision Safety matters.

A deal is not safe because the seller believes it will close. It is only safer when the buyer has done the internal work to fund it, approve it, defend it, and live with the consequences.

The moment a business starts relying on optimism to bridge the gap between promise and operational reality, trust starts compounding in reverse.

And once that happens, the forecast, the customer relationship, and eventually the economics all begin degrading together.
