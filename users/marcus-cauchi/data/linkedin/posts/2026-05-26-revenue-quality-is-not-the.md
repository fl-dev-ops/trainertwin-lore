  title: Revenue quality beats wrong revenue quantity

Revenue quality is not the same as reported revenue.

Reported revenue tells you what landed. Revenue quality tells you how safe that revenue is to protect, repeat, serve, renew, and expand.

That distinction matters because some revenue looks good before it starts creating cost.

It may close with heavy discounting. It may need more delivery effort than the model allows. It may depend on one sponsor. It may lack proof of value. It may sit in an account with no real expansion path. It may create work the business celebrates in the quarter and pays for later.

The wrong CEO-to-CFO question is, “How much did we close?”
The better question is, “What kind of revenue did we close?”

Did it fit the ICP? Did the buyer own the problem? Was the value case strong enough to defend price? Can the customer adopt? Can the account renew without rescue? Is there a credible expansion path?

This is Commercial Reality in practice.

Revenue is not healthy because it is booked. It is healthier when the evidence shows it can be protected, proven, and expanded without hidden cost.
Which revenue claim deserves the hardest evidence test?

New logo, expansion, renewal, forecast commit, or strategic account?
