When a deal changes, the obvious risk is that the economics change. The less obvious risk is that other decisions keep relying on an approval that was given to materially different conditions.

That can happen without anyone making one obviously reckless decision. Debt changes, more capital goes in, management changes, the plan gets reset, the holding period stretches. Each decision can make sense on its own while the combination becomes something nobody ever considered as a whole.

That matters to founders and investors for the same reason. Capital, hiring, incentives, forecasts, acquisitions and exit plans can all end up resting on assumptions or permissions that belonged to an earlier version of the deal.

Take the question in this post one step further.

If this exact arrangement had to be approved today, using only what is known today, what would still be approved, what would need conditions, and what would have to stop until more was known?

Then look downstream.

What in the business is currently relying on a decision nobody would make on the same evidence today?

That’s where an old approval can become a current risk.
