Your CRM says the deal is moving forward.

The buyer's world might look exactly the same as it did four weeks ago.

If you have carried a number for a few years, you have seen this play out. A stage change usually means a call happened or a deck was sent. It does not mean the customer is any closer to deciding.

The system records activity. It does not tell you whether the buyer has actually moved.

Most deals that stall were shaky from the start. The urgency was polite but not real. No one person clearly owned the decision. The budget was discussed, but never carved out. Your main contact understood the problem. The rest of the group had other priorities.

When that is the backdrop, more follow up just creates noise. You can send the proposal, tweak the pricing, schedule another check in. You can pull the close date into this quarter and tell yourself it is still alive.

None of that fixes what has not happened inside their company.

The deals that close cleanly tend to share a few traits. There is a reason to act now. One person is on the hook for the call. The money is already there. The problem is agreed beyond your champion. Doing nothing has a cost they can name.

If one of those pieces is missing, the deal drifts. The CRM will not show it right away. The forecast usually feels it first.

Before you advance the next opportunity, stop for a moment.

Has anything actually changed for the buyer, or did you just complete another step in your process?

Stages are easy to update. Real buying decisions are not.
