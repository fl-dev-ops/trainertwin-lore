This post by Roger Dooley isn’t just about yes-men.
 It’s about systemic obedience.

We reward compliance over clarity.
 We punish failure so harshly that no one raises their head.
 And then we wonder why no one speaks up.

It’s not a courage problem. It’s a design problem.
The Asch conformity experiments* showed how fast people will doubt their own eyes if enough people around them agree on the lie.

*link in comments 

 Now scale that to performance reviews, power dynamics, and unspoken org politics.

Dissent isn’t usually crushed with malice, it’s quietly starved.
The fix isn’t louder leaders. It’s better systems.
 Ones where truth can surface without career risk.
 Where saying “I disagree” is seen as trust, not threat.

Until then?
 Obedience will outperform wisdom. And yes-people will win.
