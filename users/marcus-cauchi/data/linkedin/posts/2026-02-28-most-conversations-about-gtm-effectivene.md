Most conversations about GTM effectiveness start with symptoms.

CAC is rising.
Deal volume is falling.
Average deal size is shrinking.
Velocity is extending.

When those move in the wrong direction at the same time, that is not a marketing problem. It is a capital allocation problem. Shareholder capital is being deployed into a revenue engine that is not compounding.

That diagnosis matters.

But diagnosis alone is not redesign.

Causal modelling can tell you what is ineffective. It can quantify probabilities. It can expose waste before it compounds further. For Boards and CFOs, that level of visibility is essential. Without it, capital continues to be deployed into systems that are already in arrears.

The harder question comes next.

Why was the system fragile in the first place?

In many organisations, GTM is sequenced acquisition first, expansion second, retention last. Revenue is celebrated at signature. Expansion is pushed to hit targets. Protection of value happens downstream. When volatility increases, that sequencing is exposed. But the weakness often predates the market shift. Volatility simply accelerates the feedback loop.

If you want effectiveness to compound rather than oscillate, the order has to change.

Protect must come first. The sale begins at first use, when risk transfers from vendor promise to customer reality. Time to value, adoption depth, stakeholder redundancy and margin integrity determine whether expansion will be multiplicative or extractive.

Expand comes second. Expansion should deepen embed, strengthen political resilience and improve unit economics. If it introduces custom work, roadmap distortion and preventable operational drag, it is accelerating entropy rather than compounding value.

Win comes last. Acquisition must feed a system that can protect and expand. Otherwise growth replaces churn instead of building durability.

Measurement without redesign optimises a fragile system.
Redesign without measurement is blind.

Combine rigorous causal diagnosis with durability first sequencing and you move from waste reduction to compounding architecture. You can see where capital is being misallocated and you can correct the structural order that caused it.

One elevates visibility. The other reshapes power and incentives.

Together they turn GTM from a volatility amplifier into a compounding engine.

Compounding is not a hope. It is a design choice.
