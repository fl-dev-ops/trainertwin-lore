More partner enablement will not fix weak partner economics.

A partner may understand the product perfectly and still decide not to act. They may know how to sell it, know who to sell it to and still conclude that the work is not worth the risk.

That's not always an enablement problem. It may be a sound business decision.

The deal may be too small. The sales cycle may be too long. The delivery burden may be too high. Support may be unreliable. The direct team may interfere. The customer risk may be greater than the likely return.

Vendors often respond to inactivity by adding more training, more certification and more campaigns. That can increase activity without changing the reason the partner is holding back.

Partners don't build around a vendor because the training is good. They build because the relationship improves their business.

That improvement might come through better customer access, profitable services, stronger retention, entry into a valuable market or a result they could not deliver alone.

A partnerable company does not ask the partner to subsidise its growth. It creates enough value for the partner to keep choosing the relationship when nobody is chasing them.

Before funding another enablement programme, ask what becomes easier, safer or more profitable for the partner because you exist.
