If the consequence of an AI error determines how much human checking you need, who decides what “enough” looks like?

Someone reviews the output. Fine. But what are they actually checking, and how much evidence do they need before they’re comfortable relying on it? What happens when the evidence is mixed, and who has the authority to say, “No, this isn’t good enough yet”?

“Human in the loop” doesn’t answer that. It just tells you a person is involved somewhere.

A draft email and a hiring decision shouldn’t need the same level of checking. Neither should a meeting summary and an investment recommendation. The bigger the consequence of being wrong, the stronger the evidence and checking should be.

So the question isn’t just whether the AI performed well. It’s who decides whether the result is good enough to rely on, and what they’re allowed to do when it isn’t.
