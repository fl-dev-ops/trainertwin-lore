Danny Wareham has written about the hidden emotional burden of running a business.

One idea in his article deserves more attention: confidence becomes part of what the owner is expected to provide.

Customers need reassurance. Employees want stability. Suppliers want to believe the business will endure. Family members need to know the risk is still under control.

That pressure doesn't only affect the owner’s wellbeing. It can affect the quality of the decisions they make.

When the owner must keep projecting confidence, doubt becomes harder to express. The people around them may start softening bad news, improving the forecast or delaying a concern because they do not want to add to the pressure.

The owner is still receiving information. It may now arrive incomplete, softened or too late.

This is where emotional isolation becomes commercial risk.

The problem becomes harder when the business is tied closely to the owner’s identity. Closing an offer, changing direction, admitting the forecast was wrong or stopping an investment can feel like a personal judgement rather than a commercial decision.

So the decision is delayed. The cost grows. More people are affected.

Leaders need emotional support. They also need someone who can help them separate what is known from what is hoped, test whether the evidence can be relied upon and identify the smallest safe action.

Danny’s article explains why the confident face can become necessary.
The commercial question is whether anyone around the owner has made it safe to tell them what they may not want to hear.
