Nobody owes you their attention.

I think that's the point behind the statement in Will Aitken and Keenan’s Gap Prospecting, and there's an important lesson in it for anyone doing sales outreach.

Your prospect doesn't owe you their time. You have to earn it.

Yet look at how much sales outreach starts with the seller.

"We're a leading provider of..."
"We've been in business for 25 years..."
"We've just launched..."
"I'd love to tell you about..."

Why should the buyer care?

They don't know you.  They don't know whether you're credible, whether your product is any good or whether you understand their business.  And they certainly haven't agreed to give you their time so you can tell them all about yourself.

That doesn't mean you should ignore who you are, what your company does or the value of your product.

It means understanding that those things only matter when they're relevant to the person you're approaching.

Start with them.  Their world, their priorities, their challenges and what might be worth their attention.

Give them a reason to engage.  Don't assume you've earned the right simply by turning up in their inbox or on the other end of the line.

Your job isn't to get someone interested in you.  It's to give them a reason to be interested in having a conversation with you.

There's a difference.
