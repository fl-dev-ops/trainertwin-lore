This is not how a serious public safety decision gets made Jules Anderson.

Palantir says Sadiq Khan is virtue signalling. That is a cheap shot. It turns a hard decision into a loyalty test using false equivalence and character assassination.

Are you serious about crime? Or are you hiding behind values because you dislike a supplier?

That framing is useful for winning attention. It is useless for governing.

The real question is not whether Palantir is good or bad. The real question is whether London can explain the decision, prove the case, control the risks, and reverse the choice if it goes wrong.

Joseph Bower's work at Harvard Business School on resource allocation makes this uncomfortable. Strategy is not what leaders say. It is what gets funded, built, and made normal.

So what is being made normal here?

 What public power is being handed to a private supplier?
 Who approved that?
 What other options were tested?
 What proof shows this works better than those options?
 What stops it becoming surveillance by habit?
 What happens when it gets something wrong?
 Who can challenge it?
 Who can switch it off?
 Who benefits if it works?
 Who pays if it fails?

That is the debate we should be having, not letting it descend into mudslinging and culture wars which is what Mosley did in his piece. Not crime versus values. Not Khan versus Palantir. Not a culture-war shouting match dressed up as a public safety question.

If the tool helps catch criminals, expose dangerous officers and protect the public, prove it.

If the risks are under control, show the controls.
If the procurement was clean, publish the decision trail.
If Londoners are expected to trust it, make it inspectable.
Public confidence is not built by calling critics cowards. Public safety is not protected by pretending every objection is politics.
London needs a decision that can survive daylight.

Not noise.
