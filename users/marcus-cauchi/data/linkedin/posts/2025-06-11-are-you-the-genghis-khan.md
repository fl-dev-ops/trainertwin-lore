Are you the Genghis Khan of your business?

Everyone who touches the mission…
dies with it.

The project fails, so the team is blamed.
The team gets replaced, so the managers are axed.
The managers failed,so the strategy gets scrapped.
The strategy failed, so the advisors are cut.
The advisors failed, so you fire the truth.
Then stand alone.
Proud.
Empty.
Right.

You think you’re cleaning house.
But what you’re really doing is clearing the witnesses.

Every person who tried to help, improve, challenge, or warn you
gets punished by proximity.

Your team isn’t failing.
They’re bleeding out from your belief that every mistake must be avenged.

Leadership isn’t conquest.
It’s continuity.
And if your instinct is to burn everything down
every time something doesn’t work
you’re not a visionary.
You’re a serial killer of momentum.

The sad truth?

You might believe you're building a company.
But you’re leading a funeral procession.

Last man standing
won’t be the strongest.
You’ll just be the most feared.

You.

How do your surviving people react?

They throw a wide net.
They scurry to look busy.
They chase noise over nuance, because you reward noise.
You respond positively to performance theatre
and punish thoughtful, patient construction.

You praise “wins” that were paid for
by discounting value, not creating it.
You celebrate volume
while your margins quietly bleed.
And you keep asking for more
because you’re addicted to optics.

Every quarter becomes a stage.
Every forecast becomes a lie we all agree to pretend is true.
And what happens underneath?

* CAC starts creeping upward,
because buyers don’t trust rushed strangers.
* AOV is shrinking,
because no one’s dares make the time to build real value.
* Churn jumps, because every deal was closed with pressure, not partnership
* Referrals dry up, because no one wants to be your organic ATM
* And your team?

They close the wrong deals with the wrong people
at the wrong price
to avoid the next round of blame (and PIPs).

That’s not sales.
That’s survival.

You haven’t built a revenue engine.
You’ve built a fear loop.

And the longer it runs,
the more success starts to feel like loss wearing a smile.
That means if you’re stuck in defensive loops, so are they.
If you’re telling a story that no longer fits the business, they’re still building around it.
And if you’re playing “saviour,” they’re quietly stepping back and letting you. No one loves the stench of a burning martyr.

The cost of being the unexamined founder is drift, misalignment, and silent disengagement.
It doesn’t show up on your dashboard.
It shows up in team energy.
In lost momentum.
In opportunities that feel “off” but you can’t explain why.

So here’s the question that changes everything:

Where am I the risk,masquerading as the solution?

Sit with that.
Ask your team.
Don’t defend.
Don’t justify.
Just listen.

The moment you stop trying to protect the business from the truth, 
is the moment you start actually leading it.

Then apologise!
