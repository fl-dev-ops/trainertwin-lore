Women Don’t Get Funding Because They Don’t Have Good Ideas?

Not because:

Venture Capital is Male-Dominated
Women-Centric Products are Dismissed as “Niche”
Women are asked “prevention” questions (risk-focused) while men get “promotion” questions (growth-focused), affecting how pitches are evaluated
Women are held to higher standards of proof for scalability and financial performance.
Women are excluded from Informal Networks
"Invisible Workload" of Women Entrepreneurs
Investors often see female founders as less ambitious or less capable of scaling.
Lack of Female Investors
Fewer Role Models
Even when funded, women often receive less money and less favourable terms.
Unconscious Bias leads Investors to unknowingly prioritise male founders due to ingrained stereotypes.

What Can Female Founders Do to Level the Playing Field?

Seek out female-focused VC firms 
Proactively build relationships with male investors who support diversity.
Use data to highlight market size and growth potential of "niche" products
Reframe women-centric products as universal or community-wide solutions.
Learn to reframe “prevention” questions into “promotion” answers.
*Learn the language of investors
Over-prepare with detailed financials, proof of market fit, and growth metrics.
Anticipate questions about scalability and have confident answers ready.
Leverage women-led networking events and professional organisations.
Build a strong online presence (e.g., LinkedIn, social media) to create visibility. Connect with females founders. Penelope Hope of Rebel Energy UK is utterly inspiring. 
Connect with Catherine Gray and watch Show Her The Money
Highlight your leadership experience and past achievements to build credibility.
Use testimonials or case studies to show customer loyalty and product success.
Pitch to angel investors and female-led syndicates who support women entrepreneurs.
Advocate for greater representation in VC firms through partnerships and mentoring.
Join communities of female founders (Emmie Faust  Female Founders Rise Kristen Weatherby Breakthrough Labs  to share experiences and mentorship.
Publicly celebrate your wins to inspire others and build credibility.
Use compelling storytelling to break stereotypes and connect with investors emotionally(Kyle Gray).
Address biases diplomatically by asking thought-provoking questions during your pitch (Marcus Cauchi)
Connect with David B Horne read his insightful book ‘Funded Female Founders’.
