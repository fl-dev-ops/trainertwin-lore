Before you let AI clean up your sales data, check what it’s cleaning

There’s a huge amount of effort going into getting AI to listen to calls, summarise meetings, update CRM fields, score opportunities and recommend what sellers should do next.
Good. A lot of sales administration deserves to disappear.

Check something before handing over too much of it. Can the system tell the difference between what happened and what somebody thinks happened?

A buyer says, “I’ll talk to the CFO.” That’s an intention.
Later they say, “I spoke to her.” That’s a reported action.
The CFO joins a meeting. That’s something we can observe.
The CFO approves the expenditure. That’s another event entirely.
If those all become some variation of “economic buyer engaged”, the automation has made the CRM tidier while making the underlying evidence less useful.

The same applies elsewhere.
“We’re hoping to get this done in October” isn’t a close date.
“Send me the paperwork” isn’t approval.
“That sounds reasonable” isn’t commercial agreement.
“I’ll take this to the board” isn’t a board decision.

AI can probably get very good at preserving those distinctions. That’s not an argument against using it.
It’s an argument for deciding what distinctions matter before automating the capture.

Otherwise we risk taking all the ambiguity, assumptions and shorthand already buried in sales data and making them beautifully consistent.
And beautifully consistent rubbish is still rubbish.
