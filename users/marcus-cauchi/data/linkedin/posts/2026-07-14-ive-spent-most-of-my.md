I’ve spent most of my working life asking people difficult questions.
Some people think that means I’m confident. Sometimes I am. Sometimes I’m not.

Before an important conversation, I still wonder whether I’ve missed something. 

I wonder whether I’m about to challenge the wrong thing, whether I’ve misunderstood the person in front of me, or whether I’m more attached to my own view than I realise.

That doubt can be uncomfortable. It slows me down. It makes me go back over things. It can leave me awake long after the conversation has ended, replaying what I said and wondering whether I got it wrong.

I used to think I needed to get rid of that.
I don’t anymore.

The doubt matters because people trust me with decisions, careers, teams, customers and parts of themselves they don’t show everyone. Certainty would be easier, but it would also make me careless.

I’ve met plenty of people who sound sure of themselves. I’ve also seen how much damage can be done when being certain matters more than being right.
So I’m trying to get better at owning both things at once. The courage to say what I see, and the humility to accept that I may be wrong.

I don’t always manage it. Sometimes I push too hard. Sometimes I speak too soon. Sometimes I realise later that I should have listened for longer.
I’m still learning.

Maybe that’s the part I’ve become most comfortable admitting.
