When agreement starts coming too easily ...

Things are still fine on paper.

Revenue’s steady. Forecasts haven’t moved. The calendar’s full. Decisions get made and they read clean afterward. From the outside, nothing looks fragile.

In my gut, it’s different.

The pressure has stepped up, and decisions land faster than they probably should. I meet too little resistance. Too little pushback. Agreement comes easily.

I can explain the logic of what we chose. I’m less sure I’d want to stand behind it later, when conditions change and the margin for error is thinner.

There’s no single issue to point to.
Just a sense that it’s become easier to agree than to slow things down.

I don’t notice it in the moment.
I notice it afterward. In the car. Reading the notes back. Thinking about what didn’t get said.

This isn’t a crisis. Nothing’s broken.
It’s earlier than that.

It’s the phase where things still work, but only just. Where reasonable decisions stack up and only get questioned later.

That’s usually when it becomes clear the culture did exactly what it learned to do.

I’m left with two uncomfortable questions:
Where do I mistake alignment for silence?
What has become easier to agree with than to question?
