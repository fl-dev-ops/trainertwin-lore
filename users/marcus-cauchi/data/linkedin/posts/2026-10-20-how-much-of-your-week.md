How much of your week is spent sorting out problems you’ve already helped your people solve? You rescue the deal, calm the customer, make the decision and get everyone back on track. Then, a few days later, you’re doing it all over again.

There’s a name for some of what’s happening. Upward delegation. Problems that your people should be learning to handle keep finding their way back to your desk. And the more often you take over, the more you risk teaching them that there’s no point trying. That’s how learned helplessness can develop, even among capable people.

Of course, sometimes you need to step in. But if you’re forever rescuing people, you’re knackered, they’re becoming more dependent, and the same bloody problems keep coming back.

That’s why I wrote Make Heroics Unnecessary. Being needed is part of management. Being needed for everything shouldn’t be.

Are you helping your people become more capable, or teaching them to need you?
