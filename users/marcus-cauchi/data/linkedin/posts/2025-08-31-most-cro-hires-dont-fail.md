Most CRO hires don’t fail.
They get butchered.

Founders hire for the LinkedIn dopamine hit, shiny title, 200 likes, “look at us, we’re grown up now.” Boards smile politely, then scribble in their notes: “immature leadership, vanity hire.”

Nine months later?
The CRO is gone.
Revenue hasn’t scaled.
Founder’s back in Salesforce at 2 a.m.
Investors are quietly Googling “replacement CEO.”

Why? Because almost every founder makes the same eight deadly mistakes:

They confuse CRO with CSO because they want to drop sales and make it someone else's problem.
They starve their new exec, underinvest in the operation and then demand miracles.
They grade a system-builder like a quota-carrying rep.
And then, when it inevitably collapses, they shrug and say: “Guess we hired the wrong person.”

This post is the scar-tissue field manual:
❌ How Not to Hire a CRO (the founder vanity + investor eye-roll edition)
vs.
✅ How to Actually Hire One (the boring grown-up playbook that actually works).

It’s got puppet shows, steering wheels that aren’t attached to cars, CRO graveyards, and board scribbles like “Amateurs.”

If you’ve ever:

Thought about hiring a CRO.
Wondered why your last one lasted 11 months.
Or sat in a board meeting writing “WTF?” in your notebook…

This one’s for you.

👉 Read it. Laugh. Cry. Forward it to the founder who thinks a CRO is just a shiny Head of Sales.
