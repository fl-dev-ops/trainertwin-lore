Most operators don't stall from lack of demand. They stall because the machine leaks margin as it runs.
Procurement slows throughput.
Compliance slows handoffs.
HR slows initiative.
Legacy ERP slows everything.
The book of business turns into a tax on the business.

Nick Ayton called out the velocity and cash conversion side.
Avner Baruch (B.Eng') pointed at misdiagnosis and system blindness.
Both are true on the ground.

The part I keep running into in portfolio work is simple: pruning and velocity only work once reality is seen end to end. Until earnings quality sits next to revenue, cuts are cosmetic and politics wins.

The fix is not a PMO. Not a transformation office but a small diagnostic unit with decision rights, cost to serve, and permission to surface the ugly bits without retaliation.

Once that picture is true, pruning is rational, velocity improves, and margin leaks close without heroism.

Advisors recognise this pattern instantly: system inefficiency first, cash conversion second, valuation third.

PE cares about: quality of earnings, cash conversion, causal vs visible cost.
Ops care about: interventions that compound, no politics, clarity before action.
CEOs care about: not looking stupid in front of the board, not wasting a year, not getting replaced.
