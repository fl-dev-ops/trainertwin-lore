How many businesses are scaling a false positive?

Every week another founder celebrates product-market fit because the pipeline is growing, revenue is climbing and new customers keep arriving. Those are encouraging signals, but they don’t necessarily prove that the business has found something it can repeat, defend or scale profitably.

Some companies really have product-market fit. Others are being held together by founder relationships, discounts, exceptional customer success, custom work or contracts that make leaving harder than staying. From the outside, they can look almost identical. From the inside, one business gets stronger as it grows while the other becomes more fragile.

That made me question something I’ve taken for granted for years. What if we’ve been using the wrong evidence to decide whether product-market fit is real?

That’s the idea behind my latest article. It isn’t another framework or a new definition for the sake of it. It’s an argument that product-market fit has a false-positive problem, and that the evidence most businesses rely on is often the least able to prove it.

I’d genuinely like to know whether you think the argument stands up. If you disagree, tell me where the evidence breaks. That’s how better thinking gets built.
