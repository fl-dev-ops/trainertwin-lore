Seven years a civilian.

Turns out, rank doesn't pay the bills.

Stepping outside? It's a hairy-assed leap. Especially when your brain's been hard-wired a certain way. Here's my truth:

↳ Reroute that wiring. It took me a year, minimum.

↳ Take the good stuff. Values? Standards? It's your bedrock. But put your people skills on steroids. Seriously. You'll stand out if you're the person everyone wants to be around. 

↳ Leave the drill square voice behind. It won't cut it. Unless you go into a similar culture to the military. 

↳ Prove your worth. Every. Single. Time. 

↳ Rank? Nobody gives a flying *@*!. Unless they're stuck in the "Rank this, Rank that” Brigade - it's BS. 

↳ Break the glass ceiling in your head. Don't limit yourself. 

A good friend left as a WO2; now a senior leader in a global finance giant. True story. 

↳ Network. I cut the cord. Mistake. Big mistake. People are generous. Tap into that goldmine.

↳ Don't let the transition process box you in. Want to start a business? Do it. Don't blindly follow the "CV, skills, job" route. Use your gut.

↳ Technology? AI? The world's moving fast. Don't get left behind. Learn to use it. 

↳ Find your tribe. Open doors. Go full tilt. No half measures.

↳ You'll bounce a few roles. It's stressful. Unknown. Uncertainty. Family. Finances.

↳ Look after yourself. There's no early morning PT. Self-discipline is king. Protect your mental and physical health, every single day. 

↳ Embrace the civilian world. Some wonderful people, very few complete and utter sh!ts (that I've experienced). Open routes you never imagined. 95% are on your side. Build a network. Help others.

If you're ex-military and been out a while, what's your take? 

If you're leaving soon? Ask away in the comments - start your networking today.

Turns out, civilian life isn't a demob. It's a whole new battlefield.

Please do reach out and connect.
