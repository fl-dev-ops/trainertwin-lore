Working with organizations, both locally and abroad, I've observed a common and costly mistake: managers, often with the best intentions, get pulled into the vortex of business operations and urgent tasks, sacrificing crucial coaching time.

This isn't about a lack of desire to coach; it's about the relentless demands of the day-to-day. Yet, this operational trap has profound consequences: a significant drain on revenue, wasted training ROI, and a silent erosion of top talent retention. 

Our latest insights reveal a 19% performance swing in core reps directly tied to the quality and consistency of coaching they receive.We've explored why this happens and, more importantly, how to reclaim and prioritize effective coaching to build a truly predictable revenue engine. If you've ever felt your coaching time being squeezed by other commitments, or wondered why your team isn't reaching its full potential, this conversation is for you.

I'm curious to hear your experiences: Have you worked for a manager who was an exceptional coach? Or, as a manager, do you find your coaching time is often impacted by other commitments?
