Most sales teams don't have a lead problem. They have a concentration problem.

A B2B team I sat with this week had spent four weeks on a prospecting campaign and booked two meetings. Not for want of a list, they had a good one. Not for want of people, they had a capable team.

On Monday we put everyone in one room for three hours. No new leads, no new tool, the same data they already owned. One rule: make the call, then log it, in real time.
150 calls. Nine meetings booked. Forty-seven live opportunities carried into the next round. The rest worked and qualified out.

The buyers were in the list the whole time. The activity was always within reach. What was missing was concentration and discipline: a protected block of time, a simple way to score the work, and the habit of recording it so it counts.

Most teams answer a thin pipeline by buying more leads or another tool. Usually the pipeline is already there, just under-worked and unrecorded, which makes it invisible to the people trying to manage it.

That is not a lead problem. It is a rhythm problem. And rhythm can be built in an afternoon, then kept.

A question for the sales leaders here: when a team is busy but the pipeline stays thin, where's the real blocker, the leads, the time, or the discipline to record the work? I have a firm view, but I'm interested in where you land. 

We win it, we don't wing it.
