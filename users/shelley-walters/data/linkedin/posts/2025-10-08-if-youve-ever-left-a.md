If you’ve ever left a conference thinking, “That could’ve been an email,” this one’s for you.

Today I had the privilege of speaking at Echelon’s Annual Conference at the beautiful Houghton Hotel, and it was one of the best-run events I’ve attended this year.

Everything about it worked.

The speaker lineup was tight and well curated.

The internal executives were articulate, well prepared, and confident. Their slides were clean, their stories compelling, and their messages clear.

There was space for genuine connection and conversation, not just content.

And as a professional speaker, I received exactly the kind of detailed and thoughtful briefs I need in order to meet my client's needs. 

It reminded me that a great conference doesn’t happen by accident. It’s the result of a team that:

• Values preparation, not performance
• Curates content, not just calendars
• Treats every speaker, internal or external, as part of a bigger story

Having spent years on both sides of the stage, both as a strategist and as a speaker -  I’ve seen how much care it takes to create something meaningful. It’s not only about avoiding “death by PowerPoint.” It’s about giving people space to think, feel, and connect.
