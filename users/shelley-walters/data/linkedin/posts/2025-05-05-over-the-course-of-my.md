Over the course of my life I have learned that there are seasons in life when your identity becomes the work.

As an entrepreneur, most of my energy has gone into striving, to building a business, ideation, product market for, leaning about cash flow, balance sheets and hiring people. 

But lately, it has also been a season that invited me to pause and look inward. It has been a time of insight, regrets,  excitement, reflection, new direction and quiet recalibration.

The older I get, the more I realise that knowing who you are is not just a personal mission. It is mission critical; it is how we make sense of our role in the world. It is how we bring our best to the table: in leadership, relationships, and everyday life.
Working with Maureen Grobler has been a scary and creative adventure. It has also been a deeply personal one. I have taken time to challenge a few old beliefs about myself, and it has helped me reconnect with something stronger, clearer, and more honest. And to accept some parts of me that I had been rejecting for years. 

I also need to give a big shoutout to Hendrien van Zyl, who has been part of my team for nearly seven years. Her insights and calm presence have helped me see myself more clearly, sometimes in ways I did not even know I needed to. You have to love those teammates who help you grow simply by being who they are.

People like Joe Hudson and Jim Winship have influenced my thinking, especially around emotional fluency, identity, and the stories we tell ourselves. And being part of the Civitas CEO Advisory has been such a gift. 
 It is a space where leaders can be honest, reflective, and challenged to keep growing.

I am still figuring things out. But what I know is this: the more grounded I become in who I am, the more room I create to show up fully.
Can you relate? Has getting older helped you understand yourself more clearly? Has that shifted how you show up in your work or life?
I would love to hear your thoughts.
