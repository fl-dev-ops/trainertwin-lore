The Age of IP Protection Is Over. Now What?

This week, en route to the BizNews Conference in Hermanus, we stopped by the Kimberley Club, a place steeped in history—tales of courage and sacrifice, but also documenting horrific oppression, exploitation, and power struggles.

As we paged through an old album in the foyer, reading letters and viewing historic photographs, I captured an image of a typed letter—a long one, requiring two photos. The distance and lighting made the text almost illegible.

But in four minutes (an eternity in the age of AI), the entire letter was transcribed flawlessly.

It hit me: The age of protecting intellectual property is over.

With AI capable of instant transcription, replication, and content generation, the value of mere information has shifted. We can no longer rely on what we know—we must distinguish ourselves by how we think.

For those of us who are thought leaders, speakers, and knowledge creators, the game has changed. In the coming decades, success will belong to those who are not just knowledgeable, but deeply committed, immersed, and prolific in their thinking and contributions.

The question is no longer how do we protect our ideas? but rather:

How do we remain indispensable in a world where knowledge is infinitely replicable?

Would love to hear your thoughts. What do you think the future holds for thought leadership in the age of AI?
