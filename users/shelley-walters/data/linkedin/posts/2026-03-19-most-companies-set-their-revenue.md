Most companies set their revenue targets before they have made a single strategic decision.

Many set those targets before they even have a clear picture of what their own numbers are telling them.

The data exists somewhere. In the CRM, in the finance system, in a spreadsheet someone built two years ago. But nobody has ever stood in front of the leadership team and said: here is what your client base actually looks like, here is where your profit is really coming from, and here is what the numbers are asking you to do about it.

So the plan gets built on instinct. And instinct, dressed up in a spreadsheet, still looks like strategy.

Then the first QBR arrives, and everyone is surprised.

After 27 years in B2B sales, I have sat in enough of those rooms to know that the gap is almost never effort or talent. It is that the decisions which need to happen before the year begins — which clients to grow, which to protect, which to let go, which segments deserve your best people, were never made formally. And in many cases, the data that would have made those decisions easy was never surfaced at all.

It is built for this point in the calendar, before the new financial year locks in and another twelve months begins on assumption.
