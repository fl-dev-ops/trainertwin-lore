“21 prompts to use AI for sales scripts,” this one’s for you.

Because really, who wants more prompts when you can have more petunias?

Now that the business has grown to a point where I can actually enjoy weekends again, I’ve found myself returning to the things that light me up creatively.

One of those is gardening.
Grounding, sensory, quietly instructive.

But this time, I decided to approach it differently.

Instead of scrolling through Pinterest boards or drowning in plant blogs, I used ChatGPT.
Not to make me more productive, but to help me be more creative.

I uploaded photos of our garden , the sunlight, the soil, the layout - and told it what I wanted to achieve.
I shared which plants we already had, what I wanted to reuse, and where we needed more colour and height.

ChatGPT responded like a thoughtful gardening coach.
It analysed the conditions, suggested plants that suit Joburg’s climate, and even created realistic visual mock-ups of what the redesigned garden could look like.

Then it built a shopping list, which I WhatsApped to the nursery along with a budget.
They did the shopping while my husband and I had lunch.

That experience reminded me why I love technology when it’s used creatively, not mechanically.

AI didn’t replace creativity.
It amplified it.

It freed me to think, design, and imagine — the very things that make us human.

And maybe that’s the quiet shift we’re all living through.
Not using technology to chase efficiency, but to create space for curiosity, play, and possibility.

💭 Where in your work could you use AI creatively, not mechanically, this week?
