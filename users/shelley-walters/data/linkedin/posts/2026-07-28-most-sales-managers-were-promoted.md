Most sales managers were promoted for being excellent salespeople. Then we ask them to stop selling and start leading, and wonder why the team stays dependent on them.

Here is the pattern we see on floor after floor.

The manager stands at the back of the line. They step in to close the hard deal. They dissect the loss after it’s lost. They walk the floor asking “how’s it going?” It looks like leadership. It’s pushing, and it quietly teaches the team to wait for a rescue.

The managers who actually lift a team stand at the front. They set the expectation before the deal is at risk. They review in a rhythm instead of rescuing in a panic. They coach the gap instead of covering it. And they know their team copies what they do, not what they say.

A salesperson closes deals. A sales manager builds the people and the disciplines that close deals when they’re not in the room.

If your managers are still at the back of the line, it is never too late for the about-face.
