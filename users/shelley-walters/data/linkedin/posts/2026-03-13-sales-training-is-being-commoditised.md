Sales training is being commoditised. Here is what that means for you.

AI tools, YouTube, free frameworks, downloadable playbooks, the  content of sales training is rapidly becoming freely available to anyone with an internet connection.

I don't see this as a threat, instead I view this as a filter.

Because what it means is that the providers who were selling you repackaged generic content, the ones who arrived with a brochure and a shopping list of training topics, are about to be exposed. You can get that content for free. You should not be paying for it.

What you should be paying for, and what you should be demanding before you sign anything , is a provider who won't to sell you training until they understand your business.

Be wary of anyone who approaches you with a pre-built menu of topics and asks you to choose. That is not a training solution. That is a product catalogue. And it tells you everything you need to know about whether they are actually trying to solve your problem, or simply trying to close a deal.

The providers worth working with ask for the opportunity to understand your sales ecosystem first. They will want to look at your CRM adoption, your leadership capability, your compensation structures, your pipeline health. They will want to assess your sellers against a competency model that reveals their developmental needs before they ever mention a training intervention. Because they know that training is rarely the root problem. It is often a symptom of something deeper.

The commoditisation of content is the best thing that has happened to serious buyers of sales development. It has made the distinction between a vendor and a true partner impossible to hide.

Ask better questions before you spend the budget.
