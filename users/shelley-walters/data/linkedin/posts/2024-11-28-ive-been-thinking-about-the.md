I’ve been thinking about the role questions play in sales management. Not the surface-level ones like, “What’s your target this quarter?” but the ones that make your team stop, reflect, and rethink.

Here’s the thought I keep returning to: The depth of a sales team’s performance often reflects the depth of their manager’s questions.

Think about it. If we only ask shallow questions, we’ll only get surface-level answers—and surface-level results. But when we ask deeper questions, we uncover motivations, challenges, and opportunities that drive performance.

Instead of asking, “Why isn’t this deal closing?” what if we asked, “What’s one thing you haven’t tried yet that could shift the conversation with this client?”

It’s subtle but powerful. The first question identifies a problem; the second invites the salesperson to think creatively and own the solution.

This week, I challenged myself to ask better questions in my meetings. I saw the difference immediately: more engagement, better ideas, and salespeople taking ownership of their pipelines.

What about you? What’s one question you’ve asked recently that led to a breakthrough moment? Or one you wish you’d asked?
