I've been thinking about a strange consequence of AI.

As a small business owner, I'm very grateful for what it has democratised. I have access to capabilities today that, not very long ago, would have required a much bigger team, deeper pockets and specialist expertise I simply couldn't afford. I'm absolutely a beneficiary of that.

But, of course, so is everybody else.
And that's what I've been thinking about.
If my competitors and I increasingly have access to the same machines, the same information and many of the same capabilities, then surely the things we can't outsource become more important?

Your ideas. Your experience. Your reputation. Your relationships. The body of work you've built. The way you think about a problem and articulate what you see. Whether people know what you stand for and trust you when it matters.

Your presence.

I'm not suggesting for a moment that we resist the technology. I want every advantage it can give a small business like mine. But I do wonder whether one of the consequences of democratising capability is that capability itself becomes less of a differentiator.

And if that's true, perhaps our responsibility is not simply to become better at using the machines. It's to become more intentional about developing the parts of our value that the machines cannot commoditise.
We can outsource more than we've ever been able to outsource before. But we cannot outsource our presence.

I'm curious about this one. If access to capability is becoming increasingly democratised, where do you think competitive advantage moves next?
