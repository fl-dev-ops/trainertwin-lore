Paul’s post captures a truth many executives and boards underestimate.
Thirty percent of sales teams rely on one salesperson for more than half their revenue. That isn’t strategy, it’s exposure.

In sectors like financial planning, real estate, automotive retail, and insurance sales, underperformance often goes unchecked. Because these roles are commission-only or low-base positions, they appear to carry “no cost.” Yet the real cost is hidden: lost opportunity, missed revenue, customer attrition, and the quiet normalisation of mediocrity.

This is the paradox: top performers lift the numbers, but underperformers quietly erode value.
A business that depends on a handful of stars is one resignation away from a crisis.

Training and coaching are not line items. They are the most under-utilised levers for risk reduction and sustainable growth. They raise the floor, not just the ceiling.

If leadership teams viewed capability building as risk management rather than learning and development, investment decisions would look very different.
