Two years ago today...

Am I the only one who feels challenged by being appropriately authentic on LinkedIn?

We come with our polished professional stories, our best pictures, glowing client testimonials, and a continual stream of promotions and product launches. And sometimes I feel like LinkedIn can make us forget that we’re human.

That doesn’t mean we should let all our washing hang out, but surely meaningful dialogue must have space for our humanity? 

I’ve grappled with whether or not to share this post. But this was not only a personal journey — it was a professional one too.

 And as I mark this two-year anniversary, I share it in the hope that it might encourage someone who needs perspective today.

Two years ago this week, I began a chemo-radiation journey for cervical cancer.

For weeks, my world shrank to early mornings, hospital corridors, and treatment rooms. My body was weak, my energy was gone, and my emotions were raw. I was 43 years old, suddenly in full-blown medical menopause, trying to keep our business alive while simply trying to stay alive.

I haven’t spoken much about it. Not because I was hiding it, but because I never wanted to use trauma as currency. It was a trial to endure — not a story to build my brand around.

But today, this memory came up on my phone, and I was reminded just how far grace has carried me.

Yesterday, I stood on stage speaking at the Mercedes-Benz Commercial Vehicles sales conference — one of four keynotes this week alone. Our team has grown, our business has taken offices, and we’ve had the privilege of serving extraordinary clients across South Africa, the Middle East, and the UK.

At one of my lowest points during treatment, I met someone who was then a client. Today, she’s our Chief Operating Officer, a strategic advisor, and a dear friend. Proof that even in the darkest seasons, seeds of future blessing are being planted.

God has been, and continues to be incredibly gracious. My husband’s love, the loyalty of our clients, and the faithfulness of our team held me through when I couldn’t hold much myself.

If you’re walking through your own trial right now, please know this:

- You don’t have to compare your pain to anyone else’s.

- You don’t have to perform resilience.

- And you won’t stay where you are forever.

Sometimes grace looks like just getting through today.

And one day, you might look back, as I am now - and realise that the valley became the foundation for a life even richer, fuller, and more meaningful than you could have imagined.

P.S. I am grateful to say that I am cancer free!
