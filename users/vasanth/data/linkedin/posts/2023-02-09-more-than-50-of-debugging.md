"More than 50% of debugging don't require break points"
This is one of the common mistakes that I have seen made by #junior #fresher and sometimes even the #experienced #engineers.

Let's take a simple example

"On click of a button the text field should become empty. But it is not working as expected."

Most keep a debug point in the function and evaluate line by line what is happening. You really don't have to do that.

1. Debugging involves its own time and avoids it as much as possible.

2. Instead of debugging, just iterate through the entire code that you have written and see where the problem is.

3. After you analyse it once or twice also if you're not able to make out only then put breakpoints and start debugging.

If you constantly follow this rule you will evenly become a better developer where you start thinking like a compiler instead of a developer.
