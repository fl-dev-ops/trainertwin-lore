If you're conducting frontend developer interviews, it's time to stop asking these types of questions:
1. "Guess the output" questions where there's no real-world value, but you're expecting the candidate to know obscure edge cases.
 Example: let x = 10, a, b; — now what’s the type of a? Is it let or const?

2. Pre and post-increment puzzles like ++a + --b. These are rarely used in real-world code, and instead of assessing actual skills, they often just confuse candidates without offering meaningful insights.

3. Expecting candidates to fully implement a machine coding round—including both styling and complete functionality—within 30 minutes. Let’s be honest, very few people can do that under pressure, and even fewer are expected to in real-life projects.

4. Deeply nested promise chains and then asking candidates to guess the output.  Who writes or maintains code like that in production today?

Interviews should be realistic. It’s okay to include a few edge cases or tricky scenarios, but they shouldn't be the deciding factor in evaluating a candidate's capabilities.

What do you think? Do you still include pre/post-increment questions in your interviews?
