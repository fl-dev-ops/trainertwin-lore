I used to think playing small was the smart move.

That if I supported the senior people around me, I’d be seen as a team player.

I was the newest person on the desk.

Surrounded by brilliant, fast-talking traders who’d been doing this for years.

So I figured the best way to earn respect was to help them look good:

→ Skip taking credit for my own ideas
→ Prioritize their asks over my to-dos
→ Put their success ahead of my own

It felt right at the time.
They were more senior. I was still learning.

But here’s what I wish I knew back then:

1️⃣ Putting others ahead of yourself doesn’t make you loyal.
2️⃣ Shrinking to make others look better doesn’t make you a team player.
3️⃣ Trusting your impact will speak for itself is a risky and frustrating bet.

“Being a good team player” might earn you praise.

But it won’t get you paid or promoted.

That experience taught me a lesson I now pass on to every client:

Don’t confuse building trust with downplaying your value.

You can do both. 

Build strong relationships AND own your impact.

Your voice matters, no matter your title.

Stop playing small.
