Most people don't realize this mistake until it's already slowed their growth:

They don't pay attention to how they're perceived at work.

And in corporate, that's a significant problem.

Because perception drives decisions.

Decisions about you and your future at the company.

Your boss.
Your peers.
Senior leadership.

They already have a story about you… whether you've shaped it or not.

Do they see you as leadership material?
Do they trust your judgment under pressure?
Do they believe you're someone they can bet on?

When I was chasing my next title on the trading floor, I thought if I worked hard and delivered even better results, that would be enough.

Then I found out there was a committee deciding on my promotion. And most of them didn't even know me.

Yep, that was my aha moment.

Sure, your performance matters. But you can't underestimate how much your visibility matters too.

If you're not actively shaping how you're perceived, trust me, someone else is filling in the gaps for you.

And they might be getting the story completely wrong.

Perception isn't a soft skill.

It's strategy.
It's reputation.
It's the difference between being considered and being overlooked.

And it's far too important to leave to chance.
