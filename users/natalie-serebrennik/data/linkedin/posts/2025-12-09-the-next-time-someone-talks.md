The next time someone talks down to you at work… try this 👇

Before I ever had a solid strategy for handling these moments, I did what a lot of high performers do.

I made it about me.

Early in my banking career, if someone threw a jab or used a rude tone, I’d replay it for days and wonder what I did wrong.

I’d overthink it.
I’d internalize it.
And it drained me.

As I grew more confident (or as I like to say… stepped into my boss babe energy), my response changed.

I stopped taking every comment personally and started putting the responsibility back on them with something simple like:

“That’s interesting you’d say that.”

And it worked.

Their tone changed. Their behavior softened.
And those moments became fewer and farther between.

Years later, I came across a three-step approach from Jefferson Fisher, and I was an instant fan.

The way he broke it down was clean, effective and so easy to use.

I’ve shared it with clients ever since.

Here it is:

1️⃣ Seek clarification

“Could you repeat that?”

This simple question can be a wake-up call, making them hear their tone and message.

2️⃣ Address their intentions

“What were you hoping to achieve with that comment? Did you mean to embarrass me or hurt my feelings?”

By shifting the focus to their intent, you encourage them to reflect on their behavior.

3️⃣ Maintain composure and disengage

Regardless of their response, reply with “Understood” and walk away.

By refusing to engage in conflict, you maintain control of the situation.


Remember… their actions and words reflect far more on them than they ever will on you.

Would you try this strategy?
