Feeling awkward in meetings isn't a personality flaw.

It's a skill gap. And it's one of the most fixable ones there is.

High achievers end up here all the time:

Holding back until the "right" moment.

Overthinking every word before speaking.

Letting others take the lead in conversations they're more than qualified to weigh in on.

Whether you're introverted or extroverted, speaking up strategically in a room that wasn't built for you is something nobody ever actually teaches you.

So you wing it. And winging it rarely feels good.

What I coach clients to do differently:

1️⃣ Prepare your point in advance

One clear, outcome-focused message. Not a brain dump. When you're clear, your delivery follows.

2️⃣ Speak earlier, not later

Waiting for the perfect moment often means you miss it. Add value early, even if it's just re-framing the problem.

3️⃣ Use language that signals leadership

Like "I recommend" instead of "I think maybe." Your words shape your confidence even before you fully feel it.

That room might not feel like it was made for you.

But the moment you decide to show up anyway is the same moment the room starts to adjust to you.
