I’ve always tried to take my health seriously

Even when life made it hard to.

Growing up, I watched my parents build a life from scratch after immigrating here.

They didn’t always have the time, resources, or privilege to focus on their health the way they deserved.

So when I reached a point in adulthood where I could prioritize mine, I did.

Movement became a non-negotiable.

Even on the busiest days, I focused on what I could do - like taking the stairs or going on an extra walk.

Eating well became another form of taking care of myself.

And for me, eating well has always meant moderation.

Yes, I’m absolutely someone who will have the bowl of chips, enjoy it, and feel no guilt.

That balance is exactly why I was able to maintain consistency.

Healthy enough without obsessing over perfection.

And for many of the high performers I meet, striking this balance feels impossible at times.

Because even when your health is a priority, life gets busy.

Work gets demanding.
You get pulled in every direction.

And the habits that make you feel your best are often the first to slip.

Not because you don’t care, but because you’re human and you're trying your damn best.

Which is why communities like The Full Plate resonate with me so much.

A counselor-led space focused on creating a healthier relationship with food and self-care.

Not through pressure.
Not through dieting.
Not through shame.

Just support.
Just compassion.
Just structure that helps you feel like you again.

And honestly, this kind of support is underrated.

We’re trained to excel. To push past our limits. To prioritize everyone and everything else first.

But sustaining a meaningful career requires more than ambition.

It requires feeling good in your body and mind, too.

If you’re craving that kind of balance, The Full Plate might be a great fit for you.
