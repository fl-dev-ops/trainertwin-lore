You've been reliable for so long that nobody thinks to ask what you need.

That's not a compliment. That's a pattern worth looking at.

The most loyal professionals in any organization tend to share a specific profile. 

They don't push. 
They don't make noise. 

They absorb more than their share and deliver consistently enough that nobody worries about them. 

And because nobody worries about them, nobody develops them.

I watched this on the trading floor. 

The people who received the more attractive client base, the sponsorship, and leadership attention were rarely invisible about their ambition.

Not always loud.
But never silent either.

Meanwhile, the person quietly holding everything together stayed exactly where they were:

Holding everything together.

Reliability is a strength. 

But when it becomes the thing that defines your entire relationship with an organization, it stops being an asset and starts being a ceiling.

The shift is uncomfortable but it's simple: 

Your organization needs to know that you have ambitions beyond being the person they can always count on to stay put.

That's not disloyalty. That's self-advocacy.

And if you’re not investing in this strategy, breaking through that mid-level ceiling will continue to feel like it does today.
