How much of your identity is wrapped up in how much you make?

For nearly 20 years, that single data point took up the majority of my identity.  

Working on the trading floor, success was loud and obvious.

Titles. Bonuses. Numbers on a screen.

It didn’t just measure performance, it shaped who I thought I was.

My drive.
My confidence.
My sense of worth.

So when I stepped away to build my own business, I didn’t just leave a job.

I left an identity.

And I wasn’t fully prepared for how uncomfortable that would be.

Because when you’re no longer in the constant grind 

Chasing the next deal, the next title, the next bonus…

You’re left alone with your thoughts.

No scoreboard.
No instant validation.
No external signal telling you “you’re winning.”

Just you.

And sitting with that is hard as hell.

But it’s also been one of the most rewarding experiences of my life.

Because once I stopped defining myself by income alone, I was forced to ask different questions:

What actually matters to me?
How do I want to reshape my identity?
What kind of life do I want to live, not just earn?

And speaking from personal experience, this painful exercise didn’t kill my ambition.

It refined it.

I still want success, that part of me will never change.

I just don’t let money be the only thing that defines it anymore.

And that’s been the most challenging yet freeing part of this whole journey.

Sometimes the real work isn’t chasing the next level.

It’s deciding who you want to be when you get there.

So, what’s one change you want to make next year?
