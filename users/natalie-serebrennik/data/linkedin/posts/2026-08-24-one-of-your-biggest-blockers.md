One of your biggest blockers to leadership growth is:

You're still measuring your value by how much you get done.

I see this all the time with high performers stepping into leadership.

They build their reputation by delivering.
They're known for being reliable and fast.
They take pride in being the one who figures it out.

So when it's time to actually lead, they hesitate.

Because leading means:

→ Letting others take the spotlight
→ Getting comfortable not having all the answers
→ Being valued for your thinking, not your output

And that's where growth stalls.

Not necessarily because they don't know how to delegate.

Because they don't know who they are if they're not the one doing it all.

If this is you, you're in good company.

Too many leaders lack the support to make that transition (from doer to enabler) without feeling like they're losing the thing that made them successful in the first place.

When I start working with new people-leaders, the first thing we do is redefine what success looks like in their new role.

We move the focus:

From execution to influence.
From output to impact.

From "I'll just do it" to "How do I build a team that can?"

Because leadership isn’t you vs. them.

It’s you WITH them, building something bigger than what you could have built alone.

If you’re stepping into a bigger role, try this:

Start valuing your leadership by the impact you create in others, not the tasks you complete solo.

That’s the moment your next-level identity starts to take shape.
