She'd been told to be more strategic 4x in 2 years.

4 separate conversations. 

4x she nodded, went back to her desk, and tried to figure out what that actually meant.

She's not alone. 

"Be more strategic" is one of the most common pieces of feedback I hear from clients. 

And it is almost never accompanied by anything concrete enough to act on.

Because it's not feedback. 

It's a placeholder. 

Managers reach for it when they haven't done the work of being specific.

And high performers pay the price, quietly, sometimes for years.

So that's where we started.

We built the framework that let her show her thinking in language her leadership actually responded to.

We identified where she was being tactical in rooms that needed vision. 

Where she was being visionary without anchoring it to anything measurable.

Sometimes we need to rethink the strategy completely. Other times, we just need a better way of translating the existing one.

Weeks later, a real development conversation, for the first time, with actual gaps she needs to work on. 

Stronger advocacy from her manager. 

Not just support when asked, but visibility she didn't have to create herself.

Finally, a path she could see and act on.

If you're wondering what else you can possibly be doing, start here:

Take a close look at your current strategy. What's working? What's not? 

Answer those questions from your management's perspective, not your own.

Clarity in how well your efforts land is where everything starts to get easier.

And that clarity starts with demanding better from the feedback you're given.

Vague feedback is a tax on high performers. Most pay it for years without realizing they don't have to.

Stop paying it.
