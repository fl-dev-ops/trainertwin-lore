Can we stop pretending that everyone in senior leadership knows exactly what they're doing?

Because I can assure you, they don't.

I've spent enough time working with executives to know that even the most experienced, accomplished people find themselves in situations where they have absolutely no idea what the right answer is.

And you know what, that's totally normal.

What I find interesting is how many people assume that everyone above them has somehow figured it all out.

That their boss knows something they don't.

That the people sitting around the executive table have some level of certainty they haven't earned yet.

They don't.

They're making judgment calls. They're taking risks. They're having conversations they haven't fully prepared for. And sometimes they're making decisions that turn out to be completely wrong.

The difference is that the good ones don't let that stop them.

They ask questions. They lean on people who know more than they do. They make a call.
And when it doesn't work, they figure out what to do next.

I see this come up with my coaching clients all the time.

Incredibly capable people questioning whether they're ready for a bigger role because they don't feel like they know enough yet.

Meanwhile, the people already sitting in those roles are figuring things out as they go, too.

At some point, you have to stop expecting yourself to know everything before you give yourself permission to do something bigger.

You're not going to have all the answers.

Neither does your boss.

And neither does the person sitting three levels above you.

The sooner you realize that, the less intimidating those bigger opportunities become.
