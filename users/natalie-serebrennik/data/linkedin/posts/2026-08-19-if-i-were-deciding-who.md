If I were deciding who got the next people-leader title, I'd start by asking one question:

How do they treat their team and the people around them?

Then I'd validate it by going straight to the source.

Not just their manager.

Their peers. Their team. The people who actually feel their leadership every day.

Yes, results matter. Businesses don't grow without them.

But most teams don't break because of a lack of talent.

They break because of how that talent is being led.

Here are the top 10 behaviors I’d be looking for:

You make space for any question
You admit when you're wrong, and then fix it
You encourage their rest, not just their results
You care about their growth, not just your own
You follow through, even when it's inconvenient
You treat your team like humans, not headcount
You take responsibility when things go sideways
You check in when something feels off, and listen
You advocate for your team when they're not there
You push them to grow, and break through their barriers

If companies want stronger leaders, they need to add the human element into how they make leadership decisions.

What else would you add to this list?
