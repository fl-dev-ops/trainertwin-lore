She got the promotion.

And then she got quiet.

I noticed it within weeks of us working together.

The opinions she had shared freely before suddenly came with qualifiers.

She started double-checking instincts that had always been sharp.

It had nothing to do with capability.

And everything to do with the stakes feeling different.

Almost like a target on your back.

All eyes on you, waiting to see how you handle it.

And somewhere in that exposure, the instinct to protect herself kicked in harder than the instinct to lead.

This is more common than people admit.

The professionals who fight hardest for a seat at the table sometimes become the most cautious people in the room the moment they get there.

What's actually happening is that the identity hasn't caught up with the title.

The internal story is still running on the old version.

The one that had to prove herself to get here, not the one who already belongs.

But the new seat doesn't pause while you find your footing. It starts asking things of you immediately.

She had faced new before. This was no different.

She didn't need a strategy overhaul.

She needed someone to hold up an honest mirror and reflect back the leader she already was, underneath the version of herself that fear had been narrating.

That's what changed.

Have you ever let a new opportunity make you smaller instead of bigger?
