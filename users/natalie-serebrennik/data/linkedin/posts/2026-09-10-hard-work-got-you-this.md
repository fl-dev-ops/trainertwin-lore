Hard work got you this far but it won't get you to the next level.

That's not motivation talk. 
It's how the system actually works.

Early in your career, effort is rewarded.
At the senior level, it's assumed.

By the time you're experienced:

Reliability is invisible.
Execution is expected.
Doing a "great job" is table stakes.

So the feedback starts to sound familiar:

"You need more executive presence"
"We want to see more strategic thinking"
"Just keep doing what you're doing"

Which usually translates to:

Work harder.
Wait even longer.
Don't rock the boat.

That advice is keeping you stuck.

And speaking from personal experience, it's painful as hell.

The growth lever changes mid-career.

Hard work stops being the signal.
Judgment becomes way more important.

Not how much you do, but:

What you say no to.

What initiatives you raise your hand for.

How early you spot problems before they hit your desk.

Whether leaders trust your thinking without needing to see your output first.

This is why so many high performers stall.

They keep optimizing effort in a system that now rewards something completely different:

The ability to be trusted before you've delivered, not after.

That means walking into a room and having leadership already confident in your thinking. 

Being consulted on decisions before the work starts, not debriefed after it's done. 

Having your name come up in conversations you're not in because people trust your judgment, not just your output.

That's the game at the senior level and most companies never explain it. They just watch to see who figures it out.

If you're performing well but feel oddly paused, it's probably not a motivation issue.

It's a rules change. And nobody handed you the new rulebook.
