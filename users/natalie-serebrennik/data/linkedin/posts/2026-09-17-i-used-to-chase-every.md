I used to chase every opportunity that looked impressive.

Bigger title.
Better comp.
More visibility.

I never stopped to ask one uncomfortable question:

Who am I trying to prove this to?

Early career beliefs are sneaky like that.

They sound logical, get rewarded, and before you know it they've hardened into rules you never agreed to follow.

Bigger title = I belong.
Better comp = I'm valued.
More visibility = I'm respected.

Most high performers don't burn out from working hard.

They burn out from winning games they never chose to play.

For me, it took leaving the trading floor to see it clearly:

Just because you can do something doesn't mean you should.

And the part most people miss:

These patterns don't disappear when you get promoted.

They just get more expensive.

I see this all the time in driven professionals:

Confusing being visible with being valued.

Getting paid well but still feeling misaligned.

Climbing a ladder they never stopped to check was actually theirs.

None of these feel like mistakes in the moment.

That's why they're blind spots.

Your potential doesn't have to live inside old definitions of success.

Even if you were the one who created them.

These days, I choose alignment over optics.

Work I genuinely love, with people I choose, creating impact that actually matters to me.

When's the last time you sat with that question: who am I trying to prove this to?

Maybe it's time to replace the "them" with you.
