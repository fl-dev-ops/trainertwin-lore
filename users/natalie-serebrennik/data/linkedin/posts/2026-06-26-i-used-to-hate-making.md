I used to hate making decisions when the outcome wasn't guaranteed.

The fear of getting it wrong.

Of being judged, questioned, or blamed.

It was all enough to make me hesitate.

So I'd wait.

More certainty. More data. More proof that I was on the right path.

…my eyes still twitch thinking about how uncomfortable I was.

And then a project I'd been holding off on moved forward without me.

Someone else made the call because I was too in my head.

And watching it play out as well as it did was a very memorable gut punch.

Not just because I wanted the credit.

Because it was at that moment I realized what my hesitation actually cost me, the opportunity itself.

And the way my silence had been read as a lack of leadership.

I learned a very important lesson that day.

Great leadership is making the best judgement call you can in the moment, even if you don’t have all the perfect details. 

I like to remind myself and my clients of this often… messing up is part of the experience. 

A wrong call can always be fixed. But a missed one can't.

What's a decision you've been sitting on longer than you should?
