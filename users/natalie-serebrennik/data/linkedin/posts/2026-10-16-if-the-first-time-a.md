If the first time a senior leader gets to know you is when you're asking for a promotion, you've waited too long.

I spent 17 years on a trading floor and one of the most valuable things I learned was how much relationships influence career progression.

Not just your relationship with your manager.

The relationships you build with people across the organization who understand your work, recognize your contribution, and have influence over the opportunities you want.

And yet, I see so many professionals spend years focused almost entirely on delivering results, assuming the right people will eventually notice.

Then a promotion opportunity comes up, and they realize the people involved in making that decision barely know them.

If that stung a little, good. Try this strategy going forward:

Start building those relationships before you need anything.

Ask to connect with leaders whose areas of the business interest you.

Get involved in initiatives that give you exposure outside your immediate team.

When you have an opportunity to interact with someone a few levels above, don't spend the entire conversation talking about what you're working on.

Get curious about their priorities.

What challenges are they dealing with?

What are they trying to accomplish?

Where does your work intersect with what matters to them?

And when there's an opportunity to contribute, follow through.

Over time, you're giving people a reason to know who you are, what you bring to the table, and how you think.

If you commit to this strategy, you end up building relationships with management who understand your capabilities and feel comfortable advocating for you.

Because when your name comes up during that round table discussion about the next opportunity, you don't want the first question to be:

“Who's that?”

You want someone in the room to speak up and share just how awesome you really are.
