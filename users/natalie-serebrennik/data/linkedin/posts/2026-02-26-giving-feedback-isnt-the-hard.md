Giving feedback isn’t the hard part.

Getting someone to act on it is.

That’s the real leadership work.

One of my clients kept raising the same performance issue with her direct report.

More than once.
But nothing changed.

When we unpacked it, the feedback itself wasn’t wrong.

It just wasn’t landing.

She had softened it so much it didn’t feel actionable.

It felt like a check-in.

Because she was prioritizing being nice over being clear.

And that’s such a common trap for empathetic leaders.

You want to protect the relationship.
You don’t want to discourage them.
You don’t want to be “that” manager.

So you dilute the message.

You hedge.
You over-explain.
You wrap it in compliments.

And the result?

They leave feeling fine.
You leave feeling frustrated.
And nothing changes.

So we reworked her delivery using 4 prompts:

→ Why does this matter right now?
→ What specific examples make this obvious?
→ Is the expectation crystal clear and measurable?
→ What happens if it doesn’t improve?

No vagueness.
No dancing around it.
No hoping they “get the hint.”

Within a week, her direct report stepped up.

All because she added clarity to the discussion.

And here’s the part most leaders underestimate:

Clarity doesn’t damage trust.
Ambiguity does.

You can be kind.
You can be supportive.

But if you’re not clear, you’re creating confusion - not safety.

And at higher levels, confusion is expensive.
