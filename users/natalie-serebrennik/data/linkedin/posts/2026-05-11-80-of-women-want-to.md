80% of women want to be promoted. 86% of men do.

That 6-point gap has been used to explain a lot. 

The ambition narrative. 

The idea that women are somehow less driven, less hungry, less willing to put their hand up.

Here's what the same research shows: 

When women receive equal career support, the gap disappears entirely.

This is not an ambition problem. 

It never was.

What it is: 

A support problem. 

A sponsorship problem. 

A "who gets access to the conversations, the stretch assignments, and the advocacy that actually moves careers" problem.

The organizations still framing this as a pipeline issue are solving for the wrong variable. 

The women are there. They’re performing. They’re ready. 

What's uneven is what's being built around them, or better yet, not built.

The companies living their values (not just preaching them at conferences and writing them in fancy brochures) are doing something different.

They're asking the hard questions.

Where is support actually being distributed?

Who gets access to the informal infrastructure that accelerates careers?

Do your managers know the difference between mentoring someone and sponsoring them?

And is their performance being measured against how well they support others?

Because they’re not the same thing.

And most managers have never been taught the distinction or what support actually looks like.

The gap isn't in women's ambition. 

It's in what organizations are willing to examine about themselves, and whether they have the stomach to act on what they find.

What companies come to mind that are actually making this kind of commitment? Share in the comments. Let's shine a spotlight on them!
