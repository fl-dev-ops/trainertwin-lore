You don’t feel behind in your career.

You feel like an imposter.

Like at any moment, someone might realize:

You don’t always feel as confident as you sound.
You don’t have it as figured out as they think.
You’re still working things out in real time.

So you overcompensate.

You choose your words carefully.
You prepare more than necessary.
You avoid situations where you might get caught off guard.

You make sure you sound polished.

And from the outside, it looks like it’s working.

But internally, it’s exhausting.

Because you’re not just doing your job.

You’re managing the perception of your capability at the same time.

And here’s the part most people get wrong:

You think sounding perfect is what makes you look capable.

It doesn’t.

In fact, it works against you.

Because no one connects with perfection.

And at more senior levels, people aren’t evaluating how many of your answers are right.

They’re paying attention to how you think.

That’s what builds trust at the next level.

So when you hide the process…

When you filter everything before you speak…

When you wait until your thoughts are “fully formed”…

You’re not protecting your credibility.

You’re actually limiting it.

So what do you do instead?

Let people hear you think.

Say what you know.
Be clear on what you don’t.
Talk through how you’d approach figuring it out.

Because the goal was never to prove you know it all.

It’s to show that you can navigate what you don’t.
