I used to hate making decisions when the outcome wasn’t guaranteed.

The fear of getting it wrong.
Of being judged, questioned, or blamed.

Was enough to make me hesitate.

So I’d wait.

Wait for more certainty.
More data. More proof that I was on the right path. 

(My body still shivers thinking about how uncomfortable I was.)

Until one day, a project I’d been holding off on… moved forward without me.

Someone else made the call.

And watching it play out as well as it did hit hard.

Not because I wanted credit.

But because I realized my hesitation looked like lack of leadership.

That’s when it clicked: leadership isn’t about making the right decision.

It’s about being willing to make a decision.

Period.

Because the leaders who struggle most aren’t the ones who get it wrong sometimes.

They’re the ones who avoid deciding at all.

A wrong call can be fixed.
A missed one can’t.

↳ You can course-correct
↳ You can learn from it
↳ You can rebuild trust

But you can’t build credibility through hesitation.

Certainty doesn’t come before action.

It comes from it.

Even now, outside of corporate, I think about this often while building my business:

The key to future success is trusting yourself enough to take action when it counts.

Because nothing changes without a decision.

So...what’s a decision you’ve been holding off on? 

Maybe today’s the day you can create the change you desire.
