The only thing you teach by replying right away…

Is that you always will.

I struggled with this one.

My entire career encouraged quick responses and fast action.

If the phone rang, you picked it up on the first ring. If a client emailed, you responded before they hit refresh.

It was how I learned to prove my value. Through speed.

So when I started setting boundaries, it felt wrong.

Like I was somehow dropping the ball. Like I'd be seen as less committed.

Honestly, it stressed me out.

Looking back, I wish I'd appreciated this sooner:

The more you over-deliver on availability, the more people expect it.

And what does that actually build? A reputation rooted in reactivity, not strategy.

Here's how I coach my clients to adjust this habit:

→ Batch your replies into 2-3 windows a day instead of reacting in real time

→ Use an autoresponder or status update so people know when to expect you, not whether you're ignoring them

→ Notice which "urgent" requests are actually urgent vs. just loud

Because if you're always available for everyone else, you're never fully focused on your own priorities.

And if you want to lead strategically, you need to be intentional with your energy.

To my fellow high achievers, how did you break this habit?
