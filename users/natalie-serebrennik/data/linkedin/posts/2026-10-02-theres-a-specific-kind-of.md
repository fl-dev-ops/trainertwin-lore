There's a specific kind of overlooked that flies under the radar.

Not the underperformer who gets passed over.

The one who's trusted enough to mentor everyone else but never chosen for the bigger opportunity. 

That was my client.

She'd been developing junior staff for years. Building people up, investing in their growth, showing up for everyone around her.

And still overlooked when the sexy project was delegated to someone else on the team, again. 

On paper, everything looked solid:

Consistent praise.
Strong relationships.
A reputation for being reliable.

10 minutes into our first conversation, it was clear her capability was never the issue.

It took one question to uncover what was really holding her back.

"How would your manager describe your leadership right now?"

Which she replied with… "I'm not even sure he knows I'm doing any of it."

Bingo. The perception of her capability didn’t match her reality. 

Everything she was contributing was invisible to the one person who had the most influence over her next opportunity.

So we built a strategy around making the invisible visible.

And for the first time in years, he finally appreciated just how much she was doing. 

"I had no idea you were doing all of this behind the scenes. Let's get you more involved in the next opportunity."

That's what changes when you stop waiting to be noticed and start making sure the right people actually see what you're capable of.

What's one contribution you've been making that your manager probably doesn't know about?
