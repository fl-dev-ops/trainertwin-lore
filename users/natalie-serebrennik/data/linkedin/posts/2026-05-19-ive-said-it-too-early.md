I’ve said it too.

Early in my career, I gave someone feedback that meant absolutely nothing.

“You’re doing great, keep doing what you’re doing.”

I thought I was being supportive.

What I was actually doing was leaving them exactly where they were and calling it development.

That’s the thing about vague feedback.

It’s usually not malicious.

Most managers aren’t trying to hold people back.

They’re busy.

And they haven’t thought deeply enough about someone’s growth.

Or they simply don’t know how to translate instinct into useful guidance.

So instead, they reassure.

And the person on the other side walks away trying to decode what “doing well” is supposed to mean when nothing in their career is actually changing.

I think about that conversation a lot now.

Because almost every high performer I coach has lived some version of it.

Strong performance.
Positive reviews.
Genuine praise.

And absolutely no clarity around what comes next.

That ambiguity becomes exhausting over time.

Not because people can’t handle hard feedback.

Because they can’t build strategy around vagueness.

So if you’re receiving feedback like that right now, remember this:

The absence of a clear path isn’t confirmation that one doesn't exist. 

It usually just means nobody has been asked to build it with you yet.

That's an ask worth making.
