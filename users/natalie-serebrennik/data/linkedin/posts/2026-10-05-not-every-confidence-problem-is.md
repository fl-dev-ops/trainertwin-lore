Not every confidence problem is imposter syndrome.

Sometimes you need to look at what's happening around you before deciding something needs to be fixed within you.

I've coached incredibly capable professionals who started questioning themselves after months of:

Getting very little feedback.

Being repeatedly overlooked for opportunities.

Working for a manager who rarely acknowledged their contribution.

Speaking up only to have their ideas dismissed or ignored.

After enough time, it's pretty easy to start wondering:

Maybe I'm the problem.

And sometimes there’s something you need to work on.

Maybe you need to speak with more confidence.

Advocate for yourself more directly.

Get clearer on where you need to develop.

But before you diagnose yourself with a confidence problem, get curious about where that feeling is actually coming from.

Am I doubting my ability, or have I been getting very little evidence that my work is valued?

Am I afraid to speak up, or have I learned that speaking up in this environment comes with consequences?

Am I actually underperforming, or have expectations never been made clear enough for me to know?

Those answers lead to very different solutions.

Because you can't solve the right problem until you've figured out what the problem actually is.
