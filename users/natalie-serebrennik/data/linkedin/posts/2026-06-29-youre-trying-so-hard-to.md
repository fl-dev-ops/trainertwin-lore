You're trying so hard to look like a leader.

That you've forgotten how to be one.

Any of this ring a bell:

Every word measured.
Every decision rehearsed.
Every meeting feels like a performance.

If you're anything like me, you've done an excellent job building your career on composure.

But at some point, composure turns into control.

And that's the exact thing holding you back.

The pressure to "get it right" is robbing you of the presence that makes people trust you.

The spark. The ease. The energy that makes others want to follow your lead.

When you take yourself too seriously, you stop taking risks. You stop speaking freely. You stop showing up with the kind of confidence that can't be scripted.

Looking back, the best leaders I worked with had one thing in common. They were real.

They laughed at themselves. They didn't take themselves so seriously. They created safety because they didn't pretend to have it all figured out.

Imagine that, your growth depends on finally loosening your grip. 

And giving yourself full permission to sit in the messy middle, and for once - thrive in it.
