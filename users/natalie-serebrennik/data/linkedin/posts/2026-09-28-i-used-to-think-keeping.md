I used to think keeping the peace was the smart move.

Don't rock the boat.
Don't be difficult.
Be the person who's easy to work with.

And to be fair, that approach can get rewarded for a long time.

You say yes.
You take on the extra work.
You keep your disagreement to yourself.
You become the person everyone can count on.

But at some point, being agreeable stops helping if nobody knows what you actually think.

Because the more senior you become, the more people need to see your judgment.

Do you have a point of view?

Will you challenge something you don't agree with?

Can you push back without making the conversation unnecessarily difficult?

Can you set a boundary and still be someone people want in the room?

That's very different from being difficult for the sake of being difficult.

And it's also very different from agreeing with everything because you don't want to rock the boat.

I had to learn that distinction myself.

Being a team player was never supposed to mean disappearing inside the team.

And if you're working harder than ever, doing everything that's asked of you, and still hearing vague feedback like “keep doing what you're doing,” it may be worth looking beyond the quality of your work.

What evidence are you giving people of how you think, what you believe, and how you're willing to lead?

Because at a certain level, people need to see more than what you can execute.

They need to see what you bring to the conversation.
