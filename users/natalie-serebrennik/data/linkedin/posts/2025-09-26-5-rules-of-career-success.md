5 rules of career success I learned the hard way

(from over a decade of leadership):

1️⃣ Being liked is a career advantage.

People go to bat for those they like and trust.

It’s not about being fake.

It’s about building strong relationships so when decisions get made, your name is in the conversation.


2️⃣ Asking for more is how you get more.

Raises. Promotions. Next-level opportunities.

Do they go to the most qualified person?
Or the one who had the confidence to ask?

If you’re waiting for permission, you’re already behind.


3️⃣ The best leaders adjust how they communicate to their audience.

A great leader doesn’t just deliver a message, they make sure it lands.

↳ Peers - speak to their priorities.
↳ Senior execs - get to the point, fast.
↳ Your team - give them clarity and direction.


4️⃣ Your reputation is being built with or without your input.

How you show up everyday is either working for you or against you.

Because when management is deciding if you’re ready for that next level, you don’t get to make your case.

Your reputation already has. 


5️⃣ No one remembers effort - they remember impact.

You can put in 60+ hour weeks and still be overlooked.

Because effort isn’t the currency of career growth, results are.

If you’re not consistently tying your work back to impact, you’re not positioning yourself for bigger opportunities.


The leaders I work with understand this.

They know success doesn’t come from just working hard.

I help them understand the game and we customize it to ensure they play it on their terms.

What’s one career lesson you had to learn the hard way?
