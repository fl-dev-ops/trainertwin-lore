You might be avoiding feedback without even realizing that's what you're doing.

Asking for input started to feel risky somewhere along the way.

It would signal doubt.

Or worse, confirm a fear you didn't want to face.

So you do what most high performers do instead.

Focus on results and assume they'll speak for themselves.

They don't always.

Not when peers are being pulled into stretch projects.

Not when leadership feels closer to people who are simply more visible, not more capable.

I had a client in exactly this situation.

The real issue wasn't confidence or performance.

It was this: feedback felt like a threat instead of a tool.

So he avoided it.

What he didn't see was the cost of staying in the dark.

No clear expectations.

No shared definition of what "doing well" actually meant.

No signal to leadership that growth genuinely mattered to him.

Avoiding feedback doesn't protect you.

It just keeps you uninformed.

Feedback exists whether it's spoken or not. 

The sooner you understand what people actually think, the sooner you can do something about it.

The hard part wasn't the conversation.

It was committing to have it.

Once he did, he finally had something he'd been missing for two years. 

A clear picture of exactly what leadership needed to see from him next.

If this hit a little too close to home, that's usually the signal.
