I used to think being a strong performer meant never needing help.

On the trading floor, asking questions felt risky.

And because of this, I avoided them.

Part of it came from how I was raised. By immigrant parents who taught me to be prepared for everything.

Part of it came from watching people get dismissed for asking the “wrong” question.

So I adapted.

I figured out how to always have an answer, or at least sound like I did.

How? I got really good at listening.

When a data print came out, I’d tune in:

What were the traders saying?
What were clients posting in the chat?

Then I’d piece together an opinion based on that.

Not because I always knew what I thought.
But because I knew how to read the room.

At first, it was survival.
Later, it became strategy.

Now when clients tell me they don’t speak up because they’re not 100% sure - I get it.

But here’s what I like to remind them:

Being a leader isn’t about having the perfect answer.

It’s about having a perspective you’re willing to stand behind.

Even if it evolves.

Agree?
