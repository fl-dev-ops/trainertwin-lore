I used to think being a strong performer meant never needing help.

On the trading floor, asking questions felt risky.

And because of that, I avoided them.

Part of it came from how I was raised - by immigrant parents who taught me to be prepared for everything.

Part of it came from watching people get dismissed for asking the “wrong” question.

So I adapted.

I figured out how to always have an answer.
Or at least sound like I did.

I got really good at listening.

When a data print came out, I tuned in.

What were the traders saying?
What were clients posting in the chat?

Then I’d piece together an opinion from that.

Not because I always knew exactly what I thought.

But because I knew how to read the room.

At first, it was survival.
Later, it became strategy.

And now, years later, I hear the same pattern from clients all the time.

They hold back questions.
They hesitate to speak up in meetings.

All because they’re afraid of being wrong.
Of losing credibility they’ve worked so hard to build.

Many of them tell me, “I prefer to speak up when I’m confident in what I’m speaking to.”

And I understand that instinct deeply.

But here’s what experience has taught me - both from the trading floor and in leadership:

Leaders aren’t remembered for having perfect answers.

They’re remembered for having a point of view.

For being willing to think out loud.

For engaging the conversation instead of observing it from the sidelines.

For trusting themselves enough to contribute, even when their thinking is still forming.

That’s where confidence actually gets built.

Not before you speak.
But because you do.

Agree?
