Self-doubt is the most common career killer I see.

And Jeff was moments away from letting it cost him everything.

He had been leading a high-performing team for 6 years. 

Strong track record. Respected by management. 

The kind of professional whose name should have come up immediately when a high-visibility project landed on the table.

And instead of advocating for his readiness, he defaulted to talking himself out of it before he even made the ask.

"There are probably better candidates"
"I'm not sure I have enough experience for this one"
"If they thought I was the right fit, they'd come to me"

Sound familiar?

Our coaching focused on something most people skip entirely - building the actual case for why he was the right person.

Not a confidence exercise. A real inventory of the experience, the results, and the specific perspective he would bring that nobody else on that list could.

By the end of it, the question wasn't whether he should ask.

It was why he had almost talked himself out of it.

And just a few months later he made the ask and management said yes.

But that's not where the story ends.

Because he showed up, and delivered as he always does, his manager began pulling him into even larger initiatives. 

Opportunities that wouldn't have existed if he hadn't raised his hand for the first one.

That's how visibility actually compounds.

One ask doesn't just open one door. 

It changes how people see you, and what they think to offer you next.

Stop convincing yourself you're not ready yet.

What are you currently talking yourself out of that you're more than qualified for?
