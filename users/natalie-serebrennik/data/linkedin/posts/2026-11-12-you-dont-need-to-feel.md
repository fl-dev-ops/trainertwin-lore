You don't need to feel confident to make other people confident in you.

Here's how...

1️⃣ Stop undermining your own ideas before you've even shared them.

Instead of saying, "This might be a stupid idea, but I was thinking maybe we could..." Try, "Here's what I'd recommend, and here's why."

2️⃣ Get comfortable not having all the answers.

When someone asks you something you don't know, resist the urge to over-explain or make excuses. A simple, "I don't have that information yet, but I'll look into it and come back with a recommendation" is more than enough.

3️⃣ Stop treating every challenge to your thinking as a reason to second-guess yourself.

If someone disagrees with your recommendation, don't immediately backtrack. Ask questions. Understand their concerns. And if you still believe your approach makes sense, explain why.

4️⃣ Be more direct when communicating your recommendations.

Don't spend five minutes walking someone through every detail of how you arrived at your conclusion. Start with what you recommend, explain your reasoning, and provide additional context only when it's needed.

And here's why all of this matters.

You can have years of experience, an incredible track record, and all the technical expertise in the world.

But if you're constantly apologizing, second-guessing yourself, or struggling to communicate your recommendations, people will question whether you're actually ready for the next challenge.

And that's the frustrating part. You might be far more capable than you're coming across.

Also, getting promoted doesn't magically make you feel more confident.

If anything, the stakes get higher.

So waiting until you feel confident before you start communicating differently isn't going to help.

But practice will. And the more you do, the more your confidence will catch up.
