He convinced himself asking for feedback would make him look insecure.

That it would signal doubt.

That it might confirm the worst - that he wasn’t doing as well as he thought.

So he chose to focus on results and hoped that would be enough.

But it didn’t feel like enough.

Not when peers were getting tapped for new projects.

Not when leadership seemed more connected to others who were just as competent.

That’s when he hired me.

We worked on the belief that was holding him back:
That feedback was a threat, not a tool.

And we reframed his entire approach - from mindset to strategy.

He realized how avoiding feedback was costing him opportunities.

How he had no real proof for the fear he’d built up.

And how staying in the dark was a bigger risk than asking.

I gave him a simple but effective feedback framework.

We practiced it once and that was all he needed to feel ready.

Did he get promoted from one conversation? Of course not. Let’s be real.

But he did walk away with two things every high-performer needs:

Clarity - he knows exactly what to work on.
Alignment - his boss is on board with his growth.

No more guessing what leadership thinks.

No more waiting quietly, hoping results speak loud enough.

No more shrinking to avoid discomfort.

He now has direction.

And the confidence that comes from knowing where he stands and where he’s going.

This is how growth really works.

In quiet decisions to do the uncomfortable thing that moves you forward.
