You know that moment when someone else gets the promotion or recognition you’ve been working so hard for?

And your first thought is, are you kidding me?

(As you immediately start justifying your contributions vs. theirs.)

You know your results are stronger.
You know you clock in way more hours.
You know you’ve said yes to asks they haven’t.

But here they are, getting seen and rewarded over you. 

It’s infuriating. 

And it’s the reality most high performers eventually face:

In corporate, politics often trump performance.

And I get it, it feels wrong. You want meritocracy to matter.

But waiting for great work to speak for itself is a dangerous strategy.

That’s the part no one prepares you for.

You can be the hardest worker in the room and still be overlooked.

All because you’ve been too focused on doing the work instead of showing it. 

Here’s what I tell my clients:
“Stop assuming they know. Make sure they do.”

The quicker you remove the assumptions from your decision-making, the quicker you shape their perception of you.

Some call this politics.

I call it being smart.

What do you call it?
