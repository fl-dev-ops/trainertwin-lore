You missed a deadline.

Made a bad call.

Said the wrong thing in a big room.

It happens.

What matters next is where most careers diverge.

Because in high-performing environments, mistakes aren’t what derail progression.

Poor recovery does.

Here’s how strong leaders rebuild trust after a mistake:

1️⃣ Own it early.

No hedging. No over-explaining.
The longer you wait, the more stories fill the gap.

2️⃣ Name the impact.

“I know this put you in a tough position and created extra work.”

This shows awareness, not weakness.

3️⃣ Lead the repair.

What you’re fixing.
What you’ve learned.
What will be different next time.

This is the part most people miss.

Senior leaders aren’t keeping score on who never messes up.

They’re watching:

→ Who takes responsibility under pressure
→ Who turns mistakes into lessons, not noise

That’s reputation.

And reputation is what gets discussed when you’re not in the room.

High achievers, especially, put themselves under impossible standards.

Be perfect.
Never slip up.
Always have the answer.
Bring solutions, not problems.

Honestly? I’m over it.

Over the guilt that outlasts the mistake.

Over the internal spiral that makes recovery slower than it needs to be.

Reality is, most people stop thinking about your mess up far sooner than you do.

They’ve moved on to their next meeting.

Or their own mistake. Or lunch, lol.
