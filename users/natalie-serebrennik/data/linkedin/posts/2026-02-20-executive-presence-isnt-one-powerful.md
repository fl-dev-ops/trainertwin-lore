Executive presence isn’t one powerful speech.

It’s a lot of small conversations where people feel seen.

I’ve always been good at building relationships.

The mistake I made was keeping that effort small.

I invested in my team.
The people right around me.

But when my growth plateaued, it wasn’t because my work wasn’t strong.

It was because the people deciding my trajectory didn’t really know me.

So I did something that now sounds so simple.

I widened my circle.

More quick check-ins.
More hallway conversations.
More remembering the small details.

Too many exceptional high performers underestimate small talk. 

Asking how little Johnny’s birthday went.
Following up on the vacation they mentioned.
Even asking how the commute was that morning.

Instead, they pour everything into working harder.

More skills.
More output.
More ways to impress.

Yes, earn your seat at the next level.

But don’t forget to be human while you’re climbing.

Because sometimes the next move doesn’t come from being the most impressive person in the room.

It comes from being the one people feel connected to.
