I used to think playing small was the smart move.

That if I supported the senior people around me,
I’d earn respect by staying out of the way.

I was the newest person on the desk.

Surrounded by sharp, fast-talking traders who’d been doing this for years.

So I did what felt logical at the time.

I helped them look good.

I skipped taking credit for my own ideas.
Prioritized their asks over my actual priorities.
Put their success ahead of my own.

It felt responsible.
They were more senior. I was still learning.

What I didn’t realize then is this:

Those behaviors are useful early on.
They help you learn the game.

But if you keep them too long, they cap your growth.

Because:

Loyalty doesn’t come from invisibility
and impact doesn’t speak for itself at senior levels.

“Being a good team player” might earn appreciation

But it rarely earns promotion or pay progression.

I see this pattern constantly with high performers now.

They’re still operating by rules that once helped them succeed… but now keep them overlooked.

At a certain point, leadership success depends on how well you make this shift: 

From contribution to ownership
and it's one of the hardest transitions in a career.

Also... one of the least explained.
