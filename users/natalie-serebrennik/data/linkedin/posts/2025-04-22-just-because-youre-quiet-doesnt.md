Just because you're quiet doesn’t mean you don’t belong in the conversation.

I work with a lot of introverts.

And they’re exceptional.

→ Sharp and strategic
→ Curious in the best of ways
→ Passionate about the impact they’re building

But there’s one pattern I see again and again:

They hold their thoughts in.

Not because they don’t have value to add.

But because speaking up in meetings feels extremely uncomfortable.

And when they sit on an idea too long, anxiety takes over and they convince themselves they can’t.

I like to remind them: 

You don’t need to dominate the room to influence it.
But you do need to be heard to be remembered.

A strategy that’s been working really with my clients is the commitment to speak early.

Say something in the first 10 minutes, even if it’s small.

The key is to get ahead of the anxiety. 

Here are a few examples:

“I’ve been thinking about X and wonder if we’ve considered…”

“I really liked [colleague]’s idea and it got me thinking about…”

“Just to echo what’s been said, I see a big opportunity here.”

Speaking early keeps you memorable, while also building your confidence to speak up more often. 

How do you push through the anxiety to speak up as an introvert?
