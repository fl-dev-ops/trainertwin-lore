The boldest move I ever made in my career and why I regret waiting so long.

A few years into my career, I discovered a truth that still stings:

I was significantly underpaid compared to my peers.

I brought it up with my manager, expecting action. 
Instead, I got a string of empty promises, “Next year, we’ll fix it.”

Embarrassed to admit it, but I believed him.

Year after year, I let those assurances stall my growth.

It wasn’t until I came back from my second mat leave, reporting to a new manager, that I’d had enough.

This time, I made my intentions clear:
“I’m pissed, and I’m taking this up the chain.”

What followed were the boldest career conversations I’ve ever had:

→ I outlined my contributions with clear examples.
→ I tied my ask directly to the value I delivered.
→ I came armed with market and peer data.

It wasn’t easy. At the start, self-advocacy rarely is.

But it worked. I secured a six-figure bump by the end of the year.

My only regret is not taking bold action sooner.

Self-advocacy isn’t selfish, it’s your strongest corporate strategy.

Stop waiting for someone else to notice your worth. 

Be the one who makes it known.


What’s the boldest step you’ve taken to advocate for yourself?
