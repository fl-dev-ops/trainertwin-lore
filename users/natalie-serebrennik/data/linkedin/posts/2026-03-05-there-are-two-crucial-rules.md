There are two crucial rules in leadership.

Rule #1:
Stop trying to do it all.

Rule #2:
Learn how to delegate well.

When you step into people leadership, your responsibilities multiply overnight.

If you’re still operating like an individual contributor, you’ll bottleneck your team.

And your own growth.

Yes, your career stalls when you refuse to delegate.

Because leadership isn’t about doing more and more.

It’s about delivering business-prioritized results WITH others.

When my clients stop debating whether they “should” delegate, we focus on how to do it well.

That’s when their leadership really takes shape.

All because they:

→ Build trust faster
→ Free up time for higher-impact work
→ Create real development opportunities for their team

What got you here won’t get you there.

As an IC, it was about how you win.
As a leader, it’s about how the team wins.

If you want to strengthen your delegation muscle, start small:

Delegate something you’d normally keep.
Set clear expectations and align upfront.
Then resist the urge to jump in.

Of course it will feel uncomfortable at first.
Anything new does.

That doesn’t mean you’re bad at it.

It means you’re building the skill that separates managers from leaders.

And once you master it, you stop being the bottleneck.

What’s one task you could hand off this week?
