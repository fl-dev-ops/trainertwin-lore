I wasted years waiting for a bonus that never came.

Thinking I "deserved" it.

Back on the trading floor, I traded one of the fastest-moving, highest-notional products at the bank.

You'd think the pressure from that would keep me up at night.

Wrong. It was my salary.

Yes, I made good money. My friends reminded me to be grateful for it constantly. 

But I knew I deserved more - and knowing that while staying silent was its own kind of torture.

Then I started noticing my peers. People who weren't delivering more than me, starting to earn more than me.

That's when the frustration really set in.

Not at them. But at myself. 

Because I started asking the question I should've asked a lot sooner, what are they doing that I'm not?

The answer had nothing to do with working harder or staying later.

While they were making their impact visible, I was waiting for someone to notice mine.

So I stopped waiting and started having the hardest conversations of my career.

With my direct manager:
↳ I pushed for transparency on where I stood and what it would actually take.

With senior leadership:
↳ I stopped talking about effort and started tying everything to revenue impact.

With the top of the house:
↳ I made the case (clearly, directly, and without apology) for why I deserved more.

Every conversation was uncomfortable. 

There were tears, resentment, and moments I genuinely questioned whether it was worth it.

But I stayed relentless.

And when you finally secure that six-figure bonus increase after fighting for it the way I did, it feels completely different than if someone had just handed it to you.
