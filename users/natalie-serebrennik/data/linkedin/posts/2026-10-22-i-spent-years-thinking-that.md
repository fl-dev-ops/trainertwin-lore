I spent years thinking that if I just kept doing a great job, the promotions would follow.

And for a while, that strategy worked.

I was getting promoted, great performance reviews, and taking on more responsibility at the bank.

So naturally, I figured I'd keep doing more of the same.

Work harder. Deliver more. On repeat.

Except eventually, that stopped being enough.

I was watching people around me get opportunities I wanted, and I couldn't understand what they were doing differently.

It was beyond frustrating because I genuinely thought I was doing everything right.

What took me years to figure out was that being great at your job and being positioned for a promotion are two very different things.

I had a strong reputation within my immediate team.

But outside of it... not nearly enough people knew who I was or what I was capable of.

And some of those people had a lot of influence over my career.

So I changed my approach.

I started building relationships with leaders outside my team, making my contributions more visible, and being much more direct about what I wanted next.

And not to brag, but it worked. I got the senior title and the 6-figure bonus bumps to show for it.

Today, I coach professionals who are making the same mistake I did.

They're working incredibly hard, delivering great results, and assuming someone will eventually recognize them for it.

If you want to get promoted, you can't leave your career advancement entirely in one person's hands.

You have to be willing to advocate for yourself, build the right relationships, and make sure the people influencing those decisions know what you bring to the table.
