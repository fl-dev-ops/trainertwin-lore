I was performing for an audience that wasn’t watching.

That’s why I was exhausted.

Not because the job was terrible.

Because every day became a performance:

Stay later.
Deliver more.
Say yes faster.
Take on more.

And by the time I got home?

I had nothing left.

The frustrating part, none of it was actually moving me forward.

I thought hard work was the strategy.

It wasn’t.

I was optimising for output…
when I should’ve been thinking about perception.

Those are completely different skills.

You can be excellent at your job
and invisible in the rooms that matter.

That’s the part nobody explains early enough.

I know because I spent 5 years doing exactly that.

Waiting for the work to speak for itself.

It rarely does.

Visibility isn’t about shouting louder.

It’s about making your value easy to understand.

Once I understood that, the resentment disappeared.

Not because the environment changed.

Because I finally stopped pouring energy into a strategy that was never designed to reward me.

Many of the professionals I meet aren’t burnt out from working hard.

They’re burnt out from working hard in ways nobody important notices.

Have you experienced something similar?
