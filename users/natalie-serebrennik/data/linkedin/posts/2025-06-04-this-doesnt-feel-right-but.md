“This doesn’t feel right but I don’t want to blow up my career.”

That was the core tension my client came to me with.

Leadership was rolling out an approach he fundamentally disagreed with.

It clashed with his values.
It dismissed his expertise.
And it put his credibility on the line with the team he led.

But the role itself:

Great comp.
Great company.
Great growth on paper.

So he played along.

Tried to be the team player.
Tried to convince himself it would pass.
Tried to make peace with a version of leadership that didn’t sit right.

Until it started showing up in ways he couldn’t ignore:

→ Sunday night dread
→ Second-guessing himself in meetings
→ Feeling like a watered-down version of the leader he used to be

When we started working together, my focus was to give him the space and support to figure out how he actually wanted to lead from here.

And then we’d figure out the where after.

We clarified what values were non-negotiable.

We built messaging to challenge the direction without burning political capital.

And we crafted a communication strategy that helped him influence the right people, without sounding combative or checked out.

At the start he was convinced he’d end up leaving his company.

Convinced that he wouldn’t be able to overcome the misalignment.

…but by the end, he didn’t leave.

He led.

On his terms. In his voice. With his values intact.

You don’t always need to walk away to make a change.

But you do need a strategy to lead through the tension - not around it.
