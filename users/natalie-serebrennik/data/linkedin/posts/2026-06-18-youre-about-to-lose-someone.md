You're about to lose someone you didn't know you were losing.

There's someone on your team doing exceptional work.

You know it.
They know it.
But almost nobody else does.

So when the next stretch assignment appears...
their name doesn't come up.

When promotion conversations happen...
someone else gets advocated for.

When senior leaders discuss future talent...
they're not part of the discussion.

The potential is there, but no one has made it visible.

I see this constantly.

And it's one of the most expensive leadership mistakes a manager can make.

The assumption is that great work surfaces naturally.

It doesn't.

Visibility is not a byproduct of performance.

It's a deliberate strategy.

And managers play a bigger role in that than most realize.

Because eventually one of two things happens:

1️⃣ The employee starts questioning their future.

2️⃣ Or someone else notices what you've failed to amplify.

Either way, you lose.

If I had someone on my team in this position, here's what I'd do in the next 30 days:

Find three moments to put their work in front of someone senior. 

A cross-functional meeting. 

An email where you cc your VP and attribute the win directly to them by name. 

A brief mention in a leadership update: "This came from [name] and I agree it’s worth thinking about."

Three moments of intentional visibility can completely change how someone is perceived across the leadership team.

Your job as a leader isn't just to recognize talent.

It's to make sure talent gets recognized by the people who can change that person's future.

Managers, do you agree with this statement?
