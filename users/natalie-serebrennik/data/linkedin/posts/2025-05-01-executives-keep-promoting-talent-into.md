Executives keep promoting talent into leadership for the wrong reasons.

And it’s hurting them more than they realize.

We spend so much time defining what makes a good leader:

→ Caring about their people
→ Encouraging growth
→ Supporting success

Yet time and time again, the wrong people get promoted.

Why?

Because we assume top talent wants to lead.

And that assumption is costing companies more than they think.

Here’s what doesn’t get talked about enough:

It’s hard to turn down a leadership promotion, even when you know you don’t want it.

→ You don’t want to seem ungrateful
→ You don’t want to look like you’re not invested
→ So you say yes, for the wrong reasons, to 'look good'

Reality is, not everyone wants to lead, and not everyone should!

Leadership demands a specific skillset and a specific mindset.

Instead of assuming, ask:
What does growth look like for them?

Next-level growth doesn’t always mean managing people.

It can mean deepening expertise, expanding impact, or driving results in new ways.

It’s time to stop defaulting to 'promote to lead.'

And start being intentional about how we define and support real growth.

Leadership isn’t one-size-fits-all.

And not every top performer needs to manage others to keep advancing.

Agree?
