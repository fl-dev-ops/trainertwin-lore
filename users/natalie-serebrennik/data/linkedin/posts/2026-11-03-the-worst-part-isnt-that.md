The worst part isn't that you didn't get promoted.
It's that now you have to train the person who did.

You've been exceeding expectations for years.

Your boss tells you how valuable you are. You're the person everyone relies on when something important needs to get done.

You've put in the late nights, taken on the extra work, and gone above and beyond more times than you can count.

And you genuinely thought it would pay off.

But when the promotion finally comes around, someone else gets it.

And now you're sitting there showing them how everything works, answering their questions, and helping them get up to speed.

I'd be pissed off too.

Especially if I'd spent years being told how great I was doing.

And maybe the person who got promoted really was more qualified.

Or maybe they were better at building relationships with the right people, communicating their impact, and making sure leadership saw them as ready.

Either way, I'd want to understand why I wasn't selected.

Because the last thing I'd want is to spend another year doing everything I've been doing, only to end up in the exact same position.

Something I wish I'd understood sooner in my own career is that being great at your current job and being seen as ready for the next one aren't the same thing.

You can work your ass off, deliver incredible results, and be one of the most valuable people on your team.

But if you don't understand what actually goes into those promotion decisions, you're leaving an awful lot to chance.

So yes, be frustrated. Be disappointed. Give yourself a minute to process it.

But then I'd be having some very direct conversations.

Why wasn't I selected?

What did the other candidate demonstrate that I didn't?

And what specifically would I need to demonstrate to be considered next time?

Because you can't close your promotion gaps until you know what they are.
