I did everything right for three years.

And still got passed over.

My team respected me.
My manager trusted me.
My clients loved working with me.

And the promotion I had been working so hard for?

Went to someone else.

I wasn’t bad at my job. I was great at it.

But I was so focused on performing inside my role that I completely missed where promotion decisions actually get made.

Not in your performance review.
Not in your manager’s office.

In rooms you’re not in, between people deciding who’s even visible enough to be in the conversation.

If you’re working toward your promotion this year, make sure you’re one of them.

Here’s what I did to make that happen:

I started going out of my way to connect with cross-functional leaders about the work I was doing.

Not to network for the sake of it, but to surface synergies and create opportunities for collaboration.

I trained myself to think this way in real time, in every meeting:

Which teams benefit from knowing this?
Which teams are at risk because of this?
What opportunities exist to collaborate?

Then I acted on it.

Sometimes that meant sending a quick note.

Sometimes it meant walking over to someone’s desk and sharing it on the spot.

None of this is complicated.

But very few people do it consistently.

And this is how you can stand out.

Ask yourself those questions often enough, and the answers become obvious.

And suddenly, you’re not just someone doing great work in your lane.

You’re someone decision-makers think about when the next opportunity comes up.
