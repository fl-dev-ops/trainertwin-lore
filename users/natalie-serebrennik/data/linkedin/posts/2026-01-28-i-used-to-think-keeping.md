I used to think keeping the peace was the smart move.

That not rocking the boat would earn me respect.

Instead, it buried my voice
and built resentment I didn’t know what to do with.

Because saying yes to everything.
Staying quiet when you disagree.
Taking on extra work without recognition.

That isn’t teamwork.

It’s self-betrayal disguised as professionalism.

And it shows up most often in high performers.

The ones who care deeply.
Who don’t want to be seen as difficult.
Who were rewarded early on for being agreeable.

But here’s the shift most people don’t make in time:

At senior levels, keeping the peace doesn’t build trust.

It creates doubt.

Doubt about your point of view.
Your leadership potential.
Your readiness for more.

And no, being a team player doesn’t mean disappearing.

It means:
- Challenging ideas without attacking people
- Leading conversations vs. waiting your turn
- Setting boundaries while still being supportive

You weren’t hired to keep everyone comfortable.

You were hired to make an impact.

And if you’ve been working harder than ever before, staying agreeable, and still hearing things like:

“keep doing what you’re doing” or “you need more executive presence”…

That’s not a capability issue.

That’s what it sounds like when the rules change
and no one tells you how to play at the next level.
