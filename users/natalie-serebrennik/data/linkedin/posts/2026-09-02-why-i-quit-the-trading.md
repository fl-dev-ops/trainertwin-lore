Why I quit the trading floor.

Not because something went wrong.

Because something finally went right.

For 17 years, I built a career in one of the most demanding environments you can work in.

I fought for every title, every pay bump, every seat at the table.

And when I finally got all of it... I felt nothing.

That emptiness was the most important signal of my career.

Because it pointed me toward the thing that had been filling me up all along: mentoring.

The relationships I was building on the side of my desk. 

The conversations I genuinely looked forward to. 

The feeling of helping someone navigate something I had spent years figuring out for myself.

It never felt like work. 

And the impact started to feel a whole lot more meaningful than anything else I was doing.

Yet it still took me two years to leave.

Even though I knew exactly what I wanted to do next.

Because a pivot can be scary as hell.

Especially for someone who grew up with immigrant parents.

I experienced life with a little and then experienced life with more.

And young Natalie definitely wanted more.

So walking away from financial security and a 17-year reputation wasn't romantic.

It was legit terrifying.

Easily the scariest decision I've ever made.

So I invested in the prep work.

I saved enough money to float me for the next few years.

I completed a 10-month coaching certification with a focus on Leadership.

I started coaching friendlies on the side just to prove to myself that I could.

I even hired my own coach to work through the fear of starting over, the fear of failing, the fear of... let's be honest: everything.

And when I finally had enough belief in myself (and when that final bonus payment hit my bank account), that's when I quit.

My pivot took two years.

Two years of building a new identity while slowly letting go of the old one.

That was 3.5 years ago.

And it was the best decision I ever made.

If you're contemplating a pivot right now, don't let anyone rush your timeline.

Mine took two years and I'd do it exactly the same way again.

You don't need to be brave enough to leap before you're ready. 

You just need to be honest enough to know the difference between not ready and not willing.
