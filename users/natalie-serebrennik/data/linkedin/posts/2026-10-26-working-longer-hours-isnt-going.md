Working longer hours isn't going to get you promoted.

Your manager already expects you to get the job done.

So if you've been grinding away thinking someone upstairs is taking note of how early you log on or how late you stay, I'd encourage you to think about what else they're seeing.

Because when senior leaders are assessing your readiness for bigger responsibilities, they're also paying attention to things like:

1️⃣ Whether you have an opinion when you're asked, rather than just summarizing the facts.

2️⃣ Whether you can spot a potential risk and raise it before it becomes a bigger problem.

3️⃣ Whether you ask questions that get people thinking about something they hadn't considered.

4️⃣ Whether you're willing to contribute your perspective in meetings without waiting for someone to call on you.

And none of these things require you to work extra hours.

But they do require you to show up differently in the hours you're already there.

I think a lot of professionals underestimate how much these smaller behaviours influence the way they're perceived by management.

Because there's a difference between being seen as someone who can execute really well and someone who's ready to take on greater responsibility.

And more often than not, closing that gap doesn't require doing more work.

It requires approaching the work you're already doing differently.

If I were focused on getting promoted right now, I'd start by asking myself which of these four areas needs the most attention.
