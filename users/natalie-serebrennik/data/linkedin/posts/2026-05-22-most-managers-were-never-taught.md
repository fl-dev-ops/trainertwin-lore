Most managers were never taught how to develop people.

They were promoted because they were exceptional at the work.

And somewhere along the way, organizations decided that being good at the job meant you’d automatically know how to grow other people in it too.

And to pour gas to the fire, managers today oversee nearly 3x more employees than they did in 2017.

So while their scope has also expanded.

Their development still has not.

So most managers improvise.

They copy what their best manager once did.

Or overcorrect against what their worst one did.

And that’s where a huge amount of career stagnation begins.

Where the impact = vague leadership.

Which is why we often hear feedback that sounds nice and encouraging but does nothing for our careers.

Like:

You’d benefit from more strategic thinking.
You need more executive presence.
Keep doing what you’re doing.

None of these are development conversations.

They are placeholders managers reach for when they don’t know how to translate instinct into actionable growth.

And high performers absorb the cost.

Because you can't build a growth plan around ambiguity.

Which creates a real organizational risk:

Talented people stuck for years under managers who genuinely mean well.

Until eventually those same employees become disengaged, underutilized, and open to leaving (if they haven’t already).

The fix isn’t complicated.

It comes down to 3 things:

1️⃣ Teach managers how to develop people properly.

2️⃣ Hold them accountable for growth, not just output.

3️⃣ Stop treating people leadership like a skill people magically acquire through promotion.

Most companies continue to invest heavily in technical performance.

Imagine what corporate would look like if we invested equally in the skill of developing human potential too

Do you see that ever becoming a reality?
