Turns out, learning how to play the game doesn’t stop when you leave corporate.

As 2025 comes to a close, I’ve been reflecting on what this year actually represented for me:

Getting really good at being uncomfortable.

Deliberately putting myself in unfamiliar territory and learning an entirely new game.

And in many ways, it mirrors my corporate journey more than I expected.

In corporate, you start out doing what you’re told.

You work hard.
You keep your head down.

Then at some point, you realize that effort alone isn’t enough and you change your strategy.

You learn how the system actually works.

You get more intentional.

And suddenly, progress accelerates.

Entrepreneurship has followed a similar arc.

The early years were about learning everything - the skills, the systems, the language, the mistakes.

And while I’m still learning, this year felt different.

This year was about leaning into discomfort at a higher level.

Pushing myself to do harder things, more visibly.

Owning my voice online instead of hiding behind perfection.

Building partnerships with agencies and companies.

Delivering work at a bigger scale and hiring support behind the scenes.

Not because I felt ready. 

(Is anyone really when facing the unknown?)

But because I knew staying comfortable would eventually cost me more than the risk of stretching.

And here’s what I’m most proud of:

I didn't give up when things got hard.

I didn't let myself believe the doubt that flooded my mind.

I stayed consistent even when growth required more of me than I initially planned for.

And because I pushed through, I was able to achieve some of the most meaningful wins of my career:

→ Grew my LinkedIn community to 15,000+
→ Supported 100+ clients in career acceleration
→ Built a healthy 6-fig business doing work I love
→ Was more present for my family than ever before
→ Carved out selfish time, guilt-free (bc I wanted to, not because I needed to). 

None of that happened by accident.

It came from choosing the harder path, again and again.

And trusting that staying in the discomfort would lead somewhere better.

And it did.

So as I look ahead to 2026, I’m not questioning whether this is working.

I know it is.

And I’m excited to keep raising the bar.

If you’re heading into the new year feeling stuck or uncertain, consider this: 

What will cost you more - staying comfortable or betting on yourself?

From someone who made the leap: it’s worth it.

Cheers to 2026. A year of bold moves, big growth, and creating opportunities you once dreamed about.

What are you looking forward to most next year?
