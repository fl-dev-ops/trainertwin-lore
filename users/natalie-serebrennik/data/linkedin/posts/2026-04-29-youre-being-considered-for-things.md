You're being considered for things you don't even know about.

And the person who brought up your name couldn't find the right words to describe you.

That's not a performance problem.
That's a positioning problem.

And it hurt like hell when I realized this for myself.

Because most of us aren’t told that's part of the job.

We’re told to deliver. 
To be consistent with our results. 
To let our hard work speak for itself.

And that’s what we did.

Because we’re really good at being the quiet workhorse. 

But at some point the workhorse no longer wants to be quiet.

The workhorse wants a new title.

Someone whose name comes up in promotion roundtable discussions.

Someone who's voted on in the next promotion cycle as a rising leader.

Someone who prioritizes being seen just as much as their quality of work.

And that happens when you decide your visibility is your responsibility.

Not your manager’s.
Not your company’s.
Yours.

We’re already into the second quarter. If building your visibility isn’t a top priority, and growing your career is, this is where you need to focus.

What’s your favorite visibility building habit?
