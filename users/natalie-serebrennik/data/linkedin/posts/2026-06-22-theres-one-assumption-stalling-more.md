There's one assumption stalling more careers than anything else I see.

The belief that your manager already knows what you want.

I hear it constantly from the professionals I work with.

They're performing above expectations. Checking every box in front of them. 

And yet…

They're genuinely waiting for the conversation about what comes next to happen on its own.

It rarely does.

Your manager is not sitting around mapping your next move. 

Even the most supportive, well-intentioned leaders are managing their own priorities, their own pressures, and their own blind spots. 

Your career is one item on a very long list.

And if you're not making your goals explicit, you are leaving that entirely to chance.

I've watched this play out the same way too many times.

The high performer waiting for a promotion who has never actually stated they want one.

The professional hoping for a stretch assignment who has never asked for it directly.

The person who has been underpaid for years and has never made a clear case for why that needs to change.

In every situation, the manager wasn't withholding. 

They just didn't know.

The most important career conversation you're not having isn't with your skip-level or the head of your department.

It's with the person you speak to every week who has no idea what you're actually building toward.

Say it out loud. Stop assuming they know.

What's one thing your manager doesn't know you want right now?
