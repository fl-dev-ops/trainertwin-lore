If you're the kind of leader who hates disappointing people, this is something you need to get better at.

The more senior you become, the more decisions you'll have to make that people aren't going to like.

A project gets cancelled.

Someone's responsibilities change.

A great employee doesn't get the promotion they deserve.

And if you're someone who genuinely cares about your team, these conversations can be really uncomfortable.

I worked with a client who struggled with exactly this.

He cared deeply about his people, but whenever he had to deliver news he knew someone wouldn't like, he'd start second-guessing himself.

He'd delay the conversation, soften the message, or spend so much time explaining himself that people weren't entirely sure where things stood.

And sure, it makes sense.

He didn't want to disappoint anyone or be the reason someone was upset.

But avoiding these conversations was actually doing more harm than good.

His team was left confused, expectations weren't clear, and people didn't always know where they stood.

And that's what we worked on.

Getting more comfortable having conversations that weren't necessarily going to end with everyone feeling happy.

Because that's part of being a leader.

Sometimes you have to make a decision you know someone won't agree with.

Sometimes you have to communicate a decision you don't even agree with yourself.

And sometimes you have to deliver news that's going to disappoint someone you genuinely care about.

None of that means you're being unfair or that it's personal.

But if your discomfort with these conversations is preventing you from having them, that's a leadership skill you need to work on.

Your team deserves clear communication, even when the message is difficult to hear.

And the more senior you become, the more important it is that you're able to deliver it.

What’s the toughest conversation you had to have with a direct report?
