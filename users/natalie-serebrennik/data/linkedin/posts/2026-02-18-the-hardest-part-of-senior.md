The hardest part of senior leadership isn’t strategy.

It’s knowing your decision making can directly hurt someone.

Most mid-level professionals think the next promotion is about bigger thinking.

More influence.
More visibility.
More impact.

And yes, that’s part of it.

But here’s what no one tells you.

The higher you rise, the heavier your calls become.

A budget cut kills someone’s project.
A restructure reduces someone’s status.
A promotion means someone else doesn’t get it.

You can make the right call
and still disappoint good people.

Something no one prepares you for.

When one of my clients stepped into his senior title, he assumed the pressure would be performance at a higher standard.

It wasn’t.

It was emotional.

He struggled with the fact that his clarity could feel like someone else’s loss.

So his instinct was to:

Delay the hard calls.
Soften the message.
Over-explain the decision.

He didn’t want to be “that leader.”

But avoiding discomfort didn’t make him kind.

It made him unclear.

And unclear leadership creates something far worse than disappointment:

It creates confusion.

And confusion = lack of trust.

I wish more manager appreciated this:

High performers don’t resent tough decisions.

They resent unclear ones.

They can handle pressure.
They can handle stretch targets.
They can even handle disappointment.

What they can’t handle is not knowing where they stand.

Or where the team is going.

If you’re aiming for that next promotion, this is the real upgrade.

Because without fail, this is the best way to build genuine trust that follows you throughout your whole career.

Agree?
