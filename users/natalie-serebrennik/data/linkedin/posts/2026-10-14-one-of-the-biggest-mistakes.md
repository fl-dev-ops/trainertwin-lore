One of the biggest mistakes I see professionals make when they're trying to get promoted?

They spend the entire conversation talking about how well they've performed in their current role.

And while your results absolutely matter, they're only part of the decision.

Your manager (and your skip level) need to believe you can handle the responsibilities that come with the next role.

So instead of building your promotion case entirely around what you've already accomplished, start thinking about what you need to demonstrate:

1. Get clear on what the next role requires

Ask your manager what separates someone who's performing well at your level from someone who's ready for the next one.

Get specific about the skills, responsibilities and leadership capabilities they're looking for.

2. Identify where you're already demonstrating those capabilities

Have you led cross-functional initiatives?

Influenced decisions outside your immediate team?

Taken ownership of work that would typically sit with someone more senior?

Those are the examples you want to bring into the conversation.

3. Start closing the gaps

If you need more strategic exposure, ask to get involved in work that gives you that opportunity.

If you need to demonstrate stronger leadership, find ways to take ownership beyond your individual responsibilities.

And make sure the people who influence promotion decisions know what you're doing.

4. Make the ask

Instead of:

“I'd love to keep growing here.”

Try:

“Based on the work I've been leading and the responsibilities I've taken on, I believe I'm building a strong case for the next level. Do you agree?”

And follow with:

“What would you still need to see from me to feel confident recommending me for that promotion?”

Now you're getting information you can actually work with.
