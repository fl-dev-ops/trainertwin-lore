You've been waiting so long you've started calling it strategy.

There's always a reason. 

A project wrapping up. 
A quarter that needs to settle. 
A moment on the horizon that feels more appropriate than this one.

And I understand it, genuinely.

Waiting feels responsible. 

Like you're being thoughtful rather than reactive. 

Like the timing will eventually align and then you'll be ready.

But here's what actually happens:

The busy quarter ends and a worse one begins. 

The project wraps up and a new one starts. 

The moment that was supposed to feel more appropriate arrives, and there's already a new reason it isn't quite right yet.

The conditions never fully arrive because the conditions are not actually the thing.

What's underneath the timing objection is almost always something quieter. 

A belief that the investment isn't justified yet. 

That clarity needs to come first. 

That you should have this more figured out before you bring someone else in to help you figure it out.

But clarity is rarely the thing you have before the work.

It's almost always the thing you build inside it.

And on the other side of that decision? 

A calmer, more deliberate path. 

One where the next move stops feeling like a risk and starts feeling like the only logical step forward.

Waiting didn't create that. Deciding did.

What decision do you keep holding off on?
