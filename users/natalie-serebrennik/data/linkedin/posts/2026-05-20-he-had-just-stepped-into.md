He had just stepped into the biggest role of his career.

And he wasn’t interested in leaving success in that role to chance.

He came to me right after being named co-head of his department.

Not because things were going badly.

Because he understood something most leaders realize too late:

Getting the seat is only half the work.

Leading inside it is a completely different challenge.

What mattered to him was industry context.

You can't fully explain the trading floor environment to someone who has never stood on one.

The pace.
The hierarchy.

The invisible rules around influence, pressure, and decision-making.

He didn’t want to spend half our conversations translating the environment.

So we got straight to work.

What surprised him most was where the real breakthroughs happened.

In the moments where the mirror got held up.

Because often what looked like a leadership issue… was actually a personal pattern shaping how he led.

The blind spots shaping decisions he thought were purely rational. 

Blind spots around pressure.

Communication.
Control.
Trust.

That's the thing about blind spots. They remain invisible until someone helps you see them clearly.

Which is exactly why even highly successful leaders need spaces where they can be challenged honestly.

20 years ago, leaders hid the fact they had coaches.

Now the best ones actively seek them out.

Because at senior levels, the risk is no longer lacking intelligence.

It’s operating too long without honest reflection.

The strongest leaders understand that self-awareness is not a soft skill.

It’s a performance advantage.

How do you see it?
