Dear high performer,

Your mediocre is most people’s best effort.

Read that again.

You’re so used to pushing, producing, and proving that you forget the standard you operate at isn’t normal.

Hear me out…

You’ve built your success on being dependable - the one who gets sh*t done, fixes problems, and steps up when others drop the ball.

But there’s a flip side to that strength.

You don’t know how to not deliver.

You don’t coast. You actually feel uncomfortable when you don’t have enough to do and immediately find ways to deliver elsewhere.

You don’t rest. You prioritize everyone and everything else over your own well-being and often skip the vacation days you’ve earned.

And even on your “off” days, you’re still performing above average.

The problem is, when that level of output becomes your baseline, stillness starts to feel like failure.

I see this all the time with my clients.

They’ll hit a major goal, and instead of celebrating, their mind jumps straight to, “What’s next?”

That relentless drive and high expectation of yourself is part of what got you here.

And it’s something to be damn proud of.

But it’s not what will sustain you long-term.

That beautiful ambition needs a new strategy otherwise you’ll burn out.

And you haven’t come this far to come this far.

Growth at the next stage is about doing less, but with more intention.

You’ve done enough and proven yourself 10x over.

It’s time for a reset. 

Not because you’ve fallen behind, but because you’ve outgrown the pace that got you here.

So if slowing down feels uncomfortable, good. 

That discomfort is proof you’re expanding in your best chapter yet.

You’ve got this.

What have you achieved recently by slowing down?
