I applied for an industry leadership award and like most women… 

I aimed way too low.

Not because I wasn’t qualified.
Not because I wasn’t ready.

But because I convinced myself I wasn’t.

I convinced myself the top award:

- Was too competitive
- Had too many talented leaders in the running
- There was no way I’d be chosen over them

Turns out, the selection committee disagreed.

They saw what I didn’t.

They pushed me to go for the highest tier: 

The Executive Leader Award.

And I got it.

The only one at BMO to receive it that year.

What happened next caught me completely off guard.

A bottle of Dom P on my desk. Courtside basketball tickets. A 1:1 with the Head of Capital Markets as he personally congratulated me.

I had no idea that going for something that felt out of reach would make me visible in rooms I hadn't even been thinking about.

That's the part nobody tells you.

Betting on yourself publicly changes how leadership sees you.

Not just because you won.
Because you were willing to try.

The part that sticks with me the most: I almost didn’t apply.

How many times had I been the one holding myself back, assuming I wasn’t ready when I actually was.

If I were debating going after a big opportunity right now, this is what I’d remind myself:

The biggest opportunities don’t always go to the most qualified person in the room.

They go to the one who decided to show up for them.

What advice would you give someone staring at a big opportunity and unsure whether to go for it?
