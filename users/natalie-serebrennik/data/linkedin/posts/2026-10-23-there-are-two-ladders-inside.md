There are two ladders inside every company.

The performance ladder.

And the trust ladder.

And you can be climbing one without making much progress on the other.

Think about it.

Your manager might trust you to handle your biggest client, manage a complicated project, or deliver something important under a tight deadline.

But does that mean they'd trust you to lead an entire division? Represent the business in front of the executive team? Make decisions that affect people outside your department?

Not necessarily.

Being trusted to do your current job well isn't the same as being trusted with a bigger one.

So if I were working toward my next promotion, I’d focus on these six things:

1️⃣ Who actually needs to trust you?

It's not just your manager. Think about the people who would need to feel confident putting your name forward for the next role. Do they know you well enough to do that?

2️⃣ Can you explain why your work matters?

Not what you've been busy doing. What actually changed because of your involvement? And why should someone outside your team care?

3️⃣ Are you bringing recommendations or just updates?

Management has enough information coming at them. They want people who can make sense of it, identify what matters, and recommend what should happen next.

4️⃣ Do people want to work with you?

This one gets overlooked way too often. Are you collaborative? Do you share credit? Can people count on you when they need help? Your reputation with peers matters more than you might think.

5️⃣ Would someone feel comfortable putting their reputation behind yours?

That's what advocacy really comes down to. If a senior leader recommends you for an opportunity, their judgment is on the line too. Have you given them enough confidence to make that recommendation?

6️⃣ How do you handle things when they don't go your way?

A decision you disagree with. Feedback you weren't expecting. A project that goes sideways.

How you respond in those moments tells people a lot about your judgment and maturity.

There's a lot of talk about needing to "play the game" to get ahead in corporate.

But these are the kinds of things I'd actually be paying attention to.
