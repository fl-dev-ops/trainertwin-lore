What do you do when your boss takes all the credit and none of the blame?

That’s the position Casse was in when she hired me.

She was in a toxic relationship with a manager she didn’t respect.

She loved her work.
Loved her team.
But hated her boss.

That relationship between you and your boss is one of the most important relationships in your life.

You spend more time with them than almost anyone else.

So when that connection is broken, the days start feeling like years.

Casse came to me because she knew her talent was being wasted (despite exceeding every milestone and giving more than what was asked).

She didn’t want to react and run.
But staying was becoming impossible.

So we took a two-part approach:

First, we focused on stabilizing her present.

Set boundaries.
Strategized with her skip level.
Detached from the chaos so she could think clearly again.

At the same time, we prepped her exit.

Clarified the roles she actually wanted.
Built a positioning strategy that reflected her value.
Strengthened her interview skills to crush any conversation.

Casse reunited with her power and moved from survival mode to strategy.

She was no longer a victim in an awful relationship.

The decision to stay or go is never easy, especially when there are meaningful parts pulling you to stay.

Figuring it out alone is one choice.

So is partnering with someone who knows how to help.

Remember: you’re not the problem. But you are the solution.
