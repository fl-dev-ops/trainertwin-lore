You can’t advocate for yourself in every room.

So the real question is:
who’s doing it when you’re not there?

That’s exactly what my client and I were working through a few months ago.

She’s a Director.
Over 10 years at the company.
Consistently strong performance.

She wants the next title: Managing Director.

And she’s smart enough to know that working harder isn’t what gets you there.

Not if no one is speaking your name when decisions are made behind closed doors.

So we focused on one thing.

Not just building her network.
Not visibility for visibility’s sake.

We picked one senior leader. Someone who could sponsor her growth in the room she wasn't in.

Someone with credibility and influence.
Someone who’d seen her work up close.
Someone whose voice actually carried weight.

Then we got specific about the ask.

We focused on strategy over generic scripting.

How to name her ambition without sounding awkward or entitled.

How to show appreciation without shrinking.

How to make it easy for him to say yes.

Of course, the doubts showed up.

What if I embarrass myself?
What if he says no?

Which I then replied with:

What if he says yes… and that changes everything?

That shift was enough.

She asked.
And he said yes.

This happened 6 months ago.

Today, she sits in her Managing Director title. 

Not because of one conversation.

But because that conversation changed what happened after.

Her work had context.
Her ambition had a sponsor.
Her name was being spoken in rooms she wasn’t in.

This is one of the most common blind spots I see in high-performing professionals ready for the next level.

They assume great work will speak for itself.
They underestimate how influence actually works.

Growth at senior levels isn’t just about capability.

It’s about the right kind of visibility, advocacy, and timing - aligned on purpose.
