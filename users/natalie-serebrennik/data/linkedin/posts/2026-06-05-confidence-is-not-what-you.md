Confidence is not what you feel before you walk into the room.

It's what you decide to bring anyway.

I've watched this pattern play out more times than I can count, on the trading floor and in the leaders I coach now. 

The people who command the most respect in a room aren't the ones who have everything figured out. 

They're not waiting until the outcome is certain or the risk feels manageable.

They just take steps forward like the decision is already made.

With a steadiness that says: I trust myself to handle whatever comes next.

That's a choice. Not a feeling.

The mistake I see play out too often is treating confidence like a reward. 

Something that arrives after the win, after the promotion, after enough external validation has stacked up to justify finally believing in themselves.

But it doesn't work that way.

The win comes after the decision to show up as if you're already capable of it.

Every time.

The leaders you admire, the ones who seem to have their sh*t together in every meeting...

They decided a long time ago that confidence was their responsibility, not the outcome of someone else's validation.

The question is when you'll make the same decision.

What's one room where you could show up differently this week?
