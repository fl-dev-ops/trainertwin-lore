My promotion said VP.

My performance reviews said 10 out of 10.

But behind closed doors, I was a 6.

I had been on the trading floor for 2 years.
Just got promoted.
And already grinding for the next title.

Long nights.
Early mornings.
Carrying targets on my back.

On paper, I was crushing it.

I took on more impact work.
I was reliable to my own detriment.
Delivered results leadership actually cared about.

But still... not enough people knew who I was.

My visibility was small.
Limited to just my team.
Invisible to those who decided the senior promo I wanted.

So I kept working harder.

More hours. More output.
More over-delivering.
More proving myself.

Eventually, frustration took over.

Then imposter syndrome.
Resentment toward management.
Fear of being exposed as “not ready.”

Years passed.

Still VP.
Still “high performer.”
Still waiting to be chosen.

Back to grinding harder.
Back to hoping they'd notice.
Back to trusting the damn process.

It worked the first time. 
Why wasn't it working this time?

The breakthrough wasn’t more effort. 
It was owning the right to be selfish.

No new certifications.
No fancy leadership course.
No dirty corporate politics.

Just good ol hard, uncomfortable, intentional, strategic, direct, honest, self-advocating, reputation-building, spotlight-stepping-into...

work.

It took me 5 years of trial and error to crack the corporate code.

Now I teach my clients how to do it in 3 months. 

If you’re done being the underpaid workhorse, we should talk.
