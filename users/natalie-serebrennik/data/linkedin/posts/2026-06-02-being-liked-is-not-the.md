Being liked is not the same as being taken seriously.

For a long time I confused the two.

I was good at relationships. 

People enjoyed working with me. 

I was the person colleagues came to when they needed something.

Whether it was to finalize the presentation, pull last minute analysis, or to be the last person standing at a client event. 

And none of it was translating into advancement.

Because likeable and respected are not the same currency.

Likeable gets you included.
Respected gets you considered.

And at mid-to-senior level, consideration is what changes careers.

The difference between the two is subtle.

It's whether you hold your position when challenged or soften it to keep the peace.

Whether you bring a perspective into the room early or wait to see where consensus lands first.

Whether the people making decisions about your career think of you as someone they enjoy…

or someone they need.

Both matter.
But they are not interchangeable.

A lot of high performers spend years becoming easier to work with.

And while that strategy has tremendous upside, you need to watch the line carefully.

Because once you cross it, people stop seeing you as leadership potential…

and start seeing you as “really good support.”

And eventually, that becomes the exact reason you haven’t been promoted yet.

If you’ve been labeled as the “nice” one, has this strategy worked for or against your growth?
