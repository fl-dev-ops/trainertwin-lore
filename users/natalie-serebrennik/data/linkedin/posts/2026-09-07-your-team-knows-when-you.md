Your team knows when you don't believe what you're saying.

They won't tell you. But they know.

And every time you stand up in a meeting and advocate for a direction you fundamentally disagree with, you're teaching them not to bring you the real stuff, like:

The problems they're sitting on.

The ideas they're keeping to themselves.

The honest conversations that would actually make you a better leader.

Why would they, if they've caught you being dishonest yourself?

That's the part nobody talks about when they say "just be a team player."

I had a client navigating exactly this. 

Leadership had rolled out an approach that clashed with his values, dismissed his expertise, and put his credibility on the line with the team he led.

The role itself was great. The company was great. So he played along.

Until it started showing up in ways he couldn't ignore. 

Sunday dread. Second-guessing himself in meetings. Feeling like a watered-down version of the leader he used to be.

When we started working together, the question wasn't whether to stay or leave.

It was whether he could lead on his own terms inside an environment that was pulling him away from them.

We clarified what was non-negotiable for him. 

Built a strategy to challenge the direction without burning political capital. 

And developed a communication approach that helped him influence the right people without sounding combative or checked out.

And for the first time in 3 years, he finally felt motivated by work again. 

Disagreeing with leadership isn't the career risk most people think it is.

Silently complying until you stop believing in yourself… that's where careers actually go to die.

When was the last time you disagreed with leadership? What did you do with it?
