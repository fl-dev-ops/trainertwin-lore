You can be everyone's favourite boss and still be letting your team down.

And sometimes it has nothing to do with how well you're managing their performance.

It's about the boundaries you're creating with your team.

I'm all for building genuine relationships with the people you work with.

Some of my closest friendships came from my 17 years in corporate.

But there's a difference between being a leader your team feels comfortable around and becoming so personally involved that you start blurring the lines.

Maybe you have one or two people on your team who you naturally get along with better than everyone else.

You grab coffee together, talk about your personal lives, and occasionally vent about things happening at work.

Nothing wrong with having a good relationship.

But what happens when those same people start getting access to information the rest of the team doesn't have?

Or when you find yourself sharing frustrations about senior management with people who report to you?

Or when other team members start wondering whether certain people are getting preferential treatment because they're closer to you?

And the hardest part about all of this… you might not even realize it's happening.

In your mind, you're just being yourself and building good relationships.

But someone else on your team might be sitting there wondering why they weren't included in a conversation, why someone else always seems to get the inside scoop, or whether being part of your inner circle is what it takes to get ahead.

And once people start thinking that way, it doesn't really matter whether you intended to play favourites.

The perception alone can create problems you never saw coming.

How have you navigated this in the past?
