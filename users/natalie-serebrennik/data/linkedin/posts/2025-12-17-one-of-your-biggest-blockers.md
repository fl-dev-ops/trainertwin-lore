One of your biggest blockers to leadership growth?

You’re still measuring your value by how much you get done.

I see this all the time with high-performers stepping into leadership:

→ They built their reputation by delivering
→ They’re known for being reliable and fast
→ They take pride in being the one who figures it out

So when it’s time to be the leader, they hesitate.

Because leading means:

→ Letting others take the spotlight
→ Getting comfortable not having all the answers
→ Being valued for your thinking, not your output

(at least that’s what it should mean…)

And this is where their growth stalls.

Not because they don’t know how to delegate.

But because they don’t know who they are if they’re not the one doing it all.

If this is you, you’re in good company.

Too many leaders lack the support to transition from doer to enabler.

When I start working with new leaders, the first thing we do is redefine what success looks like in their new role.

We shift the focus:

From execution → influence
From output → impact

From “I’ll just do it” → “How do I empower my team to do it?”

Because leadership isn’t you vs. them.

It’s you WITH them, building something bigger than you could’ve built alone.

If you’re stepping into a bigger role, here’s your reframe:

Start valuing your leadership by the impact you create, not the tasks you complete.

That’s the moment your next-level identity starts to take shape.
