Some of the best opportunities to build relationships with management have absolutely nothing to do with your day job.

I was working with a client who volunteered to lead a company-wide fundraising initiative.

It was something he genuinely cared about, but we also recognized that it would give him an opportunity to work with senior leaders he wouldn't normally interact with.

So we got intentional about it.

We identified two leaders involved in the initiative and strategized how he could use the opportunity to build genuine relationships with them.

Not by forcing conversations about his career or trying to impress them.

But by getting to know them, contributing to something they cared about, and giving them an opportunity to see how he operates outside his day job.

And when the initiative wrapped up, he didn't just thank everyone and disappear.

He scheduled individual conversations with both leaders to reflect on the experience, share what he'd learned, and continue building those relationships.

That's the secret sauce.

He took something that was already happening within his organization and used it to create meaningful exposure to people who could influence his career.

And he did it without asking anyone for a promotion, a favour, or an introduction.

I think a lot of people underestimate opportunities like these because they don't directly relate to their job responsibilities.

But senior leaders don't only form opinions about you by looking at your performance reviews.

They also notice how you collaborate, take initiative, communicate, and work with people across the organization.

So if you're looking to build greater visibility with senior management, start paying attention to what's happening beyond your immediate team.

What initiatives are they involved in?

Where could you contribute in a meaningful way?

Because sometimes the best way to create new opportunities in your career is to give people a chance to see you in a completely different light.
