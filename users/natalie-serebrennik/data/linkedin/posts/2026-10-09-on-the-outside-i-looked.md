On the outside, I looked like a confident leader.

On the inside, I was constantly wondering if I was good enough.

I worried someone would eventually figure out I didn't belong.

I'd leave meetings replaying what I'd said and wondering if I'd gotten it wrong.

And instead of trusting the skills that got me there, I poured more and more energy into proving I deserved the seat I'd already earned.

So I over-delivered.

I overworked.

And I stressed myself into exhaustion trying to make sure I never gave anyone a reason to question me.

I cancelled plans with friends more than I wanted to.

I worried about work while I was on vacation.

My laptop was open during movie nights with my husband.

I was so focused on not disappointing my boss, my team or my peers that I didn't notice how much of my own life I was giving up in the process.

I wish I understood this back then:

Feeling like you have something to prove doesn't necessarily mean there's anything left to prove.

I didn't accidentally end up in those rooms.

Nobody mistakenly gave me four promotions.

I hadn't somehow fooled people for 17 years into believing I was capable.

There was already plenty of evidence that I belonged there.

I just wasn't giving that evidence nearly as much weight as I was giving my own doubt.

And I wish I'd spent a whole lot less time trying to prove something that was already true.

So if you're sitting in a meeting wondering whether you really belong there, look at the evidence that got you into the room in the first place.

You might already have your answer.
