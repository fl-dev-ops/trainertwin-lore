I was raised to believe achievement was everything.

Not in a toxic way, just in a “this is how we build a better life” kind of way.

My parents had high standards.
So I learned to set high standards for myself.

The goal was always progress.

Not balance.
Not fulfillment.
Not happiness.

So that’s what I chased.

Top grades.
A finance degree.
A seat on the trading floor.

Every step of the way, I checked the boxes.

And for a long time, it worked.

I told myself I was in a steady state of achievement.

But at my core I knew I was chasing validation I hadn’t yet given myself.

I tied my value to what I produced.
And questioned my worth in moments of rest.

Even a great day didn’t count if it didn’t include some kind of win.

It took years, and a lot of internal work, to finally appreciate this:

You can be proud of what you’ve achieved AND still want a new way of working.

You can slow down AND still grow in a meaningful way.

You can advocate for yourself from a place of grounded confidence, not exhaustion.

I still hold myself to a high standard.
That part hasn’t changed. Likely won't.

But the pressure to prove myself is no longer what drives me.

Today, I’m driven by clarity.

By impact that’s sustainable.

By a version of success that feels like mine.

And I help my clients do the same.
