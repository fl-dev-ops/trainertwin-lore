Most people ask for a raise the wrong way.

They wait for their annual review.

They hope their work speaks for itself.

They walk in leading with what they feel they deserve.

And then they wonder why the answer is always some version of "not yet."

After 17 years in capital markets and coaching leaders through some of the highest-stakes conversations of their careers, here's what I know:

A raise isn't a feelings conversation. It's a business one.

Here's how to approach it like one:

1️⃣ Build your case before you ask.

Your raise isn't about tenure or effort. It's about the value you've created and what it would cost the organization to replace it. Lead with that.

2️⃣ Start the conversation earlier than feels comfortable.

Don't wait for review season. By then the budget is already allocated and the decision is already made. The best time to plant the seed is months before you need the answer.

3️⃣ Lead with data, not emotion.

Specific results. Quantified impact. The business outcomes that exist because of your work. That's the language that lands in the room where compensation decisions get made.

4️⃣ Have a plan for no.

A no today is rarely permanent. Get clarity on what "not yet" actually means, what would need to be true, and by when. Turn the conversation into a roadmap instead of a dead end.

Raises don't happen by accident.

You make them happen by following a strategy like this.
