I’ve seen grown adults ream someone out over a tiny oversight.

The kind of yelling that makes the entire floor go silent.

And 99% of the time, it was directed at someone in our back office, just doing their best with the non-stop chaos being thrown at them.

It made my skin crawl.

Same company. Same vision.
But somehow, not the same respect.

And the worst part is, that behavior was tolerated.

Even rewarded in some cases… as long as you brought in business.

I remember thinking, I’ll never be like that.

But then, in my first year on the desk, I got feedback that slapped me across the face.

We were finalizing an error on a trade booking. 
It wasn’t a big deal, nothing that couldn’t be fixed.

As we wrapped up, she paused and said:

“Hey… can I be honest with you? I almost didn’t bring this up. I wasn’t sure how you’d react.”

I froze.

My stomach dropped. My face went pale.

Was I becoming the assh*le I promised myself I’d never be?

That was my wake-up call.

And I made myself a promise right then and there:

I would never let stress or hierarchy make me forget what kind of leader I wanted to be.

If you need someone’s title to treat them with respect, you’re not a leader.

True leadership is titleless. 

From the person processing the forms to the one approving the bonuses, everyone deserves the same level of respect. 

Period.

In this industry, your name travels faster than your resume. And that matters.

If you’re the kind of leader who believes respect shouldn’t be based on your title, I’m with you.

And I help leaders like you make sure it’s not.

Have you ever caught yourself being the leader you swore you’d never become? What was your aha moment?
