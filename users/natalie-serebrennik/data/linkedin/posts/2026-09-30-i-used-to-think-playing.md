I used to think playing small was the smart move.

That if I supported the senior people around me, I'd earn respect by staying out of the way.

I was the newest person on the desk.

Surrounded by sharp, fast-talking traders who'd been doing this for years.

So I did what felt logical at the time.

I helped them look good.

I skipped taking credit for my own ideas.
Prioritized their asks over my actual priorities.
Put their success ahead of my own.

It felt responsible. They were more senior and I was still learning.

What I didn't realize then:

Those behaviors may be useful early on because they help you learn the game.

But if you keep investing in that strategy, you’ll quickly cap your growth.

Loyalty doesn't come from invisibility.
And impact doesn't speak for itself at senior levels.

Being a good team player might earn appreciation.

It rarely earns promotion or pay progression.

I see this pattern constantly with high performers.

Still operating by rules that once helped them succeed but now keep them overlooked.

At a certain point, leadership success depends on making one of the hardest transitions in a corporate career:

From contribution to ownership.

If this post made you recognize an old habit you're still carrying, you're not behind.

You're just ready for the next set of rules.
