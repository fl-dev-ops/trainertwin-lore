Corporate has a funny way of rewarding the performance of leadership more than the practice of it.

You've seen it a 100x:

The ones who talk a big game in meetings.
The ones who master optics but ignore outcomes.
The ones who know exactly how to show up when the room is watching.

They're the ones getting promoted.

Meanwhile, the leaders actually building strong teams and delivering genuine results get told:

"Be more visible"
"Be more strategic"
"Be more confident"

It feels backwards, and honestly, it is.

But it's also THE game.

With this underlining rule no one tells you:

You can be exceptional at your job and still be overlooked if you're not intentional about how you're perceived.

In other words, how people see you matters more than your amazing hard-earned results. 

None of this is a talent issue.

It's a visibility issue.
A positioning issue.
A strategy issue.

And you're not wrong for feeling frustrated by it.

But frustration alone won't move you in the direction you've been working so hard for.

When I start working with a client who's in this situation, I ask them to make a choice:

You can fight the system.
Or you can learn how to play it on your terms.

Not by faking it. Not by becoming someone you're not.

But by being more deliberate about how you speak about your impact, how you show up in high-value rooms, and how you shape the story leadership actually sees.

Because the leader they're overlooking and the leader they can't stop talking about are often the same person.

The only difference is strategy.

What's one thing you've done deliberately to shape how leadership sees you?
