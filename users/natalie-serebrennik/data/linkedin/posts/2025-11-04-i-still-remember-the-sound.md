I still remember the sound of my heart pounding.

It was a regular Tuesday team meeting.

Markets had just closed. 

12 of us packed into the big guy’s corner office.

Everyone was sharing wins, losses, opportunities.

Then my boss made eye contact with me.

I froze.

I had an idea to share - a good one actually.

But all my brain could do in that moment was spiral:

What if it’s wrong?
What if I sound stupid?
What if they judge me?

So I smiled. Nodded. And said nothing.

On the trading floor, confidence = currency.

And for a long time, I didn’t have enough of it.

I’d seen what happened when someone spoke up and got it wrong:

→ Ignored
→ Ridiculed
→ Dismissed

So I kept my ideas to myself.

Let silence do the talking.

But silence sends a message too.

And it was reinforcing the story (to them and to me) that I wasn’t ready for more.

I still remember venting to my work bestie later that night:

“Ugh, I wish I said something!!”

And the golden advice she gave me: 

“Most people won’t remember what you actually say. But they WILL remember that you said something. Might as well have a hand in shaping what they remember.”

She was right.

That night I made a new rule:

→ Speak. Even if I doubt myself.

And 10 years later, I'm still going strong.

What’s a rule you continue to stand by?
