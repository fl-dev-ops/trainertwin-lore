The mark of a great leader is this:

Having tough conversations.

And the only way you get better at them?

By having them.

Think back to the first time you had to give someone difficult feedback.

Maybe you softened the message so much they didn't understand how serious it was.

Or you were so focused on getting your point across that you came across harsher than you intended.

I've been there.

And here's something I wish more leaders understood.

Having difficult conversations isn't something you're either naturally good at or you're not.

It's a skill you develop through experience.

The more you practise, the better you get at knowing when to be direct, how to communicate expectations, and how to handle someone's reaction without getting defensive or backing down.

And yes, some conversations will still be uncomfortable.

You might have to tell someone they're not meeting expectations.

Push back on a decision you disagree with.

Or address an issue with a colleague who's making your job more difficult.

But avoiding those conversations doesn't make the problem go away.

It usually just means you're going to have a bigger problem to deal with later.

And people notice how you handle these situations.

Your boss notices whether you're willing to address problems directly.

Your team notices whether you hold people accountable.

And your colleagues notice whether they can trust you to have an honest conversation when something isn't working.

These are the moments that build confidence in your ability to lead.
