If you don't manage your own narrative, someone else will.

And chances are, they won't get it right.

Whether you realize it or not, you already have a reputation at work.

People have an opinion about what you're good at.

Whether you're strategic.
What they can trust you with.
Whether you're ready for more.

The question is: does that reputation actually match what you want to be known for?

Because doing great work doesn't automatically answer that question for you.

You have to decide what you want people to associate with your name and then give them enough evidence to get there.

If you want to be seen as more strategic, where are people actually seeing your thinking?

If you want to be seen as ready to lead, where are they seeing you lead?

If you want to be known for driving results, do the people who make decisions about your growth know which results happened because of you?

That's what managing your narrative looks like.

Not walking around telling everyone how great you are.

Making sure the way you speak, contribute, lead and advocate for your work consistently gives people the right information about what you're capable of.

Because if you leave people to fill in the blanks themselves, they will.

If someone described you in three words right now, would they match the three words you'd want them to use?

If the answer made you uncomfortable, you already know what needs to change.
