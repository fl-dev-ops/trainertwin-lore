You can’t advocate for yourself in every room.
So who’s doing it when you’re not there?

That’s exactly what my client and I were working through a few weeks ago.

She’s a Director. Over 10 years with the company.

She wants the next title, Managing Director, but she’s smart enough to know working harder isn’t the way to get it.

Not if no one’s championing her behind closed doors.

So we picked one senior leader. 

Someone who’d seen her work up close.
Someone with influence and credibility. 
Someone who could help.

Then we mapped out the ask:

- How to approach it without sounding awkward or needy (this was a big one for her)

- How to name her ambition while still showing gratitude

- And how to make it easy for him to say yes

Of course, the doubts showed up:

What if I embarrass myself?
What if he says no?

To which I said…

What if he says yes and that changes everything?

That perspective shift was enough for her to commit.

She asked.
And he said yes.

This happened last week. And now she has real advocacy, not just good intentions.

Strategic growth takes more than good work (especially the more senior you get).

It takes having the right people in your corner.
