No one at work is questioning you as much as you are.

You hold yourself to a standard that no one has clearly set.

You replay conversations.
Rewrite emails in your head.
Pick apart things no one else even noticed.

And over time, that pressure becomes your baseline.

So even when things are going well…
It doesn’t feel like it.

Because internally, the bar keeps moving.

Higher.
Tighter.
More demanding.

At some point, the job stops being the hard part.

Managing your own expectations becomes the hard part.

And until you see that clearly…
You’ll keep feeling like you’re falling short.

Even when you’re not.

Because the goalpost isn’t fixed.
You’re the one moving it.

And the more capable you are…

The more you raise the standard on yourself without even realizing it.

So nothing ever feels like enough.

Not the results.
Not the progress.
Not the feedback.

Just a constant sense that you could be doing more.

I know this pattern well.

It’s something I’ve experienced firsthand.

And if I’m being honest, it’s something I still have to actively manage.

That constant pressure to raise the bar.

To do better.
To be better.
To not settle.

On one hand, it’s what drives high achievement.

On the other, it creates a level of pressure that’s completely unnecessary.

And I see this in almost every high-performing client I work with.

Different roles.
Different industries.

Same internal standard.

Always pushing.
Rarely satisfied.

That’s why this work matters so much.

Because if you don’t learn how to manage that pressure

No promotion, no title, no external validation will fix it.

You’ll just take the same expectations with you to the next level.
