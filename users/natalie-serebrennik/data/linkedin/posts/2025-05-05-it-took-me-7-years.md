It took me 7 years to get my Director title.

And for many of those years I kept making the same mistake.

I believed I was the problem.

In banking, the unspoken rule used to be: promotions happen every 3 years.

Analyst → Associate → VP.

That was my experience. 

So when I hit year 7 as a VP, I felt stuck.

Actually, let’s be honest, I felt like a failure.

Here’s the backstory:

I worked my butt off for 3 years…
Then I took my first mat leave.

Came back and my eligibility reset to zero.

Another 3 years of grinding…
Then I took my second mat leave.

Came back right after the promotion cycle closed and was told I’d need to wait another full year.

And here’s what no one talks about…

After that much waiting, you stop blaming the system and you start blaming yourself.

Maybe I’m not good enough.
Maybe I don’t belong here.
Maybe I’ve peaked.

Corporate culture has a way of making you believe that if the system isn’t working for you, you must be the problem.

And speaking from experience, that belief will eat you alive and keep you quiet.

Oh hi stagnation - nice to meet you.

It didn’t take long before I hit my breaking point.

A few months after returning from mat leave #2, I had a new strategy:

No more wasted energy on ‘it’s not fair’.

So I suited up with my invisible armor and scheduled the most challenging conversations of my career.

That year, I made Director.

And from there, I unlocked some of the biggest wins of my career.

I wish I appreciated this back then:

The higher you rise, the more you need to advocate for yourself. This is nonnegotiable.
