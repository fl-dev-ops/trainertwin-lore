Some of the most valuable conversations I've had in my career had absolutely nothing to do with work.

I'm talking about the random conversations in the hallway, grabbing coffee with someone, or spending a few minutes catching up before a meeting starts.

Asking how little Johnny's birthday party went.

Following up on someone's vacation.

Or just taking a genuine interest in what's going on in their life.

Too many ambitious professionals underestimate how much these little interactions matter.

They're so focused on making a good impression that every conversation with someone senior becomes about work.

What they're working on. What they've accomplished. How they can demonstrate their value.

And look, there's absolutely a time and place for those conversations.

But people also want to work with people they genuinely like and enjoy being around.

I spent 17 years on a trading floor, and the strongest relationships I built, including with people who later advocated for my career growth, started with conversations that had nothing to do with business.

If you're looking to build stronger relationships with senior leaders, don't underestimate how much those small interactions can add up over time.

Because being good at your job gives people a reason to respect your work. Getting to know you gives them a reason to support you beyond it.

Don't underestimate the power of the "small talk".

Agree?
