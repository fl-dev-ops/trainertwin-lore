Your next promotion won't be decided by you.

It'll be decided in a conversation you're not part of, by people comparing names on a list, drawing on impressions that were formed long before anyone sat down to evaluate you formally.

So who's making your case when you can't?

That's exactly what my client and I were working through a few months ago.

Director level. Over 10 years at the same company. Consistently strong performance.

She wanted the next title: Managing Director.

And she knew working harder wasn't what was going to get her there.

Not if nobody was speaking her name in that closed-door room.

So we focused on one thing.

We identified one senior leader who could sponsor her growth in that room. 

Someone with credibility and influence. Someone who had seen her work up close. Someone whose voice actually carried weight.

Then we got specific about the ask.

How to name her ambition without sounding awkward or entitled.

How to show appreciation without shrinking.

How to make it easy for him to say yes.

The doubts showed up immediately.

What if I embarrass myself?
What if he says no?

My response: what if he says yes and that changes everything?

That was enough.

She asked. He said yes.

Today she holds her Managing Director title.

Not because of one conversation.

Because that conversation changed everything that came after.

Her work had context.
Her ambition had a sponsor.
Her visibility finally had a strategy. 

This is one of the most common blind spots I see in high performers ready for the next level.

They assume great work will speak for itself.

They underestimate how influence actually works at senior levels.

Growth at this stage isn't just about capability.

It's about the right visibility, the right advocacy, and the right timing, aligned on purpose.

If you can see yourself in this story, this is your sign - more of the same won’t get you where you want to be. 

Time for a change.
