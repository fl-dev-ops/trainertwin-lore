People will only take you as seriously as you take yourself.

I resisted that for a long time.

Because it felt like the burden was being put back on me. 

Like the environment I was in, the culture I was navigating, the dynamics I was managing every day, none of that counted. 

Just: take yourself seriously and everything follows.

But the more I invested in self-advocacy, the more I saw it.

Not as a motivational line. As a pattern.

The professionals who commanded the room weren't the most technically strong. 

Or the loudest. 

They simply stopped apologizing for the space they occupied. 

They stopped qualifying their ideas before anyone had a chance to reject them. 

They stopped shrinking in anticipation of a reaction that hadn't happened yet.

And leadership noticed. 

Presence isn't personality. 

It's a decision you make about how much of yourself you're willing to bring into a room.

After years on the trading floor watching who got heard and who got passed over, it was rarely about well they performed last quarter. 

It was almost always about whether someone had stopped waiting for permission to be taken seriously.

Most people are still waiting.

What would change for you if you stopped?
