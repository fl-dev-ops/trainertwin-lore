A corporate reality that just sucks:

You can do everything right in your current role and still not be the person leadership wants to promote.

Because there's a difference between explaining why you've earned a promotion and convincing leadership that putting you in a bigger role makes sense for the business.

And that's where I see a lot of experienced professionals getting stuck.

They go into these conversations talking about how hard they've worked, how much responsibility they've taken on, and how consistently they've exceeded expectations.

And listen, those things absolutely matter.

But if I'm the person making the promotion decision, I also want to understand what the business gains by moving you into that next role.

What bigger problems are you ready to solve?

What decisions can I trust you to make without needing my involvement?

How will giving you more responsibility improve the way the team or business operates?

So instead of building your entire promotion case around everything you've already accomplished…

Do this instead:

Start connecting those accomplishments to what you'll be able to contribute at the next level.

For example, don't just tell me you've successfully managed increasingly complex projects.

Show me HOW that experience has prepared you to take ownership of larger initiatives that involve multiple teams or departments.

Don't just tell me you're the person everyone comes to when something goes wrong.

Show me how you've improved processes, developed others, or reduced the team's reliance on you to solve every problem.

And don't just tell me you're ready for more.

Help me understand what the business stands to gain by giving it to you.

That's a very different conversation.

And it's one I spend a lot of time helping my clients prepare for.

Because once you learn how to do this, you’re no longer fighting against the system, you’re making it work for you.
