Your team keeps telling you everything is fine.

And while you think you're doing your job by asking the question, you may be sitting on an untouched problem.

Psychological safety is one of the most cited concepts in leadership right now.

And one of the most misunderstood. 

It's often treated as a culture initiative. 

Something you declare rather than something you build through consistent, specific behavior over time.

Real psychological safety inside a team is not the absence of conflict. 

It's the presence of honest conversation. 

The kind where people say what they actually think, flag problems before they compound, and disagree with each other without it becoming political.

Most teams don't have it.

And a big reason for that is because no one modeled what it actually looks like, in a safe and healthy way. 

The manager who says "my door is always open" has never examined whether the people on their team actually believe walking through it is safe.

Building that environment requires daily practice.

In how feedback gets given, how mistakes get handled, and whether the leader's own behavior signals that difficult conversations are welcome or quietly discouraged.

If your team isn't bringing you problems, the question worth sitting with isn't whether problems exist.

It's why they're not bringing them to you.
