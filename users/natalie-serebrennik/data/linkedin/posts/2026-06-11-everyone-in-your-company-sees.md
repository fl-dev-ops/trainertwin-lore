Everyone in your company sees your potential except the one person deciding your future.

Your peers. Your skip-level. 

The people who've worked with you closest and seen what you actually do.

But your direct manager, the one person whose opinion shapes every decision about your career…

Runs lukewarm.

Not toxic, just never fully in your corner the way everyone else seems to be..

And you can't figure out why.

This is one of the most frustrating experiences a high performer can have. 

Because the evidence says one thing and the reality keeps saying another. 

You're not imagining the gap. 

But you also can't point to anything specific enough to address directly.

What's usually happening underneath it isn't personal.

It's a misalignment in how you're communicating your value to that specific relationship. 

And that misalignment creates mistrust.

The things that land well with everyone else, like:

Your style, your approach, the way you frame your thinking…

Aren't landing the same way with the person who matters most.

And the temptation to quit grows stronger each year this dynamic plays out.

But deep down, you don't want to leave. 

You love your work, the people around you, and you actually like the company you work for.

So here's what I'd do instead:

Request a direct conversation. 

And open it like this:

"I’d like to have an honest conversation with you. From my perspective, I'm performing at a high level and I have the results to back it up. What I want to understand is whether that's translating into how you see my commitment to the team. What may be missing from your perspective?"

Most people never have this conversation.

Not from a lack of wanting to, but because they don't know how to start it without it feeling like a confrontation.

That opener changes everything.

And the answer will tell you exactly what's feeding the gap you’re currently experiencing.

And give you something specific enough to actually work with.

Quitting is one option.

Staying and improving the dynamic is another.

Either way, the decision is yours to make/
