I coach people through imposter syndrome for a living.

And yet, put me in a room full of coaches building million-dollar businesses, and guess who starts questioning whether she belongs there? 🙋‍♀️

Yep. Me.

Which is pretty ridiculous when I think about it.

I worked in one of the most intense corporate environments for nearly two decades, was one of the few women in the room, and still managed to earn multiple promotions and six-figure bonuses.

But apparently, none of that stops my brain from occasionally forgetting what I'm capable of when I'm surrounded by people who are achieving things I haven't.

I see this with my clients all the time too.

They get promoted into bigger roles, start working with more senior people, or take on responsibilities they've never had before.

And suddenly, all the experience and success that got them there takes a back seat to everything they haven't figured out yet.

It's amazing how quickly we can forget what we're capable of when we find ourselves in unfamiliar territory.

We're human. It happens.

And despite those moments of self-doubt, I'm going to keep putting myself in those bigger rooms.

In fact, I deliberately seek them out now.

Because being surrounded by people who are further ahead than me challenges me to think bigger, learn faster, and question what's possible for myself.

That, and I get bored of the status quo pretty quickly lol.

Which room are you holding off on entering because you don't want to feel like a beginner again?
