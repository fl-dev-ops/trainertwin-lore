I watched people move ahead of me for years.

And I told myself the wrong story about why.

Politics. 
Personality. 
Proximity to the right people. 

I had a reason for all of it - and it conveniently kept the focus off me.

But at some point the story stopped holding up.

Because the pattern was too consistent. 

And I was smart enough to know that if I kept looking outward for the explanation, I was going to stay exactly where I was.

So I finally asked myself the question I had been avoiding.

What are they actually doing that I'm not?

The honest answer was uncomfortable.

They talked about their work before it was finished, not just after. 

They framed results in the language of business impact, not task completion. 

They made their thinking visible in all the rooms they were were in, not just the obvious ones.

They didn't wait to be asked. They showed up to conversations I hadn't even thought to join.

And none of it was loud or political or arrogant in the way I had convinced myself it was.

It was just deliberate.

So I changed and started doing the same things. 

Advocating for myself in ways I had always told myself weren't necessary.

And it worked.

That experience is a huge part of why I coach the way I do now.

Because once you stop treating career growth like something that happens to you…

You realise how much of it can actually be influenced deliberately.

That change is available to anyone willing to ask the uncomfortable question.

I just happen to know exactly how hard it is to ask it alone.

What's the question you've been avoiding asking yourself?
