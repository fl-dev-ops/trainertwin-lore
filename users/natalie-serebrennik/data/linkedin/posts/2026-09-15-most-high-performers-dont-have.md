Most high performers don't have an ambition problem.

They have a clarity problem.

And those are two very different things.

The professionals I work with are almost never underperforming. They're usually well respected, consistently delivering, and genuinely capable of more.

But somewhere along the way, the path forward stopped being obvious.

Not because they lost their drive.

Because nobody ever helped them get clear on what the next move actually looks like, or how to position themselves to get there in an environment that wasn't designed to hand them the answer.

So they do what capable people do when they don't have a clear direction.

They work harder.
They take on more.
They wait for something to change.

And the frustration builds quietly, because they can feel the gap between where they are and where they know they could be, but they can't seem to close it on their own.

That gap isn't a talent issue.

It's a strategy issue.

With 17 years in capital markets, I understand how influence, progression, and credibility work when the stakes are high. The real world dynamics that decide who advances and who plateaus.

If any of this sounds familiar, that's not a coincidence.

What's the one thing that's been unclear about your path forward that you wish you had an answer to right now?
