“What if you spent less time complaining about your compensation and more time actually doing something about it?”

I’ll never forget this slap-in-the-face question I got from a close friend.

Because as much as I hated hearing it, she had a point.

I was frustrated with my comp. I knew how much value I was bringing to the business, and I couldn't understand why that wasn't being reflected in my pay.

And while I had no problem complaining to my friends, the thought of sitting down with my boss and having a direct conversation about it…

That was a different story.

I convinced myself that bringing it up too soon could somehow hurt my chances of getting more.

But deep down I was just afraid of hearing something I didn't want to hear.

What if they said no?

What if they didn't think I deserved it?

What if the raise I was hoping for wasn't even being considered?

And that fear cost me thousands of dollars.

Because while I was busy waiting for someone to recognize what I deserved, I wasn't actually doing anything to understand why I wasn't getting it.

And that's what I wish I'd figured out sooner.

I could have been having conversations with my boss about what was holding my comp back.

Was it my performance? Was there something more I needed to demonstrate? Was it a budget issue? Or were we simply not on the same page about what I should be earning?

Instead, I was making assumptions and growing more pissed off.

And I see so many people doing the same thing.

They'll spend months complaining about being underpaid, but haven't actually asked their manager what it would take to change that.

Imagine how much time and frustration you could save by simply having that conversation sooner.

Maybe you'll hear something you don't like.

Maybe you'll find out there's no room for a raise right now.

Or maybe you'll finally understand exactly what needs to happen for your comp to increase.

Whatever the answer, at least you'll know where you stand.

And you can decide what to do with that information instead of spending another year hoping things will magically change.
