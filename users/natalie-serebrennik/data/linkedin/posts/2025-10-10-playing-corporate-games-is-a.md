Playing corporate games is a privilege and luxury.

One my immigrant parents never had.

They didn’t know what “self-advocacy” was.

They only knew what sacrifice was.

They sacrificed their life, routines, family, and friends back in Russia to build a brighter future in Canada.

Hard work, discipline, and saying yes to every opportunity they got was how they built success.

So that’s what I did, too.

From an early age, I was taught that if I kept my head down, worked hard, and stayed grateful - opportunities would come.

When I stepped onto the trading floor on my first day of work, I was empowered to outwork everyone around me.

→ I worked twice as hard - late nights, early mornings.

→ I said yes to every ask - even the ones outside my role.

→ I avoided ‘rocking the boat’ - even when I questioned if it would help or hurt me.

I did all of this with pride.

I had:

✔ Small promotions and pay bumps
✔ Encouraging comments from peers
✔ Positive performance reviews from management

From the outside, I was crushing it.

But if that was true, why did I feel so unfulfilled?

It wasn’t until I started comparing my growth with others that reality hit.

I was growing, but at a much slower pace.

I remember thinking how unfair it was. 

Why wasn’t I given the same opportunities to grow fast?

Enter reality check: I was.

But instead of making the most of the opportunity in front of me, I waited to be noticed.

That realization stung.

Maybe hard work was the strategy my parents used because they had no other option.

But me? I have bigger options today thanks to their sacrifice.

Using hard work was the wrong way to honor what they’ve done for me. 

There was a new level for Natalie to step into.

So I did.

→ I stopped waiting
→ I started advocating
→ Before I even felt ready

And that’s when everything changed.

→ The recognition
→ The promotions
→ The bigger paychecks

It didn’t come from working harder.

It came from owning my value.

Now I help my clients do exactly this. 

If you were taught to "keep your head down," I hope this post reminds you:

Your work doesn’t speak for itself. You do.

And the sooner you start, the faster everything changes.
