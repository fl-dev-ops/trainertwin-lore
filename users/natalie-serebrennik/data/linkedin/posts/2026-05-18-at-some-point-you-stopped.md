At some point, you stopped making moves and started waiting for certainty.

I know because I did it too.

For years.

Waiting for clearer feedback.

A stronger signal.

A manager to finally explain what was missing.

Treating external validation like permission to take my own career seriously.

But here’s the part I didn’t appreciate back then:

The signal almost never comes.

Nobody is thinking about your career as much as you are.

And the longer you wait for clarity from the system, the more the system assumes you’re fine where you are.

That’s how people drift into career plateaus without noticing.

Not through one massive failure.

Through years of hesitation that looked completely reasonable at the time.

At first:

A project goes to someone else.

Then the promotion.

Then you realize the pay gap between you and your peers has become enormous.

Waiting feels responsible.

But over time, it becomes expensive.

The professionals I work with aren’t reckless.

They’re just done waiting for someone else to create momentum for them.

So instead, we focus on building leverage deliberately:

Visibility.
Positioning.
Strategic relationships.
Clear advocacy that actually moves the needle.

Because career growth rarely comes from being quietly excellent for long enough.

At some point, you have to participate in shaping the outcome too.
