Most high performers get lazy feedback.

It's either surface-level praise or vague suggestions that don't actually mean anything.

Neither does much for the kind of growth you're actually looking for.

Here's what I tell my clients: you have to train your leadership to give better feedback.

Try asking these questions at your next 1:1:

1️⃣ What would exceeding expectations in this area look like?

2️⃣ If you had to choose one thing that would accelerate my growth, what would it be?

3️⃣ What do you see leaders at the next level doing differently than me?

And here's the part most people miss…

Sometimes your leader doesn't have the answer on the spot. 

Not because they don't care, but because they haven't actually thought about your growth in that specific way.

So make it easy for them to form an opinion.

Which means… bring options to the table. 

For example, after the first question, follow up with something like:

"Which would make a bigger difference in your opinion, driving a new initiative or mentoring junior team members to scale impact?"

This-or-that questions help your manager clarify their thinking, while giving you the specific insight you actually need.

It also helps you avoid the lazy feedback that stunts growth in the first place (and I know that's not what you want).
