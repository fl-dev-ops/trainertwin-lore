Having direct reports doesn't make you a leader.

Some of the weakest leaders I've worked with had impressive titles, headcount, and authority on paper.

And some of the strongest leaders I've seen never managed a single person.

Because leadership isn't a box on an org chart.

It's behavior.

It's how you think when no one is watching.

How you carry yourself when things get uncomfortable.

How you show up when there's no credit attached.

Over the years, I've watched people without the official title lead from the middle.

They earned trust across senior leadership without formal authority.

They mentored teammates without being asked or rewarded.

They took responsibility in moments where it would've been easier to stay quiet.

They didn't wait for permission to lead.
They didn't hide behind "that's not my role."
They understood something most aren't taught:

Leadership is a behavior, not a promotion.

And promotions follow the behavior, not the other way around.

I think about this every time a client tells me they're waiting to feel more senior before they start acting like it.

You don't get to practice leadership after the promotion.

The promotion comes because you've already been doing it when nobody required you to.

That's also why some people keep hearing "almost ready" while others keep getting tapped for bigger roles.

The difference usually has nothing to do with capability.

It has everything to do with who stopped waiting.

If I wanted to get promoted by year-end, this is what I'd focus on next:

Find one problem nobody owns and own it.

Not because it's in your job description. Because solving it visibly, across the right people, is exactly how leaders without titles build the kind of credibility that makes the promotion conversation inevitable.

That's it. Start there.
