Starting a new role doesn’t automatically erase old doubts.

Even high performers carry fears into new beginnings.

That was true for my client.

She’d made multiple career moves before - but this one felt different.

The stakes were higher. The uncertainty in her head was louder.

She didn’t want a repeat of what happened last time.

She didn’t hire me to help her land her next job - she did that on her own.

She hired me to help her lead differently once she got there.

Because this time, she was done with:

→ People-pleasing
→ Mismatched values
→ Minimizing herself for others

She wanted a reset.
One that started on day one.

When I asked her what she wanted to walk away with, she said:

“I want to start strong, protect my energy, and lead in a way that actually feels like me.”

And that’s exactly what we built.

Over 3 months, here’s what shifted for her:

→ She knew how to lead with her strengths
→ She set boundaries early - without guilt or apology
→ She stopped overthinking and started trusting her voice again

She’s now navigating her new role with confidence, alignment, and ownership of how she shows up

… not just reacting to the culture around her.

Because the grass will never be greener unless you decide to lead differently.

If you’re about to step into something new, here’s one piece of advice:

Define what success looks like before anyone else gets to shape it for you.

Write it down. Revisit it often. Use it to guide your decisions in the first 90 days.
