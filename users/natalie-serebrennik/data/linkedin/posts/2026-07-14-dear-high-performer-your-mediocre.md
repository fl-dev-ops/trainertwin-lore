Dear high performer,
Your mediocre is most people's best effort.

Read that again.

You're so used to pushing and producing that you've forgotten the standard you operate at isn't normal.

You've built your success on being dependable. 

The one who gets things done, fixes problems, and steps up when others drop the ball.

But there's a flip side to that strength.

You don't know how to not deliver. 

You don't coast. You actually feel uncomfortable when you don't have enough to do, and you immediately find ways to fill the space.

You don't rest. You prioritize everyone and everything else over your own well-being, and you often skip the vacation days you've earned.

And even on your "off" days, you're still performing above average.

The problem is, when that level of output becomes your baseline, stillness starts to feel like failure.

I see this constantly with my clients.

They'll hit a major goal, and instead of celebrating, their mind jumps straight to "what's next?"

That relentless drive is part of what got you here and it's something worth being genuinely proud of.

But it's not what will sustain you long-term.

That ambition needs a new strategy, or it will eventually burn you out.

Growth at this stage isn't about doing more. It's about doing less, with far more intention.

You've proven yourself many times over.

It's time for a reset. 

You've simply outgrown the pace that got you here.

So if slowing down feels uncomfortable, good. 

That discomfort is proof you’re expanding in your best chapter yet.

What have you achieved recently by slowing down?
