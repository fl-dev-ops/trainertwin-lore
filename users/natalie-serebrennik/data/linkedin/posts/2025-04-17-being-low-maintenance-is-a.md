Being low maintenance is a horrible career-growth strategy.

It might be an easy way to be liked.
But it’s a terrible way to get promoted.

I see it all the time with high-performing professionals:

Prioritizing being liked over being seen.

They don’t ask for what they want.
They try hard not to rock the boat.
And “no” is not in their vocabulary.

And then they wonder why leadership overlooks them when the next big opportunity opens up.

Imagine a new reality:

One where you didn’t have to choose between your likability and your promotability.

One where you go out of your way to check in with your peers to find out how their weekend was, because you actually care if Billy is still sick.

And in that same reality:

→ You ask for what you need.
→ You advocate for your growth.
→ You speak with clarity and authority, even when it feels uncomfortable.

Being respected and being liked aren’t mutually exclusive.

But if you want to lead, you can’t only focus on being agreeable.

You need to be visible.

And that’s where I come in.

If you’re done being overlooked, I’ll help you change the game, without changing who you are.
