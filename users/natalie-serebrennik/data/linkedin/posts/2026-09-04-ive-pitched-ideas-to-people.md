I've pitched ideas to people who could end a conversation with one look.

And I won't lie, there were moments where my heart was pounding so hard I was convinced the room could hear it.

But I learned something in those moments that changed how I show up in every high stakes conversation since.

The goal was never to be perfect.

It was to be clear.

Most people don’t lose the room because their ideas are weak.

They lose the audience because they're trying to say too much, prove too much, and cover every possible objection before anyone has even asked a question.

And in doing that, they bury the one thing the room actually needed to hear.

What I coach my clients to do instead:

1️⃣ Narrow your focus

Stop pressuring yourself to win over the whole room. Identify the person whose buy-in matters most and speak directly to what they care about.

2️⃣ Anchor one message

What's the single thing you need them to walk away remembering? Build everything around that and cut the rest without guilt.

3️⃣ Use the pause

Silence feels awkward to the speaker and powerful to the room. Let your words land before you rush to the next point.

If you've been walking out of big meetings feeling like you left something on the table, you probably did.

Not because you weren't ready.

Because you were trying to deliver everything instead of the right thing.

Learning how to simplify what you're saying and speak directly to the person you need buy-in from is a skill.

One I had to learn to survive on the trading floor and one I love teaching my clients now.
