I compete with the top 1 percent in my field.

And some days, I feel like I don’t belong in the room.

Which is wild… considering where I come from.

Before this business, I built my career on a trading floor.

Hyper-competitive.
Performance-driven.
Multiple six figures on the line.

You don’t survive in that environment if you’re fragile.

You learn to think fast.
You learn to manage risk.
You learn to perform when everyone is watching.

And I was good at it.

That’s what makes this part of my story harder to explain.

Because I didn’t leave due to failure.
I didn’t leave because I “couldn’t handle it.”

I left while I was winning.

I walked away from the security.
The steady income.
The identity that made sense on paper.

And I started over.

From zero.

New industry.
New skill set.
New reputation.

For almost three years now, I’ve been building this business from the ground up.

No inherited audience.
No guaranteed paycheck.
No playbook with dos and don'ts.

Just belief.

And when I put myself in rooms with coaches who have built extraordinary businesses…

When I stand next to people with bigger brands and bigger teams…

There are still moments I think, "do I really belong here?"

And then I remember.

I’ve felt this before.

I felt it when I was younger on the trading floor, trying to prove I could run with the best.

I felt it when I was stretching myself into bigger deals with bigger clients.

I felt it every time I outgrew a previous version of myself.

The feeling never meant I was incapable.

It meant I wasn’t settling.

Imposter syndrome, for me, isn’t a signal to retreat.

It’s the price of admission into that next room.

So if you’re in corporate right now...
Staring at your next level…

Maybe it’s a promotion.
Maybe it’s a new industry.
Maybe it’s leaving altogether.

And your brain keeps running the "what if I fail" spiral:

What if I’m not ready?
What if I lose what I’ve built?
What if I’m not as good as I think I am?

I get it.

But now being on the other side, I can honestly say:

The fear doesn’t disappear before you move.

You move - and then you build capacity to do hard things despite the fear.

So if you’re standing at the edge right now…

Run the experiment.
Bet on yourself.
See what happens.

You might feel like an imposter for a while.

But that might just mean you’re finally in the right room.
