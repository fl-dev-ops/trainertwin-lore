Executive presence is one of those terms that gets thrown around a lot in corporate.

Especially when someone's being considered for a promotion.

"She's great at what she does, but she needs to work on her executive presence."

Okay, but what does that actually mean?

Here's how I think about it.

Executive presence is how much confidence other people have in your ability to lead.

And that confidence comes from their experiences with you.

When you're in a meeting, can you communicate your opinion clearly, even when someone senior disagrees?

When you're asked a question you weren't expecting, can you think through your response without getting completely thrown off?

When something goes wrong, can people count on you to stay composed, use good judgment, and figure out what needs to happen next?

These are the kinds of everyday interactions that shape how people perceive you.

And here's the kicker, you don't have to feel confident all the time to demonstrate executive presence.

You can be nervous. You can have doubts. You can find yourself in situations you've never dealt with before.

What matters is how you handle yourself in those moments.

Because over time, people start forming an opinion about whether they'd trust you with bigger responsibilities.

And if your manager has told you to work on how you show up, I'd encourage you to ask a very specific question:

"What would you need to see me do differently to feel more confident in my ability to operate at the next level?"

Because executive presence isn't some mysterious quality you're either born with or you're not.

It's something you can develop once you understand which behaviours are influencing how people perceive you.

And to complicate things even more, every leader will have a slightly different opinion of what that looks like.

So don't assume you know what your manager means when they tell you to work on your executive presence.

Ask.
