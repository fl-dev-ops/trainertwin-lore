There's a moment most people in corporate never talk about.

The one where you finally admit you're bored.

Not burned out. Not overwhelmed.

Just… really f’ing bored.

You're still delivering. Still showing up. Still checking every box in front of you.

But somewhere along the way, the work stopped exciting you.

And coasting, which felt like a well-earned break at first, has started to feel like you're dying a slow, painful death.

Most people live in that space way longer than they should.

Because it's easier to stay comfortable than to admit the truth out loud.

So you convince yourself it's just a phase.

That you should be grateful for what you have.

That wanting more makes you ungrateful for what you've built.

And the longer you sit in it, the easier it gets to forget what it felt like to actually give a damn about what you were doing.

The feeling you had when you were actually excited by your work.

I've been there. My clients come to me from there all the time.

And here's what I know:

The moment you stop pretending the comfort is enough is the moment things actually start to change.

You're allowed to want more.

You're allowed to admit that where you are no longer fits who you're becoming.

You're allowed to do something about it without apologizing to everyone around you.

If you're sitting in that quiet discomfort right now, that's not weakness.

That's your ambition knocking.

Answer it.
