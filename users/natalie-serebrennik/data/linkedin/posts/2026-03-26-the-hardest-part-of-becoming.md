The hardest part of becoming a people leader isn’t what most think.

It’s not the pressure of being responsible for others.

Or the added work you now have.

It’s letting go of what made you successful.

You were promoted because you proved yourself.

Fast.
Capable.
Always delivering.

But now, you’re expected to step back.

To not jump in.
To not have all the answers.
To let others figure things out.

And it feels unnatural.

Because your instinct is still to perform.

To fix.
To step in.
To prove your value.

But leadership doesn’t reward that the same way.

In fact, it can work against you.

Because the more you hold onto execution…
The less space you create for others to grow.

And the less you’re seen as someone who can lead at scale.

That’s the real shift.

From being the one who delivers…
To being the one who enables delivery.

And that’s where most people get stuck.

Because no one actually taught them how to make this transition.

They were just expected to figure it out.

And this is where corporate gets it wrong.

We put people through layers of scrutiny to earn a promotion.

We test their ability to execute above and beyond.
We reward them for doing so.

And then the moment they step into leadership…

We expect them to operate completely differently.
Without ever showing them how.

So it’s no wonder why so many default to what worked before.

If this feels familiar, it’s not a you problem.

It’s a gap in how most companies develop their leaders.

But that doesn’t mean you should ignore it.

Part of leadership is deciding to do things differently. 

Even when it feels uncomfortable.

Agree?
