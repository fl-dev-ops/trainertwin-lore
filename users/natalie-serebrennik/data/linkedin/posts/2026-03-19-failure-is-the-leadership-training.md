Failure is the leadership training no course can teach.

Most professionals try to avoid it.
Strong leaders study it.

Early in my career, I obsessed over getting everything right.

Every mistake felt like proof I didn’t belong.

Proof I wasn’t ready for bigger responsibilities.
Proof I didn’t deserve a seat at the table.

So I did what many ambitious professionals do.

I watched the leaders who kept advancing.

The ones getting promoted.
The ones trusted with bigger decisions.

And I noticed something surprising.

They didn’t avoid failure.

They used it.

Mistakes weren’t career threats to them.

They were feedback.

They treated failure as:

- Data, not disaster
- Insight, not embarrassment
- A faster path to better decisions

Seeing this changed how I approached leadership.

Instead of obsessing over perfection, I tried to learn faster.

Here are three leadership habits that came from that mindset:

1) Own mistakes immediately

Take responsibility, extract the lesson and then apply the improvement quickly.

2) Remove fear around failure

If teams fear mistakes, they hide problems and hidden problems destroy performance.

Turn every mistake into progress

3) Ask one question after every setback:

“What did this teach us about doing better next time?”

The goal isn’t avoiding failure.

The goal is shortening the learning cycle and apply that learning to grow faster.

If you're aiming for that next promotion, start treating your mistakes like the leadership training they are.
