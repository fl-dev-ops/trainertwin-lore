For the first few years of my career, I was miserable.

And not because the work was hard, or I was reporting into a toxic narcissist. 

Because I forced myself into a room that didn't fit.

Quiet office. No really, you can hear a pin drop.

Slow pace. This was probably the most painful part.

Predictable. Ugh, same day over and over again.  

And for a spicy child turned adult, I felt like I was suffocating.

But I convinced myself to suck it up buttercup.

I got to yoga at lunch. 
I was making 6-figures.
I never had to take work home.

So I did what so many grateful high performers do:

I adapted.

I ignored the "is this really it?" feeling.

I dimmed the parts of me that felt "too much" for the room.

And pretending to be someone I wasn't drained me faster than the workload ever could.

Two years in… and I was already burned out.

Then I moved to the trading floor and finally…

I could breathe.

The chaos.
The fast pace.
The adrenaline.

I found my people.

My spice fit right in. It was even celebrated.

And here's what I noticed almost immediately:

Mondays stopped feeling like a battle.
I stopped performing and finally started being.
Work became something I could actually enjoy again.

It changed because I stopped shrinking myself to fit a room that was never built for me.

If you're the one constantly adjusting, toning it down, making yourself smaller so you either fit in or others feel more comfortable:

There is nothing wrong with you. You're just in the wrong room.

Work can feel great again, but only if you choose a better, more aligned room.

For anyone who's been in a similar situation, what box did you have to break free from?
