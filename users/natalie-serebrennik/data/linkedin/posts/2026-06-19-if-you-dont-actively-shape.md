If you don't actively shape your reputation at work, other people will do it for you.

And they'll do it with incomplete information.

That's the gap most high performers never think to close.

Their manager knows how valuable they are.

Everyone else? Not so much.

The senior stakeholders. Cross-functional leaders. 

The people sitting in rooms where opportunities, promotions, and stretch assignments get discussed.

Most of them are operating with only a partial picture, if any at all.

And people make decisions based on the picture they have.

Not the one you wish they had.

That's why one of the first things I introduce clients to is a simple weekly work journal.

Not for reflection, but for visibility.

Every week, a concise summary of our work.

What problems you solved.
What initiatives you pushed forward.
What changed because of your hard work.

Shared intentionally with the people who benefit from knowing.

Most professionals communicate upward.
Very few communicate outward.

That's the difference.

Because your direct manager is not the only person influencing your trajectory.

The leaders you collaborate with matter too.

And if you're not helping them understand your impact, they're left to fill in the blanks themselves.

Over time, those small weekly updates build something far more valuable than a strong annual review:

A reputation that exists in rooms you're not in.

With people you've never had to convince directly.

How many people outside your direct manager actually know what you're working on right now?

If that’s a difficult question to answer, building out your visibility list is the first step.
