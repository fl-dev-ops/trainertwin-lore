The habit that made you successful is now holding you back.

Somewhere along the way, you learned that being valuable meant being a people pleaser.

Being the one who says yes… even when you know you shouldn’t.

The one who takes pressure off others because, if you’re honest, you like being the reliable one.

And it worked.

It earned you a promotion or two.
Even got you paid more.

But now, it doesn’t work the same way.

The wins you’re used to feel harder to get.

You don’t get credit for being helpful anymore.

It’s expected.

And the more you keep stepping in, the more you reinforce it.

So people keep coming to you for the same things.

The same type of work.
The same level of problems.

And without realizing it, you’ve made yourself essential…

In a role you’ve already outgrown.

I caught this in myself at one point.

I wasn’t saying yes because it made sense.
I was saying yes because it felt familiar.

It was how I knew how to be valuable.

But it was also what was keeping me in the same lane.

Nothing really changed until I started pausing before answering.

Giving myself a second to connect the ask to my growth.

And if I couldn’t see the connection clearly, I’d ask my own questions before committing.

Many times, I still said yes.

But it was a different kind of yes. 

A yes that made sense for where I was going.

I talk a lot about self-advocacy.

How my career trajectory changed when I took control of how I was seen.

And it’s small habit shifts like this that set the foundation for everything else.

Because people can only see you differently…

When you stop showing up the same way.
