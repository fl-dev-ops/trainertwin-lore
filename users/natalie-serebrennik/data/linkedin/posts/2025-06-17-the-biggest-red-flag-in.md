The biggest red flag in a meeting isn’t silence.

It’s hesitation.

Hesitation sends a signal, even if you don’t mean to send one.

It makes people question your conviction.
Your confidence.
Your readiness.

And no, this isn’t the same as a purposeful pause.

Let’s draw the line:

→ A pause is deliberate. 

It shows presence. It gives weight to your words and space for others to absorb them.

→ Hesitation is reactive. 

It’s that quick moment of doubt - eyes darting, voice unsure, message diluted.

One invites attention.
The other signals uncertainty.

That’s why I coach my clients to do one thing before every key meeting:

Pre-decide your point of view.

Not just your talking points, your stance.

→ Are you backing a decision?
→ Pushing for a change?
→ Flagging a risk?

When your stance is clear, your delivery follows.
Even if the conversation shifts, you’re not thrown off.

A clear stance will always land better than a perfect answer.

Because people rarely remember who was “right.”

They remember who spoke with clarity, composure, and conviction.

That’s presence. And presence builds influence.

This is exactly what I work on with senior professionals managing multi-million dollar portfolios.

And it starts with this shift:

Preparation isn’t a weakness.
It’s a smart strategy that positions you for impact.

So, how would your presence shift if you walked in already clear on your stance?
