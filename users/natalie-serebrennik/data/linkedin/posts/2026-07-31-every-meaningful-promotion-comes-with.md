Every meaningful promotion comes with the same side effect:

Doubt.

You can have the title, the track record, the experience…

And still feel the pressure when you step into a bigger role.

I like to remind my clients (and myself):

If the next level doesn't stretch you, it's not really a next level.

One of my clients had just stepped into a Group Head role.

More than capable. 20 years of experience to stand on.

But that didn't silence the thoughts running through his head.

Because no matter how far you've come, that little voice still wonders if you're going to f it up.

So he made a decision most people never give themselves permission to make.

He invested in himself.

His promotion didn't come with embedded support.

And reality is, it rarely does at the senior level. 

There's an expectation that you can and will figure it out all by yourself.

But he didn't want to wait to feel confident.

So he chose coaching for the objective, safe space to think clearly.

To rebuild trust in a frustrated team.

To practice delivering a compelling strategy to the board.

To uncover the blind spots that would make him a stronger leader. Not someday, but now.

That's what executive coaching actually is.

Not a remediation tool. Not something you need because something's wrong.

A deliberate investment in becoming a confident and grounded leader that knows they have what it takes.

Imposter syndrome doesn't disappear as you progress.

It keeps showing up as proof that you're evolving.
