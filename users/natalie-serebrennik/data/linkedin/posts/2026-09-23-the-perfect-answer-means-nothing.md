The "perfect" answer means nothing if no one cares.

I still remember the day I learned this lesson. I was presenting a market outlook to a room full of senior leaders.

You know the kind…

Heavy oak table.
Twenty people around it.
All watching and judging you.

I put in the hours. I overprepared. I did the work. 

Every stat memorized.
Every trend mapped.
Every answer polished.

And when the time came, I delivered it cleanly.

But still, I could feel it… they weren't bought in.

Not because the analysis was wrong.

Because I'd missed the only question that actually mattered:

What does this mean for them?

That moment stuck with me for years.

Because it shows up everywhere.

In big meetings.
In high-stakes interviews.
In career-defining conversations with people whose buy-in you need but can't seem to get.

Smart people focus on being right. Strategic people focus on being relevant.

And those are not the same skill.

Influence isn't about having the best answer in the room.

It's about answering the question your audience is asking themselves before you even open your mouth.

Most people struggle because they haven’t learned how to translate it into meaning for the room they’re in.

If you keep leaving presentations with that UGH feeling, it’s probably time to start paying attention to that blindspot.
