Just because you're quiet doesn't mean you don't belong in the conversation.

I work with a lot of introverts.

And they're exceptional.

Sharp and strategic.
Curious in the best of ways.
Passionate about the impact they're building.

But there's one pattern I see again and again.

They hold their thoughts in.

Not because they don't have value to add.

Because speaking up in meetings feels extremely uncomfortable.

And when they sit on an idea too long, anxiety takes over and they convince themselves the moment has passed.

Here's what I remind them:

You don't need to dominate a room to influence it.
But if no one hears you, no one remembers you.

One strategy that works like a charm every time:

Speak up early.

Say something in the first 10 minutes, even if it's small.

It gets you ahead of the anxiety instead of letting it build into something that silences you entirely.

A few easy ways in:

↳ I’ve been thinking about X and wonder if we’ve considered…
↳ I really liked [colleague]’s idea and it got me thinking about…
↳ To build on what’s been shared, I see an opportunity…

None of these require you to have the most educated or loudest voice in the room.

They just require you to use yours.

Try one in your next meeting and see what changes.

How do you push through the anxiety when it comes to speaking up?
