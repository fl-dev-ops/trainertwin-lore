You have mentors. What you need is a sponsor.

Most professionals don't know the difference, and that gap is costing them.

A mentor gives you advice. 

They listen, reflect, offer perspective from their own experience. 

The relationship lives inside a conversation. 

It's valuable. It's also entirely dependent on you doing something with what you hear.

A sponsor does something different.

A sponsor talks about you when you're not in the room. 

They put your name forward for opportunities you didn't know existed. 

They use their own credibility to vouch for yours.

Not because you asked them to, but because they've seen enough of your work to be willing to attach their reputation to your potential.

That's a fundamentally different relationship. 

And it doesn't happen through proximity alone.

Sponsors are built through visibility, through demonstrated performance, and through a track record that makes advocating for you feel like a safe bet for the person doing it.

A lot of high performers have invested in being mentored. 

Far fewer have thought deliberately about who in their company has enough visibility into their work, and enough stake in their success, to sponsor them.

Name that person right now.

If nobody comes to mind immediately, that's the work.
