The biggest red flag in a meeting isn’t silence.

It’s hesitation.

Hesitation sends a signal, even if you don’t mean to.

It makes people question:

Your conviction
Your confidence
Your readiness

And no, this isn’t the same as a purposeful pause.

There’s an important difference.

A pause is deliberate.
It shows presence as you give your words weight.

Hesitation is reactive.
It’s that moment of doubt when your eyes start darting and your message becomes diluted.

One draws people in.
The other signals uncertainty.

That’s why, before any high-stakes meeting, I coach clients to do one thing:

Pre-decide your point of view.

Not just your talking points.
Your stance.

Are you backing a decision?
Pushing for a change?
Flagging a risk?

When your stance is clear, your delivery improves.

Even if the conversation shifts, you don’t.

A clear stance will always land better than a perfect answer.

Because people rarely remember who was “right.”

They remember who actually spoke up and if they were confident in what they said.

That’s presence.
And presence builds influence.

And here’s the part most high performers miss:

Hesitation is often a symptom, not the root problem.

It’s what shows up when you’re carrying a blind spot you haven’t named yet.

The kind that quietly costs you influence and opportunities toward that next level.
