You could be leaving tens of thousands of dollars on the table every year simply because you haven't checked what the market would pay you.

There was a saying on the trading floor that I heard all the time:

“Quickest way to get paid up? Get a bid away.”

In other words, find out what someone else is actually willing to pay you.

And I don't mean looking at salary ranges on LinkedIn and assuming that's what you should be making.

I mean going through the interview process and getting an actual offer in hand.

Because there's a big difference between what a company advertises for a role and what they're prepared to pay YOU, based on your experience and the value they believe you can bring.

So if you suspect you're being underpaid, I'd start here:

Start looking at opportunities outside your organization.

Not necessarily because you want to leave.

But because you want to understand what other companies are looking for, what comparable roles involve, and what they're prepared to pay for someone with your background.

Talk to recruiters. Explore roles that genuinely interest you. Have conversations with hiring managers.

And if an opportunity makes sense, go through the interview process.

An actual offer gives you a much stronger data point than a salary survey ever could.

Now, does that mean you can walk into your manager's office, put the offer on the table and expect them to match it?

Absolutely not.

And I wouldn't recommend approaching the conversation that way unless you're genuinely prepared to leave.

But knowing what another employer is willing to pay you gives you a much clearer understanding of your external market value.

And that's valuable information when you're deciding whether to negotiate internally, what compensation to ask for, or whether staying where you are still makes sense financially.

I negotiated a six-figure increase in my comp.

And one of the biggest lessons from that experience was that you need to make a strong business case for your value, not simply tell people you deserve more.

Your results, your responsibilities, your contribution to the business, and what the market is willing to pay for someone with your experience all help inform that conversation.

So if you've been wondering whether you're being paid what you're worth, don't just look at what other companies are advertising.

Find out what one would actually offer you.

That's what it means to get a bid away.
