Most people leave corporate because they have to.

Burnout. 
A bad manager. 
A restructure that made the decision for them.

It was the opposite for me.

I left at my high point.

I had the title I wanted.
I built the relationships I needed.  
I was making more money than I ever had.

And I left anyway.

Not because I was frustrated, or passed over, or pushed out, or finally had enough. 

Because I had finally become clear about what I wanted my life to look like next.

And I realized that even a good situation can become expensive when it no longer aligns with the future you’re trying to build.

That clarity took years.

Not just professional growth.
Internal work too.

Understanding what I valued.
What I was willing to trade.
And what I no longer was.

Leaving on your own terms is a completely different experience than leaving because you had no other choice.

One is reactive. 
The other is a decision.

I think about that distinction a lot in my coaching work. 

Because sometimes the hardest career decisions happen when everything looks successful on paper…

…but you still know it’s no longer enough for the life you actually want.
