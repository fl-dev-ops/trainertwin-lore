My most hated (yet most useful) lesson in corporate: 

Being good at your job isn’t enough to get you where you want to go.

It took me YEARS to appreciate how true this is. 

Because the system trains you to believe that if you deliver great work, stay reliable, and keep proving yourself… the opportunities will follow.

But after a few disappointing year-end reviews, with the same excuse dressed up in different wording: 

“You’re doing great, keep doing what you’re doing” 

Followed by: 

“Unfortunately, we couldn’t promote you this year. There’s pressure from the top to keep numbers low.”

You start to clue in:

Your performance keeps your seat.
Your self-advocacy gets you promoted.

I had the experience.
I had the results.
I worked hard.

But none of that guaranteed my growth.

Here’s what did…

Learning good self-advocacy.

Focusing more time on how well I communicated my value (and to who)

And less on people-pleasing and saying yes to every ask that came my way.

Maybe this version already exists in you (and you just need to give yourself permission to let it out).

Or maybe it’s a version you still need to learn.

Either way, selling yourself is often the missing piece behind why your career feels stalled.

I learned this the hard way and my clients learn it inside my program every day.

But here’s the kicker - you can’t sell yourself if you don’t actually believe in yourself.

That belief comes from knowing your story inside and out.

✔️ How you’ve grown
✔️ What you’ve achieved
✔️ What you’ve overcome
✔️ Where you’re going next

When you own your story, people don’t just buy into what you can do, they buy into you.
