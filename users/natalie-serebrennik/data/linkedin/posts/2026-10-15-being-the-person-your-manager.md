Being the person your manager can't afford to lose isn't the compliment you think it is.

I see this happen with really strong performers.

They become the go-to person for everything.

They know the clients, the processes, the systems, and how to solve problems nobody else wants to touch.

Their manager relies on them for just about everything.

And then they start wondering why they're not being considered for bigger opportunities.

Sometimes, the problem is that promoting them creates another problem their manager doesn't know how to solve.

Who's going to take over their responsibilities?

Who else knows how to manage those relationships?

What happens to the team if they leave?

And hey, I'm not suggesting you should become less valuable.

I'm suggesting you need to start demonstrating that your value extends beyond the work you personally deliver.

Like this:

Identify the responsibilities that depend almost entirely on you today.

Start developing someone else who can take on some of that work.

Document processes, share knowledge, and create opportunities for others to step up.

Show your manager that you're thinking about how the team will continue performing when you're no longer doing everything yourself.
