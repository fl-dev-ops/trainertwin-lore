Not everyone who smiles at you at work has your best interests at heart.

That was a hard lesson I learned early in my career.

I trusted the wrong person.

Someone who seemed friendly, supportive and genuinely interested in helping me grow.

Until our interests were no longer aligned.

And when it mattered, they chose what was best for them.

I was blindsided.

Not because I expected everyone at work to put my career ahead of their own.

But because I had mistaken a good relationship for someone genuinely having my back.

There's a difference.

And it took me some time to learn it.

Someone can like you and still prioritize themselves.

Someone can give you great advice and still not advocate for you when something is at stake.

Someone can say they support your growth and behave very differently when your growth affects their own interests.

That doesn't mean you should walk around suspicious of everyone you work with.

It means you pay attention.

Who follows through?

Who tells you the things you need to hear, not just the things you want to hear?

Who has demonstrated through their actions that they're willing to advocate for you?

And equally important: don't hand anyone else complete responsibility for your career.

Build strong relationships, yes. Find people who will advocate for you, yes. 

But don’t forget to advocate for yourself too.

If I could go back and tell younger Natalie anything, it would be this:

Don't confuse someone being in your corner with someone being friendly to you.

Let their actions tell you the difference.
