The Story I Never Told and Why I Actually Succeeded in Dubai

I’ve never shared this story before.
Not because I was ashamed.
But because for a long time I didn’t realise this was the moment that made me who I am.

Everyone loves to talk about success and milestones.
But before that, I had six months that nearly broke me.

Back in 2014, I moved to Dubai with $10,000 and a ridiculous amount of confidence.
Stayed on my friend’s couch for a week, then moved to a shared apartment because Marina rents were already AED 120K.
I was head of sales back home and thought, how hard could real estate be?
Yeah. That confidence didn’t last.

My English wasn’t great.
The market was completely different. No AI, no YouTube sales training to explain how to get listings.
And then I made the mistake that still makes me cringe: I trusted the wrong person.

I started dating him and, under the “romantic influence”, sent photos of myself - let’s say less than formal.
He used them against me and sent them to everyone in my company.
That’s when I discovered the term “phishing” - when fake emails look like they come from your real address but are sent through spoofed servers.

One Friday at 4 pm, my colleague James calls me:
“I really like that picture you emailed me.”
I froze. I’d never emailed him that.

Then it got worse. For six months, he sent messages and pictures to agents in my area — using my name, adding my phone number and company name.
Every call could be a client or someone who’d received those photos.
Police couldn’t help. He used US numbers. No proof.

So I made a decision.
If I can’t stop it, I’ll own it.

My new pitch?
“Oh, you got that? It’s just a scammer. Not even my photos.”
And I said it with a straight face. Every time.

I refused to give him the reaction he wanted.
And something shifted.
I felt untouchable.

That was the moment I learned emotional detachment.
Not from meditation or mindset books — from survival.

If I could live through that in my first six months in Dubai — broke, alone, humiliated — nothing else could ever scare me again.
Not rejection. Not cold calls. Not failure.

So when new agents on my trainings tell me they’re afraid someone might hang up or say “no”, I smile.
Because once you’ve been “famous” for the wrong reasons and survived it, rejection becomes background noise.

That guy thought he’d ruined my career.
But he gave me the best gift of all: the ability to stay calm, no matter what happens.

Five years later, I saw him in a bar.
He looked shocked.
And I just smiled.
Because I wasn’t broken.
I was built.

If I could survive that and still show up for work every day, you’ll survive a “no” on the phone.
Courage isn’t about never being scared. It’s about not letting fear run the show.
