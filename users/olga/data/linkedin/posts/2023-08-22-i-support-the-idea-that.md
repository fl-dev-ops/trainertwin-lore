I support the idea that the best way to sell something is to help the customer discover their own needs and how your product or service can meet those needs.

The Socratic sales method is more effective than the traditional "hard sell" method for several reasons. 

First, when you ask questions and listen carefully to the answers, you show the customer that you're interested in understanding their needs. 

This makes the customer more likely to trust you and be open to your suggestions.

Second, the customer explores their own needs and options and builds a rapport with you at the same time. 

By asking open-ended questions, you can help the customer think through their needs and consider different solutions. 

This leads to better decision-making and a higher chance of closing the deal.

Third, the Socratic sales method is more respectful of the customer. When you don't try to force your product or service on the customer, you show that you respect their autonomy. 

This makes the customer feel more comfortable and in control, which can lead to a better sales experience.

The roots of this method can be traced back to the Greek philosopher Socrates, who crafted it in the 5th century BC. 

Socrates, a proponent of learning through dialogue and questioning, laid the foundation for this approach. 

Interestingly, contemporary research in behavioural science and decision-making corroborates these time-honoured principles.
