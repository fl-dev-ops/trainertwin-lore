Want to make more money? 

Stop chasing it. Start investing in what makes you better.

In 2016, I closed my first big deal in Dubai real estate.
A 25,000 AED commission — at the time, it felt huge.

And soon after, the money started coming in steadily.

So what did I do? Well… probably what a lot of people would do.

I invested.
Four personal training sessions a week — because energy matters, and I wanted to feel strong.
An MBA — because I wanted to operate at a higher level and think bigger.

And — of course — I bought a pair of very expensive designer shoes.
Because, well, I thought I should. It felt like the “right” thing to do when you’re earning more.

Today?
I’m still training. That investment gave me years of strength, clarity, and confidence.
My MBA? It helped me build the business and life I have today.

And the shoes?
They gave me two blisters and one life lesson: just because something sparkles, doesn’t mean it adds value.
They’ve been worn exactly twice — and every time I see them in the closet, I smile. A small, stylish reminder of what not to prioritize.

Over the years, my investment formula hasn’t changed.
I still invest in education. In experiences that stretch me.
In people and environments that help me think, grow, and lead better.

Because for me, it’s never really been about earning more — it’s about operating on a different level, having a bigger mission, and enjoying every single day of the work I do.

And today, when I run my trainings, I sometimes ask a simple question:

If you earned an extra 25,000 AED tomorrow — what would you invest it in?

It’s always fascinating to hear the answers.
Because the real question isn’t about what you buy — it’s about what you build.

The things that truly change your game aren’t always the things you can wear or park in your garage.
They’re the ones that expand how you think, how you operate, and how you show up in the world.

So if you’re thinking about your next investment — think beyond the price tag.

And if you’ve had your own “expensive shoe moment” — I’d love to hear it.
Thank you for reading.
