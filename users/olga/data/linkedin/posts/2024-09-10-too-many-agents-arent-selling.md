Too many agents aren’t selling at the level they could be. Even with top-notch training, mentors, and opportunities, closing deals consistently is still a challenge.
It’s tempting to think it’s a skills issue—“I just gotta get better at objection handling” or “I need to pull more pain out.” And yeah, maybe that’s part of it.
But in my experience of coaching dozens of sales reps, and being surrounded by some of the best in the industry, it’s not just about learning more or practicing harder.
I’ve been watching this new docu-series on Netflix, Sprint—have you seen it?
These are elite athletes—the top one percent of the one percent. They’re incredibly talented, but what can cost them everything? The way they think and feel about themselves and their level of certainty in their capabilities. It’s their inner game.
The same applies in sales. Your beliefs about yourself shape your performance even more than your sales knowledge.
And here’s the key: your self-belief and confidence shouldn’t be tied to whether you close the deal. It’s about something deeper—your ability to do what needs to be done every single day, no matter what. Whether you get that listing today or not, the real win is consistently making your calls, following up, doing the work, and staying on track with your daily routines.
That consistency is what builds real confidence—not the outcome of one call. It's knowing that, no matter what happens today, you’ve put in the effort. You've followed your plan. And that is where true confidence comes from.
If you believe you can only earn or sell a certain amount, you'll unconsciously seek out prospects who reflect that belief. And you’ll only do the activities that match that level of belief. You end up limiting yourself.

So while improving your sales skills is crucial, true success comes from mastering your inner world—your beliefs about yourself, your consistency, your daily habits. That’s what shapes your actions and, ultimately, your success.
It’s not just about what you know; it’s about who you are and what you do every single day. Knowledge puts you on the field, but inner mastery and consistent effort? That’s what wins the game.
