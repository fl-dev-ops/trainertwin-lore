I just watched an agent choose ethics over commissions. Here's what happened next.

While her colleagues were stealing leads behind each other's backs, she refused to play that game.

"It's against my religious beliefs," she told her manager.

Her team was toxic. Agents would call prospects already being handled by other agents. They'd backstab each other for deals. The culture was "steal or starve."

But she wouldn't do it.

Her manager even praised her for being the ONLY person on the floor who didn't approach leads already in contact with other agents.

"It's haram in my religion," she explained. "Even if you're not Muslim, I don't steal from people. Why would I do something I wouldn't want done to me?"

So what happened to the "ethical" agent in Dubai's cutthroat market?

Plot twist: She started winning.

While her colleagues were burning relationships with harassment tactics, she was doing something different:

→ Using emotional intelligence in her outreach
→ Actually listening to prospects instead of pitching
→ Building genuine connections
→ Following up without being pushy

Result? Her first qualified prospect in months.

A Syrian investor who had been approached by multiple agents chose to work with HER.

Why?

"I remember you. You were different."

Here's what I learned coaching her:

Ethics isn't a weakness in sales - it's a competitive advantage.

While everyone else is fighting dirty, ethical agents stand out like lighthouses in a storm.

Prospects aren't just buying properties. They're buying trust.

And trust? That's built through integrity, not aggression.

The agents making sustainable money in Dubai aren't the ones playing dirty tricks.

They're the ones prospects actually want to work with.

Your values aren't obstacles to success.
They're the foundation of it.
