"On a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate a meeting?" 
It sounds simple, but it's often overlooked. We're afraid to hear negative feedback or constructive criticism, but that's exactly where our growth opportunities lie.

I've learned that feedback is crucial for improving our productivity and success. That's why I always ask my clients, colleagues, friends, and even my spouse for their honest feedback on my meetings, proposals, and overall performance.

Yesterday, I finished the first part of the training program for the new joiners of our sales team. 

As part of the feedback survey, I included a similar question: "on a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate the training program?" And also "on a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate me as a sales trainer?"

But I didn't stop there. I also asked them:

 "If you rated anything less than 10, please help me improve myself and my program. What would make it a 10?"

 This way, I can learn from their feedback and make the necessary adjustments to improve the program and my training skills.

So, how often do you ask for feedback from your colleagues? And what do you do with that feedback? In my opinion, it's important to create a culture of feedback and continuous improvement in any professional setting.

If you're a sales professional in the real estate industry, I encourage you to try this approach and see how it can up your productivity and success. Let's learn from each other and keep growing!
