Why I never tell clients what to think (but they end up thinking what I want)

The secret is in the questions, not the answers.

When she said "prices are too high," I didn't argue.

Instead I asked: "Have you seen any price changes in JVC over the past year?"

She answered herself: "Yeah, definitely the prices are going up."

You see what happened? I didn't tell her prices were rising. 
She told me... and herself

The moment you start telling clients what to think, their resistance goes up.

 They know you're in sales mode.
But when you ask the right questions, they convince themselves.
Here's the structure I used with Agent in my Coaching yesterday:
Client: "I want a discount on this 4.1M property."
Me: "If you had 5 buyers for your car, would YOU give a discount?"
Client: "Of course not, I'd take the highest offer."

Me: "Why would a developer be any different?"

I never said "no discount." She figured out why discounts don't make sense.

Same with the client who had £100k sitting for 18 months, waiting for the "right time."

Instead of lecturing about inflation, I asked: "Have you been saying it's not the right time for the past few years?"
She realized her own procrastination pattern.

The psychology is simple: People believe their own conclusions way more than yours.

Your job isn't to convince. It's to guide them to convince themselves.

Next time a client objects, don't counter with facts.

Ask a question that makes them think.
