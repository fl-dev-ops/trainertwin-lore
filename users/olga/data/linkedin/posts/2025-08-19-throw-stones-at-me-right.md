Throw stones at me right from the start 

if you still believe that the louder you pitch, the more “amazing features” you list, and the more enthusiastic you sound — the faster you’ll close deals.

The truth? Clients don’t buy because you oversell. They buy because they trust you.

And how do you actually build this trust?
One of the most powerful techniques I use is called Speaking Against Your Favor.

It means openly sharing not only the strengths of a project, but also its possible downsides. Instead of pushing, you acknowledge what might not work for the client — and that honesty makes your recommendations credible. Paradoxically, by admitting what’s “less than perfect,” you become far more persuasive.
