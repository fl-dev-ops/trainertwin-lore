My personal failure experience….
or “How to Sell More by Viewing Rejections and Failures as Delayed Opportunities.” 

We’ve all been there during our careers. Failures and rejections are still very painful.

Whether a client chooses another agent or a company opts for a different training service provider, the initial reaction is often to push back, get angry, and blame the external world. This immediately puts you in a victim mentality, and spending even 20% of your money-making working hours in this space will only drag down your income.

For consistent success in sales, you can't afford to dwell in that mindset. Here's my strategy to turn rejection or failure into an opportunity:

30-Minute Emotional Detox: Allow emotions to pass by expressing them. Write everything down with pen and paper or share your thoughts and feelings with someone outside your workspace. Set a timer for 30 minutes – no more complaining afterwards! Negative energy is contagious, and venting to a colleague can lead to unproductive discussions, pushing blame on customers, managers, and the company, and risking your job.

Reflection: Once emotions have passed, look at the situation with a clear, unbiased perception. What happened? Are you catastrophizing or being one-sided?

Learning: How can you use this experience to become better? What changes can you make in your work process or approach to avoid or alter outcomes? There's no learning in winning; the most growth occurs when we take personal responsibility for our failures and learn from them.

Recently, I received feedback from my client's sales team. Some agents found my training materials too complicated, and they thought my feedback during role-play sessions was too harsh.

My process:

30-Minute Emotional Detox: Allow myself to feel the range of emotions – disappointment, betrayal, the desire to give up.

Reflection: Recognising my focus on the end result and demanding higher results from myself hindered being realistic about the level and speed of progress others could achieve.

Learning: Determine how I can break down and simplify my material and language to be effective at the beginner's level. Mastery lies in making complex things understandable as if explaining to a 10-year-old. This boosts self-esteem and improves learning ability in the future.

It's okay to be at any level of skills and knowledge; we all start from zero.
