Saudi Arabia’s Emerging Real Estate Market: What You Need to Know

My first visit to Riyadh was during a previous Cityscape Global event in 2023. 

As someone who decided to rent a car and explore the city on my own for three days, I probably raised a few eyebrows driving around like a “free-spirited European woman” in a land full of surprises. It felt bold, it felt liberating—and yes, I got lost more times than I care to admit. 
But here’s the thing: Saudi Arabia left an impression. It’s not just a country of change; it’s a country of big change. And nothing illustrates this better than its real estate market.

Now, let’s talk about Saudi Arabia’s ambitions. The country is moving fast—almost as fast as I was trying to find a parking spot in Riyadh. The population is booming (close to a million new residents each year), and with that comes a massive demand for housing. 

But here’s the kicker: most of this growth is from Saudi nationals, not expatriates. That’s a big difference compared to Dubai, where expats practically drive the market.
So, Saudi developers aren’t just thinking about skyscrapers; they’re figuring out how to meet the needs of growing Saudi families, all while pursuing luxury projects like The Line and the Red Sea Project.

Speaking of the Red Sea Project, let’s pause for a moment to marvel. This is no ordinary development—it’s practically a vacation dream machine. The first resorts, like Six Senses Southern Dunes, are already welcoming guests. By next year, the St. Regis Red Sea Resort and Ritz-Carlton Reserve will join the party. 

And by 2025, Shura Island will roll out 11 luxury resorts, a golf course (for when sand dunes aren’t enough), and a lineup of entertainment hubs. 

But here’s my question: are these projects sustainable, or are they like the desert’s mirage—exciting from a distance but tricky to navigate up close?

Now, let’s talk about investing. 
If you’re thinking, “This sounds great! Where can I buy something?”—slow down, my friend. 
Only a handful of projects are available for expatriates, like those from Dar Global, which, by the way, pays commissions to agents. The minimum investment in the Pre-launch stage of the Villa project starts from SAR 3,8 mln.
And there is a very vague understanding of how this will become your freehold property - as the law does not yet exist.

But here’s where the drama unfolds: most developers aren’t paying commissions because they don’t have to. Their properties sell themselves. So, where does that leave agents and international buyers? Well, let’s just say it’s complicated.

And while we’re on complications, let’s not ignore the infrastructure challenges. Sure, the new projects are shiny and innovative, but the existing engineering and transportation systems are…well, let’s call them a “work in progress.” 
But here’s the big question for you: Would you invest in Saudi Arabia right now?
