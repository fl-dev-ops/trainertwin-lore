If You Yourself Believe in the Objection, You Will Never Be Able to Overcome It...

Be honest—how often have you caught yourself agreeing with a client’s objection? Consciously or unconsciously?

If a buyer says, “This project is too expensive,” and deep down, you believe that’s true, you’re not going to be able to handle this objection.

An agent who personally wouldn’t spend more than $50,000 struggles to justify a multimillion-dollar deal. Because in that moment, the client is selling their belief to the agent, not the other way around.

‘Too Expensive’ Means Nothing—Until You Make the Client Define It

Most agents don’t even question what “too expensive” actually means. And that’s why they lose deals.

It could mean:

“I can’t pay today—I need time to arrange my finances.”

“For this price, I expect more features.”

“I’ve seen better deals elsewhere.”


Without clarifying the real meaning, you’re negotiating against a ghost.

Reprogramming the Way You See Objections

What if you stopped seeing objections as something to “handle” and started seeing them as concerns the client simply needs help overcoming?

Instead of freezing or starting to box the client into your point of view, parading market reports and proving your point—which, let’s be honest, usually just leads to an argument and makes the objection even stronger—what if you did something different?

That’s exactly what I do with my private coaching clients—I help them reprogram their mindset so they no longer feel stuck when facing objections. Once they shift their belief system, they see objections for what they really are: a signal that the client hasn’t yet understood how this deal fits their needs.

Most agents think objection handling is about techniques. It’s not. It’s about positioning, mindset, and the way you frame reality—not just for your client, but for yourself first.

Some agents get it, others don’t. The ones who do? They close deals that others let slip through their fingers.

And now, the question is—which type of agent are you?
