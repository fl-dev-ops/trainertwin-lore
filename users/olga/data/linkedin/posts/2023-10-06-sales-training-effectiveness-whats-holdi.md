Sales Training Effectiveness: What's Holding It Back?

"Training is something we did". 

My interaction with sales managers during a company audit is always about the lack of skills and knowledge of their sales agents. Up until this point, just a few conversations were different and pointed to a training need for managers as well.

Sales leadership in the real estate industry just does NOT consider that "Training is something they need."

But does that mean that only your own sales experience and ability to sell should grant you a management position?

Many great sales managers have simply figured out how to navigate the process and use their personality to sell. Unfortunately, this cannot be taught to the team.

If your sales manager is only bragging about how great he was in sales himself but, when asked directly for a step-by-step strategy, gives you a vague answer like "Just ask open-ended questions," I would not expect consistency in the team's sales performance.

Sales trainers in such situations are often viewed similarly to medical doctors. Sales performance is unpredictable and inconsistent, so training, like a painkiller, should fix it.

Does Panadol cure the pain, or does it provide temporary relief? The same applies to sales training, where the root cause of poor results often lies in a weak sales process and a lack of skills at the management level.

I got that off my chest.

What do you think about skills training for managers?
