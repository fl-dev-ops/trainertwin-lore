Of course, you want to detach from the sale. 

But let’s be real—when you're working in real estate and it’s commission-only, it feels like every deal matters. 

And when you know other agents are out there, pushing hard to close the same client, the pressure can be intense.

I used to think, “If I detach from the sale, what if another agent swoops in, pushes harder, and wins the deal?” It’s that fear that keeps you holding on too tightly.
But here’s what I’ve learned: detachment doesn’t mean you stop caring or that you don’t work hard for the sale. It means you stop letting the outcome control your emotions and how you show up.

Instead of pushing, proving, and trying to force a sale, you shift your focus to understanding the client, asking the right questions, and genuinely helping them find the best solution—even if that means letting go of the need to close right then and there.

And here’s where it gets interesting—by focusing on serving the client instead of clinging to the sale, you actually become more appealing. They can feel that you’re not just after their money, but truly invested in their goals.
In real estate, we’re often dealing with clients who are speaking to multiple agents. But the one who wins their trust is rarely the one who pushes the hardest. It’s the one who listens the best, who offers real value, and who helps them feel confident in their decision—without the pressure.
So, yes, other agents might be pushing to close that same deal. But by detaching from the need to control the outcome and focusing on creating trust, you’re playing the long game. 

And in the long game, the agent who serves best is the one who stands out.
