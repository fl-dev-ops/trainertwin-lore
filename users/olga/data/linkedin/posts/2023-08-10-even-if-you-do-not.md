Even if you do not know this terminology, you are definitely using it in your sales pitches. Moreover, you are yourself influenced by it in your daily life.

In this article, I want to be more specific on how you can combine it to help guide your clients to make buying decisions FASTER.

I will start with a sprinkling of science.

Loss Aversion - In the field of behavioural decision-making, “loss aversion” is a behavioural phenomenon in which individuals show a higher sensitivity to potential losses than to gains. Conversely, “risk averse” individuals have an enhanced sensitivity/aversion to options with uncertain consequences.

FOMO - This is simply the fear of missing out that can be described as the anxiety people feel when they believe that others are having a rewarding experience without them. In the context of finance, FOMO arises when people feel the pressure to invest in an asset or a stock because they fear missing out on potential gains.

Can you see the difference now?

In real estate sales, an example of using FOMO can look like this: 
“Mr. Client, we have limited developer stock and inventory is open to multiple companies at the same time. While we are talking about this particular unit, it may not be available already.”

With loss aversion (it’s very easy to make a mistake in this word and the whole conversation will slide into a different direction) you can communicate this by sharing success stories of other investors who have navigated market fluctuations and experienced positive returns on their real estate investments. 

Real-life examples can offer reassurance and instil confidence in the investor's decision-making. Incorporate numbers from Property Monitor or DXBinteract if you don't have your own success stories yet.

These strategies focus on influencing customers' emotional responses and perceptions, rather than specific techniques like negotiation tactics or pricing strategies. They should be used with one key ingredient.
If you just communicate it directly, you will come across as pushy and salesy - which immediately triggers the client's sales resistance and vanishes the trust you’ve been building from the beginning.
