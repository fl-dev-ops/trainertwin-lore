## Content vs. Self-promotion

**Product:** knowledge · **Type:** claim · **Source support:** not_reviewed
**Publication:** 2026-08-20T05:29:35.614000Z · **Author:** olgasi · **Format:** post

### Summary

- Publishing content that focuses on self-promotion can lead to readers feeling a "who do you think you are" reaction, as nothing is offered to them besides claims about the author's achievements.
  - “If it sounds like "hi, I've been in Dubai for four years, here's me flying business class, here's me closing multiple deals" - that is exactly what creates the "who do you think you are" feeling in your reader.” — [lines 10-59 part 2](../../../data/linkedin/posts/2026-08-20-whom-do-you-think-you.md) (`linkedin-posts-2026-08-20-whom-do-you-think-you-md-b3ffd89a57:u000002`)

### Original context

**authored text by olgasi** · lines 10-59 · authored_text

> might not have a content problem. You might have a self-promotion problem.
> 
> Look at what you've been publishing. If it sounds like "hi, I've been in Dubai for four years, here's me flying business class, here's me closing multiple deals" - that is exactly what creates the "who do you think you are" feeling in your reader. You've given them two options: believe your claim about yourself, or scroll.
> 
> Most people scroll. Not because they don't like you. Because nothing was offered to them. Something was only claimed about you.
> 
> Self-promotion is the asks for attention. 
> 
> So what you Should do instead?
> 
> Idea promotion!
> 
> It creates value the only thing that people would actually like and share.
