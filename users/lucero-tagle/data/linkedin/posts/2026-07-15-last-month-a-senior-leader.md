Last month, a senior leader walked into his communication coaching session convinced his problem was his slides.

Too much data. Not enough polish. He wanted a deck fix.

Twenty minutes in, our coach found the real issue. It wasn't his slides. It was everything happening around them.

We've coached hundreds of leaders through this exact pattern. Different industries, different titles, same blind spots.

Here's what most people miss about public speaking:

1/ 🚪 Entering the room
↳ The first 10 seconds happen before you say a word.

2/ 🖼️ Creating slides 
↳ Your slides should survive without you. If they can't, you're reading, not presenting.

3/ ⚡ Tech issues 
↳ How you handle chaos is a bigger presentation than the one you planned.

4/ 📊 Presenting data 
↳ Nobody remembers your numbers. They remember how the numbers made them feel.

5/ 🤲 Managing your hands 
↳ Your body talks first. Your mouth just catches up.

6/ 🎯 Starting your presentation 
↳ The first line decides if they lean in or check their phone.

7/ 💬 Answering questions
 ↳ The best answers sound like thinking, not reciting.

8/ 🔑 Delivering your key point 
↳ Repetition doesn't build clarity. Silence does.

By the end of his session, the senior leader wasn't fixing his slides anymore. He was fixing the 30 minutes around them.

Which one of these are you getting stuck on?
