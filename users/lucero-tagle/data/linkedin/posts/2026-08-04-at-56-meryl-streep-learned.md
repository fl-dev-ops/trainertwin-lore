At 56, Meryl Streep learned a negotiation lesson many people never do.

When she was offered The Devil Wears Prada, she didn't take the first number.

She asked for double.
They said yes immediately.

She later admitted it surprised her. She'd assumed they needed her more than she needed them. It turns out she was right.

That moment says something most of us need to hear.

We spend years getting better at our jobs. Learning, delivering and taking on more responsibility.

But when it's time to talk about pay, many of us negotiate from fear instead of value. 

Our coaches hear this constantly in Negotiation Ninja, our coaching program built around exactly this. Clients worried about seeming difficult. Not wanting to ask for "too much." So they take the first offer and hope the work speaks for itself.

Here's the problem, your work doesn't speak. You do.

Before your next salary conversation, ask yourself:

→ What unique problems do I solve?
→ What measurable results have I delivered?
→ What is the real cost to replace me?
→ What am I leaving on the table if I stay silent?

Negotiation is knowing your value and having the nerve to say it out loud.

Sometimes the biggest raise starts with one word: no.

What's the best negotiation lesson you've ever learned?
