You can give a polished presentation and still leave the room with nothing changed.

I often see this with leaders. They’ve practised the slides, tightened the wording and worked on their delivery. Then the meeting ends and everyone says, “Great presentation.”

But no one makes the decision. No one starts the conversation they were avoiding. No one does anything differently.

When I asked our PitchNinjas community what makes someone a great public speaker, the answers kept coming back to something simpler: give people a reason to care, and make it easy for them to remember your point.

It reminded me of a lecturer I had at uni. I didn’t particularly like him, and I hated the subject. But I loved his lectures.

He always told us about a real person or something that had happened in the industry. 
Years later, I can still remember some of those stories. I can’t remember a single slide.

Of course, you need to know your subject. That’s what gives you the confidence to look up from your notes, speak to the people in front of you and notice when you’re losing them.

So before your next presentation, ask yourself what you want to happen after you stop speaking.

🧠 Do you want people to think differently?
❤️ Do you want them to feel something?
👣 What do you want them to do?

A room saying “well done” feels good. A room that acts on what you said is better.

____
🔖 Save this for the next time you’re preparing a talk. Before you open your slides, ask yourself: what do I want people to think, feel and do when I’m done?
