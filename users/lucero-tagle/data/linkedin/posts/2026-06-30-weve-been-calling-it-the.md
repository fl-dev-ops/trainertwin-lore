We've been calling it the wrong thing.

"Imposter syndrome."

A syndrome is a disease. A defect. Something to cure.

But that knot in your stomach before the big meeting? That's not a defect.

Steven Bartlett reframed it recently and it stuck with me: it's not imposter syndrome. It's growth syndrome.

It's the feeling of being stretched into a room you haven't earned the comfort of yet.

But when nobody tells you that feeling is normal, you start hiding it.
You sit in the meeting. Someone drops an acronym you don't know. A framework you've never heard of.

And you nod.
↳ You fake the understanding to protect the image
↳ The image costs you the clarity
↳ The missing clarity quietly stalls the whole strategy

We've built workplaces where looking smart beats getting it right.
But the strongest leaders we've coached across Estee Lauder, Netflix, PwC do the opposite.

They've stopped performing "expert."
They've traded it for something harder: permanent student.

A student leader has the confidence to stop a room full of executives and ask the simple question: "Wait, what does that actually mean for us?"

So who's the smart one? The one nodding along to save face, or the one secure enough to ask, get clarity, and move the business forward?

This is the work. We help leaders put down the exhausting armour of knowing everything and build the presence to lead with clarity instead.

If you're building a culture where leaders communicate with clarity instead of jargon, that's a conversation worth having.
