I've noticed something about leaders who say they want honesty.

Then someone challenges them in a meeting, and their face says otherwise.

I spoke with Pooja Lalwani on the #BareLead podcast about the small things leaders do that make people stop telling them the truth.

A defensive reply, an impatient look, cutting someone off. Asking for honest feedback, then explaining why it’s wrong.

People notice these moments. They quickly learn what is safe to say and what is better kept to themselves.

We also talked about why leaders don't need to have all the answers, why clarity is often kinder than softening a hard message, and what happens when sounding polished becomes more important than being understood.

And of course, we talked about AI.

When anyone can produce a perfect looking email or presentation in seconds, perfect words matter less. Your judgement, courage and ability to sound like yourself matter more.

Thank you, Pooja, for a genuinely thoughtful conversation and for asking the questions that leadership discussions often avoid.
