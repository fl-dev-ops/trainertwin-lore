I never got to meet my grandparents, on either side.

I don't know what that feels like. The spoiling, the advising you or being a favorite.

My children have 3 grandparents.

We don't live in the same cities. One set speaks Spanish, the other French. No English at all.

And honestly, that's gold.

My kids speak English most of the time. But with their grandparents, they have to work for it and I love it.

They speak in Spanish. They speak in French. They point, they act things out, they make themselves understood.

They don't know it yet, but that's one of the most important lessons in communication, and it's so easy to miss.

You don't need the same words to connect. 

Since I never had this myself, I'm intentional about it with my kids. I'm like a broken record everytime we visit them and tell them constantly to:

→ Ask curious questions
→ Sit down beside them, just because
→ Help them with small things
→ Walk with them
→ Open the door for them
→ Go have lunch, just you with them

Kids don't learn this at school. They learn it by being close to the people who love them.

Distance and language were never the real barrier. I used to think they were because we don't live in the same city.

I'm making it a point to protect that time now, giving my children what I never had because grandparents are an infinite source of wisdom and time is precious.
