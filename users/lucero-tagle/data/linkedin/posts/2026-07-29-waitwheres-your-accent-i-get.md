"Wait...where's your accent?"

I get this a lot when people find out I speak Spanish.

There’s usually a brief look of confusion, as if speaking fluently in another language without a heavy accent means I’m somehow "hiding" where I came from, or that I must have scrubbed my identity to fit in.

It made me realise how flawed our assumptions about accents really are.

Gloria Pritchett said it best in Modern Family: "Do you even know how smart I am in Spanish?"

Too often, we put people in boxes based on how they sound:

↳ If you have an accent, people make assumptions about your authority or fluency.
↳ If you don't have an accent, people assume you’ve lost your connection to your roots.

Both views miss the point entirely.

When you speak multiple languages, you are constantly translating your tone, humor, and personality across different cultural contexts. 

Whether your accent is thick, subtle, or unnoticeable, it doesn't define your intelligence, your identity, or your capability.

In global communication, the goal was never "accent elimination" OR "accent camouflage."

The goal is clarity, connection, and authenticity.

At PitchNinjas, when we coach founders and leaders across the world, we don't care about fitting into a narrow, artificial standard of how a speaker "should" sound. 

We focus on helping you own your story with conviction.

Whether your accent is loud and proud, or completely seamless, stop managing how you're perceived. 

Just own it.

Has anyone ever made an assumption about you based on how you sound (or don't sound)?
