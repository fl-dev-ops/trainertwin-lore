Early in my career at Google, I thought being a good leader meant having the answers.

Fast forward through 16 years there, managing teams through growth, chaos, and everything in between, I learned the opposite was true.

The best leaders I worked for didn't have all the answers. They just made it safe to look for them.

That's the whole game.

Not perfection, not control, just the right words, said consistently, that tell your team: it's safe to speak up here.

I brought that lesson with me when I co-founded PitchNinjas.

→ "What ideas do you have?" instead of assigning the task myself 
→ "I'm here to listen" instead of defending my first reaction 
→ "You've got this" instead of taking over

Leadership isn't a personality. It's a set of habits and habits start with language.

Which one of these do you say the least and could start saying tomorrow?

👇 12 phrases, one circle, and honestly a good chunk of what I wish someone had handed me at 26.
