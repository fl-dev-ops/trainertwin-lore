For a long time, I thought the best speakers just had something I didn't.

I'd watch people like Steve Jobs or Barack Obama command a room and think: some people are just born good at this.

Then I started paying closer attention to what they said and how they said it.

Turns out it's not talent. It's a handful of things you can actually learn and practise.

It's something we think about a lot at PitchNinjas.

Look at the 8 communicators in the graphic. Each does something different. But there's psychology behind why it works.

1/  Steve Jobs ⏸️ : Pause 
↳ Silence shows confidence. When we're nervous, we tend to rush. A well timed pause says: I'm comfortable enough to let you wait.

2/  Simon Sinek 🎯 : Start with why 
↳ Facts help people understand. Emotion gives them a reason to care. Start with why it matters before getting into the detail.

3/  Barack Obama 📐 : Use the rule of three 
↳ Three just works. It gives your message rhythm, makes it feel complete and helps people remember it.

4/  Tony Robbins ⚡: Use your body 
↳ Energy is contagious. If you look flat, you'll struggle to keep people engaged. How you show up physically changes how people experience what you say.

5/  Jordan Peterson ⚖️ : Create contrast 
↳ We understand things better when we have something to compare them with. Before vs after. This vs that. What happens if we act vs if we don't.

6/  Mel Robbins 🪞 : Make people feel understood 
↳ Before you try to change someone's mind, show them you understand where they're coming from. People are far more open to listening when they feel heard.

7/  Jim Rohn ✂️ : Keep it simple 
↳ Knowing your subject doesn't mean telling people everything you know. The real skill is making something complicated easy to understand.

8/  Dale Carnegie 🤝 : Make it about them 
↳ The best communicators don't spend the whole conversation proving how clever they are. They make the other person feel important.

None of these require a "magic gene."

They're small habits.

And small habits can be practised.
