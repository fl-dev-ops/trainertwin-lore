Very few people talk about what's actually happening in your body when you get nervous before speaking.

Your heart rate spikes, your palms go damp, your breathing gets shallow and fast without you noticing.

Most people don't talk about it. They push it down, or pretend it isn't there but wonder:

What if I go bright red?
What if I forget everything the second I start speaking?
What if my voice cracks before I get the first sentence out?

If you think about it, your body is doing exactly what it's designed to do under pressure, the fight or flight response. 

Nerves don't vanish but you can stop them from taking over.
Nervous energy isn’t something you can usually think your way out of. You have to give it somewhere to go.

A few things help:
🧠 Rewrite the story you’re telling yourself before you walk in.
📝 After every talk, write down what actually worked.
🎙️ Don’t walk in cold. Give your head, body and voice five minutes to catch up.
🔁 Prepare until it feels almost boring. Certainty leaves less room for panic.
🤝 Talk with your audience, not at them.

This is one of the areas I personally like to focus on and talk about most.

Managing nervous energy is a practice, it can be done and it’s one you can start today.
