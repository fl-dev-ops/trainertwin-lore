A founder told me her pitch needed more confidence.

I told her it needed more risk.

She looked at me as if I’d said something slightly offensive which was fair.

But I think we often get storytelling coaching wrong.

People assume it's about sounding more polished, charming, or relatable. Often it's about helping founders say the part they don't want to say like the part where something was genuinely at stake.

This founder wanted to open with traction. The numbers were strong. But the story that made the room sit up was the moment she almost shut the company down. 

Three weeks before her Series A closed, she'd been ready to make the call. She hadn't told her investors and she'd barely told herself.

That was the moment everything changed not just for her, but for the pitch. Suddenly the room understood what she had risked, what she had learned, and why the company mattered so much to her.

After coaching more than 200 founders, these are the seven storytelling principles we keep coming back to:

1/ Start with tension ⚡
↳ What almost stopped you?

2/ Make it human 🫶
↳ When did you seriously doubt it would work?

3/ Speak in images 🎬
↳ What did that moment actually look and feel like?

4/ Make it about them 🪞
↳ Where does your story meet the listener’s world?

5/ Show the stakes 🎯
↳ What could have been lost?

6/ Close the loop 🔄
↳ What changed because you kept going?

7/ Leave a decision 👉
↳ What do you want your audience to do next?

Most communication advice tells you to sound more confident.
Sometimes, the more powerful choice is to be honest about the moment you weren't.

That's often when the room starts listening.

What's the part of your story you've polished out?
