Founders, if you only have three minutes to pitch, you cannot spend the first minute warming up.

On 23 September, the founders I'll be working with from the SPARK 2.0 program at King's College, Cambridge E-Lab will each deliver a three-minute pitch at Demo Day, hosted at Balderton's London office.

One of the first things we'll work on is the opening.

Investors might hear dozens of pitches in a day. By the time you stand up, they may already be tired, distracted, or still thinking about the founder who went before you.

Your opening has one job: make them want to keep listening.

You could:
↳ Ask a question that makes them stop and think
↳ Share a statistic that's hard to ignore
↳ Tell a short story that makes the problem feel real
↳ Make a bold claim that challenges an assumption
↳ Show a visual they will remember
↳ Get straight to the problem your business exists to solve

What I don't recommend is a hook simply stating your name and what your company does.

You don’t have to explain everything in the opening.
You just have to earn the next 20 seconds.

I’m looking forward to getting started with the SPARK 2.0 cohort and helping these founders make every second of their three-minute pitches count.
