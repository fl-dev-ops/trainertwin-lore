I used to think listening meant sitting quietly and nodding.

I was wrong. And honestly, I still catch myself doing it.

Most people think listening is a single step. It’s actually eight:

↳ HEAR - sound hits your ears.
↳ ATTENTION - you choose to focus (this is where most people stop).
↳ UNDERSTAND - the words actually land.
↳ REMEMBER - you keep it past the next five minutes.
↳ ANALYZE - what is actually going on here?
↳ EVALUATE - do I believe this? does it matter?
↳ EMPATHIZE - what does this feel like for them?
↳ RESPOND - proof any of the above actually happened.

Two steps. That's where most conversations die. 

People hear you, they nod, they're already mentally drafting their next sentence. I used to think that was fine. It's not. It's just efficient, and efficient isn't the same as present.

I catch myself doing it in my own team meetings. Someone is talking and I'm three moves ahead, already solving the issue. Which feels like listening. It's not.

The leaders we coach who actually get through to people are willing to sit in the pause a bit longer than feels natural. Genuinely uncomfortable sometimes. 

That’s half of what our coaches work on at PitchNinjas, not what to say next, but resisting the urge to say it too soon.

Be honest, where does your spiral usually stop?
