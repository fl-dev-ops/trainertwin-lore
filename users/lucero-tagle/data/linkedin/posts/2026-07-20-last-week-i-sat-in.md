Last week I sat in on a pitch rehearsal.

The founder had ninety seconds. She used four minutes.

Great idea. Strong numbers. But by minute two, I'd lost the thread, buried under context, jargon, and a slow crawl toward the actual point.

Afterward I asked her one question: "What's the one sentence you needed me to remember?"

She paused. Then said it perfectly, in a couple of sentences. 

That was the pitch. Everything else was noise.

Most people think communication is about saying more, explaining more, covering every angle so nothing gets misunderstood.

It's not. It's a filtering problem.

We over explain when we're nervous. 

Presence isn't about how much you can say, it's about how much you're willing to cut.

If you want your words to land, here are 10 rules worth adopting:

1️⃣ Skip the warm-up. 
↳ Nobody needs three sentences of throat clearing before your point, lead with it.

2️⃣ Stop when you've landed it. 
↳ The moment you've made your point, close your mouth. Talking past it dilutes it.

3️⃣ Choose your words wisely. 
↳Every extra one taxes your listener's attention.

4️⃣ Answer what was actually asked. 
↳ Not what you wish they'd asked. Give them the direct hit first.

5️⃣ Retire the corporate filler. 
↳ "Circle back," "synergistic alignment," nobody's impressed, they're just tuning out.

6️⃣ Trust the full stop. 
↳ A clean period carries more authority than a sentence that trails off.

7️⃣ Pick one takeaway. 
↳ If they remember nothing else from this meeting, decide right now what that one thing is.

8️⃣ Speak in pictures. 
↳ A sharp analogy beats a paragraph of abstraction every time.

9️⃣ Listen more than you plan. 
↳ The best communicators aren't rehearsing their next line, they're reading the room.

🔟 Let the pause sit. 
↳ Say the hard thing, then stop. Silence is authority.

Next time you open your mouth, ask yourself: am I adding value, or just filling air?

Which of these is hardest for you to actually do?
