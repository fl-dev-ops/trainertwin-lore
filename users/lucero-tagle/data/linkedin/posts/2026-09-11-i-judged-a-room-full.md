I judged a room full of founders this morning.
 
They had three minutes each. Three minutes to explain what they had built. Three minutes to make the room care. Three minutes to earn the next conversation.
 
This was the #SPARK 2.0 Incubator E-Lab programme pitch practice, supported by King's College, Cambridge and Innovate Cambridge, and it was a tough format.
 
A good deck helped, but it couldn’t rescue a pitch that wandered. When every minute counts, you could feel the room drift the moment a sentence stopped pulling its weight.
 
The founders who stood out could say their business proposition plainly. They had conviction, but they also had the substance to back it up.
 
That’s what a strong pitch does. It doesn’t squeeze in everything you know.
 
It makes people care about what they need to know.
 
We see this whenever we coach founders through Pitch for Success at PitchNinjas: clarity earns attention, conviction earns trust and substance earns the next conversation.

Nearly 200 founders from the University of Cambridge and Anglia Ruskin University ecosystems applied for this year’s one month residential programme. Just 30 ventures joined the 2026 cohort.

The programme culminates in Demo Day on September 23, hosted by Balderton.
