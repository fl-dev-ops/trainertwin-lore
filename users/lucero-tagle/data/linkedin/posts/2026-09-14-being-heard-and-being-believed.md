Being heard and being believed are not the same thing.

Terry Szuplatt knows that better than most.

He spent eight years as one of President Barack Obama’s longest-serving speechwriters, helping him find the words for some of the biggest moments of his presidency.

So when Terry joined us on the PitchNinjas podcast, I wanted to ask him something simple:

What makes people believe you?
His answer: People may hear your words, understand your argument and even agree with your facts. But that doesn’t mean they believe you.

Belief comes when your words sound like they belong to you, when there is conviction behind them and when people can feel that you mean what you say.

That’s why good communication isn’t about sounding clever or perfectly polished.
It’s about clarity, honesty and giving people a reason to trust you.

Being heard might get you a moment of attention. Being believed is what gets people to act.

This was one of my favourite moments from my conversation with Terry on the PitchNinjas podcast.
