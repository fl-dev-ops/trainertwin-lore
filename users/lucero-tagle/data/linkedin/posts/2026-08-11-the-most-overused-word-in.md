The most overused word in the workplace isn't "busy."

It's "Sorry."

Early in my career, I said sorry a lot.

"Sorry to bother you."
"Sorry for asking."
"Sorry, I don't know."

One day, someone I respected pulled me aside. He said,
"Why do you keep apologising?"

I remember looking at him, completely confused.

He continued,
"You haven't done anything wrong."

From that day on, I made a conscious effort to stop apologising for things that didn't need an apology.

And now it's become one of my biggest pet peeves.

Whenever I hear someone say "sorry," I stop them.
I ask one question: "What are you apologising for?"

They're often apologising for asking a question or needing clarification.
For having an opinion or taking up space.

But when you say it a lot (and sometimes people don't realise how much they do it), people start hearing hesitation instead of confidence.

Instead of:
❌ "Sorry I'm late."
Try:
✅ "Thank you for waiting."

Instead of:
❌ "Sorry to bother you."
Try:
✅ "I appreciate your time."

Instead of:
❌ "Sorry I don't know."
Try:
✅ "Let me find out for you."

One thing though, if you've genuinely hurt someone, missed a commitment, or made a mistake that affected someone else, say sorry.

A sincere apology builds trust.

This post isn't about removing "sorry" from your vocabulary.
It's about stopping yourself from apologising simply for existing.

Your words shape how people experience you.
Make them count.
