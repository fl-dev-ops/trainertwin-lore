I caught myself doing it again this week.

Ten minutes on what someone needed to change. Then a rushed "but overall, good job" at the end.

The useful part got buried. They walked away knowing exactly what they had done wrong, with no idea what they should repeat.

I see this constantly in our coaching when clients are preparing to give feedback.

Simon Sinek makes a simple point in the video below: catch people doing something right and say it out loud.

Obvious, right? But most of us are far quicker to spot what's broken than what's working.

This is the part that's pretty powerful: "Give feedback in a way the other person can hear it, not simply in the way you prefer to give it."

That means actually knowing the person in front of you.

→ What motivates them 
→ What makes them defensive 
→ What gives them confidence 
→ What helps them take the next step

I'm still learning this. At work, and even more at home with three kids who all take feedback completely differently. I'm the same parent, with the same intentions but with three very different audiences. 

I’ve become much more conscious of how I give feedback and it isn't leaving someone with a list of things to fix. It's helping them see what they’re already capable of and how to build on it.
