Steve Jobs was asked a question and waited almost 20 seconds before answering.

I watched the clip 👇  and found myself counting.

Twenty seconds is a long time when a room full of people is waiting for you to speak.

Most of us would panic and start filling the silence.
We’d repeat the question. Say, “That’s a great question.” Or begin talking before we’d worked out what we actually wanted to say.

Jobs just stood there and thought. He didn’t seem worried about the silence or what anyone might be thinking.
And when he finally spoke, everyone was listening.

This comes up all the time in our coaching at PitchNinjas.
People think they need to answer immediately to appear confident.

You don’t.

Taking a few seconds gives you time to shape your answer rather than saying the first thing that comes into your head.

It can also help you cut out the ums, unnecessary words and answers that go on for far too long.

You don’t need to start with 20 seconds.

Try three.

The next time someone asks you a question:
↳ Breathe and give yourself a moment before answering.
↳ If that feels uncomfortable, take a sip of water while you gather your thoughts.

A better answer is worth waiting a few seconds for.

And remember: silence always feels longer to the person speaking than it does to everyone else.
