My 14 year old has this move where she doesn't say anything. She just watches you.

We were having dinner, phone on the table like always, and a notification came through. I glanced at it. Then I picked it up.

She didn't say a word. Just stared at me until I looked up.
"Seriously?"

That's it. One word, full eye roll, the works.

I said something like "sorry, one sec" which, if you have a teenager, you already know is the wrong answer. 

She pushed her chair back. "It's fine, I wasn't saying anything important anyway."

She was being dramatic about it, obviously. She's fourteen, drama is the whole operating system. But she also wasn't wrong.

I picked up the phone the second it lit up. Didn't even think about it. That's the part that got me, how automatic it was.

So now the rule in our house is the phone doesn't come to the table at all. Not face down, not on silent, not there.

Small thing. But she noticed within a day, and so did I,  how often I used to reach for it without even registering I was doing it.

Jefferson Fisher said at the end of the this video: "if you want to be a better communicator, you have to understand what true connection actually means. It's keeping the line taut."
