I watched a clip of Bombas CEO Jason LaRose describing how he takes the edge off his communication.

He uses corporate speak on purpose.

"That's a bad idea" became:
"Talk me through your thought process."

"You're wrong" became:
"Maybe we can look at another option."

"I don't have time for this" became:
"What can I take off your plate?"

It's funny because it's true. And honestly, it's a useful skill.

At first I thought it meant I was being fake. Like I was sanding down what I really meant until it sounded harmless.

But good communication isn't saying every thought exactly as it shows up in your head.
It's pausing for half a second and asking:

Will this move the conversation forward, or will it make the other person shut down?

→ Too blunt, and people get defensive.
→ Too polished, and nobody knows what you actually mean.

The point isn't to bury the truth in corporate fluff.
It's to say the hard thing in a way someone can actually hear.
Not softer. Just better aimed.

Which corporate phrase do you use the most?
Mine is still "let's take this offline and let's park this for now" 😅
