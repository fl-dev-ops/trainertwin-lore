The question I get asked most after sessions, panels, and podcasts isn't about the content.

It's "how do you stay so calm up there?"

Yesterday I did a live interview with Terry Szuplat, Barack Obama's longest-serving speechwriter.

Someone who's spent decades thinking more carefully about words than almost anyone alive. The stakes felt real.

And five minutes before we went live, I was standing in a corridor with my eyes closed, breathing like I was trying to defuse something.

That's the part people don't see.

It starts days before, not minutes.

I go deep on whoever I'm speaking with. Not just what they've said publicly, but what they actually care about.

Then I decide the one or two things I want to leave in the room.
Then I rehearse. Out loud. Alone, which is its own kind of strange.

What makes sense in your head and what comes out of your mouth are often two very different things. And you don't find out which until you hear yourself say it.

The goal isn't to memorise. It's to reach the point where the thinking feels like mine not something I'm trying to hold onto.

Closer to the moment, I breathe. Michelle Flynn taught me a breathwork technique that changed how I approach this. Not elaborate. Just deliberate.
A slow inhale, a longer exhale. I do it until I feel my shoulders drop.
That's when I know it's working.

It sounds almost too simple to mention. But nerves live in the body, you can't talk your way out of them. You have to breathe your way through.

The visualisation starts weeks out.

I close my eyes and walk through it. Not a perfect version. Just a present one.

Where I am. Who I'm with. How I want to sound and feel.
I do it again and again in the days before, so that by the time I walk in, it already feels familiar. 

The confidence people notice isn't something I arrived with.

It's something I built quietly, in the time before anyone was watching.

You can build it too. It starts before the room does.

At PitchNinjas, that's exactly what we help people learn.
