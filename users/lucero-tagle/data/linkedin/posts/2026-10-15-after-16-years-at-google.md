After 16 years at Google and five running PitchNinjas, I keep seeing the same 10 mistakes make hard conversations harder.

I’ve seen them with people I worked with at Google, with our PitchNinjas clients, and I’ve made a few myself.

People go in wanting to help, clear the air or sort something out. Somehow, they leave with a bigger problem.

See if any of these feel familiar:

1/ The compliment sandwich
When you squish criticism between two compliments, people can start questioning the compliments too.
↳ Be clear about what you want to discuss and why it matters.

2/ Venting instead of talking
You’re still angry, and suddenly you’re unloading everything that’s annoyed you for six months.
↳ Give yourself time to cool down. Decide what actually needs addressing.

3/ Guessing at motives
“You obviously don’t care” gives someone something to defend themselves against.
↳ Describe what happened and the effect it had. Ask for their perspective.

4/ Waiting too long
Something small becomes a pattern while you’re waiting for the right moment.
↳ Raise it while the example is still fresh.

5/ Doing most of the talking
You’ve prepared what to say so carefully that you forget to hear their side.
↳ Ask a question. Give them time to answer before jumping back in.

6/ Leaving without agreeing on what happens next
You think you’ve been clear. They leave with a different understanding.
↳ Agree on who will do what, and by when.

7/ Softening it into nothing
You say “maybe”, “a little” and “possibly” until the point disappears.
↳ Say the part you’ve been rehearsing in your head. Plainly and respectfully.

8/ Launching straight in
You’ve been thinking about this for days. They’re hearing it for the first time.
↳ Give them some context: “I’d like to talk about what happened in yesterday’s meeting.”

9/ Letting updates take over the 1:1
You get through the task list and run out of time for the thing you needed to discuss.
↳ Make space for it at the start.

10/ Never coming back to it
One conversation won’t always be enough.
↳ Send a short recap of what you agreed, then check in on how it’s going.

You don’t have to feel completely comfortable to have the conversation. But it helps to know what you need to say, be willing to listen and leave clear on what happens next.

Which one do you catch yourself doing?
