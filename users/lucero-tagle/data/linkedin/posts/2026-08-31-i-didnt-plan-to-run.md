I didn't plan to run through Mexico City this weekend.

My cousin and her husband were running the marathon and invited me to join them for a stretch. Of course I said yes.

I caught up with them around kilometer 23, somewhere between Reforma and the Soumaya Museum.

There’s something about being around family, in a place that holds so much of your history. You remember parts of yourself you don’t think about much in your day to day life.

Before I even had a word like "entrepreneurship," Mexico City was where I learned to hustle.

I grew up watching relatives build businesses from scratch, driven by necessity, by pride, by love for the people who depended on them.

By kilometer 30, my cousin’s husband was running on pure willpower. My cousin was right beside him, with a look that said, “We started this together. We’re finishing it together."

And I was grateful to be there for a small part of it.

That's what made it so magical. A hand on the back, a nod, a ‘you’ve got this’ shouted over the crowd. Small things, but when someone is struggling, they matter.

I spend so much of my working life thinking about what people say and how they say it. But running alongside them, I was reminded how much you can communicate just by staying beside someone.

You don’t always need to know the right thing to say. Sometimes, they just need to know you’re still there.

I hadn’t planned on joining a marathon this weekend. I’m so glad I did.

Gracias, Ciudad de México. And to my cousin and her husband, I’m so proud of you both. ❤️
