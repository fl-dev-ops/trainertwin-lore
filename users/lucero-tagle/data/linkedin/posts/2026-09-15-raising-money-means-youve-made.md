‘Raising money means you’ve made it.’ 
Me: ‘Not if customers still haven’t proven they’ll pay.’

There was one line from the Bootstrappers' Breakfast in Cambridge that stood out for me:

"Are you a business if you're fundraising?"

I left Google with no salary and no safety net to build PitchNinjas. No investors, just clients who paid us because the work was worth paying for.

Fundraising FOMO is real. It’s loud because it’s visible. Funding rounds make headlines. Bootstrapped growth usually doesn’t.

So we start measuring progress against announcements instead of our own numbers and I think that’s the trap.

A few myths I’ve had to unlearn:

❌ Myth: raising money means you’ve made it.
✅ Reality: raising money means you now owe someone else’s money back, with their expectations attached.

❌ Myth: bootstrapped means small.
✅ Reality: bootstrapped means every decision has to earn its place. That teaches you how to survive.

❌ Myth: you need capital to move fast.
✅ Reality: you need paying customers to move fast. Capital just changes who you answer to.

Here's my take: fundraising isn’t a milestone. It’s a tool. And not every business needs the same tool.

If you’re buying market share before anyone else can, raise.
If you’re proving people will pay for what you built, prove it first.

My co-founder and I built PitchNinjas on the second path.
Slower some days but mine, every day.
