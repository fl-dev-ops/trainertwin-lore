This week in London, I sat in a room with two of my favourite Google friends.

Tony McGaharan and Martin Gonzalez

About eight years ago, we were working together on the same project with the Google for Startups team, helping founders build their dreams without completely falling apart in the process. Then, life happened, and we went our separate ways.

This week was the first time we’d all been in the same room in years.

Martin recently co-wrote The Bonfire Moment, a book that gets real about why co-founder teams actually break down. He’s now leading AI org design research at Google DeepMind, which is exactly where a brain like his belongs.

Tony built People Playbook and is now the official delivery partner for Martin's Bonfire Moment workshops. If you’ve ever met Tony, you know he was practically born to facilitate this stuff and make it land.

Listening to Martin talk about the book, a couple of things hit a little too close to home:
→ 😬 The critical, difficult conversations you avoid because you're "too close.
→ ⚡ The classic speed trap: moving so fast you forget to check if the team is actually aligned.

I recognised these because, as a founder myself, I’m navigating some of this right now.

But honestly, as brilliant as the talk was, that's not what stayed with me. The reunion was 🫶 

We don’t work together anymore, maybe one day we will. It's been about eight years, yet we still show up for each other.

At PitchNinjas , I talk constantly about communication as a strategic leadership tool. But sometimes, the most important communication isn't strategic at all.

It’s just staying in touch.
