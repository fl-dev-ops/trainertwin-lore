Ten people looked me in the eye and said no. 

In one day.

We had a booth at a conference, and I spent hours talking to stranger after stranger about PitchNinjas only to watch stranger after stranger walk away.

I won't pretend it didn't hurt.

But rejection doesn't necessarily make you tougher. What it does is teach you to stop one bad interaction from ruining the next conversation.

And this is the skill that matters.

When it's 3 pm, your feet ache, your voice is nearly gone, and there's still someone else to approach, a no can feel deeply personal. 

But most of the time it isn't. Whether you're raising money, closing customers, recruiting top talent, or seeking partners, you're going to hear no a lot.

The real challenge is deciding:

→ Should I keep pushing
→ Should I change my approach
→ Or walk away?

There's a story about Ken Griffin that stuck with me. Early in his career, he flew to Switzerland for a meeting. The person realised he had the wrong guy and walked out.

Later that same day, a banker told him he was a smart kid who had chosen the wrong career.

Two gut punches in a day.

His reaction? Get used to it, this is the job.

Learning to sit with rejection is essential to improving at anything. Don't let the weight of the outcome undermine how well you make the next ask. 

What's a no that ended up teaching you more than any yes could have?
