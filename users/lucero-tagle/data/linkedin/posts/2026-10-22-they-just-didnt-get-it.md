“They just didn’t get it.”

A founder said this to me straight after her investor pitch.

She’d spent hours on the deck. The slides were clear and the numbers made sense but she felt the room wasn't following along.

When we went through it together, she’d explained her business in detail. What was harder to see was why someone would need it.

Our PitchNinjas coaches and I see this all the time. The better you know your subject, the more you want to explain all of it.

But the people listening need a way into it. A real example helps them follow what you’re saying and understand why it matters.

Here’s how we rebuilt her pitch (infographic below):

1. Start late
↳ She opened on a customer’s 11pm phone call, right when things had gone wrong. The company history could wait.

2. Tell it forward
↳ The problem. What the customer tried, what happened next, in that order.

3. Use their exact words
↳ The customer described the problem better than any slide could.

4. Make it about the room
↳ We linked the problem to businesses the investors already knew.

5.  Show what’s at stake
↳ What would the customer lose if nothing changed? Once you can picture the person, the numbers start to mean something.

6. Close the loop
↳ She ended back on that 11pm call, this time with her business in the picture.

7.  Leave a decision
↳ A clear ask: what she needed, and what the money would unlock.

We gave people a way to understand why they mattered.

You don’t need an extraordinary story. Think about a customer you’ve helped or a moment when you saw the problem for yourself.

What happened? What was at stake? What changed?

Say it out loud before you open your slides again. That could be the part your audience needs to hear.
