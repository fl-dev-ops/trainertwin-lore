You’ve read your slides ten times. So why are you still worried you’ll go blank?

You know your subject and you’ve spent hours preparing yet, you’re still thinking:

“What if I forget what I’m saying?”
“What if they ask something I can’t answer?”
“What if I sound like I don’t know enough?”

So you open your deck again and keep tweaking sentences, graphics. Maybe you add another slide and it all feels like preparation. 

But you still haven’t said it out loud.

I know this habit well. I still catch myself doing it.

After 16 years at Google in learning and development, and five years building PitchNinjas, this is something I wish more people gave themselves time for.

Actually saying the words, out loud, before the moment that matters.

Before your next presentation, try this:

🎯 Close your slides and explain your main point. What do you want people to understand?
🗣️ Run through it out loud. Notice where you get stuck or take too long to get to the point.
⏸️ Practise what you’ll do if you lose your place. Pause, look at your notes and come back to what matters.
💭 Visualise yourself giving the presentation. Picture yourself taking your time and getting your point across.

At PitchNinjas, we’ve been giving people space to work through these moments with a coach, using their real pitches, presentations and conversations.

You don’t have to wait until you feel confident to practise.

Practising is part of how you get there.
