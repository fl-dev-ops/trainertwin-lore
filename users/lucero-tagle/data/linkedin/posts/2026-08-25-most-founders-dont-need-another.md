Most founders don’t need another playbook. 

They need enough space to hear themselves think.

Spark 2.0 kicked off yesterday at King's College, Cambridge E-Lab, and I'm joining this cohort as one of the Entrepreneurs in Residence.

I've been thinking about what the role means and it isn't to walk into the room knowing all the answers. 

Yes, I bring eight years of experience as an angel investor. I’ve also experienced the other side as a founder who has navigated fundraising and built a bootstrapped business.

I’ll draw on my time working with Google for Startups at Google, too, as well as everything we’ve learned at PitchNinjas coaching founders through accelerator programmes around the world. Founders we’ve supported have collectively raised more than $500 million.

BUT experience only gets you so far. 

Most of the time, the real work is sitting with a founder while the idea is still messy. The pitch isn't ready. The next step isn't as obvious and the uncertainty is very much in the room. 

And somewhere underneath all of that, they usually know more than they think.

Our job is to listen properly, ask the question they might be avoiding and resist the urge to fill every silence.

I asked Anthony Finbow, an EiR in last year's program, what advice he would give me. "Be malleable," he said.

Standing in the room yesterday, I understood exactly what he meant. 

So, in the next coming month, I’m going to:

→ Show up curious, not certain
→ Ask the questions that challenge their thinking
→ Keep them focused on weekly deliverables 
→ Be a sounding board when things feel messy
→ And, of course, help them nail that three-minute pitch 🎉

The founders will shape this programme. We’re there to help them make the most of it.

I'm grateful to be doing this alongside such a sharp group of EiRs: Anthony Finbow, Maximilian Ge, Ameen Mirjan, MBA, James Risley, Pilgrim Beart and Yunus Akseki.
