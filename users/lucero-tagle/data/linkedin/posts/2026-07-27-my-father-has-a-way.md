My father has a way of saying things that make me want to cut him off mid-sentence.

For years, I did exactly that.

He'd start talking, I'd feel the heat rising, and I'd jump in before he even finished.

It never went well.

I'd shut down. He would get frustrated and we both would walk away annoyed.

Then one day I just...waited. I let him finish and took a breath before saying anything back. 

And still I disagreed with his opinion.

But it landed completely differently and that was because I stopped reacting and started responding.

It reminded me of a story Mohammed Qahtani, World Champion of Public Speaking, tells about his own son.

The first time his son painted on the wall, Qahtani lost it.

"What is wrong with you? Never do that again."

The following week, his son did it again right in front of him.

So the second time, Qahtani tried something different.

He knelt down. Lowered his voice.
"Don't do that. You're a big boy now." And it never happened again.

It was a completely different response and a completely different result.

Lesson learned:
🛡️ Aggressive feedback gives people something to defend against.
🌱 Good communication gives them something to live up to.

We spend a lot of time telling people what they did wrong.

Sometimes the more powerful move is showing them who we believe they can be.

The words you choose don't just change the conversation.
They can change what happens next.

What's a subtle shift in language that changed an outcome for you?
