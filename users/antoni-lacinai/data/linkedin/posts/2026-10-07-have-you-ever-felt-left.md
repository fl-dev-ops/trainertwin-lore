Have you ever felt left out? Not part of the in-group? Alone? It hurts, right? 

Loneliness is “𝗶𝗻𝘃𝗼𝗹𝘂𝗻𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘆 𝘀𝗼𝗰𝗶𝗮𝗹 𝗶𝘀𝗼𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻.” 

Gallup reports that 𝟮𝟬% 𝗼𝗳 𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗼𝘆𝗲𝗲𝘀 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗹 𝗹𝗼𝗻𝗲𝗹𝘆 𝗱𝗮𝗶𝗹𝘆. Research shows that chronic loneliness has the same health risks as smoking 15 cigarettes a day. 

When people feel isolated, the brain’s survival mechanisms activate, leading to stress, lower motivation, mental health issues, and burnout.

In the workplace, this can lead to declining creativity, higher employee turnover, and the loss of the psychological safety employees need to contribute. Many start looking for new jobs, hoping to find a stronger sense of belonging elsewhere. 

Can something be done? 𝗔𝗯𝘀𝗼𝗹𝘂𝘁𝗲𝗹𝘆. 

As leaders, you have both an ethical and business responsibility to reduce loneliness at work. 

Hosting a monthly AW or playing ping pong on Fridays is fun but seldom enough. It’s about building a culture of belonging where employees feel connected and perform as a team. 

So, what if you...:

𝗙𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝗮 𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗲 𝗼𝗳 𝗯𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗴?
Create opportunities for employees to connect, both digitally and in person. Encourage team projects, breakfast meetings, and informal coffee breaks. 

𝗗𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗽 𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗮𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗰 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗵𝗶𝗽?
Leaders who show empathy drive significantly higher employee engagement. Being present, listening, and offering encouragement can make a huge difference.

𝗗𝗲𝘀𝗶𝗴𝗻 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗰𝗲𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻?
Ensure there’s a balance between quiet areas for deep work and spaces that encourage “casual social interaction.” Lounges, meeting zones, and coffee corners all help reduce isolation.

𝗟𝗲𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝗻𝗼 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗱?
Invite the newcomer to lunch. Call your remote colleague. Encourage the quiet ones to join discussions. Show appreciation. Ask for their input. Give them your time.
