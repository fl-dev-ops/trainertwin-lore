If I can have a strong sense of "we" with Nigeria, then surely you can have a strong we-feeling with your colleagues...

A few years ago I got a DNA test that showed where I have my roots. I already knew that my mother is from the old Austria-Hungary empire and that my father is from Albania. But was there anything more? I was curious.

I got my test kit. Two tops went into my mouth and later sent to a lab in the US. A month later I got an email from the supplier with a verdict!

The result:
- I was 51% from the Balkans. No surprises there.
- I was 48.2% from Greece. Oops! This was completely new to me. The cradle of civilization. I saw how my ancestors walked around in toga clothes or farmed the land.
- But then came the most surprising part: I was 0.8% from Nigeria! What? Has anyone seen my straight hair and pale skin? I wondered: Was it a slave who came to Greece, or maybe a rich person who was going there for studies?

I called the supplier and started asking. "How do you know this? How far back is it?" Unfortunately, the answer was disappointing: “Such a small percentage means that your test has become a bit polluted. You have no DNA from Nigeria."

Do I feel different now? Yes a little actually. From now on, I will not only root for Serbia, Croatia, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Sweden in international matches. Now I'm also cheer leading Greece. 

And - during the 2018 FIFA World Cup, Nigeria had qualified, and every time they played, I shouted "Come on, my countrymen!"

I see many workplaces with conflicts and a clear Us-vs-Them feeling. Nowadays I think: If I include Nigeria in my WE/US, even after I found out that we do not share even 0.8% DNA, then colleagues should be able to start talking to each other. Sort out their problems. Get along. Do good things. 

Thank you for reading my mind
