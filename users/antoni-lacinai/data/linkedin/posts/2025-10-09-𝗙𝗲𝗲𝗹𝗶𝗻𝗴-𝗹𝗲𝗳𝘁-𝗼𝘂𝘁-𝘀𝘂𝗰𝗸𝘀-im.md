𝗙𝗲𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗹𝗲𝗳𝘁 𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝘀𝘂𝗰𝗸𝘀!

I’m a member of a great golf club. Friendly people. Perfect course. No excuses for my bad shots. 
Except one: I often feel excluded.

Why? Every event is meat-only. Ham sandwiches for breakfast, hot dogs, and burgers on the course. When I ask for a veggie option, I get 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 look—like I just opened a fridge with something rotten. 

So I grab an empty bun with ketchup and fried onions. At least it gives me something to blame  when my golf balls fly everywhere...

This is a banal example, but exclusion—even in tiny doses—hurts. Science shows that over time, it raises stress and is linked to heart disease, dementia, and depression.

Other examples:
• A friend group that forgets to invite you.
• A lecture to 50 people, but no microphone provided, leaving some behind.
• Remote workers watching office colleagues bond over free pastries—and get promoted faster.

These are all easy fixes. An invite. A mic. A fair chance of that promotion. One more option on the menu. Each builds trust, dignity, and belonging.

Yes, it can feel hard to meet every need. I struggle with a relative’s preferred pronouns (being "it"). The advice I got? Use their name instead. Simple. Respect given. Problem solved.

One action of kindness can create a great feeling of belonging. How's that for leadership?
