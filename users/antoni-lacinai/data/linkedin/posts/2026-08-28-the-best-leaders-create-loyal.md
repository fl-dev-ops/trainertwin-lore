The best leaders create loyal, engaged teams. The worst ones alienate them. And this is a story about the latter:

I remember working in sales for a global IT company when the country manager called for an all-employee meeting. Then he spent one hour yelling at us, telling us that we were useless. 

He said that his boss yelled at him, so he was just passing it on. 

A few weeks later, I quit. The same day, four other colleagues quit too, independently of each other. It seems we weren't all that useless after all, but rather in high demand.

Low engagement is rarely about lazy employees.
It’s about unclear leadership, bad communication, and a lack of trust.

A negative side effect of losing people (apart from the cost of hiring and training new ones)  is that they talk to other people. The brand is at risk. If organizations want to keep their staff, promote leaders who care for their teams. Upskill leaders so they can strengthen the company culture. 

The cliché seems to be true: 
People don’t quit companies – they quit managers.

But also: People stay when their managers care for them and help them grow.
