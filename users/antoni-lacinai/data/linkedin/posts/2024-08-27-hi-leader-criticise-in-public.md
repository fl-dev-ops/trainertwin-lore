Hi leader!
Criticise in public - Not in private

Yes you read it right
It's the opposite of what we are taught

Here is what I mean
If someone is openly being rude, or mean to a colleague, and you have established rules on how you meet, greet, and treat each other - why wait? If you do, the team will think it is ok to behave like that. 

What do you think about that?

> I agree, I like to structure it this way:
> Set rules where you go over, for example, how we behave in meetings/treat each other in the group. These can then be reminded of every quarter within the group.
> If you are in the room when someone breaks the rules, you can bring it up in the room. However, there is a risk that the person might become defensive, so I think it would be beneficial to also address it privately afterward.
> "Antoni, I just wanted to follow up on what happened during the meeting. I know you didn't mean any harm, and I know this isn't something you usually do, so I just wanted to check what happened?"
