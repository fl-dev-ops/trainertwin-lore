If only we knew what we know… START SHARING KNOWLEDGE

I remember attending a conference in Florida many years ago. Out of the 100 attendees, 20 came from the same company as me. I had only recently been recruited and I was a bit naïve, (which still seems to be my default mode). I went around and said to my colleagues: “If you can email me your three most valuable insights from this conference, I will gather, structure all of our findings, and send them out to the organization. I believe more people than us can benefit from the learnings here.” 

Everybody politely agreed. But not one single soul came back to me. Not one. It was frustrating. I went to my boss’s boss and explained the situation. “I have calculated that this conference alone cost us around 50,000 dollars in travel costs and entrance fees. Shouldn’t we share the knowledge with  more people, to at least get a higher return on investment?”
His answer was one of great indifference. He said: “Nah, let’s just call this Personal Development and get on with our chores.”

I wasn’t a line manager back then, but I swore to myself that when I was promoted I would for sure be very positive to every employee who wishes to elevate their knowledge and skills – as long as they shared their insights with the rest of the team. That was my one condition. And so, I did. Not one complained. In fact, they liked that they could bring more value to us all. 

Managing knowledge and finding ways to share it isn’t easy, especially for large organizations. Collective intelligence is great, but without a structured approach, communication can still be poor and hinder us from letting the right person easily find the knowledge they need. Instead, they acquire that knowledge elsewhere to an unnecessary cost because they don’t know what their colleagues knew. 

The best teams share. 
Sharing is caring.
Share!
