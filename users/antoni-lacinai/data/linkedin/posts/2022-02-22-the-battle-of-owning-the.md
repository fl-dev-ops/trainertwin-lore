The battle of owning the narrative
 
From a communication standpoint, I find the scary development at the border (and inside) Ukraine interesting and sad at the same time. It’s a game of thrones where Russia, USA, Nato and EU are trying to communicate warnings, threats, innocence, etc. And sometimes there is a total incongruence between words and actions. Examples:
 
Russia demands that the US (not really Nato) back off from their former Soviet Union territory, (are they doing the move because they perceive the current US president being old and weak? A president who replaced someone they could control?) They placed a huge military force around Ukraine while at the same time repeating how they had no intention of going to war. And then they did. 
 
EU try to balance the unhealthy dependency they have for Russian gas, not upsetting them more than necessary by for instance hindering aid from EU countries to Ukraine, while at the same time saying that the behavior from the big bear is really bad. Equally incongruent.
 
The US don’t want a war with Russia and threatens Russia with severe financial strains if they invade Ukraine. They have communicated to the world that Russia will attack. Why did they do this? Well, when Russia do invade their neighbor, then Russia has proved that they lied all along. If they didn't, then the US can say that the exposure prevented it from happening. The US wins the moral game either way. 
 
Nato tried to reason with Russia saying that they can be buddies and playmates of certain military exercises, as long as they respect every country’s right to choose their own friends. It didn't work.

Meanwhile, Ukraine is the biggest loser, at a war they never wanted, losing eastern territory at minimum.

The communication term Incongruence means that what you say doesn't match what you do. This sad era shows examples of that.
