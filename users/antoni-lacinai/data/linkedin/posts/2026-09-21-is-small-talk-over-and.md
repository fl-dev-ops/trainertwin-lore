Is small talk over? And if so, how does that affect us in the workplace?

I came across this BBC interview where they claimed that 20 years ago, both men and women spoke about 16,000 words per day. Now it is down to about 12,000 words per day. 

The researchers' speculation was that we had less small talk, less chitchat, less "hi there," while we probably increased digital, written communication/information

What does it do to a team if we skip small talk and just focus on information? 

What about "management by walking around and talking to people"? Is that gone?

I believe that this is detrimental to team spirit. Why should anyone care about their colleagues if they don't bond with them? It's probably time to dust off the Johari Window theory, where the smart move is to learn more about each other so you can collaborate better. 

And it starts with your communication. 

I am all for fewer, better words. I get itchy when meaningless transportation words fill the air, and there is no relevance. But that is more for presentations. In conversations and especially incidental interactions, chitchat is worth more than we might think. 

What are your thoughts on this? Is small talk over?
