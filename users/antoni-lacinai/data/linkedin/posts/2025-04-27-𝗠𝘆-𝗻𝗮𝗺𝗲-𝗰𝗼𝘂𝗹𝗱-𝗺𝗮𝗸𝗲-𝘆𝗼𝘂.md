I recently had lunch with an HR director in Stockholm. We started talking about names. Mine—𝘓𝘢𝘤𝘪𝘯𝘢𝘪—often raises eyebrows in Sweden. 

In school, it got me teased (briefly). 
In work life, it made some people hesitant.

Like the manager who skipped my job application because they weren’t sure if I spoke Swedish. They played it “safe.” 

That’s 𝘧𝘭𝘶𝘦𝘯𝘤𝘺 𝘣𝘪𝘢𝘴 in action: our brains trust what's familiar and easy to pronounce. Unfamiliar names feel risky. Kahneman even got a Nobel Prize for showing how that works.

But here’s the thing: I’ve spent my career helping leaders communicate with more empathy, clarity, and energy — and this is a perfect example of why communication matters.

Because when we 𝘥𝘰𝘯’𝘵 examine our bias, we risk missing out on talents. But when we 𝘥𝘰 make the effort to connect, pronounce, and stay curious, we build stronger teams, better results, and way more trust.
