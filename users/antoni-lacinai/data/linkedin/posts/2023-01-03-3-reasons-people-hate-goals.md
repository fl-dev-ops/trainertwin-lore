3 reasons people hate goals

"Oh no, not another manager talking about goals! Not another performance review. I can’t stand it!”

It is interesting to see how many people actually think that goal setting is about as fun as hitting themselves on the thumb with a hammer. Why is it like that?

There are three main reasons:

1: Ignorance

Many people confuse objectives with fluffy strategies (We are to be regarded as innovative), stupid assignments (Each incoming call to be completed in a maximum of one minute), or wishful thinking (I want to lose weight).

If you can answer the question: "How do we know that we have succeeded?" Then have a goal. Otherwise not. A goal has one single criterion: It must be clear.
	
2: incompetence

Especially from managers who have a great need to control paired with minimal flexibility. They like to micromanage their environment and if conditions change, they are unable to adapt.They are bonus-, policy- and rule-based managers. They say: "If we fix the KPI’s, meaning my detailed goals, then I get my bonus even if the goals do not lead to our overall objectives and our vision."

3: Suffering and fear

We are so busy today that everything is about efficiency. But that is one-dimensional thinking. If we forget that we also need energy, engagement, a higher purpose, motivation, and team spirit, we will end up in the same pain as when we go to all those endless PowerPoint meetings we so sadly waste our time in. Add to that our fear of being a failure where we confuse Identity and Activity, and you have a recipe  for "goal setting disgust"

It doesn't have to be like that. Goals can be fun, motivating, inspiring and meaningful. If we do it right.
