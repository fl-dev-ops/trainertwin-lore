𝗔𝗿𝗲 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗡𝗲𝗮𝗿𝘀𝗶𝗴𝗵𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝗼𝗿 𝗩𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗿𝘆? How far can you see?

I've learned that most people can only see three months to a maximum of two years ahead. That’s not very far. It means living “hand-to-mouth” and not having the energy to care about what might happen further down the road. Very few people can see five to ten years ahead, and an even smaller number can visualize a future beyond that.

How about you? What time horizon do you base your decisions on? 

Are you more of a quarterly-report person, or do you focus on long-term sustainable growth? Like the Native Americans of the past. Somewhere around the 1500s, several Native American tribes came together to create peace and unity—both with each other and with the nature they lived in. They called their alliance the Iroquois Confederacy. 

One of the most fascinating aspects of their governance was that they based their decisions on the impact they would have seven generations into the future. Today, this is known as 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗦𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗻 𝗚𝗲𝗻𝗲𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀 𝗣𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗰𝗶𝗽𝗹𝗲. They understood that their actions could have either positive or negative effects that would ripple through time. 

If we assume each generation spans 25 years, they were thinking 175 years ahead! What a contrast to today’s culture of instant gratification.

Imagine that they wanted to increase buffalo hunting to secure more food immediately. That sounds reasonable. But if it meant that their descendants four, five, or seven generations down the line would starve because they had decimated to stock too hard, then it suddenly doesn’t sound so smart. 

As a leader, can you think long-term? Can you make decisions that will benefit your company today and still hold value long after you've retired—or even after you’re dead? 

This is called 𝗚𝗲𝗻𝗲𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗶𝘁𝘆—creating for the future. You develop new leaders, mentor others, establish a culture based on strong values and behaviors (which always outweigh flashy value statements), contribute with goodwill, and leave something better behind. That is a beautiful definition of greatness.

𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗹𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝗮 𝗿𝗼𝗰𝗸 𝗱𝗿𝗼𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗱 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝘄𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗿. 

Some people are merely grains of sand. A rare few are like a large rock (think Mandela, Gandhi, but also some dictators and fascists). The ripples in the water represent the impact you have on others—not just those you meet, but many more beyond them. You are constantly influencing people you will never even meet.

So, are you a grain of sand? Or a large rock? And is the wave you send out good or evil? The choice is yours. If you choose a good path, I’d be happy to help. I’ll be your Sherpa all the way.

• Think really long-term and visionary first. 
  What scenarios do you foresee?
• Then think mid-term.
  What is the desired state? What path will you take?
• Finally, make your decisions. 
  How will you get there?
