Nine takeaways for the customer after a keynote
I am trying out a new gadget called Plaud. (Thanks for the tip Maria Franzoni). It's a digital recorder that transcribes what I say and, with some clever AI, summarizes my main points. I must say, I am impressed. 

Once I am done, I can add extra value and send a short summary packed with my most important messages. Like the list below, after a speech  to a global sales team. 


Top 9 Insights

𝟭. 𝗔𝗰𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄𝗹𝗲𝗱𝗴𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝘀𝗲𝘁𝘀 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗮𝗽𝗮𝗿𝘁. The bar for service is very low. Seeing people, greeting them, and acknowledging them puts you ahead of most.

𝟮. 𝗜𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝗰𝘂𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗺𝗲𝗿 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲. How people meet, greet, and treat each other directly affects how customers are treated.

𝟯. 𝗘𝗺𝗽𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗯𝗲𝗮𝘁𝘀 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝘂𝗮𝗹𝘀. Memorable service comes from people who feel trusted to act, not from scripts and policies.

𝟰. 𝗢𝗻𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗻𝘆 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝘀𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝘁 𝗼𝗻𝗰𝗲. Customers notice when departments operate with different attitudes. The organization must “sing the same song.”

𝟱. 𝗠𝗶𝗻𝗱𝘀𝗲𝘁 𝗱𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗲𝘀 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝘄𝗵𝗶𝗰𝗵 𝗱𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗲𝘀 𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁. The chain is: 𝗠𝗶𝗻𝗱𝘀𝗲𝘁 → 𝗖𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 → 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 → 𝗣𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲 → 𝗜𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗰𝘁 → 𝗦𝘂𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗴𝗿𝗼𝘄𝘁𝗵
Fear can drive performance short term. This chain drives it long-term.

𝟲. 𝗣𝗼𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗽𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝘀 𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲. High-performing teams have far more positive interactions than average teams. A positive mood measurably improves effectiveness.

𝟳. 𝗘𝗺𝗼𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗮𝗴𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗶𝘀 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹. Mood spreads. People carry your energy to others. The key question: 𝗔𝗿𝗲 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝗵𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗶𝗲𝗿 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗼𝗼𝗺, 𝗼𝗿 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝘃𝗲?

𝟴. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗿𝗲𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝘀𝘂𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗽𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝘀
• 𝗘𝗻𝗲𝗿𝗴𝘆 – People believe that you believe (non-verbal impact)
• 𝗘𝗺𝗽𝗮𝘁𝗵𝘆 – People feel understood (listen to understand)
• 𝗖𝗹𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘆 – People understand and remember (simplicity beats complexity)
Empathy is the foundation. If you don’t understand others, you cannot make yourself understood.

𝟵. 𝗖𝗹𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗶𝘀 𝘀𝘂𝗯𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻. People cannot act on what they don’t remember. Stories, metaphors, and the power of three make messages stick. Remove clutter in slides, emails, and language.
