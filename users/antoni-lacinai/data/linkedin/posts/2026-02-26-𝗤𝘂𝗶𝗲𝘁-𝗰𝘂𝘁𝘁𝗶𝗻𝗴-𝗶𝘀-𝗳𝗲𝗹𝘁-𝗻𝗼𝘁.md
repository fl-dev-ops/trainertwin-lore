It can look like this: 
• A senior employee loses key projects without a clear reason. 
• A role retains its title but loses decision-making authority. 
• Development talks disappear. 
• Feedback becomes thin.
• Promotions stall. 

Sometimes this comes from pressure and uncertainty. Other times, it is intentional. Like my favorite horror story about the French CEO who got sentenced to prison for creating a hellish, toxic environment based on Quiet Quitting. He went all-in, to the point where some employees left, not by resigning, but by committing suicide! Yes, it is true.

Sure, Quiet cutting can be a way to reduce headcount without firing. Make the role smaller. Remove the future. Wait. But is it good leadership? No, it is not. 

If you are a leader who practices Quiet Cutting, your team knows what's going on. From the inside, the message is clear, even if it is never spoken. You communicate volumes. What you say and don't say. What you do and don't do...

My advice:
• If a role is changing, say so. 
• If growth is no longer possible, be honest. 
• If you must layoff people, do so. Don't pretend. 

People can handle tough conversations. They disengage when those conversations never happen.
