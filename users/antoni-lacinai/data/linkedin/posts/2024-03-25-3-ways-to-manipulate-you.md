> Interesting perspective as always Antoni. Obviously the complexity and size of the sale matters. 
> 
> These tactics often work in your low value, low sophistication purchases. Those made by a single person in the moment with complete financial authority up to the value of the purchase.
> 
> But when there is a lot of post decision due diligence (around a complex decision), what has to be true is that when the customer and their stakeholders are by themselves, they are convincing each other that this is the best place to invest their limited capital / time / talent. 
> 
> They will only do that if the sales person has really listened and understood their short and longer term ambitions and found a way to help each achieve them, throughout the sales campaign. 
> 
> This is where being open minded (to the customers priorities), honest (about your capabilities), clear about the investment (no gotchas), innovative (to meet diverse needs) and resilient (as internal needs are often not aligned / in sync etc) get you through. 
> 
> The true 'win' then isn't a single sale (objective of 'manipulation'), it's when the customer buys the second time, because you've really added value to them and their agenda.
