If you ever feel awkward when you have to communicate and mingle, here is what I will share with 60 scientists later today that will help them expand their comfort zone. 

Many of us feel uneasy when we are forced to mingle or network. People don't believe me, but I do too. 

One would think that I, the communication expert, would love these situations. I can rock the stage in front of 2000 people, but still feel a bit lost later during the conference when I go to lunch and don't know anyone. I have to put in real effort and I have trained myself to cope. 

Many of my executive clients feel the same, while others love the small settings and completely freeze on stage. I normally help the latter, but today I will focus on the art of small talk. (Did you know we say 25% fewer words today than 20 years ago?)

What I've learned is that my mindset is the key. If I am curious and I listen to understand and to encourage or help, it is so much easier to say hi and ask a few questions.

My favorite question is: "What is the most fun part of what you do?"
That can create wonderful conversations

I could always skip the whole thing and go hide somewhere, but that will get me absolutely nowhere. So I'd rather feel a bit lost at first and get the reward of listening to brilliant people and their stories. And even get new business relationships as a bonus.


PS: What is the number one thing people want to talk about?
Answer: Themselves. But only if people seem interested
