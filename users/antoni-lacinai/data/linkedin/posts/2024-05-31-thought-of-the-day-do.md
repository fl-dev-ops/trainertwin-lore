Thought of the day: Do you hire people based on their ability to contribute...

...or on their ability to commute?

This week I coached a management consultant from the US. Most of her colleagues live around Minneapolis but she resides on the east coast. She got hired many years BC (Before Corona) where the majority of companies still had the "assembly line mentality" of keeping people in the office. 

She was hired based on her ability to contribute. The company understood her value and she has been a valuable employee ever since. 

Then I met another person who worked remotely already in 2006 (you have to understand that there was no broadband at the time, but rather "narrowband" data. Still she managed) The problem wasn't the employer but the municipality. They refused to let her children go to daycare since she was home. 
"But I am working," she said. 
"No you are home so your kids will stay home too," they said

This concept could not be true according to the person in charge. That was simply unheard of. 

If you know me or have seen my TEDx, you know that I cherish analog communication. Still, many organizations have come a long way to realize that not everything has to be done in an office. And not all the time. 

That's what I think. How about you?
