WHAT HABITS WILL YOU HAVE IN THE NEW NORMAL?

In a new uncertain situation, like for instance when we now will work in a hybrid situation, you will use many parts of your brain. Initially for grasping the situation. Then, to navigate in an unknown territory. Finally, you do something good which is rewarded with a nice feeling. A  dopamine high.

But after you've done it a few times, your conscious actions will transform into a programmed unconscious habit. 40 percent of all your actions are habits. The brain shut down most of the other parts to save energy. (Yeah, it’s lazy.) The only part that is now active is called the basal ganglia, whose sole purpose is to keep track of hundreds of small subroutines or programs that are all habits.

Each program is started by a trigger (e.g Time for a hybrid meeting), then goes over to the routine (Designing the meeting to be a virtual meeting) and end with some kind of reward (We did it!).

What is interesting is that many times we know that bad habits are just stupid (like no eye contact with the camera) But - we continue to do it anyway. It is extremely difficult to break a pattern.

So what can you do then, if you want to avoid the bad habit? The author Charles Duhigg has the answer. In his excellent book, The Power of Habit, he gives us three keys
1.     Keep the trigger and the reward but replace the behavior. The program is hard coded so you can not delete it. But you can control your action. If the boss tells you to give a presentation and you want to feel useful; start by finding out the purpose, the objectives, the target audience and the key messages. Use pen and paper. Then practice how to present content without slides. When presenting: Notice how the participants are awake and alert.
2.     Find your desire to change. You must really want something, for it to be worthwhile replacing the bad habit with a new better one. It requires effort, motivation, drive etc. Find your inspiration. Is it appreciation you are looking for? The power to influence? The pride in doing a good job? Find your reason, your purpose.
3.     Believe that you can. Believe it is achievable. If you fall you stand up again. Give yourself a pat on the back every time you succeed. Ask others to do it too and support you. The first time you present without slides maybe it won’t be perfect. Don’t give up. Soon enough, the new behavior will be a habit if you persevere.

Is it that simple? Yes! In theory. It is of course easier said than done. As with most ambitious goals.
What are your good and bad habits? Which ones do you want to keep? Which one will you replace?
