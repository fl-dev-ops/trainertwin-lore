THE RIGHT INTENTIONS DON’T ALWAYS TRANSLATE TO THE RIGHT COMMUNICATION

I remember when I was coaching a manager who wasn’t too pleased with his boss. He explained that he didn’t feel supported by her and that she probably wouldn’t help him develop the way he wanted to grow. I listened, asked some questions, and listened some more. Then I asked: 
“So, what was her response when you approached her with your views?”
“I haven’t talked to her… Do you think I should?”
“Well, how else will you know her opinion and how else can she know what you think?”
“Yeah, hmm, perhaps I should talk to her…”
Shortly after, we ended the session.

Afterward, I felt ambivalent. On the one hand, I really wanted him to succeed and grow. Talking to the manager was for me an obvious next step. But on the other hand, I found myself being very manipulative by approaching the topic like it was obvious that he should do it my way. That is as far from professional coaching as you can imagine. Now, even if I am a certified ICF coach, I seldom do pure coaching according to the defined process of never having any suggestions yourself. My clients hire me because they know I have insights that can help them. They want those insights. But still, I felt I failed in the conversation I just described. 

The right intentions don’t always translate to the right communication. What could I have done instead? Here are three ways:

-	Ask the client to explore options. “What different paths can your take? What are the pros and cons?
-	Ask for permission to share an insight or experience. “I am getting this image/model/process in my head. Can I share it with you?”
-	At least say: “How do you feel about talking to your boss and sharing your worries?”

What are your thoughts on  this? What else would you have done?
