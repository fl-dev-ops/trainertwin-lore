Is it ok to SUCK?
Yes of course it is - initially…

I remember when my son put on ice skates as a young boy. He took three steps on the ice and fell. I held my breath. As a protective parent, you can dread what will happen when your child faces setbacks. But he just laughed, got up, and tried again. And again. And again.

None of us are particularly good when we start doing something, whether it's playing a guitar, riding a horse, or taking on our first management position. 

When I became a manager early in my career, one of my employees was jealous and he began to protest and resist in different ways. He would often say, “Why should I do that?”

It didn’t take long before I, with no formal leadership training, snapped back with, “𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱 𝗱𝗼 𝗶𝘁 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗮𝘂𝘀𝗲 𝗜’𝗺 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗯𝗼𝘀𝘀, 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗜 𝘀𝗮𝘆 𝘀𝗼!!!”

Newsflash: He didn’t get motivated at all—quite the opposite.

Maybe you are working as a CEO or part of a leadership team. Maybe you’ve promoted a new manager? Or hired a young talent with great potential? Help them understand that it’s okay to fail (or fail to succeed) and that they shouldn’t compare their year one to someone else’s year ten. The most important thing is that they want to grow and improve. If they don’t, then you’ve hired the wrong person.

𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗿𝗲𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝘀𝗶𝗴𝗵𝘁𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝘆𝗼𝘂:
• 𝗖𝗔𝗥𝗘 - 𝗯𝘆 𝘀𝗲𝗲𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗼𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗶𝗮𝗹 𝗶𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗼𝘆𝗲𝗲𝘀: attitude, effort, willingness to learn, teamwork, and more. All the good qualities.
• 𝗖𝗮𝗿𝗲 - 𝗯𝘆 𝗱𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗽𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗺 𝘁𝗼𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗶𝗿 𝗽𝗼𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗶𝗮𝗹: Over time and with your help, they will become future masters, and along the way, they will contribute with engagement and motivation.
• 𝗖𝗔𝗥𝗘 - 𝗯𝘆 𝗯𝗲𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗱, 𝗳𝗿𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗱𝗹𝘆, 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗽𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗲𝗻𝘁: Rome wasn’t built in a day, as they say. And people perform better if their boss is a good person rather than the opposite.

Are you curious and willing to try something new? Who knows, maybe you will suck. At first. And that’s okay.
