How easy it is to misunderstand people...

I remember quite a few years back. I was about to give a keynote for  200 of my client’s VIP customers. This was in my birth town and I decided to bring my youngest son. He was about six years old at that time. 
I asked my mother, who had never seen me on stage, if she wanted to tag along and see me perform, while watching her grandson during my lecture. She happily accepted and off we went.

While I was speaking, a lunch was served. Mushroom soup, fresh baked bread and more. After the speech, people approached me to have a chat. It took an hour before we could leave for my mothers home. When she put the keys in the door she said “Well, at least I got some soup.”

I was appalled. “What do you mean at least you got some soup? Didn’t you just see me, your son, on stage for the first time? I have yet to hear a simple Thank you. I am glad you enjoyed the food but was I that bad?” I was furious.
“No, no, no” she said. “That’s not what I meant. It was great to see you. I was only thinking about your son. He didn’t like the food and it is way past lunchtime. I need to fix him some­ flod right away. I’m sorry, that’s all I meant.”

How easy it is to misunderstand people. I guess I was hungry too, acting out like that, but it isn't an excuse. I was ashamed and - yet again - learned a lesson:

Don't assume you know what people mean with what they say. You don't. I don't either.

How do you make sure you're not trapped in a mind-reading exercise?
