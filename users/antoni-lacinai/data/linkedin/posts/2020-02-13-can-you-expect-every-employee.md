### Thomas Otnes (Product developer and engineer)
> Antoni, I agree that point 3 is nonsense. Point 2 should be nonsense, but is harmful greed. And point 1 is just confused.
> 
> Points like these are why employees stop engaging. If the leadership listens and work at honestly explain themselves they will have greater engagement.
> 
> Sadly, human nature leads us to go power mad once we get a leadership position and forget that the same colleagues which had good ideas when we were the same level still have good ideas even after the positions have changed.
> 
> If you want employee engagement, I'd say;
> I. organize the company so it doesn't make great employees feel as if the company is preventing them from doing their job at the best of their abilities.
> II. make policies, processes and routines which make work easier for the employees. This is all to often missed with the result of cumbersome and painful processes etc. to work in for the employees.
> III. Listen! Don't ask to be listened to half as much as you listen to the employees. They have often far more insights into the work than any boss.
> IV. After doing I - III explain your actions to the employees and they will understand you and be more involved in the changes.
> 
> 
> Engagement is communication, not just a monologue!
