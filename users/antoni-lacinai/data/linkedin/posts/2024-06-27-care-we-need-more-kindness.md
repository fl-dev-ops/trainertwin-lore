CARE
We need more kindness in the world
 
Most of us want a work culture where we feel comfortable, are kind, and where we help each other. We want to feel that the boss cares about us, that colleagues say, “good morning,” and that we receive praise when we’ve done something well. 

It’s time to increase the level of consideration and care in the workplace!
 
But why should we care? If nothing else, so for selfish reasons:
 
• We feel better ourselves. 
• We get a high from helping others. 
• We reduce our stress and feel more trust, well-being, and closeness to others.

But more for other people:
• Those we meet and help will also feel better. They feel seen and acknowledged, part of the group which gives them the same positive substances.
• People we don’t meet will also benefit. Emotions create ripple effects. If you become happy after meeting someone, you’ll pass it on to the next person you meet, who will do the same. Win/win/win.

There is more: 
• It also builds resilience and counters all the misery we’re fed from bitter people at work and trolls on social media.
• It’s profitable. Countless studies show that people who feel good are more productive, creative, and engaged and that customers appreciate this. All of this leads to higher profitability.
 
So, how can we show more consideration as a boss, leader, or colleague?
Here are my top 7 tips:
 
1. Give a smile to everyone you meet; it costs nothing.
2. Show appreciation. A simple “THANK YOU" is great. 
3. Do good deeds. They can be a game-changer
4. Give your time to someone who needs it. 
5. Think of those who make you happy
6. Give praise. People love praise.
7. Stop the negative talk.

But is there a risk of being "too kind" and showing too much consideration? No. The problem is not that we’re too kind or show too much consideration. We need much more of it. However, we might need to shift our focus.

• It’s not kind to stay silent when a colleague mistreats someone else. That’s weak. Here, you need to show consideration for the entire group.
  
• It’s also not considerate to yourself to give in during every discussion or agree to things you don’t want to do. Put on your oxygen mask first! Call it healthy sustainable selfishness if you will.
