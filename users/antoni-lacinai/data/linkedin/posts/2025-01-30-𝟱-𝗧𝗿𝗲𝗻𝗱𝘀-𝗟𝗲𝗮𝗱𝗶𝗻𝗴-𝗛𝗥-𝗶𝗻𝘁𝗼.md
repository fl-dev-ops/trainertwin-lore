𝟱 𝗧𝗿𝗲𝗻𝗱𝘀 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗛𝗥 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗙𝘂𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲
And I focus on 2, 3, and 5

I am constantly looking for the future of work trends, both when I am giving keynotes around the world, and from all sorts of international sources. It's mostly fun, sometimes, scary, and I feel I am on this quest. The better we are at meeting, greeting, and treating each other, the bigger chance that we can do good in the world. I often meet HR professionals, and the world of HR and leadership is evolving fast. Here are 5 trends where I believe you can make a difference: 

1.	𝗔𝗜: From Buzzword to Action
Every HR conference today seems to focus on AI. HR is playing catch-up - unwillingly. 
• Have you learned to ask better questions (known as “prompt engineering”) to get more useful answers?

2.	Bring Back 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁
We are at a crossroads right now between trusting our team members (freedom with accountability) and distrusting them (whips, carrots, forcing them back to the office five days a week without any proper explanation). At the same time, loneliness is becoming a big problem. Gallup says 20% of workers feel lonely every day, which affects their mental health and performance. 
• What do you do to create a culture where everyone feels connected—whether they’re in the office or remote?

3.	Soft Skills for Hard Results – Present the 𝗯𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗰𝗮𝘀𝗲
Here’s a challenge: HR departments often struggle to prove the value of their initiatives. Why? Many don't know how to present business cases for competence programs. The truth is that soft skills deliver hard results. I saw the impact when HR and Marketing joined forces to create a professional learning program (where I happened to be a supplier on parts of it). 
• How do you build a business case that links your L&D efforts to real outcomes, like improved engagement or better performance?

4.	𝗚𝗲𝗻 𝗭 is here – deal with it
I am not a big fan of grouping people like this (for me it’s a bit like astrology; too broad demographic to make sense) but still, according to surveys, as Gen Z enters the workplace, they bring new priorities. Many want stability, flexible work options, and work-life balance. But here’s the catch: fewer of them are interested in leadership roles. 
• How do you grow the next generation of leaders? What about offering mentoring, short-term leadership projects, or shadowing opportunities to help Gen Z test the waters before committing to management?

5.	Focus on 𝗛𝘂𝗺𝗮𝗻 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀
So we’re back full circle. Even with all the talk about AI, and other digital technology, what really makes workplaces succeed is human connection. Foster a culture of CARE. Care for the team, the mission, the vision, the clients, and the whole planet to create sustainable growth. As I stated: Soft skills drive hard results. 
• How can you from HR stay curious, connected, and focused on people?
