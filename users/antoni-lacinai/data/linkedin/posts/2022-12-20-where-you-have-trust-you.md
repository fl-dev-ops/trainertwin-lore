Where you have TRUST, you have ENGAGEMENT

In a now-famous study where two hospitals were compared, one of them reported a high volume of errors being made by the staff. Naturally, the scientist believed that this hospital must have serious problems, low engagement, and poor leadership, especially compared to the other hospital which reported way fewer accidents and errors. 

But when they dug deeper, a new reality appeared. The hospital that reported fewer errors did so because the staff was afraid of being punished if they revealed that a doctor of high status did a mistake. So, they kept their mouth shut, which of course meant that they never improved. The hospital that did report errors had a culture that rewarded continuous improvements, and they had something called “Psychological safety”. They knew that they wouldn’t be punished if they spoke up. They knew that real development comes from both appreciating and learning from all the things you do right, but also by learning from your mistakes. 

I remember being in my first full-time job as a young adult. I was in a junior sales position and at one point I listened to the senior sales colleagues complaining over something. I cannot remember what, only that it made sense to me. So, when the sales manager stepped into the room, I turned to him and said: “Hi boss. We’ve been talking and we believe that this (whatever it was) isn’t working for us.” He looked at me with a smirked  smile and said: “Really? Is that so?” he turned to the others. “Anyone else who feels  the same way as young Antoni here?”
Nobody said a word. They silently shook their heads and stared into their computer screens. “Come on!” I said. “You literarily complained about this two minutes ago.” Still, nobody dared to speak up. The sales manager shook his head and left. And I had just experienced something new. My colleagues were afraid of our boss and threw me under the bus to save themselves. 

Question: How engaged do you think they were? Answer: Not much. 

All studies show that high engagement leads to high performance, fewer absences, fewer errors and accidents, and higher customer satisfaction. And you cannot get a high engagement from your team if they don’t feel safe and if they cannot trust that their bosses have their backs. 

How do you make sure that you have a culture based on trust? How do you act? How do you communicate? Communication, motivation, and performance are interlinked. Make sure you master this and trust that your people will contribute. Your trust will charge the engagement engine and off you go! Feel the wind in your hair and the laughter in your ears as you together race toward your goals!

Thanks for reading my mind. 
Antoni Lacinai
