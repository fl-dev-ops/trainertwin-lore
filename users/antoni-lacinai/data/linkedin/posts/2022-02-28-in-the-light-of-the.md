in the light of the invasion of Ukraine, it feels petty to talk about workplace communication. I write my insights and schedule them in advance here on LinkedIn. But this week I have removed all those tips. 

Instead: let me share my two pennies of thoughts on motivation (and how it can explain the situation) by giving you a simple, yet effective formula:

M = G - R
Motivation (to move) equals Goals minus Resistance. 

Basically: If you want to move, then make your goals big and shiny while at the same time find ways to minimize resistance. 

So imagine that you are a superpower who, for decades had been losing authority and your goal is to restore the former "glory" and yet again be a dictator of a massive empire. But you face internal resistance from opponents and the will of the people, and external resistance by mostly a superior and tough US army. What do you do?

Well, you minimize resistance by getting rid of anyone with a different opinion while doing what you can to weaken your biggest external threat by meddling in elections, spreading disinformation etc. 

So what we see now is the logical next step. The goal is now bigger than the resistance. The motivation therefor is high enough to make you move. The rest of the world is now trying to increase the external resistance by sanctions. So far, that resistance is still too low compared to the feverish goals you have of a new Soviet union where you are the emperor...

But what happens if the internal resistance increases to a point where it boils over? When the citizens say "We never wanted this. Stop this madness or move aside before you turn the planet into a wasteland!"

Ukraine's reaction is also completely logical.
- Goal: To be a free democratic country, free from a dictator's chains and defend that right with all they got. 
- Resistance: A superpower who invade you. No active military help from friends. 

So far, the goal is stronger than the resistance. Every day undefeated is a victory. They are motivated. Highly motivated. Not strange when you are in a win-or-die situation. 

Those are my thoughts from a motivation perspective. It's a sad era to live in when leaders go for a lose- lose situation and we should be spending all that energy in keeping the 1,5 degree target instead of this. That's a goal worthy of mankind. Not this.
