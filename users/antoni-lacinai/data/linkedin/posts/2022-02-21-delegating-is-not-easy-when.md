DELEGATING IS NOT EASY WHEN YOU ARE STRESSED.
ATT DELEGERA EN KONST

My latest column, translated here:

The boss was stressed. She ran past John on her way to a meeting and waved at him on the fly. “I am double booked. You take care of our Chinese colleagues. They will arrive at one o'clock. " 
John asked her what the purpose of the meeting was and what he should say, but the boss just said: “You solve it. Just tell them about us. By the way… ”she said and looked at Sara, another of her team members. "Join him!
Then she was gone…

John took his laptop and did what so many people do; he gathered some  org chart PowerPoint slides in his computer. Then he went to the meeting, plugged in his computer and started showing the slides. One pointless slide after another made their colleagues' eyes more zombie-like by the minute. The atmosphere was uncomfortable.

Luckily, John was not sitting there alone. Sara asked to speak. "Okay now you have seen how we are organized, but you could just as easily read that on our intranet. How about you tell us what you would like out of the rest of the meeting? ” The colleagues from China lit up. Finally, they could focus on things they really needed to know. When the meeting was over, the mood was much better.

A stressed person doesn't think straight. The boss may have thought she was fantastic at delegating in her haste, but she forgot important parameters like providing the right context, purpose and goal.
John did not think clearly either. He did not want to prove himself incapable of the task. Two stressed people will just double the stress. 

How is it at your workplace? 

Are you and your manager colleagues high paced, handing out orders to the right and left? Do you look down at your mobile phone in the morning or do you greet your colleagues? Everything you do communicates. 

Delegating is smart. Abdicating is not. You are accountable. Help your employees grow by delegating and communicating with high empathy, positive energy and perfect clarity. Then they will face challenges in a much better way than John was given the opportunity to do.

Thank you for reading my mind
