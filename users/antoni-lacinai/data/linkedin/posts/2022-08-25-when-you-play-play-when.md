When you play, Play. When you rest, Rest. When you work, Work

Lions are amazing creatures, and these ones, only five meters from our car, were so relaxed. Two males with their large manes, about half a dozen females,  and three lion cubs. One of them was lying on her back with her legs in the air, like a lazy house cat. Several others lay on their sides, completely still. One used a flat rock as a pillow... 

Afterward, I thought:  “How often are we immersed at the moment as those lions were? How often can we let go of a thousand distractions like email, social media, or thoughts that linger on what happened or maybe will happen? How often are we just here and now?”
In my case, not very often. I am living a large part of my life in the future or in the past. Being completely present requires effort. But then and there, the day after a keynote, when we were next to these majestic lions outside Johannesburg, it was hard not to be wide-eyed and just enjoy the moment.

How is it for you? When you are at your workplace; Do you have an environment that even allows you to be fully present? How often are you interrupted in the middle of a task? How often does someone look at their mobile phone when you have meetings (in the digital space it's very easy)? How often are you completely absorbed in a task?

All the research points to us performing worse when we try to do several things that require a cognitive effort simultaneously. Yet we do it. Today, I even find it difficult to read a good book, let alone watch a good movie without glancing at my phone. I jump between tasks and apps when I work. Can you imagine that we didn't even have social media twelve years ago…

I want to be more like the lions. When they run, they run. When they rest, they rest. When they play, they play. This does not mean that I should stop setting goals, analyzing my efforts at work, or creating a post on LinkedIn. Only that when I do it, I do it wholeheartedly.

Wish me luck in my strive for more immersive experiences, and I'll do the same for you.

Thanks for reading my mind.
