PRAISE IN PRIVATE. CRITICIZE IN PUBLIC

Yes, you read it right. This column (in Swedish) is flipping the old classical saying within leadership, that you should praise in public and criticize in private. All thanks to a conversation I had on stage with complexity expert Åsa Lundquist Coey a few years ago.

The reason for criticizing in public: If somebody is mean or rude toward their colleagues and you wait with your criticism to later, you have just sanctioned that behavior. 

Åsa is a smart person!

How do you communicate in your workplace when it comes to feedback, praise and criticism?
