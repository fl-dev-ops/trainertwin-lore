The Curse of the Specialist is real. Here is a very recent memory: 

"Are you really going to drive home yourself?" the ophthalmologist asked me. I sat on a chair in the examination room with my hands around my exploding head and replied "Yes, I have no choice"

"Ok," said the ophthalmologist. That was all. Nothing about giving me pain relief or anything at all. On the way home, which took just over an hour, I cried tears of pain. When I got home, I took off my shoes and jacket and threw myself into bed with the blinds down. I sobbed for half an hour before the worst, excruciating pain began to subside.

It started with me suddenly losing some of my vision in one eye. I went to the ophthalmologist who noticed a swelling on the optic nerve. I was sent to the emergency room where I stayed overnight. There was a magnetic X-ray, blood tests, ultrasound of the neck and also a painful spinal cord test where I actually fainted after a few minutes. They found nothing. No tumor, stroke, etc. No Lyme disease or anything at all that could explain my vision problem. And so, a few days later, the headache from hell appeared. I lay in bed for five days in a dark bedroom. When I still got up to eat, or turned on my laptop to answer emails, the migraine-like pain came back, and I retreated to the bedroom. I was called to the ophthalmologist again. My wife offered to drive but I said I could do it. It was painful.

I told the ophthalmologist about the headache. He only said that there was no connection between the eye and the headache. If I had a headache, I was adviced to visit the emergency room. There was nothing he could do about it. His focus was on the small round globe that we call “the Eye”. He knew nothing about the rest of the body.

This is what I call “the Curse of the Specialist”. I, who really love nerds who know so much about so little (maybe because I myself feel like a typical generalist), got to experience the painful drawback with the specialist's inability to see the bigger picture, to see the whole person.

This is how it is in many workplaces. “Do not come to me and ask about customer needs. I only work with supply chain and container logistics. If the customer has not received their stuff, and it is not a container problem, there is nothing I can or want to do to help. ”

How do you communicate in your workplace? Do you work in silos? Or maybe even straws? Or do you have the ability to work together to solve a problem with joint efforts, regardless of what detailed job description you have? Can you instead use your collective intelligence and become an incredibly strong team together?

I still have eye problems. I do one test at a time. The weeks go by. The doctors are puzzled. But the headache has subsided. A completely different doctor said that it is a common side effect of the spinal cord test. "Strange that no one told you that." she said. I agreed.

Thank you for reading my mind
(Oh, I am otherwise in good shape and happy with life :)
