Fear, Anxiety, stress. Are they holding you back when you want to speak English?

Well, let me give you… a hand 🖐 !

Actually, I’m going to ask Dr Daniel Siegel to give us all a hand.

He has developed a hand model of the brain, which can shed light on this common challenge and provide insights into overcoming it 🧠 .

In Siegel's model, our brain is represented by a hand, 

the thumb – the “downstairs” brain symbolizes the lower parts responsible for our basic survival instincts ⬇ .

the fingers – the “upstairs” brain represent the higher cognitive functions ⬆ 

When we feel scared or "blocked" in language learning or speaking, we often find ourselves stuck in the "thumb" – the reactive and instinctual part of the brain.

Our hand is open, our fingers are lifted, our fist is released. This is when we lose connection, we can ‘t control our emotions and we can’t effectively communicate with others.

It’s the “fight or flight” mode, where fear takes over and we are unable to speak effectively.

Survival instinct.

How do we overcome this status?

It’s as simple as curling our fingers over our thumb. Now our upstairs and downstairs brain are connected ✊ !

We activate the higher brain regions associated with rationalization, learning, and creativity. 

By engaging these upper parts of the brain, we can transcend our fears and limitations, enabling us to communicate more effectively and confidently.
 
As non-native professionals, here's how the Hand Brain System Model can apply:

👉 Overcoming Speaking Anxiety
Imagine you're about to deliver a presentation in English to your international colleagues. 

As you feel the familiar pang of nervousness creeping in, remind yourself to shift your focus from the "thumb" to the "fingers" of the brain. 

Take deep breaths, visualize success, and engage in positive self-talk to activate your cognitive resources and deliver your message with clarity and confidence.

👉 Language Learning Challenges
When faced with complex technical documentation or conversations in English, it's natural to feel overwhelmed. 

Instead of allowing fear to paralyze you, approach the challenge with a growth mindset. 

Break down the task into manageable steps.

Visualize your fist closing and the upper part of your brain embracing the lower part, creating real connection.

Remember, progress is not linear, but with persistence and practice, you can connect all the parts of your brain and deliver your best performance yet!
