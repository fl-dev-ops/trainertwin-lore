Martino is a developer.
In 1995.

Bulky monitor.
Local code.
No Slack. No GitHub. No AI writing half the function for you.

It’s quiet.
Peaceful.

Then Doc appears.

“Where we’re going, we don’t need floppy disks.”

Next stop: 2026.

Martino opens his laptop.

𝗙𝗶𝗿𝘀𝘁 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗰𝗸.

Everything is in English.
Documentation. Tickets. Meetings.

He shrugs.
“I’ll manage.”
  
𝗦𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗱 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗰𝗸

Camera on.

10 people. Different countries.
“So Martino, what do you think?”

He knows exactly what to say.
Crystal clear in his head.

But then...
“Uhm… so… the thing is…”
He searches. Rephrases. Pauses.

Someone jumps in.
“Yeah, we should refactor the service.”

Martino nods.
That was his idea.
  
𝗧𝗵𝗶𝗿𝗱 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗰𝗸.

“Who wants to present this feature?”

In the 90s, he just coded it.
Now he has to explain it.

In English.
To people who don’t think like him.

𝗙𝗼𝘂𝗿𝘁𝗵 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗰𝗸.

Coffee break.

“So, what are you working on these days?”
Strangely, this feels harder than the meeting.
  
𝗙𝗶𝗳𝘁𝗵 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝗰𝗸

Doc appears again.

This time holding… Claude Code.

“Great Scott. It writes code!”

Martino watches functions appear in seconds.
He leans back.
“So… what’s left for me?”
  
Doc smiles.

“You still need to understand the problem.”
“You still need to make decisions.”
“You still need to explain them.”
“And now… you need to do it in English.”

Same brain.
Same logic.
Same skills.
Different game.

The rules changed.

So the real question is:
What makes a developer valuable now?
