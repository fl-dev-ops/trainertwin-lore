Developers don't need to become writers.

But they do need to become storytellers.

That might sound surprising.

After all, nobody hires a software engineer to become a storyteller.

But think about your last few weeks at work.

You probably had to:
• explain a technical decision to a product manager
• justify a solution during a sprint review
• update stakeholders on a project
• convince your team that one approach was better than another

Whether you realised it or not...

𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝘁𝗲𝗹𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮 𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘆.

Of course, not a fictional one.

A technical one.

And that's becoming even more important.

As AI writes more code, generates documentation and speeds up development, one skill becomes harder to replace:

Helping other people understand why a technical decision matters.

That's where many brilliant professionals struggle.

They start with the architecture.
The framework.
The implementation.
The API.

But the people listening are still wondering...

"𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱 𝗜 𝗰𝗮𝗿𝗲?"

The best communicators tell a different story.

𝟭. 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗹𝗲𝗺.
What isn't working?
What's the risk?
Why does it matter?

𝟮. 𝗘𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗲𝗰𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻.
What are we proposing?
Why this option?
What trade-offs did we consider?

𝟯. 𝗦𝗵𝗼𝘄 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻.
Keep it simple.
How will it work?
What are the key steps?
Only the details your audience actually needs.

𝟰. 𝗙𝗶𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗰𝘁.
Will it reduce costs?
Improve performance?
Lower risk?
Make customers happier?
Help the business move faster?

That's storytelling.

You’re not creating a new Hollywood saga.

But solid technical storytelling.

Because every important technical decision eventually reaches someone who isn't writing the code.

A manager.
A customer.
An executive.
A stakeholder.

And if they don't understand the story...
they can't support the decision.

That's one of the biggest changes I'm seeing in international tech teams.

Communication + technical expertise = Greater 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗰𝘁.

💬 I'm curious...

What's harder in your experience?

Building the solution...
or helping everyone else understand why it's the right one?
