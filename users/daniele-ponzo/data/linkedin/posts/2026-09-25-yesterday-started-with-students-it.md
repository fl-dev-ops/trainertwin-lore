Yesterday started with students.

It ended with senior developers.

Surprisingly, they were both trying to solve the same problem.

In the morning, I had the privilege of taking part in the Innovators for Tomorrow School Hackathon as a Rome Future Week Ambassador and mentor.

Teams of students worked on real challenges and, by the end of the day, had just three minutes to convince the audience that their idea mattered.

They all delivered a great final pitch.

But everything that happened before it really impressed me.

Ideas becoming clearer.
Questions becoming sharper.
Teams realising that having a great solution isn't enough if people don't understand it.

Simply amazing.

A few hours later, I found myself in a very different room.

Together with Emiliano Pisu, we hosted Developers in the Age of AI: Code & Communication.

Different audience.
Different conversation.

But halfway through the event, I realized something.

Neither session was really about technology.

One room was full of students.
The other was full of experienced developers.

Yet both were wrestling with the same challenge:

How do you turn knowledge into impact?

During the discussion, the room quickly came alive.
🟢 Agree.
🔴 Disagree.

People challenged us.
They challenged each other.
And, sometimes, they even changed their own minds.

We talked about AI.
Code.
Judgement.
Communication.
Leadership.

But for me, the most interesting part wasn't the technology.

It was watching people think together.

Listening.
Questioning.
Explaining.
Building on someone else's idea.

That's what made both events memorable.

Yesterday reminded me why I care so much about communication.

Because every innovation...
every promotion...
every great technical idea...

eventually depends on someone being able to explain it.

Maybe that's my biggest takeaway from this year's Rome Future Week.

The future isn't something we simply predict.
It's something we build through conversations, ideas, and people.
