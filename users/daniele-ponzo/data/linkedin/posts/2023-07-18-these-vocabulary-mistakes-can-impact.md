These Vocabulary mistakes can impact your English conversation

I was talking to a client the other day, the conversation was going quite well until…

Until he got stuck on this pair of confusing adverbs:

Actually/currently

It is not the first time I see it…so, let’s clarify it:

Currently = at the moment
Actually = In fact, to tell the truth

Here are a couple of examples:

✅Mike currently works in Finance (at the moment)
✅Despite the workload, I am actually very happy with my new position (in fact)

So, here are some more confusing couples with examples:

Hard (not easy)/ Hardly (not enough, barely)
✅I am working really hard on the new project
✅I can hardly imagine a worst scenario

Late (not early)/lately (recently)
✅ Jim was late for the meeting
✅ I’ve met a lot of people from Supply Chain lately

Specially (goes with an adjective)/ especially (in particular)
✅The report has been specially prepared for our customer support team
✅We are experiencing some issues with the LATAM market, especially with Argentina.

At the end (to be used with a noun)/in the end (finally)
✅You can find the relevant data at the end of the report
✅And then, in the end, our sales meeting was really successful.

Eventually (in the end)/Possibly (perhaps, maybe)
✅we worked a few months on the new design, and eventually we completed it.
✅I asked HR to change my office and give me a bigger one, and, possibly, closer to Finance.

Never get confused by these expressions again!
