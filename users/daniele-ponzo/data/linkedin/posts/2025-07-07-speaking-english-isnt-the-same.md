Speaking English isn’t the same as connecting globally.

English may be the common language in international business…
But it’s not always a shared one.

You can have the right words.
The right grammar.
The right tone (in your culture, at least)…
 …and still miss the mark entirely.

Why?

Because language is not enough
We need to be aware of 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗲𝘅𝘁.
And we need to understand what different cultures expect, and what they silently assume.

What feels polite in one country might sound vague in another.
→ For example: “I’ll try to finish it this week”. Is it respectful? Or indecisive? Or even evasive?

A “yes” might mean “no”… or “maybe, but I’m not sure how to say it.”
→ In some cultures, saying “no” directly is seen as rude, so “yes” might actually mean “I heard you,” not “I agree.”

Even how we write dates, close emails, or handle silence in meetings can shift meaning.
→ Sending a meeting for 04/08 could mean April 8th or August 4th, depending on where you’re from. 

Communicating across cultures means going beyond fluency.
It means becoming curious, adaptable, and clear without losing your voice.

Because the real goal isn’t just “good English.”
It’s understanding each other.

This week, I’ll share more ideas, examples, and strategies to help you communicate more effectively in international contexts, especially if you’re working in tech or business.

P.S. I’ll go first: I remember a meeting a few years ago where one of the participants wanted to confirm he was on top of things. He said something like “𝘐𝘯 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘧𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘦𝘪𝘨𝘩𝘵 𝘸𝘦 𝘸𝘪𝘭𝘭 𝘨𝘦𝘵 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴 𝘥𝘰𝘯𝘦”. 
Everybody looked puzzled… The joke works well in Italian, but it can’t be translated directly into English...
