I’ll be speaking at We Make Future in Bologna this June…

about communication under pressure.

Which means I now get to experience communication under pressure…

under actual pressure.

Perfect.

My talk is built around something I’ve seen constantly in global tech environments:

A lot of brilliant professionals think they have an English problem.

But often?

They have a cognitive overload problem.

They’re solving the issue,

translating it,
structuring it,
and managing how they sound…

all at the same time.

At some point, the sentence crashes before the idea does.

I know this because:

I spent 13 years in IT,
worked in international environments,
and I’ve lived this myself.

You understand the technical problem perfectly…
but suddenly explaining it in English feels 10x harder when the stakes are high.

And in tech?

Being right is not always enough.
Your idea also needs to land.

So at WMF, I’ll be talking about:

• communication as infrastructure
• reducing cognitive load in meetings
• why clarity beats complexity
• and how tech professionals can sound more confident without sounding “perfect”

Basically:

less “advanced English”
more “making your ideas impossible to ignore.”

Also:
there’s a non-zero chance I’ll be discussing cognitive overload while actively experiencing it live on stage.

Very immersive learning experience.

I’d genuinely love to connect.

>  It’s a procedural fluency problem.
> Until English becomes automatic — stored in procedural memory, not declarative memory — even the smartest professionals will feel slow, hesitant, or “not themselves” when speaking.
> Fluency isn’t intelligence.
>  Fluency is a skill.
>  And skills must be trained, not memorized.
