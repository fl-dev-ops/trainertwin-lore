This skill is often overlooked but crucial to get effective communication.

And it is something that can truly change how you interact with others.

Recently, I had the opportunity to read the insightful book "Ascoltability," a gift from Marco Labate. 

This gem sparked reflections on “attending behaviour” as defined by Allen Ivey, EdD, ABPP.

But what is “attending behaviour”?

It’s a crucial aspect of active listening.

And active listening can be a real game-changer for leaders and coaches.

Here are its 4 dimensions:

𝐕𝐢𝐬𝐮𝐚𝐥 𝐂𝐨𝐧𝐭𝐚𝐜𝐭: 👀
It's not just about hearing the words; it's about seeing the non-verbal cues. Are you engaged? 
Visual connection, can boost understanding beyond spoken language.

𝐕𝐨𝐜𝐚𝐥 𝐐𝐮𝐚𝐥𝐢𝐭𝐲: 🗣️
The tone, pitch, and pace of speech carry nuances. Pay attention to vocal quality—it's a roadmap to emotions and intentions. 
Practice varied vocal expressions for impactful communication.

𝐕𝐞𝐫𝐛𝐚𝐥 𝐓𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐤𝐢𝐧𝐠: 📝
Show that you're tracking by paraphrasing and summarizing key points. This not only clarifies understanding but also demonstrates your engagement and commitment to effective communication.

𝐁𝐨𝐝𝐲 𝐋𝐚𝐧𝐠𝐮𝐚𝐠𝐞: 🕺
A powerful communicator knows the language of the body. Pay attention to gestures, posture, and facial expressions. 
When words and body language align, the message becomes crystal clear.

So, why does this matter in the world of 𝐈𝐓 𝐥𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐞𝐫𝐬𝐡𝐢𝐩? 🌐

✅ Avoiding Misunderstandings:

Effective listening reduces the risk of misunderstandings, ensuring that instructions, feedback, and ideas are conveyed accurately.

✅Building Trust: 
Trust is the currency of effective teams. Active listening is the key to empathy, which creates a sense of trust and openness, essential for smooth collaboration.

✅Enhancing Collaboration: 
Clear communication fuels collaboration. Diverse voices are heard, promoting innovation and collective problem-solving.

✅Cultivating Confidence: 
Confidence comes from clarity. When you're connected to all dimensions of listening, your communication becomes clear, leaving no room for doubt or ambiguity.

In conclusion, listening is not just a passive activity but an active, deliberate practice. 

It's the cornerstone of effective communication, coaching and, ultimately, successful IT leadership.
