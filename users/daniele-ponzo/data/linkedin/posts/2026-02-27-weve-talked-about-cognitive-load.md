We’ve talked about cognitive load this week.

What it looks like.
How it affects performance.
Why technically strong professionals sometimes sound less decisive in English.

So here’s the practical question:

How do you actually reduce it?

Recently, a senior engineer I work with told me this:

🗣️“I like these sessions mostly because you give me feedback, good feedback.
It’s very 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲𝗱 and it points to where I can improve.

It helps me identify the pain points and how I can improve my English.
The structure you gave me — before/after, fact/consequence, opinion → reason → example — helps me a lot in speech.

I internalised this 𝗽𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻 a little bit, and surprisingly I used it without forcing myself.
The benefit is visible. 𝗧𝗼𝘂𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲.”  

There are three important elements in what he said.

1️⃣ Structured thinking patterns
When you don’t have to invent structure while speaking, your brain is freer to focus on meaning.

2️⃣ Deliberate practice
Not random conversation. Not generic exercises.
Targeted practice around real professional scenarios.

3️⃣ Precise feedback
Not “good job”.
Not “your English is fine”.
But: this is where friction happens. This is how you fix it.

When structure becomes automatic, 𝗰𝗼𝗴𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱 becomes manageable.

You stop translating everything.
You stop over-monitoring grammar.
You stop fighting your own sentences.

Speaking feels lighter.

Clearer.

More intentional.

That’s not more vocabulary.

That’s reduced cognitive load.
And when cognitive load drops, authority rises.

Let me ask you:

When pressure rises
are you relying on instinct…
or on structure?
