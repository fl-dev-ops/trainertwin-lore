Demystifying Business jargon #10: "Rat Race"

Power, money, competition. And stress.

A while back I got a message from a friend in the corporate world: “Really thinking of getting out of the rat race...” 

Have you ever heard the term 🐭? 

Let's break it down.

In the world of tech (but not only), the term "rat race" refers to the relentless competition and hectic nature of the industry. 

It's the daily hustle and bustle to stay ahead of the curve and outperform the competition.

For IT managers, navigating the rat race means juggling tight deadlines, constantly evolving technologies, and the pressure to deliver top-notch results. 

It's about keeping pace with the ever-changing landscape of the tech world while striving for excellence in every project.

Here are a few of examples of how you might hear "rat race" used in an IT context:

1.    "In the rat race of software development, it's crucial to stay agile and adapt to changing customer needs."
2.    "The cybersecurity sector is a constant rat race, with hackers always one step ahead of our defences."
3.    “He decided to get out of the rat race and went on a sabbatical”.

This idiom has a compelling story.

In the early 19th century, actual rats raced in carnivals as spectators gambled on them 🎡 💵  .

In the ‘30s was used in aviation. A rat race was a game of "follow the leader" in fighter planes (source: Etymonline).

The writer Vance Packard used it in his 1959 book "The Status Seekers" to describe the competitive and relentless pursuit of wealth and success in modern society.

Probably you guessed it’s not a positive and inspiring concept…

Nevertheless, it’s one of those expressions which may come up.

So, understanding and embracing the concept of the "rat race" can help IT professionals thrive in this dynamic industry. 

Or at least give them an idea of what they want to leave behind 😊
