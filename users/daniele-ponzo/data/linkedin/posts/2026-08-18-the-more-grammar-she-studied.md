The more grammar she studied...

the less she wanted to speak.

That sentence stayed with me.

A few weeks ago, a professional contacted me for advice.

Her company had asked her to pass a B2 English exam.

So she did what many people would do.

She bought a grammar book.
And started studying everything.

Past perfect.
Mixed conditionals.
Reported speech.
Future in the past.
Rule after rule.
Page after page.

Then she said something that completely changed the conversation.

"Since I started studying grammar, I'm actually more afraid to speak."

Of course, her English was not becoming worse.

(quite the opposite, actually)

But every sentence now came with a mental checklist.

Is this the right tense?
Should I use a conditional here?
Was that grammatically correct?

By the time she'd answered those questions...
the conversation had already moved on.

That's not a grammar problem.

It's cognitive overload.

Your brain is trying to:

• remember grammar rules
• choose the "perfect" words
• sound professional
• communicate your idea

...all at the same time.

Something has to give.

Usually...

it's your confidence.

Years ago, I experienced something similar.

I showed one of my grammar exercises to my Irish flatmate.

He looked at it for a few seconds and laughed.

"Honestly... I'm not sure I'd get this right either."

That changed the way I thought about English.

Native speakers don't communicate because they remember every grammar rule.

They communicate because they're focused on the conversation.

Today, when I coach software engineers, architects and tech leaders, I rarely tell them to think more about grammar during a meeting.

I tell them to think less.

Start with one idea.
Add one reason.
Finish with one example.

Simple structure.
Lower cognitive load.
Clear communication.

Grammar matters.
Exams matter.

But meetings aren't grammar exams.

They're communication events.

And under pressure, people won't remember whether you used the past perfect.
They'll remember whether they understood your message.

💬 Have you ever noticed yourself becoming more hesitant after learning a new grammar rule?

I'd love to hear your experience.
