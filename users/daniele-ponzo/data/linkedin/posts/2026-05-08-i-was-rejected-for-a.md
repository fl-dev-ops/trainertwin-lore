I was rejected for a job.

The reason?

I wasn’t “commercial enough.”

At the time, it stung.

You know the email.

“After careful consideration, we regret to inform you…”

Translation:

Nope.

But that rejection gave me something useful.

It showed me where I was forcing myself into a version of success that didn’t really fit.

I thought my career had to be linear.

Entry level.
Junior.
Senior.
Manager.
Director.

A neat little staircase.

But careers rarely work like that.

Sometimes the “wrong” door shows you the right direction.

That rejection showed me something I wasn’t seeing:

I wasn’t at my best in sales.

But I was good at communicating.
Good at helping people understand.
Good at making complicated things feel clear.

That insight changed everything.

I moved into Learning and Development.
Then team management.
Then projects with senior professionals.

Eventually, I became a full-time language coach.

And now I help professionals do something similar in English:

Communicate their value clearly.
Confidently.
In their own voice.

Because many professionals don’t lack ability.

They just haven’t learned how to express that ability clearly yet.

Funny thing is, years later, a former manager told me:

“Daniele, you were always a good communicator. You’ve become a great one with practice.”

That meant a lot.

Because sometimes rejection is not the end of the story.

Sometimes it’s the feedback that finally points you in the right direction.

What piece of feedback changed the direction of your career?

P.S. If you know you’re good at what you do…
 but it doesn’t always come across that way in English, we should talk.
