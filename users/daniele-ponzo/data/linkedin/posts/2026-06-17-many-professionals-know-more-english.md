Many professionals know more English than they can actually use.

Especially under pressure.

That’s why progress often feels slower than it should.

Not because you’re not learning.
Because you’re stuck between knowing and doing.

There’s a learning model that explains this perfectly:

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗶𝗼𝘂𝘀 𝗖𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗠𝗼𝗱𝗲𝗹

And if you work in English, understanding it can save you years of frustration.

Here’s what it looks like in practice:

1️⃣ 𝗨𝗻𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗶𝗼𝘂𝘀 𝗜𝗻𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲
You don’t know what’s holding you back.

Maybe you think:
“My English just isn’t good enough.”

But the real issue could be:

• over-explaining
• weak structure
• losing your point halfway through a sentence
• struggling under pressure

At this stage, awareness is the breakthrough.

You can't improve what you can't see.

2️⃣ 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗶𝗼𝘂𝘀 𝗜𝗻𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲
Now you see the gap.

And honestly?

This is where most people get discouraged.

You start noticing every hesitation.
Every filler word.
Every moment where the sentence sounded better in your head.

It feels uncomfortable.

But it's actually progress.

Because awareness comes before improvement.

3️⃣ 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗶𝗼𝘂𝘀 𝗖𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲
Now things start working.

You can structure your ideas.
You communicate more clearly.
You stay on track more often.

But it still requires effort.

You have to think about it.

You’re driving the car consciously.

And under pressure?

Old habits can still come back.

4️⃣ 𝗨𝗻𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗰𝗶𝗼𝘂𝘀 𝗖𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲
This is where communication becomes natural.

You stop searching for every word.
You stop overthinking every sentence.
You stop trying to sound confident.

Because you no longer need to.

The structure is already there.

You can focus on the conversation instead of managing yourself.

And that’s where many professionals want to get.

But here's the important part:

You don't reach this stage by studying more.

You reach it through repeated use.

Meetings.
Presentations.
Difficult conversations.
Unexpected questions.
Real situations.

That’s why communication confidence rarely appears first.

It appears after enough successful repetitions.

Practice → Experience → Confidence.

Not the other way around.

So if you're currently noticing all your mistakes...
Good.

You're probably not failing.
You're probably progressing.

Which stage do you think you're in right now?
