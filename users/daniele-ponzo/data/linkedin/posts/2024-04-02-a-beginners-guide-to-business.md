A beginner’s guide to business English words in the workplace.
 
Have you just started working for a multinational company?

Or maybe you’re planning to find a new international job?

There are some work-related words that are essential for you to know.

Why?

Because they are used daily.

And because nobody is going to explain or translate them for you in the office.

So, here’s a quick list (with examples) of some of the most common words:

🔘 Apply:
Example: "John decided to apply for the position of IT project manager at the multinational tech company."

🔘 Do overtime:
Example: "Due to the upcoming deadline, the IT team had to do overtime to ensure the successful implementation of the software update."

🔘 Get promoted:
Example: "After years of dedication and hard work, Sarah was finally promoted to the position of IT director."

🔘 Resign:
Example: "James made the difficult decision to resign from his role as senior software engineer to pursue a new opportunity."

🔘 Retire:
Example: "After a long and successful career in IT, David decided it was time to retire and enjoy his well-deserved rest."

🔘 Do a course:
Example: "In order to enhance her skills, Emily decided to do a course in cybersecurity to stay updated with the latest trends in the field."

🔘 Run a company:
Example: "As the CEO of the IT consulting firm, Mark was responsible for running the company and overseeing its operations."

🔘 Set up (start):
Example: "The IT team worked tirelessly to set up the new server infrastructure to support the company's growing data needs."

🔘 To be made redundant:
Example: "Unfortunately, due to budget cuts, several employees in the IT department were made redundant."

🔘 To be sacked (also: to be fired):
Example: "After repeated warnings for poor performance, Alex was eventually sacked from his position as IT support specialist."

🔘 Work shifts (flexible work schedule):
Example: "As part of the IT operations team, Sarah had to work shifts to ensure round-the-clock monitoring of the company's network infrastructure."

🔘Remote Working:
Example: "In remote working, employees are located away from the traditional office setting, working from home or other remote locations. They rely on digital communication tools to stay connected with their team".

🔘Smart Working:
Example: "Smart working goes beyond remote working by focusing on outcomes rather than just physical presence. It involves flexible work arrangements (hours, location) to achieve work-life balance.
