Demystifying Business Jargon #14: What Does "No Brainer" Really Mean in the Tech World? 🤔

I used to hear the expression "no brainer" thrown around in the boardroom or during project discussions quite often🧠.

It's one of those executive jargons that could leave people scratching their heads. 

But fear not, because I'm here to shed some light on what it actually means and how it's relevant to our work in the IT industry.

In simple terms, a "no brainer" refers to a decision or choice that is so obvious and straightforward that it requires little to no thought or consideration 👍 . 

It's like picking low-hanging fruit – it's right there in front of you, and there's really no reason not to go for it.

Let's break it down with a couple of examples relevant to IT managers:

🔵 Software Upgrade Decision:
Imagine your team is discussing whether to upgrade to the latest version of a software tool. 

If the new version offers significant performance improvements, enhanced security features, and seamless integration with existing systems, then opting for the upgrade becomes a "no brainer." 

It's an obvious choice that will benefit the team and the organization without any drawbacks.

🔵 Hiring a Skilled Developer:
Suppose you're in the process of hiring a new developer for your team, and you come across a candidate with an impressive track record, extensive experience, and glowing recommendations from previous employers. 

In this case, hiring that candidate becomes a "no brainer" – it's a clear choice that will undoubtedly strengthen your team and contribute to its success.

In both examples, the decision at hand is so obvious and advantageous that it requires little deliberation. 

It's a "no brainer" to move forward with the option that offers the most benefits with minimal risk.

📖As many other idioms, it is connected with the world of sport. Gary Martin (a writer and researcher on the origins of phrases) reports the first example of the term with the meaning of 'easily made decision'.

It comes from the Canadian newspaper The Lethbridge Herald, January 1968, in a report on an ice hockey game:

𝘏𝘦'𝘥 𝘣𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘬 𝘪𝘯 𝘰𝘯 𝘢 𝘨𝘰𝘢𝘭𝘪𝘦 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘯𝘦𝘵𝘮𝘪𝘯𝘥𝘦𝘳 𝘸𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥 𝘮𝘢𝘬𝘦 𝘰𝘯𝘦 𝘰𝘧 𝘵𝘩𝘰𝘴𝘦 𝘴𝘢𝘷𝘦𝘴 𝘵𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘰𝘶𝘳 𝘮𝘢𝘯𝘢𝘨𝘦𝘳-𝘤𝘰𝘢𝘤𝘩, 𝘚𝘪𝘥 𝘈𝘣𝘦𝘭, 𝘤𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘴 "𝘢 𝘯𝘰-𝘣𝘳𝘢𝘪𝘯𝘦𝘳."

Remember to use it as a noun: A "no brainer".

So, the next time you hear someone say that something is a "no brainer," you'll know exactly what they mean – and you can confidently join the conversation.
