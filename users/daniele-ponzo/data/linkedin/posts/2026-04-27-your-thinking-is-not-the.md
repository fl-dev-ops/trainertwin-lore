Your thinking is not the problem.

The delay is.
  
In your own language:

→ you’re sharp
→ you’re fast
→ you explain complex ideas clearly
→ you argue your point

Then you switch to English…

And suddenly:

→ you start running around in circles
→ you lose your words
→ you hesitate
→ you sound… junior
  
So you start thinking:

“Maybe my English is not good enough.”
“Maybe I need more vocabulary.”
“Maybe I just need more confidence.”

But that’s not what’s happening.
  
What’s actually happening is this:

Your brain is doing two jobs at once:

→ build the idea
→ translate it
 
That creates:

→ delay
→ pressure
→ overthinking
  
Under pressure, that means:

→ speed drops
→ clarity drops
→ confidence drops
  
So it’s not that you’re less intelligent.

It’s that your intelligence is stuck behind a translation process.

And people don’t see the thinking behind it.

They only see the output.
  
That’s why strong professionals:

→ stay quiet in meetings
→ keep answers short
→ avoid complex explanations
  
Not because they don’t know.

Because they don’t have time to translate everything.
  
This is one of the exact dynamics we’ll break down in our live session tomorrow with David H. Stirling.

You don’t “become more fluent”

You just start sounding like yourself again.

👉 meetings
