"Wrong, Do it again! "Wrong, Do it again!

I guess this reminded you of “certain teachers who would hurt the children in any way they could”

You know, pointing their fingers every time you made a mistake.🫵

Not giving you a chance to speak.

Well, I believe things can be done in a different way.

I met E. a few months ago. She was used to the standard English class: grammar, grammar and, guess what? More grammar. 
And every time she found herself in an international situation, she would keep quiet as she felt ‘embarrassed’.

The interesting bit?

Her English was actually very good. She could articulate, she could speak and explain her ideas. And her pronunciation was clear.

What was the problem?

She was ashamed of her accent. She was told in the past that her accent was too strong, and she wouldn’t be able to communicate and be understood.

So, she ended up believing it and skipping all speaking opportunities.

“Speaking? No, thanks. I can do exercises, homework… but no speaking, please”.

And what did we do?

We spoke, of course. At first about doubts, examples, words. And gradually, we incorporated more topics, work related situations, role-plays.

➡️ We practiced the scenarios in a safe environment. Without exposing weaknesses or “pouring derision”.

➡️ We worked on interaction and focused on clarity rather than “accent perfection”.

➡️ We focused on tearing down the wall to give her confidence.

The result? 

An enjoyable exchange, fruitful conversations about opinions, aspirations and a renewed confidence to go out there and face those tricky work situations.

I believe knowing a language without speaking it is pointless.
And speaking a language without enjoying it is dull.
