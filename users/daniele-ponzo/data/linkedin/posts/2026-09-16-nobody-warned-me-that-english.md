Nobody warned me that English wasn't the hardest part of working internationally.
  
During my years in IT, one meeting could include:
  
• a developer from Poland
  
• a client in the US
  
• a stakeholder in Germany
  
• a manager from Chile
  
• colleagues from across Europe.
  
Same company.
  
Same project.
  
Very different ways of communicating.
  
At first, I assumed that if my English was good enough, everything else would fall into place.
  
It didn't.
  
I realised people weren't misunderstanding my English.
  
They were interpreting my communication through their own cultural lens.
  
Sometimes being direct was appreciated.
  
Sometimes the same message sounded too abrupt.
  
Sometimes silence meant disagreement.
  
Sometimes it meant respect.
  
Nobody had taught me those things.
  
I learned them one meeting at a time.
  
Looking back, I think that lesson matters even more today.
  
AI can translate our emails.
  
Summarize our meetings.
  
Even help us write better messages.
  
But it still can't tell us:
  
• when to challenge an idea
  
• how much context someone expects
  
• what builds trust in another culture
  
• how to adapt your communication without losing authenticity
  
That's still our job.
  
And I believe it's becoming one of the most valuable skills for anyone building an international career.
  
Because working globally means: 
making people from different backgrounds want to work with you.
  
💬 What's one cultural difference that completely surprised you when working with an international team?
  
I'd love to hear your story.
