One of the most frustrating things that can happen when speaking English at work is this:

You start explaining…

and you end up 𝗴𝗼𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝗰𝗶𝗿𝗰𝗹𝗲𝘀.

You enter a meeting.
You know the answer.
You know the problem.
You know exactly what you want to say.

You start talking.

At first it sounds fine.

Then you add one detail.
Then another.
Then you try to clarify something.
Then the sentence gets too long…

So you try to fix it while you’re still speaking.

Now your brain is overloaded.

You’re:
– thinking about the idea
– translating
– checking grammar
– searching for words
– monitoring how you sound

And suddenly:

“Uhm… yes… what I mean is… eh… maybe… the problem is… how to say… the… the configuration…”

Silence.

You feel everyone waiting.

You rush.
You add more words.
You lose the point.

Someone interrupts.
Or worse, the meeting moves on.

Same thing in presentations.
Same in interviews.

The Paradox? You know English.

But nobody trained you for real speaking situations.

How to:
– structure before speaking
– keep the focus
– simplify without losing authority
– recover when a word is missing
– stay clear under pressure

When you train this way, something shifts.

You stop building sentences randomly.
You start using patterns.

You stop translating.
You start thinking directly in English.

Of course, your English doesn’t become perfect.

But it definitely becomes more effective.
