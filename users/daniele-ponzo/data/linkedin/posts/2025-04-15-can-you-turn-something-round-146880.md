Can you turn something round into something square?

That’s what many professionals are unknowingly trying to do with English.

They learn the language in a “round” way:
🔘 Memorize vocabulary
🔘 Study grammar rules
🔘 Watch videos and listen to podcasts

But then they’re asked to perform in a “square” environment:
🔲  Speak clearly in meetings
🔲  Present to clients or stakeholders
🔲  Handle fast-paced conversations on the spot

And suddenly… it doesn’t fit.

It’s because their English is shaped for 𝘶𝘯𝘥𝘦𝘳𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘯𝘥𝘪𝘯𝘨, not 𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘮𝘶𝘯𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘵𝘪𝘯𝘨.

👉 Knowing English and using English under pressure are two different skill sets.
👉 Understanding content and leading a conversation are two different realities.

If you’ve ever gone blank in a call, hesitated during a 1:1, or watched someone else get credit for an idea you had but couldn’t express, you know this gap.

And here's the truth: you can’t force a round solution into a square problem.
You need a new shape. A new strategy.

Use the language first, and shape it as you go.

Because real growth doesn’t happen in silence
it happens when you start participating.

And when you do, the transformation goes far beyond language.

you become more 𝘷𝘪𝘴𝘪𝘣𝘭𝘦, 𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘤𝘵𝘦𝘥, and 𝘤𝘰𝘯𝘯𝘦𝘤𝘵𝘦𝘥.

And you start to:
✨ explain complex ideas with clarity
✨ earn trust from international colleagues and clients
✨ build global connections and open new opportunities
✨ get invited to lead projects and join high-impact meetings

This isn’t about perfect grammar.
It’s about building a voice that moves your career forward.

So if your English feels “off” in real situations, maybe the problem isn’t you.
Maybe it’s the shape of your learning.
Maybe it’s time to change that.
🔘⇢ 🔲 

P.S. If you could change the shape of one thing... what would it be?
