7 lessons I learned when I was president of a local community garden in Dublin inner city

🔅  The Fine Art of Patience: 
Growing vegetables is an investment in the future. Prepare the soil, select the seeds, give them the best care, mind the seedlings, protect them, water them, help the young plants and (if you’re lucky) finally harvest your fruit.

🔅  The Power of Collective Decision Making
What and where to plant, when and how to put seedlings in the soil. In a community garden you try to make decisions based on consensus, which means negotiation, active listening always keeping in mind the greater good (garden and community).

🔅   A Community is About People: 
Sometimes, a noisy group of local kids would visit, eager to try all the gardening tools, and know everything about plants. I used to give them little pots with their names to put their seeds in. Then, the week after I would put grown seedlings in their pots, pretending the plants had grown during the week. This used to amaze them, so they kept coming and learning.

🔅  The Value of Hard Labour and Consistency
Cutting, watering, digging, trimming, pruning, composting, shredding, repairing, ploughing. For sure, I didn’t need to go to the gym back then. But it was mind-blowing to see the “before” and “after”, you could feel the concrete difference your work made.

🔅  Nature’s Way
Observing plants gives you great insights on how nature works. Plants that go well together and plants that hate each other. Vegetables looking for the sun and others looking for some shadow. All of this in a continuous circle of life, death and rebirth (compost). It never ceases to amaze me.

🔅  Metaphorical vs. Real
When you work in a busy corporate environment, you often have to deal with “metaphorical manure”: moving it around, piling it up, spreading it around, giving everyone its fare share and, most important, avoid leaving it all in one place. In a community garden you do the same, but the manure isn’t metaphorical.

🔅  Celebrate in Style
Harvest time is always precious. Gathering fruit and vegetables, sharing recipes with community members, having dinners, “potluck”, taking part in community events. There’s a time for work and a time for rest. 

Gardening in a community is not only about food and is not only about people. It’s about making a difference, creating meaningful connections and constantly improving yourself 🌱 .

Like a tree in a forest, you can only thrive if you find a way among your peers.

Over to you now. I’d love to know your “unconventional” stories. What have you learned?
