Does your English improvement depend on structured courses?

How can you generate real impact?

Last week I was lucky enough to have a conversation with Catherine McDonald and the “70:20:10” model of learning came up.

So, I decided to dig deeper and get a better understanding of the model.

Essentially, it is about switching priorities.

From formal learning to integrating learning in the workflow.

The 70:20:10 method is all about learning by doing. Here's the breakdown:

𝟕𝟎%: Hands-on Experience: Dive into real-world situations where English is used daily. Use opportunities like client calls, meetings, and presentations to improve your skills.

𝟐𝟎%: Social Learning: Engage with peers, mentors, and language partners to exchange ideas, receive feedback, and learn from each other's experiences.

𝟏𝟎%: Formal Learning: Supplement your hands-on experience with structured learning materials like online courses, textbooks, or workshops to fill in any gaps and reinforce your understanding.

This has a strong impact on how we approach learning.

In one sentence?

Put yourself out there.

Start applying the H.A.C.K. strategy today 

🅗 𝐇𝐨𝐧𝐞: refine your skills by practicing daily (at work, with friends or by interacting through voice messages)

🅐 𝐀𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: Presentations, meetings, calls… every interaction is a chance to learn and grow.

🅒 𝐂𝐨𝐥𝐥𝐚𝐛𝐨𝐫𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: Create a strong network with your peers, exchange ideas and feedback.

🅚 𝐊𝐮𝐝𝐨𝐬: Recognize and celebrate milestones along the way to boost morale and motivate continued growth.

This is a direct consequence of the 70:20:10 model and it gives an effective plan on how we can improve.

It’s continuous learning in action.
