What’s The Psychology Behind Real English Confidence?

A million dollar question.
One that’s not easy to answer.

I was lucky enough to be invited by Richard Marshall on his podcast “𝗙𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝗟𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗥𝗶𝘃𝗲𝗿” to try and answer the question.

And I’m truly grateful, because he allowed me to look back into my own experience.

It took me right back to when I first landed in Ireland - new country, new culture, new job… and a new language to work in.

At the time, I thought confidence came from perfection.
Flawless grammar, zero mistakes, polished pronunciation.

But I soon realized confidence comes from 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻.
From showing up, being present, and speaking even when you feel unsure.

During the episode, we explored how:
▶️ Language and culture shape each other and how learning a language means learning a way of seeing the world.
▶️ We’re not just teachers, we’re also learners constantly evolving with every conversation.
▶️ Speaking with intention and authenticity matters more than chasing perfection.
▶️ The mindset behind language learning (handle it with care!)

😅 And yes, I even put myself on the spot and spoke 𝗦𝗽𝗮𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗵 during the podcast (you know, talk the talk and walk the walk…)!

So, I’m not sure we go to answer the question fully
After all, it’s a million dollar question

But we do agree on what real communication is about:
👉 Not being flawless.
👉 But being human.

That’s where real English confidence begins.

I’d love to hear from you:
 💭 What does real confidence mean to you when you speak another language?
