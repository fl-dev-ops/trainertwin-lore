“Should I say it? Will it be offensive?”

Ever had that haunting doubt?

Back in my IT years, my work environment couldn’t be more diverse.

I was managing a team of experienced service delivery professionals from:

🌍 Germany, France, Spain, Brazil, Romania, Italy, Ireland, UK, Portugal…

Some team members were younger than me.

But some were older (and more senior in the role).

Some were in the office with me, and others were working remotely.

🖥 I was coordinating projects with software developers from Poland.

I had clients in the USA, Belgium, Russia, Germany, Scotland, Nigeria...

🛫 I would regularly visit stakeholders in Germany, Spain, France and Romania.

And my manager was from Chile.

One vital lesson I learned?

Be aware of cultural nuances.

For me, it was like walking on eggshells.

What may be considered appropriate in one culture could be deemed offensive in another.

Gestures or phrases that are harmless in one culture may be offensive in another.

Humour and jokes which seem perfectly fine for you, might not always be taken easily by colleagues, clients, and stakeholders from different backgrounds.

Be mindful of these differences.

Adjust your communication style to build stronger and more meaningful relationships.

Acknowledge and appreciate different cultural backgrounds to create a sense of belonging where everyone feels valued and heard.

You see, it’s not only about learning or improving your English.

It’s more about: 

🔵 Navigating diverse cultural landscapes effectively.

🔵 Analyzing common cultural misunderstandings and learning strategies to address them proactively.

🔵 Role-playing exercises to practice communicating in cross-cultural settings to feel more confident.

🔵 Providing targeted guidance to identify areas for improvement and develop cultural competence over time.

Today I work with professionals from many different countries.

My coaching program aims to equip IT managers with the knowledge, skills, and mindset needed to navigate cultural nuances confidently in their English communication.
