Direct, diplomatic, senior, junior… 
How do you speak when every personality has a different ‘setting’?

We tried to answer this question in episode 32 of The Non-Native Leader - Podcast
I sat down with Pieter Van Der Meer, a Data Engineer at Dataworkz, currently working for Booking.com

He is familiar with multicultural, cross-functional teams across the World.
And we talked about 𝗮𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀…
the awareness behind how we communicate in real international workplaces.

Here’s what we dug into 👇

𝟭. 𝗖𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗹 𝗔𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀: 𝗠𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗧𝗵𝗮𝗻 𝗝𝘂𝘀𝘁 “𝗡𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗲𝘀”
Sure: speaking to a German, an American, a Dutch, or an Italian can feel very different…

But Pieter took this further:
➡️ Some departments are naturally direct (engineering, dev teams, ops).
➡️ Others tend to be diplomatic (HR, communication, leadership).
➡️ And hierarchy adds another layer: what sounds “confident” to a peer might sound “too strong” to a manager… or not strong enough.

Pieter shared how being naturally direct sometimes caused friction…
This part is gold for anyone working in global teams.

𝟮. 𝗟𝗮𝗻𝗴𝘂𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝗔𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀: 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗗𝗼 𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗙𝗮𝗶𝗹 𝗬𝗼𝘂
We all freeze sometimes.
The word disappears.
The sentence breaks.
And the awkward silence arrives…

But Pieter shared how he gets around it, not by knowing more vocabulary, but by using workarounds:
✨ Describe the idea
✨ Use simpler words
✨ Buy time strategically
✨ Rephrase the concept
✨ Keep the conversation flowing

His examples were so real and so relatable: the kinds of mistakes everyone makes, but nobody talks about.
And honestly, these strategies can save you in a high-pressure English discussion.
