If you're a Tech professional working in English, this might explain why meetings feel harder than they should.

The problem often isn't your English.

You’re in a meeting.
You understand everything.
You even have the answer.

But part of your brain is somewhere else.

Checking a word.
Rephrasing a sentence.
Thinking ahead.
Holding back… just in case.

So by the time you’re ready to speak…
the moment has already moved.

And this is where many professionals get stuck.

They think it's a knowledge problem.

But actually, it's a "pressure" problem.

Which impacts how you access your knowledge:

You’re thinking.
Translating.
Structuring.
Filtering.
Monitoring reactions.

All at the same time.

The message gets buried under everything happening around it.

That’s why just "studying more" rarely solves the problem.

You didn’t train this version of yourself.
The one under pressure.

So we focus on something different.

▶️ First, we identify where communication starts breaking down.

Where you lose the thread.
Where hesitation appears.
Where the sentence becomes heavier than the idea.

▶️ Then we build something to rely on.

Simple structures.
Clear starting points.
Ways to stay on track when the conversation becomes unpredictable.

▶️ And then we practise using them in situations that feel real.

Questions.
Interruptions.
Disagreement.
Pressure.

That’s when things start to change.

Not perfectly.
But noticeably.

One client told me:
“I used to panic every time I had to speak unexpectedly.
Now I don’t panic anymore.”

Another said:
“I stopped getting lost halfway through my sentences.”

A senior engineer shared this after a few weeks:
“I can organise my thoughts and handle complex conversations more naturally.”

And one comment I still remember:
“I can finally improvise in a meeting.”

Not perfect English.
Something much more useful.

The sentence comes out.
The idea lands.
People follow.
You stay with it.

The pressure is still there.

But it no longer controls the conversation.

That’s what 𝗦𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸 𝗟𝗶𝗸𝗲 𝗮 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿 is designed to help with.

Not perfect English.

Clear thinking when the moment matters.

Confidence was never the starting point.
It was the result.

What changes first for you when pressure enters the conversation?
