Your technical English is fine.

It’s the unscripted moments that expose the gap.

You can read documentation in English.
You can follow technical tutorials.
You can understand code reviews.
You can explain how a system works or why a deployment failed.

Then something completely ordinary happens.

You join a meeting five minutes early.
Someone asks how your weekend was.
A colleague makes a joke you don’t fully understand.
Your manager asks a question you weren’t expecting.

And suddenly...

You feel your English simply disappeared.

The problem?

The structure is not supporting you anymore.

Technical conversations give you context.

You know the topic.
You know the vocabulary.
You know the goal.

But spontaneous conversations are different.

No script.
No slides.
No familiar framework.
No time to translate, edit and prepare the perfect sentence.

That’s when the pressure increases.

You start searching for the right words.

You monitor your grammar.

You worry about sounding awkward.

You wonder whether it’s better to say nothing.

And sometimes...

you stay quiet.

This is something I see often with tech professionals.

Their technical English is not the real issue.

The real issue is the gap between:

and

𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘆 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗳𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗲.

And that gap can affect more than conversations.

It can affect your visibility.

Your relationships with international colleagues.

Your confidence in meetings.

Your willingness to contribute when it matters.

We call it “small talk”...

but it isn’t always small.

It’s often where trust starts.

Before the meeting begins.

After the presentation ends.

During the informal moments where people get to know how you think.

So, I believe the goal is to become more comfortable using the English you already have...
even when the conversation is unexpected.

In international teams, technical knowledge helps you do the work.

But spontaneous communication helps people connect with you.

And that connection matters more than many professionals realize.
