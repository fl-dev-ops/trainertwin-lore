Your mind goes blank… even though you know English? 🧠😬

Many professionals tell me:
“It’s not like I don’t know English. I read in English. I even think in English…
But when I try to speak, my mind goes blank.
I can’t find the right words to say what I mean.”

Usually they tell you to “expand your vocabulary”
Which is fine. It surely helps.

You learn a lot of English words.
But what about finding and using them quickly when it matters?

Here’s a new perspective: building 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 vocabulary is different from passive learning.

Instead of memorizing endless word lists, 
focus on habits that make your brain retrieve words faster under pressure.

3 Ways to Grow Vocabulary (That Actually Stick)

1️⃣ 𝗦𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘆 𝗦𝘄𝗮𝗽 – 𝗠𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗣𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹
Instead of memorizing new words from a list or repeating them 10 times, put the word into a mini story about your own life.

When you connect a new word to something real and personal, your brain is far more likely to remember and retrieve it when speaking.

Example 1:
New word: "stumble upon" (to find by accident)
Story:
“Last week, I stumbled upon a little café near my office that serves the best croissants.”

Example 2:
New word: "hesitate" (to pause before speaking or acting)
Story:
“During yesterday’s meeting, I hesitated before answering because I wasn’t 100% sure of the data.”

📌 Why it works:
Real stories create emotional hooks in your memory
You learn the word in context, so it’s easier to recall
You can reuse your story as a practice tool when speaking

2️⃣ 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝟯‑𝗦𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗥𝘂𝗹𝗲
When you discover a new word or phrase, challenge yourself to:
Say it out loud
Write 3 different sentences with it
Use it in conversation within 24 hours

Example: New word: "overwhelmed"
“I feel overwhelmed by emails on Monday mornings.”
“She looked overwhelmed with all the new tasks.”
“I get overwhelmed when I have to speak suddenly.”

📌 Why it works: Active use in multiple contexts cements the word in memory.

3️⃣ 𝗠𝗶𝗰𝗿𝗼‑𝗟𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗣𝗵𝗿𝗮𝘀𝗲𝘀
Spend 2–3 minutes daily listening to English speakers (podcast, YouTube, series).
Pick one phrase they use naturally
Repeat it, mimic the intonation, and try to use it that day

Example: Hear “That caught me off guard”
Repeat it, write it in a sentence, and drop it in conversation

📌 Why it works: You learn chunks of language instead of single words, which makes speaking faster and smoother.

So, you need to learn new words
But you also need to learn how to use them quickly and confidently.

Active practice beats passive memorization every time.
Try one of these methods today, and use a new word in a sentence out loud.
