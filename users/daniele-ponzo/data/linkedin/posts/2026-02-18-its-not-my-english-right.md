“It’s not my English… right?”

You know that moment.

You’re in a meeting.
You know exactly what you want to say.

It’s clear in your head.
Logical. Structured. Senior.

And then…

It comes out slightly… off.

Why?

I recently spoke with several senior IT leaders who work internationally and use English daily.
Here’s what they said:

- “It’s about adapting the idea to the person in front of me.” 
- “It’s clarity, structure, and positioning.” 
- “Even strong technical points get lost if they’re not framed well.” 
- “Sometimes it’s nerves, context, or mental fatigue.” 
- “Starting with the main point first helps.” 

Notice something?

These are not vocabulary problems.

These are 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗵𝗶𝗽-𝗹𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 problems.

Most Senior IT & Tech professionals don’t struggle with English basics.

They struggle with:
• Positioning
• Framing
• Adapting to C-level vs technical peers
• Structuring ideas under pressure

In other words:

It’s not about language — the WHAT.
It’s about credibility — the HOW.

And here’s how it feels:

In your head:
🧠 “Crystal clear. Logical. Bulletproof.”
Out loud:
😅 “So… basically… what I’m trying to say is…”

Same brain.
Different delivery.

And delivery is what people respond to in high-stakes workplace English.

So let me ask you:

When your message doesn’t land,
is it really English…
or is it structure under pressure?
