I’m sure this is confusing.

Are they really bringing Apocalypse on us? 🌩 

Probably not. But throwing apostrophes around like sprinkling oregano on a bruschetta is not a good idea.🧂 

Not only it is incorrect;

It doesn’t look professional and can have a negative impact on your message.

Not sure when you need to use it? Let me give you some examples of some common uses (and some mistakes to avoid):

𝐏𝐨𝐬𝐬𝐞𝐬𝐬𝐢𝐨𝐧

✔ Singular: The project manager's decision was crucial for a perfect delivery.
✔Singular: James’s expertise in AI technology is impressive.
✔Plural (Regular): The developers' collaboration resulted in a breakthrough.
✔Plural (Irregular): Good working conditions can improve people’s lives.
✔A collective possession: Bart and Ahmed’s position in the company is the best.
✔2 separate items: Robert’s and Ricardo’s projects were selected for the final review.
✔Each other: The team members were supportive and respected each other's opinions during the project.

🔴 Be careful:

the Manager’s ideas [= the ideas belong to the Manager]
the Managers’ ideas [= the ideas belong to the Managers]

𝐓𝐢𝐦𝐞:

✔My job was much easier in the ’90s.
✔Let's meet at 7 o'clock.

𝐋𝐞𝐭𝐭𝐞𝐫𝐬

✔O’s and 0’s are surely different. I’s and l’s look the same, so we must use uppercase L’s.

𝐂𝐨𝐧𝐭𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐞𝐝 𝐟𝐨𝐫𝐦𝐬

✔Have: I’ve already planned my next project.
✔Be: He’s running the new script.
✔Be: It’s been a long but very productive meeting.
✔Would: I’d like to talk to our Finance department. 
✔Not: I don't agree with the latest developments

🔴This is when we DON’T use it:

❌ 𝐈𝐭𝐬 (pronoun): 
"The software's success is evident from 𝐢𝐭𝐬 user-friendly interface." (In this case, "its" indicates that the user-friendly interface belongs to the software.)
“The Company is very proud of 𝐢𝐭𝐬 success”. (the success belongs to the company)

It’s ≠ its
