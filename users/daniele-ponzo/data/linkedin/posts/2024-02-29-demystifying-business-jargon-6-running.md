Demystifying business jargon #6: “🚩 Running It Up the Flagpole”.

This is relevant for IT leaders and people working in startups.

Ever heard that phrase? Let's decode this jargon.

🔘 Understanding the Phrase:

Thinking Tech, IT: decisions often need to be swift and strategic, 
"Run it up the flagpole" is a metaphorical expression. It means to present an idea or a proposal to see how others react or respond.

🔘Why It Matters for IT Professionals:

As IT leaders, effective communication is key. Knowing how to run ideas up the flagpole can determine the success of a project or initiative. 

It's about testing the waters, gaining insights, and making informed decisions.

🔘Examples in the IT Context:

Before Implementing a New System: 
"Before we finalize the new cybersecurity measures, let's run it up the flagpole with the security team and get their feedback."

Pitching an Innovative Solution: 
"Considering the latest advancements, I suggest we run the idea of incorporating AI in our customer service platform up the flagpole with the executive team."

🔘Where does it come from?

According to "𝘞𝘰𝘳𝘥𝘰𝘳𝘪𝘨𝘪𝘯𝘴" The phrase “run it up the flagpole and see if anyone salutes” is credited to Madison Avenue admen of the 1950s in the context of brainstorming ideas.

One of the first uses is in the brilliant 1957 film “12 Angry Men” by Sidney Lumet. Here’s the phrase:

“It’s the weirdest thing, the way they sometimes precede their idea with a phrase. Like, some account exec will get up and he’ll say: ‘OK, here’s an idea. Let’s… run it up the flagpole and see if anyone salutes it.’ I mean, it’s idiotic, but it’s funny.”

(If you haven’t watched the film, I highly recommend it).

🔘Takeaway for IT Leaders:

Running ideas up the flagpole is about collaboration and ensuring alignment within the team. It's not just jargon; it's a strategy to promote innovation and make well-informed decisions.

Remember, decoding jargon is a step toward effective communication.
