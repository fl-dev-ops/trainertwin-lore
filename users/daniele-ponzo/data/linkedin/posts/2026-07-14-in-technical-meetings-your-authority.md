In technical meetings, your authority is often judged before you've finished your first answer.

I call this 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝘆.

It's the mental lag between the moment someone asks you a question...
and the moment you can confidently respond.

If you work in English, you've probably felt it.

A senior stakeholder asks a perfectly reasonable question.

You know the answer.

But suddenly your brain is doing far more than answering.

🔺It's translating.
🔺Searching for the right words.
🔺Checking the grammar.
🔺Wondering how you sound.
🔺Reading the room.
🔺Trying not to make a mistake.

Your brain's CPU is already running at 100%.

Then someone says:

"So... what would you recommend?"

And everything slows down.

To avoid the silence, you start building the perfect sentence in your head.

The pause gets longer.
The pressure increases.

So when you finally speak...

you start panic explaining.
You add every technical detail.
Every exception.
Every piece of background information.

Not because the audience needs it.
Because your brain is trying to prove you know what you're talking about.

Meanwhile, the people listening start losing the thread.

Your solution may be excellent.

But your communication makes you sound less senior than you really are.

That's the frustrating part.

The problem isn't your expertise.
And it usually isn't your English either.

It's the amount of processing your brain is trying to do in real time.

That's why I rarely tell people to study more vocabulary.

Instead, we reduce the number of processes running at once.

Simple communication structures.
Repeatable patterns.
Clear starting points.

When your brain no longer has to build every sentence from scratch...
your communication latency drops.

You respond more quickly.
You stay with the message.
And people hear the expertise that was already there.

I'm curious...

When your mind goes blank in an important meeting, what's your first instinct?

Do you pause...

or do you find yourself panic explaining?
