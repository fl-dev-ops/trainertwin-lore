Global teams need more than just English to connect.

Managing a culturally diverse team (part of which was remote) was one of the most rewarding (and enriching!) experiences of my career.

We all spoke English.
But that didn’t mean we always understood each other.

That’s when I learned:
🌍 International communication needs more than just language.
It’s built on 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝗰𝗹𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘆, 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗰𝘂𝗿𝗶𝗼𝘀𝗶𝘁𝘆.

Here are a few simple things that made a big difference for me:

✅ 𝗗𝗼 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲 𝗰𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗹 𝗵𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸
When you’re leading a team with Irish, Polish, Italian, Spanish, French, German, Brazilian, Romanian, and Portuguese colleagues, assumptions don’t get you far.
Reading up on communication styles (like how directness is seen in Germany vs. Portugal) helped me understand unspoken cues and avoid misunderstandings.

✅ 𝗟𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻 𝗮 𝗳𝗲𝘄 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗹𝗼𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝗹𝗮𝗻𝗴𝘂𝗮𝗴𝗲
A quick “Bună ziua” during a call with my Romanian teammates instantly changed the energy.
It wasn’t perfect, but it showed respect, effort, and a human touch.

✅ 𝗞𝗲𝗲𝗽 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝘀𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗰𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿
No buzzwords. No idioms. No long, run-on sentences.
Just short, clear language so everyone has a chance to contribute.
(And no one feels "less than" because they need a second to process.)

✅ 𝗔𝗹𝘄𝗮𝘆𝘀 𝗳𝗼𝗹𝗹𝗼𝘄 𝘂𝗽 𝗶𝗻 𝘄𝗿𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴
After a long call across time zones, a short summary email helped us stay aligned and gave everyone time to revisit key points.

✅ 𝗕𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝘁𝗮𝘀𝗸 𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁𝘀
Spending some time to talk about music, films, food, traditions and personal interests (life outside work!) gave me an opportunity to connect deeper and understand people’s perspectives.

✅ 𝗠𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗲 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝟭:𝟭𝘀
Especially in remote teams, 1:1 conversations helped me learn about people’s comfort zones, hidden strengths, and challenges they’d never share in a group.

You don’t need to be a culture expert to lead a global team.
But you do need to be intentional.

Because when people feel safe, seen, and supported, they speak up.

And when that happens, everything else improves:
Communication, trust, creativity, outcomes.

If you're working across cultures or leading an international team,
what’s one small thing that’s helped you create more connection?
