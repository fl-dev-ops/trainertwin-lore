Why you “don’t understand native speakers”… even when you know the words 😅

Yesterday we talked about those moments when someone speaks to you in English and you smile, nod, and pretend everything is fine…
while internally thinking:
“What language was THAT?”

Today, let’s uncover one of the biggest reasons behind that feeling:

𝗖𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝗦𝗽𝗲𝗲𝗰𝗵
If you haven’t heard the term before, here’s the simple version:
Connected speech = the way native speakers blend, reduce, and merge words when they speak fast.

And here’s the key:
English is a stress-timed language, not a syllable-timed language (like Italian, Spanish or French).

This means:
✔ Native speakers stress important words
✔ Everything else gets shortened, blended, swallowed
✔ The rhythm becomes irregular
✔ Words melt together like snow in spring

That’s why you can know every word in a sentence… and still not recognise it when someone says it out loud at full native speed.

Let’s look at the 3 main “culprits.”

1️⃣ 𝗟𝗶𝗻𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 (words join together)
When two words connect smoothly as if they were one:
“Go on” → “Go-on”
“Do it” → “Do-it”
“Far away” → “Fara-way”

Suddenly you’re not hearing two words anymore… you’re hearing one long sound.

2️⃣ 𝗔𝘀𝘀𝗶𝗺𝗶𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 (sounds change)
A sound becomes more like the sound next to it:
“Did you” → “Didjoo”
“Could you” → “Coudjoo”
“Don’t you” → “Don’tcha”

It’s English… just not the English from your textbook.

3️⃣ 𝗘𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 (sounds disappear)
Some sounds simply vanish:
“Next week” → “Nex’ week”
“Want to” → “Wanna”
“Going to” → “Gonna”

Yes half the word disappears… and expect you to understand anyway. 😅

So what can you do?
Here’s the good news:
You don’t need to speak super-fast to sound natural.

Start by practicing connected speech slowly.
Say the linked/assimilated/elided version…
but at a comfortable pace.
“Cou-djoo”

Then gradually increase your speed.

Of course, we don't need to sound “native.”
But we want to sound smooth.

And most importantly:
Once you learn how connected speech works, you suddenly hear English differently
and understanding becomes much easier.
