It’s Monday, a new week.

And if you’re a non-native English speaker working in IT or tech… maybe this week feels a little heavier than it should.

Why? Because for so many professionals, English is still the barrier.

Here are some things I hear again and again:
🔹 “Even in my own language I struggle to speak in meetings… in English, it’s even worse.”
🔹 “When I finally get the words, the moment has already passed.”
🔹 “I always feel like my English sounds broken. I lose confidence and keep quiet.”
🔹 “In emails or async communication I’m fine… but in real time, my mind goes blank.”
🔹 “I’ve lost opportunities because my English wasn’t strong enough.”

Where do these struggles come from?

𝗣𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗶𝘀𝗺— waiting until your English is flawless before daring to speak.
“I need to sound like Bill Gates before I can speak”

𝗜𝗺𝗽𝗼𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗿 𝘀𝘆𝗻𝗱𝗿𝗼𝗺𝗲 — believing your ideas aren’t “good enough” to share.
“Me? No, it’s impossible… I’m just “not enough”, seriously”.

𝗟𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗼𝗳 𝗽𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗲 — Only “study” English instead of actually using it in real conversations.
“Give me more past perfect and 3rd conditional, please!”

Unfortunately, there are consequences.
While we're waiting for the “perfect moment”, the world keeps moving.

I’ve been there myself at the beginning of my international career.
And I’ve seen them all:
🚫 Missing out on promotions and leadership roles.
🚫 Avoiding networking opportunities where real connections are built.
🚫 Staying invisible in meetings while colleagues get recognition.
🚫 Watching career opportunities pass by, simply because you stayed quiet.

So, what’s the solution?
Shift your focus:
👉 You don’t need to wait until your English is “perfect.”
👉 You don’t need another grammar book or certification before you speak.
👉 What you need is confidence, practice, and the right strategies for real conversations at work.

That’s where growth begins… not in chasing perfection, but in taking action, even if it feels uncomfortable.

And a world of possibilities will open.

Over to you, now: which of these pain points have you experienced?
