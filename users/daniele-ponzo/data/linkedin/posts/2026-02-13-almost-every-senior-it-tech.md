Almost every Senior IT & Tech professional I work with starts with the same sentence:

“I know what I want to say.”
“It’s clear in my head.”
“But it doesn’t come out right when I speak.”

They assume the problem is:
• vocabulary
• confidence
• speed

It’s not.

The real issue?

They’re thinking like experts
but speaking like they’re still trying to prove themselves.

So they:
– add too much context
– qualify every statement
– lose the main point

In high-stakes workplace English, that costs authority.
And senior professionals feel it immediately.

Yesterday, I received a message from one of my clients.

He said:

💬“I decided to start a freelance career… something I’ve been thinking for a while.
I landed a contract with a large.
The entire backend team is in India.
I did the interview in English.
I passed the interview.
And I got my first client.”

Read that again.

No need to overcomplicate things.
No need to look for perfection

Clear. Structured. Direct.

That’s 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗵𝗶𝗽-𝗹𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻.

Same knowledge.
Same person.
Same English.

Very different authority.

When professionals shift from explaining to positioning…
from qualifying to deciding…
from translating to structuring…

Everything changes.

This is not an English problem.
It’s a credibility problem, not a fluency problem.

And credibility is trainable.
