What do most tech professionals do when they want to improve their English?

They study more.

Courses.
Apps.
Grammar.
Vocabulary.

And yet…

They still freeze in meetings.

At some point, it starts to feel like this:
• you’re experienced… but you sound less sharp (and feel less smart...) than you are
• you know exactly what you mean… but it doesn’t come out clearly
• you’ve studied for years… but without daily use, it feels like you’re losing it

In real work situations, you’re not translating sentences.

You’re:
• structuring ideas on the spot
• reacting to unexpected questions
• simplifying complex topics
• managing how you sound
All in real time.

When language knowledge meets Performance...

And I’ve seen this many times.

Senior professionals who write perfectly…
…go quiet in meetings.
Or start a sentence and lose it halfway.
Or simplify so much that their message loses impact.

And the worst part?

They start questioning their own competence.

So here’s the real question:
👉 What do you rely on when your brain goes blank?
👉 How do you stay sharp if you don’t use English every day?

These are exactly the topics we’ll explore in a live session with Nick Bussola, who works on AI-based systems that help professionals train speaking in realistic scenarios.

We’ll break down:
• why studying more isn’t enough
• what actually causes the freeze moment
• how to stay in control when a word is missing
• how to keep your English active, even without daily meetings
• how AI can support real communication training

If you work in tech and use English in meetings, interviews or presentations…
this conversation is for you.
