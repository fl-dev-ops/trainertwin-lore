𝐀 𝐏𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐜𝐚𝐥 𝐆𝐮𝐢𝐝𝐞 𝐟𝐨𝐫 𝐍𝐨𝐧-𝐍𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐯𝐞 𝐄𝐧𝐠𝐥𝐢𝐬𝐡 𝐒𝐩𝐞𝐚𝐤𝐞𝐫𝐬

I still remember my first days studying in New Zealand as if it were yesterday. A girl from my dorm asked me a question in English, and I had no idea what she was talking about. I panicked. 𝘎𝘰𝘥, 𝘐 𝘸𝘢𝘯𝘵 𝘵𝘰 𝘨𝘰 𝘣𝘢𝘤𝘬 𝘩𝘰𝘮𝘦, I thought.

Reading Daniele Ponzo's book, "𝐄𝐚𝐬𝐲 𝐄𝐧𝐠𝐥𝐢𝐬𝐡 𝐟𝐨𝐫 𝐈𝐓," brought that memory back. Many people who learn English assume—incorrectly—that fluency means speaking with advanced vocabulary and the “correct” accent. During my first weeks, I only smiled and said nothing for fear of saying something “stupid” or speaking with a “funny” accent. I sabotaged my own success by waiting to be perfect.

Daniele understands this struggle. As a native Italian working for many years in a multinational IT corporation, he saw how English works as a global language. He highlights something important: " 𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐭𝐞𝐱𝐭 𝐦𝐚𝐭𝐭𝐞𝐫𝐬 𝐦𝐨𝐫𝐞 𝐭𝐡𝐚𝐧 𝐩𝐞𝐫𝐟𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧". Different IT roles use different terminology, and the only way to grow your vocabulary is by actively using it.

Even though the book is written for IT professionals, many tips apply to everyone. Daniele covers English for interviews, networking, and even public speaking with a clear and logical approach.

At the end of the day, speaking with clarity, purpose, and engagement beats perfection every time.
