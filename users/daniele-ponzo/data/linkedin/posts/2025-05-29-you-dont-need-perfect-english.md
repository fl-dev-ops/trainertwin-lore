You don’t need perfect English to have a powerful voice.

I’ve been reading 𝗟𝗼𝘀𝘁 𝗶𝗻 𝗧𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 by Eva Hoffman, and I have to say
I’ve never felt so seen as a language learner📖.

In the book, Eva moves from Poland to America, and what struck me most wasn’t the cultural change, but the silence.

The way she describes trying to express complex thoughts in English… and nothing comes out right.

How English felt “foreign, disjointed,” unable to carry the weight of who she truly was inside.

How she’d freeze in social moments because she didn’t quite understand the tone, the rhythm, the unspoken rules.

And yet, she kept writing. 
Kept speaking. 
Kept building sentence by sentence.

Her story reminded me of this truth:

You don’t arrive at confidence in English by mastering grammar or memorizing the right phrases.

You build it slowly. Word by word. Mistake by mistake.

You reclaim your voice in a new language the way Eva did 
not by chasing perfection, but by refusing to stay silent.

And one day, you look back and realize:

That voice you were searching for?

You were building it the whole time.

✍️“It’s not that we all want to speak the King’s English, but whether we speak Appalachian or Harlem English, or Cockney, or Jamaican Creole, we want to be at home in our tongue. We want to be able to give voice accurately and fully to ourselves and our sense of the world.”
