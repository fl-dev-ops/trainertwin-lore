You’ve probably experienced this.

Someone says something in a meeting.

The words are clear.

But the reaction is… off.

Silence.
Tension.
Misinterpretation.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth:

In international teams, communication breakdown rarely starts with vocabulary.

It starts with invisible layers:

– Linguistic friction
– Cultural coding
– Interpretation bias
– Psychological safety
– Perceived authority

I work with senior IT professionals who are technically brilliant.

But when they speak in English:

• They soften too much
• Or sound harsher than intended
• Or lose structure under pressure
• Or get perceived as less confident than they are

And this is not related to competence.

Performance in a second language changes delivery.

And delivery changes perception.

In the next "People Developers' Playground roundtable, we’ll unpack this from four angles:
– Language as a performance tool under pressure: Daniele Ponzo
– Cognitive & cultural interpretation patterns: Nadege Welsch
– Leadership clarity and meeting design: Caroline Jacob
– Measurable human dynamics: Kasia Grabda

So, when it comes to working in an international environment “speaking better English” is not enough.
There are invisible barriers to performance.
And we need to remove them.
