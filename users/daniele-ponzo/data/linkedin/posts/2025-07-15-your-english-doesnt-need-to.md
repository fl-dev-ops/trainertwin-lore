Your English doesn’t need to be broader. It needs to be sharper.

The Cambridge Dictionary defines “𝗵𝗼𝗻𝗲” as:
“𝘛𝘰 𝘮𝘢𝘬𝘦 𝘢𝘯 𝘰𝘣𝘫𝘦𝘤𝘵 𝘴𝘩𝘢𝘳𝘱; 𝘵𝘰 𝘮𝘢𝘬𝘦 𝘴𝘰𝘮𝘦𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘱𝘭𝘦𝘵𝘦𝘭𝘺 𝘴𝘶𝘪𝘵𝘢𝘣𝘭𝘦 𝘧𝘰𝘳 𝘪𝘵𝘴 𝘱𝘶𝘳𝘱𝘰𝘴𝘦.”

That’s exactly how I think about English for professionals.

You don’t need to know every word.
You need the right words sharpened for the job you do, the meetings you lead, the emails you send, and the clients you manage.

You 𝗵𝗼𝗻𝗲 your communication when you stop focusing on abstract grammar…
 …and start learning the vocabulary, expressions, and sentence patterns that fit your world.

▶️  Need to lead a meeting? Learn expressions like:
 "Let’s circle back,"
 "Can we align on this?"
 or "I’d like to flag something important."

▶️ Preparing for a presentation? Work on transitions like:
 “Let’s move to the next point…”
 “Here’s what this means for your team…”
 “Let me give you a quick example.”

▶️ Negotiating a deal? Hone conditional grammar and polite forms:
 “If we were to reduce delivery time, would the budget allow flexibility?”
 “I wonder if we could explore an alternative option.”

▶️ Interview coming up? Practice past tenses and storytelling techniques to talk about your experience with structure and confidence.

This is what it means to hone your English. 
You study, but also sharpen your skills for your context.

That’s the “H” in my HACK strategy.
