Don’t make this mistake when you talk about your feelings.

It’s something I notice all the time.

And it can be the cause of communication breakdown.

The difference between "-ing" and "-ed" adjectives in English.

For example:

“I’m working on a very 𝒆𝒙𝒄𝒊𝒕__ project”.

Do we say exciting or excited?

Yes, the project is exciting. And I am excited because I’m working on that project.

EXCIT𝐈𝐍𝐆: the cause
EXCIT𝐄D: personal feeling

There are several adjectives in English which follow the same pattern:

⭕ Excited vs. Exciting:
Example: "I'm excited about the upcoming product launch." (personal feeling)
Example: "This new feature is exciting for our users." (causing excitement)

⭕ Interested vs. Interesting:
Example: "I'm interested in exploring new market trends." (personal feeling)
Example: "The keynote speaker shared interesting insights during the conference." (capturing attention)

⭕ Satisfied vs. Satisfying:
Example: "The customer was satisfied with our service." (personal feeling)
Example: "Providing excellent customer service is satisfying for our team." (causing satisfaction)

⭕ Disappointed vs. Disappointing:
Example: "I was truly disappointed with the delay in project delivery." (personal feeling)
Example: "The quarterly results were disappointing, falling short of our expectations." (causing disappointment)

⭕ Bored vs. Boring:
Example: "I was bored in the meeting without relevant discussions." (personal feeling)
Example: "Repetitive tasks can be boring for employees." (causing boredom)

⭕ Surprised vs. Surprising:
Example: "I was surprised by the unexpected announcement." (personal feeling)
Example: "The sudden increase in sales was surprising." (causing surprise)

⭕ Motivated vs. Motivating:
Example: "I'm motivated to achieve my career goals." (personal feeling)
Example: "Recognition can be motivating for employees." (causing motivation)

⭕ Focused vs. Focusing:
Example: "I remained focused during the entire project." (personal feeling)
Example: "Setting clear goals is essential for focusing on tasks." (engaging attention)

⭕ Relaxed vs. Relaxing:
Example: "I felt relaxed after completing the project." (personal feeling)
Example: "Taking short breaks can be relaxing during busy workdays." (providing relaxation)

⭕ Exhausted vs. Exhausting:
Example: "I was exhausted after the long hours of work." (personal feeling)
Example: "Working on complex issues can be exhausting." (causing exhaustion)

⚠ Be aware of some exceptions: stressed/stressful; offended/offensive; impressed/impressive.

Understanding the nuances between these adjectives is crucial for effective communication.
