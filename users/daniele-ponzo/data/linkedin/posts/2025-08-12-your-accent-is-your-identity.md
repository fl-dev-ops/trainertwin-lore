Your accent is your identity. Don’t erase it. Own it.

But what if the way you say something gets misunderstood — again and again?

In Episode 29 of The Non-Native Leader - Podcast, I sat down with Karuna Dwivedi, a passionate English language specialist, doctoral candidate, and international teacher who moved from India to the U.S. to help multilingual students find their voice.

Yes, we talked about grammar and language learning.

Here is the heart of the conversation:
🌍 Feeling misjudged for your English — even when it’s correct
🌍 Navigating the emotional weight of accent and belonging
🌍 Relearning how to communicate across new norms and systems

At one point, Karuna shared a moment that stuck with me:
“I didn’t speak wrong — I just spoke differently. But that pause, that second-guessing... it makes you shrink.”

If you’ve ever:
👉 Repeated yourself because of your pronunciation
👉 Felt like your skills were being overlooked because of your accent
👉 Questioned your worth in a meeting, even though you knew your stuff
…this episode will speak to you.

What I loved most about our chat?
Karuna doesn’t just teach English.
She brings empathy, cultural awareness, and honesty into the classroom and into the world of work.

English is a skill, sure. It goes on your CV.
But more than that, It’s an emotional journey filled with doubt, adaptation, and resilience.
