Demystifying Business Jargon #18: " It’s on my Radar".

Seriously, this exists.

I’ve often heard executives and managers using the phrase, “It’s on my radar.”📟 But what does it actually mean? 

And, more important, how does it rank on the “most hated business jargon” chart?

Let’s break it down so you can understand the next time you hear it.

What Does "It's on My Radar" Mean?

When someone says, “It’s on my radar,” they mean that they are aware of an issue, task, or project and are keeping an eye on it. 

They want to say that while the item might not be the top priority at the moment, it’s being monitored and will be addressed in due time.

It’s a subtle way to indicate that you are on top of things without committing to immediate action.

Examples

➡ Project Monitoring:

Manager: “Have you seen the latest issues in the bug tracking system?”

You: “Yes, those bugs are on my radar. I’m planning to address them after we complete the current sprint.”

➡Feature Requests:

Team Member: “When will we start working on the new security feature?”

You: “That feature request is on my radar. We’ll prioritize it in our next planning meeting.”

It can be useful to show that you are aware and proactive. And it’s a great way to manage expectations without overcommitting. 

Here are a few tips on using it:

✔ Acknowledge Awareness: Let your team know you are aware of their concerns or requests.

✔Set Expectations: Indicate that while it’s not an immediate priority, it will be addressed.

✔Follow-Up: Make sure to actually follow up on the items that are on your radar to maintain trust and reliability.

⚠ Be careful!

There are similar expressions which have a different meaning:

“Under the radar” or “Off the radar”: something is happening quietly, unnoticed and ignored.

For example: “Your business has grown under the radar, without you investing in ads or public campaigns. Well done!”
