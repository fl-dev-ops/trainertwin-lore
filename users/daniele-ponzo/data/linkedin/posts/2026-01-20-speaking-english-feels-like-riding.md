“Speaking English feels like riding a bike.
 A backwards one.”

Let me explain.

When we’re kids, we learn our native language the same way we learn to ride a bike🚲.

At the same time, our brain learns to:
- keep balance
- pedal
- steer
- react automatically

No grammar explanations.
No theory.
Just practice, mistakes, repetition.

Now imagine this.

You take a bike you’ve been riding for years…
and change 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴.

When you turn the handlebar right, the wheel goes left.
When you turn left, the wheel goes right.

Same bike.
Same pedals.
Same balance.

And suddenly… you can’t ride anymore.

That’s exactly what happens when you speak a second language.
You know the rules.
You studied pronunciation.
You understand English.

But your mouth, tongue, sounds, rhythm, and patterns are now wired differently.

Your brain says:
❌ “This doesn’t match the old algorithm.”
❌ “Something feels off.”
❌ “Stop. This is risky.”

So you freeze.
You feel awkward.
You hesitate.

In the famous backwards bicycle experiment, it took an adult 8 months of daily practice to rewire the brain.
A child did it in 2 weeks.
Why?

👉 Neuroplasticity.

Adults can learn.
But first, we have to 𝘂𝗻𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻.
That’s why:

- knowing grammar isn’t enough
- understanding English doesn’t equal speaking it
- confidence doesn’t come from rules, but from repetition

The good news?
Your brain can adapt.

But only if you:
- practice regularly
- accept discomfort
- allow mistakes
- stay consistent long enough for the “click” to happen

So next time you think:
 “Why is this so hard for me?”

Remember:
You’re not bad at English.
You’re just learning to ride a 𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗱𝘀 𝗯𝗶𝗸𝗲.

And that takes patience, not perfection.

Are you giving your brain time to rewire or quitting before it clicks?
