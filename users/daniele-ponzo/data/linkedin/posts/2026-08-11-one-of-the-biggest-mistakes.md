One of the biggest mistakes I see in technical meetings...

...isn't bad English.

It's trying to explain everything before making your point.

I see it all the time.

An engineer is asked a simple question.

Instead of answering it, they start with:

the background
the history
the architecture
the edge cases
the exceptions

Five minutes later...
everyone is still waiting for the answer.

Here's a simple technique I recommend.

Instead of thinking:
"What do I need to explain?"

Think:
Point → Reason → Detail

For example:
❌
"We've been reviewing the system over the last few weeks and we've identified a few issues that might create some problems in production..."

✅
"I recommend delaying the release by one week. We still have two critical issues in the payment flow."

Now people know:
• your position
• why it matters
• where the conversation is going

The interesting part?

This doesn't mean generically "improve our English".

But it meansreducing the effort people need to understand you.

We have a prejudice that simple communication is junior.
In reality, the opposite is true!

That's one of the reasons I decided to write Easy English for IT.

Not another grammar book.

A practical guide for tech professionals who want to communicate more clearly in meetings, presentations and international workplaces.

If you'd like more practical techniques like this, you'll find them throughout the book.
