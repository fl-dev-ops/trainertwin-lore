'My English is good, but I have problems when I need to speak'.
 
 
Sound familiar?🤔
 
This is one of the most common problems I hear.
 
We have some amazing ideas, but sometimes we are not confident enough to give our contribution in:
 
✔ Meetings
✔ Conference calls
✔ Social situations
 
And we have great skills but feel we can’t get the message across.😒
 
We can’t explain our job role.
 
We can’t tell potential customers what our startup does.
 
That’s exactly how I used to feel when I started working for an international company.
 
I could not take active part in my meetings.
 
I was struggling to get my voice heard.
 
I was missing out on the opportunities.
 
The turning point?
 
Draft a plan and stick to it.
 
Make it SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-Bound) so it’s easier to commit.
 
This is what worked really well for me:
 
1. 𝐏𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐜𝐞 𝐒𝐩𝐞𝐚𝐤𝐢𝐧𝐠: Engage in daily conversations in English, both inside and outside the workplace (use chats, audio messaging platforms, AI), ➡ so it becomes natural.
 
2. 𝐄𝐱𝐩𝐚𝐧𝐝 𝐕𝐨𝐜𝐚𝐛𝐮𝐥𝐚𝐫𝐲: Make a habit of writing down new vocabulary encountered during meetings, presentations, or discussions,➡ so you will make them yours.
 
3. 𝐋𝐢𝐬𝐭𝐞𝐧 𝐚𝐧𝐝 𝐌𝐢𝐦𝐢𝐜: Podcasts, TED Talks, Videos, managers at work. Try to replicate accents, intonation, and pronunciation➡ so you can unlock the essential elements of the language.
 
4. 𝐑𝐞𝐜𝐨𝐫𝐝 𝐚𝐧𝐝 𝐑𝐞𝐯𝐢𝐞𝐰 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫𝐬𝐞𝐥𝐟: Online meetings, presentations, or live practice sessions. Review the recordings to identify areas of improvement (pronunciation, fluency, or grammar), ➡so you don’t make the same mistake twice.
 
5. 𝐑𝐨𝐥𝐞-𝐏𝐥𝐚𝐲 𝐖𝐨𝐫𝐤 𝐒𝐜𝐞𝐧𝐚𝐫𝐢𝐨𝐬: Practice client meetings, presentations, or negotiations through role-play. 
This is where coaching can make a difference. Role-Play is crucial to gain confidence and receive immediate feedback on your language skills.
 
P.S. Remember this is a journey, so set achievable goals, celebrate your wins, no matter how small, and embrace mistakes (they are a natural part of the learning process).🌈
