How to nail your next behavioural interview if English is not your 1st language.

All you have to do is shoot for a STAR 🚀 ⭐ .

Using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) can help you structure your answers effectively.

Take time to prepare highlighting the context, the challenges, the steps you took and the final outcome.

Here are 4 examples of behavioural questions and sample answers ⬇ 

1. Tell Me About a Time You Solved a Complex Problem.

𝐒𝐢𝐭𝐮𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: Our e-commerce website was experiencing frequent downtime, impacting sales.
𝐓𝐚𝐬𝐤: As the lead developer, I had to identify the root cause and implement a solution quickly.
𝐀𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: I conducted a deep analysis, discovered that the server’s capacity was being exceeded during peak hours, and upgraded the server infrastructure and optimized the code.
𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐮𝐥𝐭: This led to a 99.9% uptime and a 15% increase in sales during peak periods.

2. Describe a Situation Where You Worked Under Pressure.

𝐒𝐢𝐭𝐮𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: During a major software rollout, we found a critical bug just hours before the launch.
𝐓𝐚𝐬𝐤: As the QA lead, I needed to resolve the issue to avoid delaying the launch.
𝐀𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: I quickly assembled a team, identified the bug’s source, and implemented a fix. We also ran additional tests to ensure stability.
𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐮𝐥𝐭: We successfully launched on time, and the software performed well, earning praise from both clients and upper management.

3. Give an Example of When You Took the Initiative.

𝐒𝐢𝐭𝐮𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: Our IT team was struggling with managing multiple projects simultaneously.
𝐓𝐚𝐬𝐤: I saw an opportunity to improve our workflow.
𝐀𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: I proposed and implemented a project management tool to better track and prioritize tasks.
𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐮𝐥𝐭: This increased our productivity by 30% and allowed us to meet deadlines more consistently.

4. How Do You Handle a Disagreement with a Colleague?

𝐒𝐢𝐭𝐮𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: I had a disagreement with a colleague over the best approach to optimize database performance.
𝐓𝐚𝐬𝐤: We needed to reach a consensus to move forward efficiently.
𝐀𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧: I scheduled a meeting to discuss our viewpoints, presented data supporting my approach, and listened to my colleague’s concerns. We then collaborated on a hybrid solution.
𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐮𝐥𝐭: The compromise improved database performance by 20%, and our collaborative effort improved team spirit.

Remember to:

-     Keep it relevant.
-     Prepare a few good examples.
-     You are the STAR: showcase your work.
-     Rehearse and practise.
