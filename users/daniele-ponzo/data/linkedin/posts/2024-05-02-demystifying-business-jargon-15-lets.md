Demystifying Business Jargon #15: Let's Get the Ball Rolling!

In my meetings at work, I used to hear the phrase "get the ball rolling" quite often⚽. 

But what does it really mean and how can it benefit us? Let's find out:

"Get the ball rolling" is a common business jargon that means to start or initiate something. 

It's about taking that crucial first step to kickstart a project, launch an initiative, or spark a discussion.

For IT managers and team leaders, getting the ball rolling is essential for driving progress and innovation in our organizations. 

Here are a couple of examples of how we can use this concept in our day-to-day work:

Example in a Team Meeting:
👉 "Alright team, let's get the ball rolling on our latest project. I'll assign tasks and set deadlines to ensure we're off to a strong start."

Example in a Strategy Session:
👉"We've discussed the potential benefits of implementing AI technology. Now, let's get the ball rolling by scheduling a demo with our vendors to explore our options further."

Not surprisingly, this expression (like many business idioms) comes from the world of sport, particularly the game of croquet. Croquet came into existence in Britain in the mid-1850s.

The website “Theidioms” reports a different story: “In the American presidential election of the year 1840, the phrase in discussion came into existence. General William Harrison used a tin and leather ball which was ten feet in diameter as his victory ball. This was pushed by his supporters from one town to another, as in, to keep the ball rolling”.

This expression is essential if you want to take action, drive results, and achieve success.
