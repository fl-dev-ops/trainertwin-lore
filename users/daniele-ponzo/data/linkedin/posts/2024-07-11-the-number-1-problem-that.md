The number 1 problem that keeps you from advancing your career.


It’s called language.

And in today’s world, English is THE language.

Whether you’re working in a big multinational company, or you want to have a career on the global stage, you need to have a good level.

And even if you think you don’t need to level up right now, there is always a chance that:

➡ your company becomes international (I saw it with some of my clients who were caught in a big company merge).

➡ you get promoted and your role now needs global exposure (a client of mine is now in this situation).

➡ your boss asks you to expand the market and your clients are now French, German and English (from a conversation I had with someone interested in my program, not a client yet).

What usually happens is that you think you’re ok with your current level.

Maybe you studied at school back in the days.

Or you are consistent on Duolingo.

But the reality is different.

❌ The first time you’re in a meeting everyone is too fast, and you get lost.

❌ When you take that international call, you can only reply “yes! or “no” (50% chance of getting it right).

❌ If your manager asks you to present your solution you start panicking.

And all you want to do is disappear, to avoid embarrassing situations. This happened to me a good few years back.

But I’m not a fan of getting embarrassed. And I’m sure you aren’t either.

There is a way to avoid this problem.

Focus on what you REALLY need to shine at work.

The situations, the strategies, the language that people use in the real world. 

This is why I sought the support of a mentor back in my days in IT and I could level up my game.

So, the question is: can you afford to invest in yourself?

👎 No: that’s ok. There are things you can do for free right now (courses, peer support, online communities). If you are self-motivated and well organized, you can do it.

👍 Yes: don’t delay it. Be prepared. Guidance and support can truly make a difference for your career (and save you from embarrassing situations later). 

Let me know what you think.
