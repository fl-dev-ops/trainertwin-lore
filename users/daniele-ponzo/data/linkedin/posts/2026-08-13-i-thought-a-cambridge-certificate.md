I thought a Cambridge certificate would change my career.

It didn't.

22 years ago, I was working in an entry-level Service Delivery role at an American multinational in Dublin.

Like many people, I thought I knew the formula.

Improve your English.

Pass a prestigious exam.

Wait for the opportunities.

So I enrolled in a traditional English course.

Spent months studying grammar.

Passed a Cambridge exam.

I still remember walking into the office with that certificate.

I genuinely thought:

"This changes everything."

It didn't.

The months went by...

and I watched colleagues with less "perfect" English get the opportunities I wanted.

They led client meetings.
They joined international projects.
They were promoted.

Meanwhile...

I kept waiting until my English felt perfect.

Looking back, I realise I wasn't held back by grammar.

I was held back by pressure.

I was trying to build perfect sentences instead of sharing clear ideas.

The people who progressed weren't speaking perfect English.

They were communicating with confidence.
They spoke up.
They kept things simple.
They accepted that making the occasional mistake was better than saying nothing at all.

That changed the way I looked at communication forever.

And it's one of the main reasons I decided to write 𝗘𝗮𝘀𝘆 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗜𝗧.

I wanted to create the practical guide I wish someone had given me back then.

Not another grammar book.

A book for software engineers, IT professionals and international teams who want to communicate more clearly when the pressure is on.

Here's one small challenge for your next meeting:

Don't wait for the perfect sentence.

Speak once.

Even if it's as simple as:
"My recommendation is..."

or
"I agree because..."
Confidence doesn't appear first.

Participation does.
