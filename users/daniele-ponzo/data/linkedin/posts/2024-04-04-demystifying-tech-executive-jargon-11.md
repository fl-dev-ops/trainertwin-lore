Demystifying Tech Executive Jargon #11: "Sh*t Hits the Fan" 

The world of business can be harsh.

And not always polite.

I know we don’t normally use bad words over here, but I used to hear this expression every day in internal meetings, calls and 1 to 1’s (luckily, not in front of the clients 🙂 ).

When we say "sh*t hits the fan," we're referring to a situation where everything suddenly goes wrong or becomes chaotic. 

It's often used to describe moments of crisis or unexpected challenges that require immediate attention and action.

Here are some examples relevant to IT managers:

"During the software deployment, the sh*t hit the fan when we discovered a critical bug that caused the system to crash."

"We were in the middle of an important client presentation when the sh*t hit the fan and the power went out, causing us to lose all our progress."

“We need a backup plan in case sh*t hits the fan.”
 
As you can imagine, it is not easy to find written sources for this idiom, but “phrases.org.uk” reports the earliest example of it in print in Gyrene a novel by the American writer W. D. Jones, circa 1943.

Funny enough, before becoming a writer, William Daniel Jones was part of the “Bonnie and Clyde” crime gang🦹…

So, I’m not a fan (pun intended)🥁 of bad words myself, but we need to be aware that this type of language is actually quite common.

Understanding this jargon is crucial in navigating high-pressure situations and effectively communicating with your team.
