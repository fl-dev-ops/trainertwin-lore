One missing letter nearly changed my career.

The letter?

𝗔.

A few weeks after moving to Dublin, I got a call from a recruitment agency.

"We have an interesting opportunity for you."

For someone whose work experience consisted mainly of waiting tables, working as a hotel porter, and doing a bit of data entry at university...

that phone call felt huge.

An IT role.
An American multinational.
An international team.

I wanted it more than anything.

I prepared obsessively.

Suit.
Tie.
Copies of my CV.

I met the recruiter, went through the technical interview, the behavioural interview, and everything went well.

Then she explained how to get to the company's HR interview.

This was long before Google Maps lived in our pockets.

So out came the pen and paper.

"Take bus 38 from the city centre."

Simple enough.

On the day of the interview, I arrived at the bus stop early.
I checked my watch every few minutes.
Got on the number 38.
Relaxed.

Twenty minutes passed.

No business park.

Twenty-five minutes.

Still nothing.

At thirty minutes, I finally walked up to the driver.

"Excuse me... when do we get to the business park?"

He looked at me.

Then smiled.

"Oh... this bus doesn't go there."

"You needed the 𝟯𝟴𝗔."

The 𝗔.

One missing letter.

That tiny detail left me about three kilometres from the business park.
On a road I'd never seen before.
Mostly uphill.

It was already 1:40 pm.
The interview was at 2.

So I did the only thing I could do.

I ran.

Imagine a man in a suit and tie sprinting uphill through an industrial estate in Dublin.

That was me.

At the time, it wasn't funny.
In my head, I'd already lost the job.

I'd only just moved to Ireland.
This felt like the opportunity that could change everything.

And I was about to miss it...

because of one letter.

Then something happened I'll never forget.

A car slowed down beside me.

The driver rolled down the window.

"Are you alright?"

I gave him what was probably the fastest explanation of my life.

He smiled.

"Get in."

A complete stranger drove me the rest of the way.

At 𝟭:𝟱𝟴 𝗽𝗺, we pulled into the business park.

I jumped out.

Straightened my tie.

Took one deep breath.

Tried to pretend I hadn't just run what felt like a half marathon.

Walked to reception.

"Good afternoon.
I'm here for the interview."

"Perfect," the receptionist smiled.

"Please take a seat.
The hiring manager will be with you in a moment."

I had made it.

And yes...

I got the job.

Looking back, that afternoon reminds me that careers are rarely built through perfect plans alone.

Sometimes they're built through persistence.
Sometimes through luck.

And sometimes because a complete stranger decides to stop their car and help someone they've never met.

I've never forgotten that kindness.

And every time I drive past someone who looks like they might need a hand...

I think about that afternoon in Dublin.

What's one small moment of kindness that unexpectedly changed the course of your career?
