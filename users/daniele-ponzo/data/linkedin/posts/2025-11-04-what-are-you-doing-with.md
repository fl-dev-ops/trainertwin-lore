What are you doing with your language?

“I studied English for 8 years at school.”
“I can’t speak English in a meeting.”

Sounds like a paradox?
It is.

But it’s also the reality for so many professionals I speak with.

The truth is, our school system often has a flipped approach to language.
Think about it.

When you’re a baby, you start speaking first.
You spend months listening, absorbing, trying, failing… and trying again.
You make mistakes, but nobody cares — because people want to connect with you.
They build bridges 🌉.
Later, you learn the rules.
Grammar. Structure. Vocabulary.

But when you learn a language at school, it’s the opposite:
You start from the rules.
From the grammar.
From the fear of being corrected.
And when you finally get to speak, “the authority” is there to rate, correct, and point out your mistakes.
That’s when the walls start rising 🧱.

The result?

Self-conscious learners who can read and write… but freeze when it’s time to speak.

So how do we change this?

There’s only one way 👇

👉 Start speaking today.
Get comfortable with your first steps, even the wobbly ones.

Mistakes aren’t failure. They’re proof that you’re learning.

Here’s how to start small:
🔵 Shadow listening → Imitate pronunciation and intonation (podcasts, music, videos)
🔵 Record yourself → Notice progress and pronunciation patterns
🔵 Relax → Deep breathing or visualization to calm your nerves before speaking
🔵 Set realistic goals → Ask a question today. Speak in a meeting tomorrow. 

Small wins build confidence.

Remember: communication is about building bridges, not walls.

And those bridges are built one word at a time.

What’s one small step you can take today to start speaking more confidently?
