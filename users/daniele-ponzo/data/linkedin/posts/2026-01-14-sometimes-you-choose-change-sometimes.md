Sometimes you choose change.
Sometimes it’s forced on you.

A new country.
A new culture.
A new language.

And suddenly, everything that used to feel natural… doesn’t.

Your way of working.
Your way of communicating.
Even your sense of who you are.

Change is rarely comfortable.
Especially when it wasn’t part of the plan.

What people don’t talk about enough is this part:
You need to adapt for practical reasons, sure.
But change impacts you at a personal level too.

New environments.
Different expectations.
Moments of doubt, discomfort, and not fully belonging yet.

And still
it is possible to navigate all of this with curiosity, resilience, and even a smile.
Not in a motivational-quote way.
In real life.

That’s exactly what we talk about in the latest episode of The Non-Native Leader - Podcast.

My guest is Mely Lerman, founder of 𝗖𝗢𝗕𝗢𝗟 𝗧𝗼𝗼𝗹𝗸𝗶𝘁.
We met in Milan during Codemotion, and what started as a casual conversation quickly turned into something much deeper.

We talked about:
🔹 working across countries and cultures
🔹adapting without losing yourself
🔹embracing change instead of resisting it
🔹finding meaning even when you didn’t choose the situation
🔹staying human in highly technical environments

I genuinely learned a lot from Mely’s story and from the calm, grounded way he approaches change, work, and life.

This episode is for you if:
▶️ you work internationally (or want to)
▶️ you’ve moved countries—or are about to
▶️ you feel the discomfort of “not fully belonging yet”
▶️ you’re learning how to adapt without giving up who you are

Life doesn’t wait until everything feels stable.
But you can learn how to move forward while things are still uncertain.
