If you’re senior in your role but still “explain too much”, this is for you.

You’re trusted technically.
People come to you when things break.
Your expertise isn’t questioned.

And yet…

In meetings, you notice something uncomfortable.

You speak.
But people don’t follow.

You explain carefully.
But someone else summarises and gets the nod.

You give context.
But the discussion moves on without you.

This happens to many Senior IT & Tech professionals, especially in high-stakes workplace English.

Not because they lack confidence.
Not because their English is “bad”.

But because authority isn’t carried by explanations alone.
In leadership rooms, credibility, not fluency is what people respond to.

And credibility sounds different.

When leadership-level communication is missing, senior professionals often:
- over-explain to prove competence
- soften points to avoid friction
- wait until everything is “clear” before speaking

The result?

They are trusted…
but don’t own the room.

Present…
but not followed.

Expert…
but still invisible.

In 𝗵𝗶𝗴𝗵-𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗰𝗲 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵, authority comes from clarity, positioning, and direction. 
Not from how much you explain.

This is the mirror moment.

The one where you realise:
Your seniority is recognised on paper.
But leadership-level communication hasn’t fully caught up yet.

And when that gap exists…

English didn’t hold you back.
Communication did.
