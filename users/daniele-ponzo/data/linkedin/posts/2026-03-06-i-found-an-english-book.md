I found an English book from 1928.
It might teach us something we forgot in 2026.

It’s called “𝙇’𝙄𝙩𝙖𝙡𝙞𝙖𝙣𝙤 𝙚 𝙡’𝙞𝙣𝙜𝙡𝙚𝙨𝙚.”
“Theoretical and practical method for Italians who want to learn English”.

Before Duolingo, before YouTube, before AI…

First, it starts by stating that 
“the most difficult thing for whoever studies the English language is the pronunciation”

And I believe we can hardly disagree.
Especially for people in Italy 100 years ago.
No videos, no exposure to the language, no BBC.

Then, it does something fascinating.
It writes English words the way Italians would actually pronounce them:

My father (mai fathoe)
A daughter (dotoe)
Time (taim)
Ship (scip)
She should (sci sciud)
Island (ailand)

And full phrases like:

« Pliis, ghiv mi uoen or tu teligrafik forms and e pen tu rait a messedg »

I found it very direct, but also incredibly effective
A person with zero exposure could get surprisingly close to correct pronunciation.

And then I thought about 2026.

My niece is studying English at school.

Zero speaking.
Lots of grammar.
Test after test.

She’s learning to spell English.
But she’s unlearning how it sounds.
Because it makes it easier for her to remember the correct answer in the exam.

Somewhere along the way, we started prioritising written correctness over spoken clarity.
And maybe that’s why pronunciation still feels hard.

So, I imagine this dialogue between a man from 1928 (Mr Genzardi) and my niece:
-         Mr Genzardi : hau is thi uethoe?
-         My niece: Te veater is good
-         Mr Genzardi: It bighins tu be hot, and ui scial scioertli go tu thi lek ov Como
-         My niece: I tink it is vindi today

What a mess…

I’d like to have your opinion:

Are we improving English today or just passing tests?
