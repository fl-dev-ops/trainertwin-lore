“You have a dream”. But what about realizing it?

When it comes to language improvement, It can get complicated

🔵 Defining a clear objective
🔵 Making the tasks enjoyable
🔵 Building up a new routine

You may hit roadblocks.

Start running in circles 🔄 .

And your language, your job, your career and your desires are affected.

Why?

You might be experiencing one of these:

🔴 Procrastination (I’ll start next week, next month, next year…)
🔴 Fear (new habits, what if they’re too hard?)
🔴 Disillusion (I’ve failed too many times, this is not for me)
🔴 Lack of strategy (where do I start? Will I be able to keep up?)

So, this is your chance to reflect.

And to identify the #1 issue that is stopping you from realizing your dream.

If you want, share your idea in the (anonymous) poll below📊.

Take this opportunity to start facing your obstacles.
And begin to work on the solution.

P.S. if your answer is not in the list, feel free to add it in the comments below.

I’d be happy to chat and share some ideas with you 🤝 .

> As a Non-Native English speaker, I relied heavily on strategies to start communicating and being more visible:
>  
>  🔹 Be ok with ok: I started speaking even though my English level was far from perfect. 
>  ↳ I gave myself weekly objectives, like preparing a quick update to share in a team meeting. 
>  
>  🔹 Mimic: I took the habit of observing “influencers” at work: managers, mentors, senior leaders, great communicators. 
>  ↳ My goal was to get the best intonation, posture, body language.
>  
>  🔹 Polish: I consciously decided to refine my language. Learned the differences between “common” English and “Professional” English. 
>  ↳ I wanted to be ready to communicate in every context.
>  
>   🔹Repetition: I “trained” my communication skills by repeating the phrases and the language I needed in the office. 
>  ↳ I wanted to become confident in my meetings, and comfortable when presenting in front of the customer.
>
