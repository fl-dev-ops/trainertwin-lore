English doesn’t need another slot in your calendar.

It needs a place in your real work.

Many IT professionals already spend their days learning.

New technologies.
New frameworks.
New tools.
New ways of working.
New expectations around AI.

And then there’s English.

Another thing to study.
Another skill to improve.
Another task on an already crowded to-do list.

So you set ambitious goals.

“I’ll study English for one hour every day.”
“I’ll finally master all those grammar rules.”
“I’ll become fluent in three months.”

And maybe it works for a while.

Then life happens.

A deadline appears.

A sprint becomes stressful.

A production issue takes over your week.

Motivation drops.

And English goes back to the bottom of the list.

Unfortunately, free time and motivation are unreliable.

Here’s a different way to think about it.

Instead of asking:
“How much English should I study?”

Ask:
“Where can I add small English reps to what I already do?”

With the goal of becoming more comfortable using it.

Especially when you need to explain your ideas at work.

A few examples:

→ After watching a tech talk, explain the key idea out loud in 60 seconds.
→ After a code review, summarise one decision in English.
→ Before a meeting, prepare one sentence you want to contribute.
→ After listening to a podcast, record a short voice note with your opinion.
→ When reading documentation, explain the problem it solves in your own words.
→ When you disagree with an idea, practise saying why clearly and respectfully.

Nothing revolutionary.

But that’s the point.

You’re building small communication reps into your normal week.

And those reps matter.

That’s the skill that helps you speak in meetings.
Explain technical decisions.
Join conversations with international colleagues.
Share your opinion without overthinking every sentence.

Of course, improving your English still takes effort.

You still need feedback.
You still need deliberate practice.
You still need correction sometimes.

But it doesn’t always have to feel like homework.

My recommendation?

Reduce the friction.

Make practice easier to repeat.

And find a way for English to keep showing up in the work you already do.

Consistency ≠ more pressure.

Consistency = a better system.

What’s one small English rep you could attach to something you already do this week?
