7 fundamental pieces of English grammar you need to master if you want to work internationally.


1/ Polite question forms, so you sound more professional and get help and results.

→"Could you please provide an update on the status of the server migration?"

2/ Past simple and questions about the past, so you can dive deep and find out about events.

→"What issues did you encounter during the last system upgrade?"

3/ Future: will vs. going to, so you can talk about planned and unplanned decisions.

→"I will attend the cybersecurity conference next week." (Unplanned decision made at the moment of speaking).
→"We are going to implement the new software update on Friday." (Planned decision).

4/ Comparatives and superlatives, so you have the essential language to evaluate proposals, solutions and offers.

→"This new software solution is more efficient than the one we currently use, and it offers the best performance we've seen so far."

5/ Present Perfect to give you what you need to talk about your experiences, work history and achievements when presenting yourself in a job interview.

→"I have managed several large-scale IT projects successfully, improving system performance by 30%."

6/ Have to/should, so you can confidently talk about your current role, your tasks and commitments.

→"I have to complete the network security audit by Friday, and I should review the new compliance regulations."

7/ Conditionals (Real and Hypothetical) to be able to negotiate and evaluate pros and cons.

→First Conditional: "If we upgrade our servers, we will increase our system's efficiency."
→Second Conditional: "If I were in charge of the project, I would prioritize updating the cybersecurity protocols."
