I heard the same sentence over and over again this week.

Not exactly the same words.

But the same problem.

At We Make Future, I spoke with founders, engineers, consultants, and business owners from different industries.

Many of them told me some version of this:

"I know English.
But speaking is different."

They already had what most businesses spend years trying to build.

A good product.
Real expertise.
A clear vision.
Something valuable to offer.

But when the conversation turned to international opportunities...

the confidence started to disappear.

"I can read."
"I understand almost everything."
"I can have a simple conversation."

"But presenting our company in English?"
"Meeting international clients?"
"Pitching our solution abroad?"

"That's different."

One conversation stayed with me.

A business owner said:

"Right now I'm okay because I mainly work in the Italian market.
But I feel limited."

That word stayed with me.

Limited.

Because that's the real problem.

Not bad English.
Not a lack of intelligence.
Not a lack of expertise.

A limitation.

A communication barrier that quietly becomes a business barrier.

You can explain your product in Italian.

You can build trust.
Handle objections.
Negotiate.
Lead.
Show your expertise.

But when the opportunity becomes international...

something changes.

You stop thinking about the customer...
and start thinking about the words.

You simplify ideas that deserve more depth.
You avoid conversations you should be leading.

You stay inside the market where communication feels safe...
even though your ambitions reach much further.

And that's frustrating.

Because you're not starting from zero.

You can read.
You can listen.
You can understand.
You can survive.

But surviving in English is very different from:

selling,
leading,
presenting,
negotiating,
or representing your business internationally.

Those moments demand something different.

Not more grammar.
Not bigger vocabulary.

Better communication.

More structure.
More confidence under pressure.
More practice in the situations that actually matter.

Another professional summed it up perfectly:

"I don't want perfect English.
I want to stop feeling limited."

That sentence says everything.
Because that's what people are really looking for.

Freedom.

The freedom to speak without mentally translating every sentence.
The freedom to present without shrinking your message.
The freedom to build relationships across borders.
The freedom to sound as clear, capable, and credible in English as you already are in your own language.

This is why I don't see English as just another professional skill.

For many international professionals...

it becomes the ceiling that quietly limits growth.

And when communication improves...

that ceiling starts to disappear.

At what point did English stop feeling like a language...
and start feeling like a career or business limitation for you?
