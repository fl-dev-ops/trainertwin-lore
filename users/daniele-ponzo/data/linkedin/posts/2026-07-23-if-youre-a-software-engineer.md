If you're a software engineer working in English...

your brain is probably running at 120% during important meetings.

Is it because your English isn't good enough?

Probably not.

I believe it's because your brain is trying to do too many things...

...at the same time.

👇 This is exactly what I explain in this short clip.

When someone asks you a difficult question, your brain isn't just solving the problem.

It's also:

• translating
• choosing vocabulary
• checking grammar
• thinking about how you sound
• reading everyone's reactions

That's 𝗰𝗼𝗴𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱.

Eventually, something has to give.

Some people freeze.
Others start panic explaining.

Neither is an English problem.

It's a processing problem.

That's why I rarely tell people to study more grammar.

Instead, we reduce the number of mental processes running at the same time.

▶️ Simple communication structures.
▶️ Thought chunks.
▶️ Clear openings.
▶️ Repeatable patterns.

When your brain has fewer jobs to do...

communication latency drops.

Your ideas become easier to express.

And people finally hear the expertise that was there all along.

I'm curious...

When you feel under pressure in English...
what overloads your brain first?

• Translation?
• Grammar?
• Vocabulary?
• Worrying how you sound?
