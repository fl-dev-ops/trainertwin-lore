Demystifying Business Jargon in IT #12: "Hit the Ground Running"

When we think IT, business, workplace, we think fast, quick, rapid🏃‍♀️.

But what does it really mean to "𝐡𝐢𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐠𝐫𝐨𝐮𝐧𝐝 𝐫𝐮𝐧𝐧𝐢𝐧𝐠" in the tech world?

This common business jargon refers to starting a new project or role with immediate and maximum effort, efficiency, and effectiveness. 

It's about diving in headfirst, without hesitation, and making an immediate impact.

For IT managers and team leaders, mastering this concept can be a game-changer. Here's how:

▶️ Launching a New Project: When taking on a new project, "hitting the ground running" means quickly understanding the objectives, assembling the team, and setting clear goals from day one. 

For example, instead of spending weeks planning, we jump right into action, leveraging our expertise to deliver results swiftly.
 
👉"As the new project manager, I'm ready to hit the ground running and lead our team to success from day one."
 
▶️  Onboarding New Team Members: When welcoming new team members, we strive to help them "hit the ground running" by providing comprehensive training, access to resources, and clear expectations from the start. 

This sets the stage for seamless integration and productivity.
 
👉"Our company expects all employees to hit the ground running when they start a new role, ensuring efficiency and productivity from the get-go."

It is a fascinating idiom, and the origin is not 100% clear.

Here are some options:

🪖 Troops in a combat zone. They have to start running as soon as they get out of their plane.
🚂 A stowaway jumping off a train near a station, so they don’t get caught.
🐎Pony Express riders changing horses without delay to deliver their parcels.
 
Remember, next time you join a company, you might be asked to “hit the ground running”. Will you know what to answer?
