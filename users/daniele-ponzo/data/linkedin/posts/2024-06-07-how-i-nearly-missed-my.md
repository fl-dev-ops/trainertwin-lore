How I nearly missed my first important job interview and ended up running “Usain Bolt” style uphill, suit & tie, on a road I had never seen before.

All because of an “A”.

I had moved to Dublin just a few weeks earlier

I didn’t have much work experience – waiter, porter, some data entry job back at uni. When I got a call from the recruiting agency “we have an interesting role for you” I was over the moon. IT, US multinational, international team. Wow!

So, I made sure I was well prepared and ready to rock it.

I went to the agency, all jazzed up. Technical interview, behavioural interview, background, desires, I nailed it! I was ready for the real thing and now I was getting briefed on where and how I was meant to go for the HR interview.

This was before I had internet on my mobile, so pen & paper was still king.

Location, interview time, how to get there. “You need to go to this business park, Northeast of the city, 30 minutes from here, be there at 2PM, catch bus 38 from the city centre, allow enough time in case of traffic”.

I made sure I was spot on as I wanted everything to be right: clothes, time, journey.

At 12.50 I was at the bust stop. 10 minutes later I was on my number 38 bus, constantly checking the time to make sure I didn’t miss the business park. 20 minutes, still no business park. 25 minutes, still nothing. 30 minutes, I went to speak to the driver.

“Yes, you are on number 38, no, I’m not going to the business park, you should have taken bus 38A”.

38A. The lady at the agency forgot the “A”. So, now he was leaving me as close as possible, it was 1.40PM and I was 3 kilometres away from the business park, on a kind of a desert road going dauntingly uphill. Run, Daniele, run! This is all I could tell myself.

And run I did, as fast as possible, suit & tie like a corporate jogger.

Desperation and anxiety kicked in, it was getting late, I would never have made it on time. I already had catastrophic pictures in mind, How I missed the opportunity of my life, how I would disappoint everyone forever. Until something happened.

A random act of kindness saved the day.

The sight of an elegant long-distance runner attracted curiosity. A driver pulled over and asked: “what are you doing, if I may?” Gave him a 35-second briefing and the next thing I knew I was in his car driving up the hill towards the business park.

There it was, in all its splendour, ready to receive me. It was 1.58PM.
I was 2 minutes earlier. I couldn’t believe it. 
So, I quickly made myself presentable, had a sip of water and I walked in, still full of adrenaline and excitement and willing to ace my interview. 
I went to the reception.

“Good afternoon, I’m here for the interview”.

“Hi there, please take a seat. The hiring manager will see you in a minute”.

I had made it.
