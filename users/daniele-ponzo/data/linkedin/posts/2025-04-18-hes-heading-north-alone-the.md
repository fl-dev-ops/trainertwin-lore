He’s heading North. Alone.
The car engine hums as the road unwinds.

His heart is racing. His mind is spinning.
"𝘏𝘰𝘸 𝘥𝘰 𝘐 𝘦𝘹𝘱𝘭𝘢𝘪𝘯 𝘪𝘵 𝘤𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘳𝘭𝘺?"
"𝘞𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘪𝘧 𝘐 𝘤𝘢𝘯’𝘵 𝘧𝘪𝘯𝘥 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘳𝘪𝘨𝘩𝘵 𝘸𝘰𝘳𝘥𝘴?"

He’s had English lessons before. Studied it for years.
But this? This is different.

Until now, things were simple.
A few teammates, some friendly chats.

No pressure. No spotlight. A “quiet” role that felt safe.

But today is different.

Today he’s travelling North to meet a client.
And he’s not there to deliver good news.

There was a mistake in the invoice. A big one.
The system didn’t register the correct setup, the client was undercharged for several months.

Not his fault. But now his job is to explain it.
Face to face. In English. With clarity and confidence.

The building appears. Big. Corporate. Cold.

He walks in.
The receptionist barely notices him

“Hi...”

His voice is low. He can feel the tension in his shoulders.

The IT manager is about to come down.

He’s thinking of running away.
He feels like an impostor

“Am I ready for this? 
“I always try to find the right words… but I always end up expressing hesitation.”
"𝘞𝘪𝘭𝘭 𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘺 𝘵𝘢𝘬𝘦 𝘮𝘦 𝘴𝘦𝘳𝘪𝘰𝘶𝘴𝘭𝘺?”

That’s how he feels right now.

But then…

He pauses.
He breathes.
And he remembers.

He’s been preparing for this.
Not just studying, but speaking. Rehearsing.

He practiced this conversation with his mentor.  
Word by word, gesture by gesture.

He’s visualized this moment.
He’s learned how to stay present. How to own the room.

The elevator doors open.
“Good morning, Ms. Manager.”

She smiles.
“Good morning, 𝘋𝘢𝘯𝘪𝘦𝘭𝘦. Good to see you!”

The meeting begins.
