I once was rejected at a job interview because of my language.

I didn’t use swear words.

I didn’t make grammar mistakes.

And my English level was already good.

BUT my language wasn’t powerful enough.

Studying English as a non-native speaker isn’t only about the language.

If you want to sound convincing, sell, get your message through, you need to work on your communication as a whole.

I had taken a very expensive “general English” course, which taught me everything I needed to pass an exam, get a certification and have flawless grammar.

My Irish flatmate back then was making fun of me.

“I wouldn’t be able to resolve most of these grammar exercises, honestly”.

Anyway, I went to my job interview for a managerial position.

They started with some routine questions.

I was quite happy with my answers.

I thought I was nailing it.

Then we started the “presentation” mock-up.

I had prepared my slides.

And I was flying through them.

Then the hiring manager interrupted.

Pretending to be the customer, he asked: 

“How will you guarantee the extra margin there?”

And that’s when my language went wobbly.

“I will show you later in my presentation”.

I said, hoping to buy time.

It didn’t work.

They wanted an effective answer straight away.

I hadn’t rehearsed enough.

I hadn’t prepared well for unexpected questions.

Unfortunately, I didn’t sound too convincing.

But my grammar was spotless.

Did I get the job?

No, I didn’t.

Did I learn anything?

Of course I did.

I invested in becoming a more effective communicator.

After that day, I lead 2 international teams.

I trained and coached dozens of IT pros on the job.

And helped many of them move to higher positions within the IT world.

I taught hundreds of students.

And supported them with their studies, with their jobs and with their confidence.

The bottom line?

Language is a powerful tool.

Only if you use it effectively.
