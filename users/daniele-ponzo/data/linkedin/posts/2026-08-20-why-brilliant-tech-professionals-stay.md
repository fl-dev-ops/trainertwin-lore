Why brilliant tech professionals stay silent in meetings.

Let's use the Lean 5 Whys to find the real root cause.

Many software engineers think their problem is English.

I don't think it is.

Let's debug it together.

1️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗱𝗶𝗱𝗻'𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸 𝘂𝗽?

Because I wasn't confident enough in my English.

Seems reasonable.

But let's ask why again.

2️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗻'𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗳𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗻𝘁?

Because I was trying to build the perfect sentence before speaking.

Choosing the right tense.
The right vocabulary.
The right grammar.

So let's ask why one more time.

3️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝘁𝗿𝘆𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗼 𝗯𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲?

Because I didn't want people to think I wasn't competent.

Now we're getting somewhere.

This isn't really about English anymore.
It's about perception.

4️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗱𝗶𝗱 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗯𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗲𝘃𝗲 𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗳𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝘄𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗱 𝗺𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁?

Because that's how most of us learned English.

Grammar first.
Communication later.

We were taught to avoid mistakes.
Not to communicate under pressure.

One last why.

5️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲 𝘀𝘂𝗰𝗵 𝗮 𝗯𝗶𝗴 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗹𝗲𝗺 𝗶𝗻 𝗺𝗲𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀?

Because while your brain is busy checking grammar...
the meeting keeps moving.

Your working memory fills up.
Your communication latency increases.

You hesitate.
Someone else speaks.

And the opportunity disappears.

𝗥𝗼𝗼𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝘂𝘀𝗲?

Not bad English.

Cognitive overload.

Years ago, I thought my problem was English too.

Then one day I overheard a senior manager talking about me.

"He's good... but he has to come forward."

That sentence stayed with me.

I realized people weren't questioning my technical ability.

They simply weren't hearing enough from me.

That's when I stopped treating communication like a grammar exam.

Instead, I started learning how to communicate under pressure.

How to organize my thoughts.

How to reduce cognitive load.

How to deliver ideas clearly, even when I didn't have the perfect words.

Everything changed.
I became more visible.
I led projects.
I presented to international audiences.

Eventually, I built a business helping other tech professionals do the same.

Today, that's exactly what I teach.

Not endless grammar rules.
Not impossible vocabulary lists.
But practical communication systems that help you think more clearly when the pressure is on.

Because in international meetings, people rarely remember whether your grammar was perfect.

They remember whether your message was clear.
