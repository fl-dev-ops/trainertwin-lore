If you wait until January to work on your communication...

you're already behind.

Here's why.

Many tech professionals think career opportunities begin when new job openings appear.

In reality...

that's often when the decisions have already been made.

Long before a promotion is announced...

people have already noticed:

Who speaks up in meetings.

Who explains complex ideas clearly.

Who stakeholders trust.

Who can represent the team in front of customers, executives, or international colleagues.

Visibility doesn't appear overnight.

It compounds.

Month after month.

Meeting after meeting.

Conversation after conversation.

That's why I believe the next four months are one of the best investments you can make in your career.

Not by learning more technical skills.

Most of the people I work with are already exceptional engineers.

Their bottleneck isn't technical.

It's communication under pressure.

It's the hesitation before speaking.

The mental overload of translating while thinking.

The fear of sounding "less competent" in English.

The professionals who overcome those barriers don't suddenly become native speakers.

They become easier to understand.

More structured.

More visible.

More trusted.

That's what opens doors to:

• technical leadership

• international projects

• stakeholder-facing roles

• promotions

• and, eventually, higher salaries.

Communication isn't something you fix the week before an interview.

It's a capability you build long before the opportunity arrives.

That's exactly why I'm opening a small number of places for the next "Speak Like a Leader" cohort.

Because by the time January arrives...

I want my clients walking into those conversations already prepared, not wishing they'd started sooner.

💬 One question for you.

Imagine we're having this conversation one year from today.

What's the role you'd love to be doing?

Engineering Manager?

Staff Engineer?

Solutions Architect?

Head of Engineering?

Something else?

I'd love to hear it.
