You're halfway through explaining your idea...

...and suddenly you forget where you were going.

If you've ever presented in English, there's a good chance you've experienced that moment.

You know, that feeling of going round and round...
And not getting anywhere.

Your brain was trying to do too much at once.

You're thinking about:

• the technical solution
• the next sentence
• the right vocabulary
• how you sound

Eventually, something gives.

You lose the thread.

Then the panic starts.

So you keep talking.

More details.
More explanations.
More context.

Hoping you'll eventually find your way back to the point.

I call this 𝗽𝗮𝗻𝗶𝗰 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴.

Here's one simple technique that can stop it.

Instead of memorizing your presentation...

memorize the 𝗷𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗻𝗲𝘆.

Use four simple anchors:

➡️ First...
➡️ Then...
➡️ Next...
➡️ Finally...

For each point, add just two things:

✔️ one reason
✔️ one example

That's it.

You're no longer trying to remember paragraphs.

You're simply moving from one anchor to the next.

It reduces cognitive load.
Keeps your ideas organised.
And makes you much easier to follow.

Ironically, the simpler your structure becomes...
the more senior you sound.

Simple.
Practical.
Repeatable.
