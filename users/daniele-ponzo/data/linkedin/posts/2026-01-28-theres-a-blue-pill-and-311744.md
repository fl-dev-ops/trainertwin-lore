There’s a Blue Pill and a Red Pill with English at work.
Most people never question their choice. 

🔵 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗕𝗹𝘂𝗲 𝗣𝗶𝗹𝗹

You stay in the Matrix.

You tell yourself:
“I’ll watch movies in English.”
“I’ll use Duolingo every day.”
“One day I’ll go to London and finally speak English properly.”

It feels productive.
It feels safe.
It feels like you’re doing something.

Then Monday comes.

And so do:

meetings where you stay quiet
presentations you’d rather avoid
interviews where your mind goes blank

Same pain points.
Different week.

🔴 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗥𝗲𝗱 𝗣𝗶𝗹𝗹

You step out of “armchair English”.

You stop preparing to prepare
and start practicing what actually matters.

You train:
how to speak in meetings without panic
how to explain problems and solutions clearly
how to respond when you don’t find the right word
how to sound confident even when your English isn’t perfect

It’s uncomfortable at first.

You feel exposed.
You make mistakes.

But something changes.

You get noticed.
You speak up earlier.
You handle pressure better.
You start being seen as someone ready for more responsibility.

And yes:
better roles, better projects, better pay tend to follow.

Nobody was born confident in English.
Nobody magically “had it”.

The difference between those who 𝘁𝗿𝘆
and those who 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗿𝗼𝘃𝗲 is simple:

They choose discomfort over illusion.
Practice over theory.
Reality over excuses.

So I’ll ask you:
👉 Are you still preparing to speak or finally training for it?
