If you’re a software developer or engineer working in English, read this carefully:

You might not have an English problem.
You might have a pressure problem.

Most professionals don’t notice it at first.

They notice the consequences.

Meetings become harder.
People interrupt more often.
Ideas get ignored more quickly.
Confidence disappears in moments where it matters most.

Not because they’re not competent.

Because pressure changes the way they communicate.

I see this constantly with tech professionals.

They solve difficult problems every day.
They write clean code.
They explain things perfectly in their own language.

But in meetings?

Everything changes.

A simple explanation suddenly becomes difficult.

- The sentence starts growing.
- They hesitate.
- Over-explain.
- Search for the perfect word.
- Lose direction halfway through the point.

Fill silence with:

“uhm…”
“maybe…”
“how to say…”

And while they’re trying to organise the sentence…
the conversation keeps moving.

This is the part most people misunderstand:

The problem usually isn’t grammar.
It’s cognitive overload.

Because under pressure, the brain is trying to do too many things at once:

- translate
- structure the idea
- choose vocabulary
- monitor reactions
- avoid mistakes
- sound confident

That’s why communication collapses.

Not because you lack knowledge.
Because the brain loses structure under pressure.

And over time, something dangerous happens:

talented professionals start sounding less senior than they really are.
