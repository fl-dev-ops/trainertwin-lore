Communication is both digital and analogue. Are you using both?

(I don’t mean using a computer instead or a typewriter)

Paul Watzlawick, one of the great communication scientists, reminded us that human communication has two dimensions:

🔹 𝗗𝗶𝗴𝗶𝘁𝗮𝗹 communication → the content. The exact words we use. Clear, factual, precise.

🔹 𝗔𝗻𝗮𝗹𝗼𝗴𝘂𝗲 communication → the context. The tone of voice, the facial expressions, the gestures, even the pauses between words.

Think about it this way:
When you say “I’m fine” with a smile, it lands one way 🙂 .
When you say it with a sigh and rolled eyes🙄… it means something completely different  .

Now, here’s why this matters for non-native English speakers building an international personal brand:

1️⃣ In English, we often focus too much on the digital side — the grammar, the vocabulary, getting the sentence “perfect.”

2️⃣ But the analogue side — your confidence, tone, presence, energy — often speaks louder than your words.

3️⃣ When networking globally, people connect not just with what you say but with how you make them feel.

Practical tips to strengthen both dimensions:

✅ Digital: Keep your English simple and clear. Clarity beats complexity. Use LinkedIn posts, articles, and summaries to show expertise in straightforward language.

✅ Analogue: Practice your delivery. Record yourself, watch your gestures, notice your tone. Non-verbal cues build trust and confidence.

✅ Combine them: A strong personal brand uses both: valuable content (digital) delivered with authentic presence (analogue).

Because in the global job market, your words get you noticed.
But your whole communication: digital + analogue
is what makes people remember and trust you.

Have you ever noticed how your tone or body language changed the way your English was received?
Share your experience 👇
