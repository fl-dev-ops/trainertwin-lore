What are you doing with your language?

“I studied English for 8 years at school”.

“I can’t speak English in a meeting”.

Sounds like a paradox?

Well, I think it is.

But it is actually the reality for many people I speak with.

The school system often has a flipped approach to language.

Think about it. 

When you are a baby, you start SPEAKING a language. 

It’s a basic communication need we all have.

You spend the first part of your life listening and absorbing.

And then you speak.

You make a lot of mistakes, but nobody cares, as people want to communicate with you.

They want to build bridges 🌉 .

Later on, you learn the rules, the grammar, the structure.

When you learn a language at school, you typically start from the structure, the grammar, the literature.

But you seldom get a chance to speak.

And when you do “the authority” points out your mistakes and rates the quality of your speaking.

They start building walls🧱.

Not the ideal environment, I believe.

The result?

Self-conscious learners who can write English but shy away from a conversation.

How do we change this?

There is only one way.

START SPEAKING TODAY

And become comfortable with your tentative steps, flaws, and mistakes.

They’re a natural part of every learning process.

So, here’s how you can do it:

🔵 Shadow listening-> Replicate pronunciation and intonation (podcasts, music, videos)

🔵 Record yourself-> Listen to the recordings to identify areas for improvement and track your progress over time.

🔵 Practice relaxation techniques-> Deep breathing or visualization to calm your nerves and feel more at ease.

🔵 Set Realistic Goals-> ask a question today, raise your hand in a meeting tomorrow, have a casual conversation this week.

Remember: communication is about building bridges, not walls. 

And it takes two to communicate effectively.

So, our goal is to meet halfway through.

But we’ve got to take the first step.
