"Here is a quick summary of the system migration issue."

Ten minutes later, you are still talking. 
You’ve covered five different edge cases, a legacy database problem from three years ago, and a tangent about stakeholder alignment.

Meanwhile, the executives in the room have completely tuned out.

This is the "Panic Explaining" trap. 

When pressure hits in a technical meeting, our natural instinct is to overexplain everything at once to prove we know our stuff. 

The results are disastrous for your tech career. 

A chaotic stream of technical details completely buries your core point. 

Your listeners lose the thread, get confused, and ultimately lose confidence in your ability to lead. 

You walk out of the room knowing you are the smartest technical mind there, but realizing you completely failed to influence the decision.

Structure creates authority. 

To sound like a senior leader, you must force your communication into predictable, clean beats that work under any amount of stress. 

Instead of a rambling narrative, elite communicators use a three-beat framework: 

The Main Issue: "Here is the main issue."
 
The Impact: "The impact on our timeline is..."
 
The Recommendation: "My recommendation is..."
 
This gives your brain an immediate rail to slide on when stress hits, keeping you concise and authoritative.
