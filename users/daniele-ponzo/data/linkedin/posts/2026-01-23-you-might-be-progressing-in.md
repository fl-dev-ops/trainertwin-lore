You might be progressing in English
without realizing it.

This week, during a class, one of my clients said something that really stayed with me.
Not because it was polished.
Not because it was planned.
But because it was real.

He told me:
 “When we started in October, I remember I had a meeting at work and they said: ‘Your English is good… not perfect, but good.’

Just before the holidays, after another meeting, my manager said:
‘Oh, but your English is a little bit better now.’

And I didn’t really understand why.
I just know that every Friday I was practising English with you, and… that’s it.”

That’s progress.

Not a certificate.
Not a test score.
Not a dramatic before-and-after moment.

But 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗱 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗻𝗼𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗲.
Colleagues.
Managers.
Clients.

They notice:
• the words you choose
• how you structure your ideas
• how you react under pressure
• how you show up in conversations

And often, before you notice it yourself.

What I love about this feedback is the honesty:
“I don’t know if it’s what we do together or the practice we do together.”

And the answer is simple.
▶️ It’s the practice.
▶️ It’s the consistency.
▶️ It’s using English in real situations instead of waiting to feel ready.

Progress isn’t loud.
It doesn’t always feel spectacular.
But it becomes visible, especially to others.

So let me ask you:
What’s the first sign others noticed when your English started improving?
