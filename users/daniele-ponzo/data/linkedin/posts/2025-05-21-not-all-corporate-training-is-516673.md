  ➡️ Have you ever had to sit through a long training session that felt completely disconnected from your actual job?
  I have. More than once.

  The content didn’t reflect our work.
  The examples were outdated.
  And the feeling in the room was… “let’s just get this over with.”

  That experience has stayed with me.
  Now, when I design training, I focus on one thing above all:
  Make it relevant. Make it real. Make it worth people’s time.
  Because when training connects to someone’s actual job — it doesn’t feel like a task.
  It feels like support.

Not all corporate training is created equal.

Every time I start a new Business English course in a company, I notice the same look in the room:

Skepticism 🤔 .

People are polite, but I can tell they’re thinking:
"Here we go again."
"Another course we have to attend."
"Will this actually help me or just take up time?"

And honestly? I don’t blame them.

Many of them have been there before
 
Courses that felt generic, disconnected from their real work, and “forced” more than helpful.

The result?

They show up… but they don’t speak up.
They learn (somehow)… but they don’t gain confidence.

And the cycle continues 🔁 .

So what does it take to break that cycle and actually help people grow in how they use English at work?
