English Pronunciation is Easy

Said…nobody ever

Sometimes you wonder: are there more rules or more exceptions?

Take the two letters ‘GH’. How do you pronounce them? 🤔 

“Although I thought I bought enough gherkins, I am still not delighted. I ought to get more. I will have the last laugh”.

GH can be pronounced in 3 different ways:

-  𝐒𝐢𝐥𝐞𝐧𝐭: although, thought, bought, delighted, ought
-  ‘𝐆’: gherkins
-  ‘𝐅’: enough, laugh 

And what about silent letters? We go through the pain of writing the, spelling them and then…we don’t pronounce them:

“He was in debt, so he removed his wristwatch and gave it to the knight”

-  De𝐛t: the ‘B’ is silent
-  𝐖ristwatch: the ‘W’ is silent
-  𝐊night: the ‘K’ is silent

With so many exceptions, how do we improve our pronunciation?

Increasing our exposure to the language and doing some targeted practice.

✅ Listen to interesting talks. Pause. Listen again, using subtitles, so you connect words with pronunciation.

✅ Use AI tools (like ELSAspeak) to check the right pronunciation and get your own pronunciation evaluated by the tool.

✅ Get familiar with the phonetic alphabet (IPA, like the beer): [aɪ̯ pʰiː eɪ̯], so you can quickly check the pronunciation of every single word. The Oxford pronunciation chart is really useful.

So, besides learning the common pronunciation rules, we also have to memorize and learn all the exceptions, I’m afraid.

Where do you start?

Challenge yourself. Can you read this?

“ɪŋɡlɪʃ prəˌnʌnsiˈeɪʃᵊn ɪz nɒt ˈiːzi, bʌt wɪð ɪˈnʌf ˈpræktɪs ænd ˈplɛnti ɒv ɪkˈspəʊʒə, wiː kæn meɪk ɡreɪt ˈprəʊɡrɛs”.
