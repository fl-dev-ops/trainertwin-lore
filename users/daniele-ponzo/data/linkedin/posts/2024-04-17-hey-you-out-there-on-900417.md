"Hey you, out there on a call.

Always acting like you’re small. 

Can you hear me?"

Today I’m paraphrasing a great song from one of my favourite bands to grab your attention.

Why?

Because I love music. And because I love the intrinsic message in the song.

We all strive for meaningful connections, valuable relationships and visibility.

It’s what pushes us forward both in our personal and professional life.

As non-Native English speakers, we might find ourselves struggling more to come up with the right language and the best strategies to get noticed, offer solutions and show confidence.

The result?

🔴 We might end up avoiding productive and meaningful interactions.

🔴 We may resort to email and distant communication rather than face-to-face conversations.

🔴 We could look uncertain and shy when presenting ourselves.

In the medium to long term, this is the best recipe for stagnation and missed opportunities.

Think about it. You find the same 2 things in any given IT job in every IT company:

A Manager and a Team.

Whatever your role, department, position…it’s always about building connections and meaningful relationships.

The good news is that there are ways to align your inner strengths with action.

There are strategies to gain visibility. And language to express ourselves in all situations.

🔵Using phrases to talk about personal interest, so you don’t keep your relationships purely about work and can connect on a deeper level.
🔵 Crafting meaningful questions, so you can seek feedback and improve.
🔵 Sharing your solutions and your perspectives in an effective way so people notice you in meetings, calls and 1 to 1’s.
🔵 Using your body and your voice to present yourselves confidently, so you can put into practice your idea of success.

It’s about drawing a steady line between your abilities and the language to express them.
And finally thriving both at work and in your personal life.

Now, if you feel you could use some support, just reach out and let’s chat. I’d be honoured to back you up.

“Hey you, don't tell me there's no hope at all.

 Together we stand, divided we fall”
