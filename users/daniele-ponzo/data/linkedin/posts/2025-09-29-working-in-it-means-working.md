Working in IT means working globally.

Developers, managers, cybersecurity experts, project managers, consultants…
we’re constantly connected across borders.

 And yet, many professionals tell me:
👉 “I’m afraid to speak up.”
👉 “What if they don’t understand me?”
👉 “I can’t apply for that job, my English isn’t good enough.”

If you are looking for perfect English...
You will never get there.

I think it’s more about communication.

Think of it like code.
When you write code, you don’t just make it “compile”. 
You make sure it’s clean, efficient, and understandable, so that anyone on the team can read it, maintain it, and build on it.

Communication works the same way.

It’s important to know the “syntax” (grammar or vocabulary).
But at the same time your message needs to be clear, structured, and easy to follow, so your colleagues, clients, or managers understand you, no matter where they’re from.

The good news? Just like with code, you can debug, refactor, and improve your communication. Step by step.

And when you do, something 𝗽𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗳𝘂𝗹 happens:
You stop being “the quiet one on the call” 
and you start being the professional whose ideas lead the conversation.

💬 Over to you:
Have you ever held back in an international meeting because you were afraid your English wasn’t “good enough”?
