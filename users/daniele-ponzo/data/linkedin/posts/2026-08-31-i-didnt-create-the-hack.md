I didn't create the HACK Framework because people needed better English.

I created it because traditional Business English courses kept solving the wrong problem.

For years I watched brilliant developers...
Architects.
Project Managers.
CTOs.
Service Delivery Managers.

...freeze during international meetings.

Not because they didn't know English.
Because they were trying to do too many things at once.

They were solving the problem.
Translating.
Choosing vocabulary.
Checking grammar.
Managing how they looked.
Trying not to make mistakes.

The CPU was overloaded.

And overloaded systems don't become faster by adding more features.

They become faster by redesigning the architecture.

That's when something clicked.

As someone who spent over a decade working in IT before moving into coaching, I realised I was approaching communication differently.

I wasn't thinking like a language teacher.

I was thinking like someone debugging a system.

So I stopped asking:
"How do we teach more English?"

And started asking:
"How do we reduce communication latency?"

That question eventually became the HACK Framework.

Not another language method.

A communication system designed for high-pressure technical conversations.

H — Hone
Stop collecting vocabulary.
Start identifying the language you'll actually use.
Refine before you expand.

A — Action
Nobody ships perfect code on day one.
Communication works the same way.
Practise under pressure.
Ship Version 0.1.
Iterate.

C — Collaboration
Developers don't improve software in isolation.
Neither do communicators.
Feedback shortens the learning loop.

K — Kudos
Confidence isn't a personality trait.
It's evidence.
Every successful meeting...
Every presentation...
Every difficult conversation...
becomes another commit in your communication history.

That's how confidence is built.

Not overnight.

Version after version.

Because communication isn't about sounding more native.

It's about reducing friction between your ideas...
...and the people who need to understand them.

The more I work with tech professionals around the world, the more convinced I become of one thing.

The future won't belong to the developers with the biggest vocabulary.
It will belong to the ones who can explain complex ideas with speed, clarity, and confidence.

That's what the HACK Framework was built to do.

💬 If you had to improve just one part of your communication, which would it be?
Honing, Action, Collaboration, or Kudos?
