The best communicators aren't always the ones speaking.

Sometimes they're the ones creating space.

That was one of the biggest lessons from my latest conversation with 
Melinda Ng on The Non-Native Leader - Podcast.

During the episode, she shared a story I haven't stopped thinking about.

Someone later thanked her for giving him time to answer a question.

English wasn't his first language.

He needed a few extra seconds to think.

To translate.
To organise his thoughts.
To prepare his response.

And instead of jumping in or filling the silence...
she waited.

That moment captures something many international professionals experience every day:

cognitive load.

When you're communicating in English, you're often doing several things at once:

thinking about the idea
choosing the right words
checking how you sound
managing your tone
reading the other person
and sometimes translating before replying

That's a lot of mental work.

And this is where many conversations go wrong.

This has nothing to do with being slow or lacking confidence

There are times we simply need a moment to process.

We often assume silence means:
uncertainty
disengagement
lack of confidence

But silence can also mean "thinking".

Sometimes silence is processing.
Sometimes silence is respect.
And sometimes silence is the space someone needs to give you a better answer.

That's what stood out most from my conversation with Melinda.

Good communication isn't only about expressing yourself clearly.

It's also about creating the conditions for other people to communicate clearly.

Especially in international teams.
Especially across cultures.
Especially when different communication styles meet in the same room.

We spend a lot of time talking about how to speak with more confidence.

But strong communicators also know:
when to wait.
when to listen.
and when not to interrupt the thinking process.

Because if we want better conversations across cultures...
we need to stop treating every pause as a problem.
