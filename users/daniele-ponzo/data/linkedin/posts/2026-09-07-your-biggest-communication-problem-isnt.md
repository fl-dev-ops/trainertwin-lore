Your biggest communication problem isn't English.

It's latency.

Developers spend years learning how to reduce latency in software.

Lower response times.
Faster APIs.
Optimised databases.
Efficient systems.

But almost nobody thinks about communication latency.

Here's what it looks like.

A stakeholder asks you a question.

You know the answer.

Your brain starts running multiple processes at once:
⚙️ Solve the technical problem.
⚙️ Translate into English.
⚙️ Check the grammar.
⚙️ Find the right vocabulary.
⚙️ Think about how people will judge you.

By the time you've processed everything...
...someone else has already spoken.

It wasn't a lack of expertise.
It wasn't even a lack of English.

Your idea simply arrived too late.

That's communication latency.

And in international tech teams, latency has a cost.

Not because people think you're less competent.
But because the clearest ideas are usually the ones that influence decisions.

That's why I encourage my clients to think less about "learning more English" and more about reducing latency.

How?
• Lead with the conclusion.
• Use shorter sentences.
• Refactor your message before you speak.
• Practise under pressure, not just in comfortable situations.

The goal isn't perfect English.
The goal is reducing the time between having a great idea and communicating it clearly.

Because in global tech, the developers who create the most impact aren't always the ones who know the most.

They're the ones whose ideas arrive first.
