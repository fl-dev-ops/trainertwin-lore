We don’t want to work anymore!

Or maybe, we want to work in a different way…

Last week I had the pleasure of hosting an event as part of Rome Future Week. And what an inspiring conversation it was!

The world of work is changing at a speed we’ve never seen before. It’s no longer just about sending the perfect CV

Success today also means building transversal skills, personal branding, adaptability, and career strategies that go beyond the traditional path.

Together with Stefania Marinelli, Vittorio Verardi, and Danilo Spanu, we spoke about the job market, hybrid careers, freelancing, and entrepreneurship, networking, branding, and continuous learning

3 topics were especially “hot”:

⚡️𝗪𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗔𝗜 𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗰𝗲 𝘂𝘀?
Yes, it will; No, it won’t.
The discussion showed how nuanced this is:
AI can accelerate collaboration and save us time, but we must remain in control. 
So, let's not delegate the whole thinking process to AI, please!

⚡️ “𝗬𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗴 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲 don’t want to work anymore” vs. “Young people have understood everything” 
Black or white?
Of course the truth is in between. 
We all agreed there’s a lot to learn from the younger approach to work: attention to wellbeing, balance, and healthy environments.
Work is no longer our full identity. Personal interests, hobbies, passions are as important to shape who we are. 

⚡️𝗡𝗲𝘁𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 and visibility are as powerful as the 𝗖𝗩 itself. 
A strong LinkedIn presence and authentic connections can open doors that traditional methods can’t. 
A CV is mandatory for official application processes. 
An online presence guarantees people know who you are and what you do.
The truth? Again, it’s in between 😊

And of course we had fun connecting with brilliant minds in such an energizing space 🥂 .

A big thank you to dotcampus for hosting us and letting us witness an amazing Roman sunset from above. 
And to our partner Engel & Völkers Roma for their support. 🙌 Federica Musenga and Giorgia Morgia helped us with their unique perspective!

Rome Future Week showed once again: the future of work is not something to fear — it’s something to build, together. 🚀
