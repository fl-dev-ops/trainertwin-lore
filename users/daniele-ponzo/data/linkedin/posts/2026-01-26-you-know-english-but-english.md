You know English.…
but English doesn’t know you when you open your mouth.

Let me guess.
✔ You understand meetings perfectly… until it’s your turn to speak.
✔ You have great ideas… but they come out slower than you think.
✔ You search for the right word, the right tone, the right structure…
✔ And while you’re still thinking, someone else jumps in.

Sound familiar?
You’re not alone.

This is exactly what IT professionals, engineers, managers, and directors tell me every week.
Here’s the frustrating part:
Most people think the problem is 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵.
It’s not.

The real problem is trying to:
be perfect
be formal
sound “smart”
translate in your head
use async-brain (emails) in real-time conversations

That’s like trying to debug production code…
while Googling syntax…
while explaining your reasoning out loud…
in a language that’s not yours.

No wonder it feels exhausting.

The people who actually improve do something different.

They:
👉 accept “good enough” English
👉 simplify their message
👉 focus on clarity, not complexity
👉 speak before they feel ready
👉 get comfortable sounding imperfect

That’s how they:
• survive high-stakes meetings
• manage international conversations without panic
• stop freezing
• stop apologising
• start being heard

And yes, it feels uncomfortable at first.

But that’s not failure.
That’s 𝗴𝗿𝗼𝘄𝘁𝗵 𝗶𝗻 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗴𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀.

So here’s the real question for today:
Are you ready to feel uncomfortable enough to stop being invisible?
