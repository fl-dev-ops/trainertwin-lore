Most professionals don’t sound less senior because of their English.

They sound less senior because of how they communicate.

What does it actually mean to sound senior?

It’s not complex vocabulary.
It’s not perfect grammar.

It’s how you perform under pressure.

Here’s what that looks like 👇  

𝟭. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗿𝗲𝗺𝗼𝘃𝗲 𝗵𝗲𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗺𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝗰𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿

One director I work with said:

“I’m now able to think in English naturally without a lot of stops and hesitation.”

No fillers.
No searching mid-sentence.
Just a clear flow of ideas.

That alone changes perception.
  
𝟮. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗿𝗲𝗹𝘆 𝗼𝗻 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗼 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗱𝗼𝗻’𝘁 𝗴𝗲𝘁 𝗹𝗼𝘀𝘁

After a job interview, one engineer told me:

“My content was good and I didn’t get lost… I was also able to apply ideas on tone, intonation… I’m glad we had that lesson.”

Same knowledge.

But now:
• clear structure
• controlled delivery
• stronger impact

𝟯. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘄 𝘂𝗽 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗳𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲, 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀

A developer in my program said:

“I got more confidence while I’m talking in English with my boss.”

Not because his English suddenly improved.

But because:
• he knows how to organise ideas
• he practices under pressure
• he recognises what “good communication” feels like
  
Here’s the pattern:

👉 Remove hesitation
👉 Use structure
👉 Train under pressure

That’s how you start sounding senior.

Not by studying more.

By changing how you communicate.
  
If you’re already working in English but your message doesn’t always land…

you might not need more English.

You might need a different way to train it.

When you speak in English…
do you sound like your level or below it?
