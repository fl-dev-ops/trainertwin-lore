"It’s better if you don’t tell them you’re Italian".

"So, here is your new name".

This was me.

Every time a language school hired me.

And together with me, many other teachers.

From Italy, Romania, Poland, Ukraine…

Getting a new “British” identity.

Somehow, I get it.

Schools have a precise position on the market.

🇬🇧 “Speak like a native”
🇬🇧 “Our teachers are all mother tongue, so you can speak like a Londoner”

This is a marketing strategy.

But at the same time, I have a couple of questions🤔.

👉 Does this still make sense in a globalized world?
I mean, English is now the “lingua franca”. 57 countries have English as an official language. And it is used in international communications worldwide.

👉 Is it really down to the teacher nationality?
You know the language well, you can craft interesting lessons, you understand your students’ struggles because you struggled with the same problems in the past as learner.

So, do I really need to show you my passport 📓 ?

My view is different.

It shouldn’t be about Native vs. Non-native.

It should be about:

✅ The teacher’s capabilities and willingness to help.
✅ The individual teacher's ability to help their learners.
✅ Making connections, building relationships, leaving an impact.

Knowledge, Preparation, Empathy

If you can prove you have them, then you can keep your passport in your pocket.

I’d like to have your take on this.
Have you ever had a similar experience?
Let me know below 👇
