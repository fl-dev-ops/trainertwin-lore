Are you working in an international company?

Then you’re guaranteed to meet phrasal verbs at some stage.

Understanding phrasal verbs can sometimes feel like decoding a secret language.

With all these “up”, “over”, “down”, “in”, “through”, we feel dazed and confused😕.

Like we’re building a Tower of Babel every time we’re in a meeting🧱.

Yes, because this is the way people usually speak.

Phrasal verbs are very common in casual conversations, so knowing them will help you navigate the nuances of business English.

Also, it is great to know when to switch to a more formal equivalent (letters, client meetings, business proposals).

Here are some examples of common phrasal verbs and their more polished equivalent:

⭕ Phrasal verb: call off
Formal equivalent: cancel
Example: "Due to unforeseen circumstances, we had to call off the meeting scheduled for tomorrow."

⭕Phrasal verb: get around to
Formal equivalent: find the time to
Example: "I'll get around to reviewing the project proposal as soon as possible."

⭕Phrasal verb: go under
Formal equivalent: go bankrupt
Example: "Unfortunately, the company went under due to financial difficulties."

⭕Phrasal verb: to be off
Formal equivalent: take leave
Example: "I'll be off next week for a well-deserved vacation."

⭕Phrasal verb: get through
Formal equivalent: make contact
Example: "I'll try to get through to the client and provide an update on the project."

⭕Phrasal verb: do up
Formal equivalent: renovate
Example: "We're planning to do up the office space to create a more modern work environment."

⭕Phrasal verb: back (someone) up
Formal equivalent: support
Example: "As a team, we need to back each other up and collaborate effectively."

⭕Phrasal verb: go through
Formal equivalent: be formally approved
Example: "Before implementation, the proposal must go through the necessary approval process."

Remember to switch between phrasal verb and formal verb according to the situation.
