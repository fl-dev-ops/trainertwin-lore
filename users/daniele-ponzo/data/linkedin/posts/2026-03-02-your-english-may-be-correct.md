Your English may be correct.
But does it sound decisive?

Many senior IT professionals focus on grammar and vocabulary.

Yet in high-stakes conversations (meetings, presentations, interviews) what shapes perception isn’t only what you say.

It’s how your voice carries it.

Flat delivery can make strong ideas sound uncertain.
Misplaced stress can weaken key messages.
Monotone explanations can dilute technical authority.

Pronunciation isn’t about accent.

It’s about signalling clarity and confidence.

Here are three often-overlooked delivery skills that directly influence how senior you sound:
  
1️⃣ 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗱 𝗦𝘁𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀: 𝗘𝗺𝗽𝗵𝗮𝘀𝗶𝘀 𝗦𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗮𝗹𝘀 𝗔𝘂𝘁𝗵𝗼𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘆
In technical discussions, stress determines what people remember.

Compare:

“I think we could consider changing the architecture…”
vs
“I recommend changing the 𝗮𝗿𝗰𝗵𝗶𝘁𝗲𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲.”

The emphasis directs attention.
Attention shapes authority.

📌 Focus on stressing the words that carry decisions, risk, or impact — not filler phrases.
  
2️⃣ 𝗜𝗻𝘁𝗼𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻: 𝗧𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗦𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗮𝗹𝘀 𝗖𝗲𝗿𝘁𝗮𝗶𝗻𝘁𝘆
Your voice pattern signals intention.

Rising tone ↗️ can sound tentative.
Flat tone ➖ can sound disengaged.
Falling tone ↘️ signals completion and certainty.

In leadership communication, controlled falling intonation often communicates clarity and decision.

📌 Practice finishing recommendations with a downward tone. It subtly increases perceived confidence.

3️⃣ 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝗻𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝗦𝗽𝗲𝗲𝗰𝗵: 𝗙𝗹𝗼𝘄 𝗥𝗲𝗱𝘂𝗰𝗲𝘀 𝗙𝗿𝗶𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
When speech flows naturally, listeners focus on meaning instead of form.

If delivery is too segmented, your audience works harder to follow you — and cognitive load shifts to them.

Smoother linking improves:

clarity in fast exchanges
listening comprehension
executive presence

📌 Record short work-related sentences. Notice where your speech becomes rigid or over-controlled.
  
Mastering delivery is not about sounding native.

It’s about reducing friction between your thinking and your audience’s understanding.

In international IT environments, that friction often determines whether you sound:

Competent
or
Convincing.

This week, pick one area: stress, intonation, or flow
and observe how it affects your impact in meetings.

Let me ask you:

Have you ever felt your idea was strong
but your delivery made it sound smaller?
