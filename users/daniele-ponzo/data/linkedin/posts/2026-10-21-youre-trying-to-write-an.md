You're trying to write an email in the middle of a meeting.

Not literally. Let me explain.

You're in a meeting with your international team.

You have an idea worth sharing.

But before speaking, you start drafting the sentence in your head.

You look for the right word.
Check your grammar.
Rephrase something.
Start again.

Only when everything sounds right do you feel ready to hit "send".

Except... you're not writing an email.

Someone else starts talking.
The conversation moves on.
Your idea never gets heard.

And you leave the meeting wondering:

"Why can't I communicate as confidently as everyone else?"

Maybe your problem isn't just your English.

Maybe you're applying an editing strategy to a live conversation.

Think about it.

When writing an email or a Slack message, you can take your time. Edit. Rewrite. Polish.

But meetings work differently.

You need to communicate your ideas while the conversation is happening.

So instead of waiting until you've built the perfect sentence, try reversing the order.

1️⃣ 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗺𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝗽𝗼𝗶𝗻𝘁.
"I recommend delaying the release."

2️⃣ 𝗘𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝘄𝗵𝘆.
"We still have two critical issues to fix."

3️⃣ 𝗔𝗱𝗱 𝗱𝗲𝘁𝗮𝗶𝗹𝘀 𝗶𝗳 𝗻𝗲𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗮𝗿𝘆.
"Let me explain the risks."

Simple? Yes.

Point. Reason. Details.

You don't need to have the entire explanation ready before you start.

And even if someone interrupts after your first sentence, your main message has already been heard.

Communicating effectively in a meeting means helping people understand your thinking while the conversation is happening.

Sometimes, that requires a different approach.

𝗟𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗲𝗱𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱.

𝗠𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗰𝗹𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁.

So you can 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗽𝗼𝗶𝗻𝘁 𝘀𝗼𝗼𝗻𝗲𝗿.

Have you ever finished mentally editing a sentence after the conversation had already moved on?
