A normal workday.
An invisible mental load.

If English isn’t your first language, you know this feeling

08:45
Laptop on. Coffee ready.
Today is going to be a busy one.

09:00 – 𝗧𝗲𝗮𝗺 𝗺𝗲𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴
International team.
Germany, France, India, Ireland.

You understand everything.
Great start.

Then your manager asks:
“So, what do you think?”

Your brain instantly switches to survival mode:
- How do I start?
- Which tense do I use?
- Don’t speak too fast. Don’t speak too slow.

You say something.
It’s… okay.
Not perfect.
But someone else jumps in and takes the space you were still building.

You make a note: “Next time, speak earlier.”

10:30 – 𝟭:𝟭 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗿
Performance review.

You want to talk about:
- growth
- responsibilities
- career progression
But the words feel heavier than usual.

You soften everything:
“I think maybe… in the future… I could possibly…”

Your manager nods politely.

You leave the call thinking:
“I didn’t say what I really meant.”

12:00 – 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗼𝗻 𝗮 𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗼𝗿𝘁
One colleague sits next to you.
Two more are online, different time zones.

You write a message.
Rewrite it.
Simplify it.
Second-guess it.

You don’t want to sound:
- rude
- unclear
- too direct
- too weak
You hit “send” anyway.

14:30 – 𝗖𝗮𝗹𝗹 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝗮𝗻 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗹𝗶𝗲𝗻𝘁
There’s an issue with the service.

You need to:
- explain what happened
- reassure them
- propose corrective actions

You know the solution.
You’ve solved harder problems than this.

But now you’re managing:
- vocabulary
- tone
- clarity
- client emotions
- your own nerves

You slow down.
Choose simpler words.
It works.

The client says:
“Okay, that makes sense.”
Small win. Big relief.

16:30 – 𝗣𝗿𝗲𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗽𝗿𝗲𝗽
Another client.
New solution.
Contract renewal.

You prepare the slides carefully.
You practice alone.
Out loud.

You still hear that voice:
“What if I forget a word?”
“What if they ask something unexpected?”

You remind yourself:
“I don’t need perfect English. I need clear English.”

18:30 – 𝗘𝗻𝗱 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗮𝘆
You’re tired.

That’s the invisible 𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗮𝗹 𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱.
Constantly using English.

For a second, you think:
“𝘔𝘢𝘺𝘣𝘦 𝘐 𝘴𝘩𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥 𝘤𝘩𝘢𝘯𝘨𝘦 𝘫𝘰𝘣”

Then reality kicks in.

For your role…
the interview will definitely be in English.

And tomorrow?
Another meeting at 9.

If this felt a little too familiar…
you’re not alone.

And no, the problem isn’t your English.

It’s the fact that you’re expected to perform, influence, and lead
𝗶𝗻 𝗮 𝗹𝗮𝗻𝗴𝘂𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘄𝗮𝘀 𝗻𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗹𝗶𝗳𝗲 𝗮𝘁 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸.

The question is:
👉 Do you keep surviving your workdays in English or start training for them?
(And yes, they are two very different things.)
