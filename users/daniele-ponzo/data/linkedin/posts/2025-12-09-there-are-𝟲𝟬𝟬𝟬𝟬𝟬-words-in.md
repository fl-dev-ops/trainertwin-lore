There are 𝟲𝟬𝟬,𝟬𝟬𝟬 words in the English language.

Do you think you can remember all of them?

Well, here’s the good news: you don’t need to.

Most professionals panic when they forget (or don’t know) a word.
They freeze, go silent, or start translating in their head.

But fluent speakers (native speakers included) forget words all the time.

The difference is simple: they have a 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗴𝘆.

Here are two powerful strategies you can start using today 👇

1️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂’𝗿𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗼𝗿 𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴 → 𝗙𝗜𝗡𝗗 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗺𝗲𝗮𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗲𝘅𝘁

Instead of running to Google Translate, do this:
➟ Guess the meaning from the context
➟ Check your guess with a monolingual dictionary (Cambridge, Oxford, Merriam-Webster…)
➟ Use the word immediately (say it out loud, write your own example, add it to your list)

🟦 Example “streamline”
Sentence: “This new tool will streamline our workflow.”
➟ You might guess: make the workflow easier/faster.
➟ You check the dictionary, confirm, and write:
“We need to streamline our onboarding process to save time.”

2️⃣ 𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂’𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 ➟ 𝗚𝗢 𝗔𝗥𝗢𝗨𝗡𝗗 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗱
(aka the Taboo Technique 😄)

Think of the board game Taboo:
You must explain a word without using the obvious words.
This is EXACTLY the skill confident speakers use at work.

If you forget a word, instead of panicking, you can:
👉 Describe it
“It’s the tool that helps us…”
“It’s the system where we track…”
“It’s the process that allows us to…”
👉 Or use a simpler synonym
You don’t need fancy words. You need clarity.

🟩 Example. Forgot “deadline”?
Say:
“the last day we can finish the task”
or
“the final date for delivery.”
Both communicate the meaning clearly.

And THAT is fluency, not perfection.

Fluency is not knowing every word.
Fluency is knowing what to do when you DON’T know the word.

Start using these strategies this week, you’ll feel the difference immediately.
