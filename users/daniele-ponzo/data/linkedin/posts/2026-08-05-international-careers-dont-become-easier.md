International careers don't become easier.

Sometimes, you become different.

That was my biggest takeaway from my latest conversation on The Non-Native Leader - Podcast with Camila Ferreira.

Camila has spent more than 20 years building businesses across Latin America, the US and Europe.

Looking at her career today, it's easy to think:

"She always knew what she was doing."

The reality was very different.

Her first international leadership role was in Colombia.

She already knew the country from previous business trips.
She loved it.

But living there...
was a completely different experience.

She had no mentor.
No roadmap.
No one explaining what it actually feels like to build a life and lead a team abroad.

She felt lonely.
She struggled to build a network.
She found herself comparing reality with the international career she had imagined.

Then something happened.

She received an offer to go back to Brazil.

A senior leadership role.
A great company.
Everything familiar again.
Everything comfortable.

She told me it was incredibly conflicting.

Because she wanted an international career...

but she also wanted the comfort she had left behind.

That's when she realised something that stayed with me.

If she truly wanted to build a global career...
she had to stop waiting for the experience to become comfortable.

She had to change.

Not the country.
Not the opportunity.

Herself.

One sentence from our conversation captures it perfectly:

"𝗪𝗲 𝘀𝗲𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗹𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝘄𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗲, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗹𝗱 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗶𝘀."

Later she said something else that I found just as powerful.

Nothing around her had changed.

She had simply decided to embrace the experience from a different perspective.

Listening to her, I realised I see the same pattern every week with the professionals I coach.

Not only when they move abroad.
When they start speaking English in meetings.
When they lead international projects.
When they present to senior stakeholders.

We often tell ourselves:

"I'll do it when I feel ready."

But readiness rarely comes first.

Growth does.

Comfort protects us at the beginning.

Then, quietly...

it starts making decisions for us.

That's when 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝘂𝗯𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝗮 𝗰𝗮𝗴𝗲.

English is part of an international career.

But it isn't the whole story.

The bigger challenge is learning to stay curious when everything feels unfamiliar.

To adapt instead of retreat.

To keep moving even before confidence arrives.
