The Paradox of English Learning

Let’s be honest.
Many professionals I work with have studied English for years.

They know the grammar.
They can read articles, write emails, even watch Netflix without subtitles.
But when it’s time to speak… everything freezes. 🥶

💬 The meeting starts — and you go quiet.
💬 The job interview — and suddenly, all your words disappear.
💬 The presentation — and you think, “I’ll never sound good enough.”

Sounds familiar?

Here’s the paradox:
You’ve learned English for years…
But you’ve rarely used it under real pressure.

The reality is simple: speaking is a skill.
Just like coding, driving, or playing an instrument.
You don’t read about it. You train it.

And the good news? You can start today.

Here’s how 👇
1️⃣ Stop comparing yourself.
Everyone’s pace is different. Progress happens when you focus on your next step.

2️⃣ Slow down.
You don’t need to jump straight into meetings. Start small and build confidence.

3️⃣ Set micro targets.
 Start with easy challenges:
👉 Describe your room for 30 seconds.
👉 Then describe your job or a project you’re proud of.
👉 Next, set a timer: try speaking for one minute without stopping.
👉 Finally, record yourself and listen back.

You’ll start noticing patterns, improving your flow, and most importantly: building your speaking muscles. 💪

Fluency? It doesn't need more theory. 
It needs practice, pressure, and progress.

If speaking is a skill, what’s one small challenge you can set for yourself this week?
