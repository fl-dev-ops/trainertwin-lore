You know the solution.
But when it’s time to say it in English… it disappears..

If you work in an international environment, you probably recognize this.

Not when you’re coding.
Not when you’re solving problems.
Not when you’re working on your own.

But when the stakes go up.

𝗝𝗼𝗯 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘃𝗶𝗲𝘄𝘀

You prepared.
You know your experience.
You know the projects you delivered.

But during the interview something shifts.

You start searching for the 𝘱𝘦𝘳𝘧𝘦𝘤𝘵 word.
You translate your thoughts while speaking.
You hesitate on sentences you would say effortlessly in your own language.

You leave the interview thinking:

“I should have explained that better.”

And three hours later, the perfect sentence appears in your head.

Too late.

𝗜𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗺𝗲𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀

You follow everything.
You understand the problem.
You even have the solution.

But while you're structuring the sentence in your head…

Someone else jumps in.
The moment is gone.

And suddenly it looks like you had nothing to add.

Meanwhile your brain was doing five things at once:

• listening
• translating
• structuring
• checking grammar
• thinking about how you sound

No wonder the sentence comes out slower than the idea.

𝗣𝗿𝗲𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀

You know the project better than anyone.

But when presenting in English you notice something:

You soften your message.
You over-explain your point.
You avoid strong statements.

Not because you lack knowledge.

Because you're trying to sound 𝗰𝗼𝗿𝗿𝗲𝗰𝘁 instead of 𝗰𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿.

And then comes the worst part.

That little voice saying:

Maybe my English is not good enough.
Maybe I’m not ready for this role.
Maybe I should just let someone else speak.

But if you’ve ever experienced this…

It’s not because you’re not competent.

It’s because your brain is overloaded..

And that pressure shows up exactly when the stakes are highest.

Job interviews.
Meetings.
Presentations.
Calls

𝗟𝗲𝘁 𝗺𝗲 𝗮𝘀𝗸 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴.

Have you ever left a meeting thinking:
“I knew exactly what I wanted to say…
but it didn’t come out the way I wanted.”

If that happened to you, you’re definitely not the only one.
