The fastest way to lose authority in a meeting isn’t grammar.
It’s hesitation.

This is something Senior IT & Tech professionals run into more often than they realise, especially in 𝗵𝗶𝗴𝗵-𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 workplace English.

You walk into a meeting prepared.
You know the system.
You know the risks.
You know the decision that needs to be made.

And yet… authority leaks.

Not because your English isn’t perfect.
But because of how you speak.

Here are three patterns I see constantly in senior IT meetings:

1️⃣ 𝗢𝘃𝗲𝗿-𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴
You give context.
Then more context.
Then background to the background.

What it signals:
“I’m not sure this will stand on its own.”

In leadership-level communication, clarity beats completeness.
State the decision first. Explain after (if needed).

2️⃣ 𝗦𝗼𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗱𝗲𝗰𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀
“I think maybe we could consider…”
“Perhaps one option might be…”

What it signals:
“This isn’t firm.”

Senior stakeholders don’t expect certainty about the future.
They expect 𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗱𝗶𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆, not fluency.

Clear recommendation. Clear reasoning. That’s authority.

3️⃣ 𝗔𝗽𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗯𝗲𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴
“Sorry, just one thing…”
“This might be a stupid question…”

What it signals:
“Lower priority. Lower confidence.”

In high-stakes workplace English, pre-apologies shrink your presence before you’ve even spoken.

Here’s the key shift:
Communicating at Leadership-level doesn't mean you need to sound perfect.
But surely you need to sound decisive, structured, and intentional.

For Senior IT & Tech professionals, authority isn’t lost in mistakes.
It’s lost in hesitation.

So the real question is:
👉 Where are you still trying to sound correct,
 instead of sounding credible?
