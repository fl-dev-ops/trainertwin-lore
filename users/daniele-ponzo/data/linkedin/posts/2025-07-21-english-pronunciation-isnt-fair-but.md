English pronunciation isn’t fair.
But it is fixable.

Sometimes, pronunciation slips just confuse the listener, 
Repeating the words normally helps.

But the main problem is that they can also unintentionally affect how we’re perceived. 
Especially in professional contexts, sounding clear and confident matters.

I’ve heard these slip-ups in coaching sessions and global conferences.
Let’s look at some recurring challenges and how to fix them:

1. event
✅ Correct: /ɪˈvɛnt/
e-𝗩𝗘𝗡𝗧 (2 syllables, second stressed)
Sounds like “went,” but with an "e" at the start

2. significant
✅ Correct: /sɪɡˈnɪf.ɪ.kənt/
sig-𝗡𝗜𝗙-i-cant (4 syllables, stress on second)
“gn” = /ɡ/ + /n/ sound
Compare: magnificent /mæɡˈnɪf.ɪ.kənt/

3. arrived
✅ Correct: /əˈraɪvd/
a-𝗥𝗜𝗩𝗘𝗗 (2 syllables, stress on second. We pronounce "AI")
Compare: survived /sərˈvaɪvd/

4. talk
✅ Correct: /tɔːk/
tawk (1 syllable) — note silent “l”
Similar to walk, should, could: no 𝗟 sound in these

5. source
✅ Correct: /sɔːrs/
𝘀𝗼𝗿𝗰𝗲 (1 syllable) — straightforward once you drop the silent “𝗨”

6. launch
✅ Correct: /lɔːntʃ/ (long sound)
𝗹𝗮𝘄𝗻ch (1 syllable) — Not to be confused with lunch /lʌntʃ/ (short sound)
“We launch a product, then eat lunch to celebrate.”

7. management
✅ Correct: /ˈmæn.ɪdʒ.mənt/
𝗠𝗔𝗡-age-ment (3 syllables, stress on first)
Compare: development /dɪˈvɛl.əp.mənt/ de-𝗩𝗘𝗟-op-ment  (words with "-ment", stress on "second third from last" rule)

8. quit / quite / quiet
quit /kwɪt/ – one syllable (“kwit”)
quite /kwaɪt/ – one syllable but with diphthong (“kwite”)
quiet /ˈkwaɪ.ət/ – two syllables, stress on first (“KWI-et”)

These aren’t “mistakes to feel bad about”, they’re just patterns.
And with a bit of focus, they’re totally fixable.

Want to sound more natural and confident in your next meeting?
Start by picking one of these words to practise out loud.
