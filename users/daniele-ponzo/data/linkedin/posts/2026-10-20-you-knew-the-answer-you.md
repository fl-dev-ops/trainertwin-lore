You knew the answer.

You just couldn't get it out fast enough.

You're in a meeting with your international team.

A technical problem comes up.

You've dealt with something similar before.

You know what could fix it.

But before you speak, your brain starts working overtime.

"𝘏𝘰𝘸 𝘥𝘰 𝘐 𝘦𝘹𝘱𝘭𝘢𝘪𝘯 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴 𝘤𝘭𝘦𝘢𝘳𝘭𝘺 𝘪𝘯 𝘌𝘯𝘨𝘭𝘪𝘴𝘩?"
"𝘞𝘩𝘢𝘵'𝘴 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘳𝘪𝘨𝘩𝘵 𝘸𝘰𝘳𝘥?"
"𝘞𝘩𝘢𝘵 𝘪𝘧 𝘐 𝘮𝘢𝘬𝘦 𝘢 𝘮𝘪𝘴𝘵𝘢𝘬𝘦?"

You finally start talking.

You hesitate.

Search for a word.

Try to rephrase.

Then someone else jumps in.

And says almost exactly what you were trying to say.

Everyone nods.

The conversation moves on.

And you let it go.

But the frustrating part comes later.

After the meeting, you keep replaying that moment in your head.

Not the technical problem.
Not the solution.
𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗳𝗮𝗰𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱𝗻'𝘁 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗮𝗹𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗱𝘆 𝗸𝗻𝗲𝘄.

And slowly, another thought creeps in:

"Maybe I'm not as good as everyone else in that room."

I've heard it many times from experienced tech professionals.

Software engineers.
Tech leads.
People who solve complex problems, manage projects and make important decisions every day.

They know their stuff.

But put them in an international meeting where the conversation moves quickly, and suddenly they start questioning themselves.

And here's what makes this so frustrating.

𝗬𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝘁𝗲𝗰𝗵𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗿𝘁𝗶𝘀𝗲 𝗵𝗮𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗰𝗵𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗲𝗱.

𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗲𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿𝘀𝗲𝗹𝗳 𝗵𝗮𝘀.

You start confusing difficulty expressing your knowledge with a lack of knowledge.

You start measuring your professional value by how quickly you can express it in English.

And when this happens repeatedly, something else can change.

You hesitate a little longer before raising your hand.

You wait for someone else to speak first.

You start wondering whether your ideas are really worth sharing.

You're becoming less confident in your ability to communicate.

Struggling to communicate under pressure has an immediate impact:

➡️ You miss the contribution during the meeting

And a more dangerous long-lasting impact:

➡️ 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗹𝗼𝘄𝗹𝘆 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗹𝗼𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝗳𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗮𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘄𝗮𝗿𝗱𝘀.

Of course, you didn't suddenly become a less capable professional.

But you just couldn't show what you knew in that moment.

And those are two very different things.

𝗛𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝗹𝗲𝗳𝘁 𝗮𝗻 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝗺𝗲𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴, "𝗜 𝗸𝗻𝗲𝘄 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝗮𝘆. 𝗜 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱𝗻'𝘁 𝘀𝗮𝘆 𝗶𝘁 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗲"?
