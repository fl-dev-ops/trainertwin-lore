Are you “up to speed”?

Let's talk about a phrase you might hear often in the tech world: "To get someone up to speed." This is common business jargon.

𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗗𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗜𝘁 𝗠𝗲𝗮𝗻?

"To get someone up to speed" means to bring someone to the level of knowledge or competence required for a particular task or project. 

It's about ensuring they have the necessary information and skills to be productive and effective.

Literally? “To brief” someone.

𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲 𝗲𝘅𝗮𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲𝘀:

Onboarding a New Team Member:

👉 Example: "We hired a new developer last week. Can you spend some time getting her up to speed on our current projects and development practices?"

Translation: Make sure the new developer understands what we are working on and how we do things.

Project Transition:

👉Example: "I need to step away from the project for a few weeks. I'll make sure to get you up to speed on all the details before I leave."

Translation: I'll give you all the necessary information so you can continue the project without any issues.

𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝘁𝗼 𝗨𝘀𝗲 𝗜𝘁

Using this phrase can help you communicate clearly and show your understanding of business jargon:

👉When Delegating Tasks: "Before you start on this new task, let's get you up to speed on the client’s requirements and our timeline."

👉In Team Meetings: "Can we take a few minutes to get everyone up to speed on the recent changes to the project scope?"

𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺?

The first examples of this idiom used with the meaning of “to brief someone” come from the ‘70s. Apollo 13 🚀 .

John Swigert was a last-minute replacement for another astronaut: “𝘐𝘵’𝘴 𝘳𝘦𝘢𝘭𝘭𝘺 𝘢 𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘱𝘢𝘵𝘪𝘣𝘪𝘭𝘪𝘵𝘺 𝘴𝘰𝘳𝘵 𝘰𝘧 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘵𝘰 𝘨𝘦𝘵 𝘩𝘪𝘮 𝘶𝘱 𝘵𝘰 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘦𝘥, 𝘪𝘯 𝘭𝘢𝘯𝘨𝘶𝘢𝘨𝘦 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘳𝘦𝘴𝘱𝘰𝘯𝘴𝘦𝘴.”

Before that?

It was mostly referred to machines and animals performing at their best:

A newspaper ad from 1867: “𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘸𝘩𝘦𝘦𝘭 𝘥𝘳𝘪𝘷𝘦𝘴 𝘢𝘭𝘭 𝘮𝘺 𝘴𝘢𝘸𝘴 𝘵𝘳𝘶𝘭𝘺 𝘶𝘱 𝘵𝘰 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘦𝘥, 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘨𝘪𝘷𝘦𝘴 𝘢𝘣𝘶𝘯𝘥𝘢𝘯𝘤𝘦 𝘰𝘧 𝘱𝘰𝘸𝘦𝘳 𝘵𝘰 𝘥𝘰 𝘢𝘭𝘭 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘸𝘰𝘳𝘬 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘴𝘢𝘸𝘴 𝘢𝘳𝘦 𝘤𝘢𝘱𝘢𝘣𝘭𝘦 𝘰𝘧 𝘥𝘰𝘪𝘯𝘨.”

New York Times, 1879: “𝘛𝘩𝘦 𝘮𝘢𝘳𝘦 𝘸𝘢𝘴 𝘴𝘩𝘰𝘸𝘯 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘩𝘦𝘳 𝘲𝘶𝘢𝘭𝘪𝘵𝘪𝘦𝘴 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘳𝘦𝘤𝘰𝘳𝘥 𝘸𝘦𝘳𝘦 𝘦𝘹𝘱𝘢𝘵𝘪𝘢𝘵𝘦𝘥 𝘰𝘯. 𝘚𝘩𝘦 𝘭𝘰𝘰𝘬𝘦𝘥 𝘥𝘦𝘤𝘦𝘯𝘵 𝘢𝘯𝘥 𝘶𝘱 𝘵𝘰 𝘴𝘱𝘦𝘦𝘥.”
