You know English.

But when the meeting switches to English...
your brain suddenly forgets it.

You understand pretty much everything.

You know the technical answer.

You already solved the problem in your head.

Then somebody says:

"Daniele, what's your view?"
...
And suddenly your internal CPU hits 100%.

Your brain starts running four processes at once:

• solve the technical problem
• translate your thoughts
• search for the "perfect" words
• worry about how you'll sound

That's not an English problem.

That's 𝗰𝗼𝗴𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱.

And when your brain runs over capacity...
communication slows down.

You hesitate.
You over-explain.

Or worse...
you say nothing at all.

That's why I believe the focus should not be on talking like a native speaker.

But on reducing the processing load.

Instead of building the perfect sentence...
start with the message.

Instead of searching for sophisticated vocabulary...
use a simple communication framework.

For example:
𝗣𝗼𝗶𝗻𝘁 → 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝘀𝗼𝗻 → 𝗗𝗲𝘁𝗮𝗶𝗹

Your audience doesn't care whether your grammar is perfect.

They care whether they understand you.

Clear beats clever.

Every single time.

The irony?

Most international professionals already know enough English to do their jobs.

What they're missing isn't more vocabulary.

It's a communication system that still works when the pressure goes up.
