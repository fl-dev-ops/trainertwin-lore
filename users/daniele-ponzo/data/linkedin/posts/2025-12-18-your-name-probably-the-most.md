Your name.

Probably the most precious thing you own.

Also… the most mispronounced 😅

It doesn’t just happen when we travel abroad.
Sometimes it happens in our own country too.
And it turns out… it’s very common.

Research based on Google Trends showed that in Britain the two most mispronounced names were:
• Niamh (pronounced NEEV)
• Saoirse (pronounced SER-sha)
Beautiful names.
Absolutely confusing for anyone seeing them written for the first time.

Then there’s the legendary Starbucks effect ☕️
You say your name clearly…
You receive something entirely different on the cup:
• Nathan → Satan
• Lauren → Lorrin
• Donna → Lann
• Ellen → Hellwyn
At that point, you just smile and accept your new identity.

And in Italy?
We go one step further.
We adapt British names, so they feel more familiar:
• Brian → Braian
• Ryan → Raian
• Kevin → Chevin
The “revised” versions now exist on ID cards. What will happen when they travel to Britain? 🤔 

And then… there’s my name.
Easy in Italy.
As soon as I travel abroad?
The fun begins.

I’ve been called:
Danielle (on my first day at work, my colleagues were expecting a French lady… then I showed up), Daiello, Daniello, Danieli, Daniel (some people like to simplify life), Daniela…

Turns out I’m not alone.
The Telegraph shared a lovely video where their own collaborators talked about how it feels when people get their names wrong.
How did they feel? Not happy, I’d say…
'it's not that hard to say my name!'

Ultimately, names are part of who we are.
Our identity.
Our story.

And now I’m curious 👇
Has it ever happened to you?
What’s the funniest (or most painful) version of your name you’ve heard… and how did it make you feel?
