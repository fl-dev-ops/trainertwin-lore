I need your advi...

wait, is it adviCe or adviSe?

Last week I was talking to a friend and this topic came up.

There are some words in English which follow this pattern.

It's a little difference (C or S) but it can have a negative impact on your message:

❌ unclear communication
❌ you might sound unprofessional
❌ confusion between 🇺🇲 and 🇬🇧

C is a noun.

S is a verb.

So, here are a couple of examples: 

👉I'm not sure how to complete the report, I need your adviCe".

👉We need the missing data by the end of the month. Can you please adviSe?"

There is also a little difference in pronunciation:

Advise: ədˈvʌɪz (like "size")
Advice: ədˈvʌɪs (like "ice")

Other words follow the same pattern:

Practice/Practise; Device/Devise:

👉I need to practise English to get more confident.

👉I need a lot of practice to become a good developer.

👉We need to devise a new strategy to tackle the EMEA market.

👉Our new App works on all the devices.

P.S. Practice is both a noun and a verb in 🇺🇲, but NOT in 🇬🇧. 
And there is not a difference in pronunciation.
