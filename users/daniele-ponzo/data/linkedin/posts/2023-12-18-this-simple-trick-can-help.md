This simple trick can help you sound more professional in English.

As an IT Team leader, I had to write hundreds of emails weekly. 📧 

Team members, clients, managers…

And I had to prepare and deliver presentations. 🖥 

To illustrate our solutions to our customers.

Or highlight the team strategy in our meetings.

I used to experience ups and downs.

Is my email professional enough?

Am I using a formal tone when required?

But I learned one quick trick to instantly make my English more formal and professional👩‍💼:
 
Use the Passive.

With the Passive, you have 3 instant benefits:

✔ “you” is not mentioned.
✔ The action is highlighted.
✔ The result is underlined.

 Here are 3 examples👇:
 
"𝑻𝒉𝒆 𝒓𝒆𝒑𝒐𝒓𝒕 𝒘𝒂𝒔 𝒑𝒓𝒆𝒑𝒂𝒓𝒆𝒅 𝒃𝒚 𝒐𝒖𝒓 𝒕𝒆𝒂𝒎 𝒐𝒇 𝒆𝒙𝒑𝒆𝒓𝒕𝒔."
✅the emphasis is placed on the action of preparing the report. This creates a more formal tone, allowing the focus to be on the report itself. 

"𝑻𝒉𝒆 𝒅𝒆𝒄𝒊𝒔𝒊𝒐𝒏 𝒘𝒂𝒔 𝒎𝒂𝒅𝒆 𝒃𝒂𝒔𝒆𝒅 𝒐𝒏 𝒄𝒂𝒓𝒆𝒇𝒖𝒍 𝒂𝒏𝒂𝒍𝒚𝒔𝒊𝒔."
✅We highlight the decision itself rather than specifying who made the decision. 

"𝑻𝒉𝒆 𝒑𝒓𝒐𝒋𝒆𝒄𝒕 𝒘𝒊𝒍𝒍 𝒃𝒆 𝒄𝒐𝒎𝒑𝒍𝒆𝒕𝒆𝒅 𝒃𝒚 𝒕𝒉𝒆 𝒆𝒏𝒅 𝒐𝒇 𝒕𝒉𝒆 𝒎𝒐𝒏𝒕𝒉."
✅The attention is placed on the completion of the project and not on who will be responsible for completing it.
 
In these examples we use the passive to maintain a professional and formal tone by emphasizing the action, object, or result.
 
 And we don't mention the subject performing the action.
 
This can give an air of objectivity, formality, and professionalism to written and spoken communication.
 
And how do you make passive in English?

You can use all these combinations of the verb “be” ➕ Past Participle (3rd column of the irregular verbs list):
 
𝘢𝘮/𝘢𝘳𝘦/𝘪𝘴                                      𝘴𝘦𝘯𝘵
𝘸𝘢𝘴/𝘸𝘦𝘳𝘦                                      𝘱𝘳𝘦𝘱𝘢𝘳𝘦𝘥
𝘪𝘴 𝘣𝘦𝘪𝘯𝘨                                         𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘱𝘭𝘦𝘵𝘦𝘥
𝘸𝘢𝘴/𝘸𝘦𝘳𝘦 𝘣𝘦𝘪𝘯𝘨             ➕          𝘥𝘦𝘭𝘪𝘷𝘦𝘳𝘦𝘥
𝘩𝘢𝘴/𝘩𝘢𝘷𝘦 𝘣𝘦𝘦𝘯                               p𝘰𝘴𝘵𝘱𝘰𝘯𝘦𝘥
𝘩𝘢𝘥 𝘣𝘦𝘦𝘯                                       𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘣𝘪𝘯𝘦𝘥
𝘸𝘪𝘭𝘭 𝘣𝘦                                           𝘤𝘰𝘯𝘴𝘰𝘭𝘪𝘥𝘢𝘵𝘦𝘥
𝘪𝘴 𝘨𝘰𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘵𝘰 𝘣𝘦                                adjusted
𝘮𝘶𝘴𝘵/𝘴𝘩𝘰𝘶𝘭𝘥/𝘤𝘢𝘯/𝘮𝘪𝘨𝘩𝘵 𝘣𝘦           applied
 
And voilà: a professionally written report which leaves a great impression.

Or a nice, professional email which showcases our expertise.
 
P.S. Remember⚠️
Use the passive sensibly: always remember to consider the context and the desired effect.
