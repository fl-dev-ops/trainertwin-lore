That morning, I got to the office as usual, entered the huge open space where we were all working, switched on my laptop and logged in. It looked like a normal working day in a busy call centre.

Except, there was a strange silence in the building.

Being surrounded by different teams (Spanish, German, French, Irish), the environment was usually noisy, with people dealing with their customers in every possible European language. But that day there was no English in the air.

By 9 o’clock we realised that the Irish corner was nearly empty.

That meant that the English-speaking line was getting crazy. Our supervisor started looking for some lucky individuals to be put on the English support line. I was one of the lucky ones.

I was new, it was my first work experience and I had mainly worked in Italian.

So, to say I was nervous would be an understatement. First call comes in, a gentleman from England needed some ink, got his data and opened a ticket.
Wow, that was easy, I thought, I’m rocking it!

But then the calls started raining down on me.

Call number 10, my first experience with the Scottish accent. I was staring into the void trying to connect what I was hearing with my English school books. Absolute blank. I was already lost when noting down the numbers.

And it got worst as people kept phoning.

Call number 15, my first encounter with an angry Englishman. I mean, we’ve all been there. You need your printer, it doesn’t work, you call the customer support. This time I was on the other end of the line.

“Can I have your serial number, please?”

That was the question that triggered him. His sharp and blunt reply was: “do you mean MY PRINTER’S serial number. I don’t have a serial number”. “Sure”, I replied apologetically… then he went on describing the pains he was suffering because of me.

And he didn’t stop there, unfortunately.

He burst: “My printer is not working; can I speak to your manager?” I was staring at an empty corner and I knew the manager wasn’t there, so I could only reply: ”no! You can only speak with me and if you want my help, you need to cooperate, sir”.

And then there was silence.

Next thing I heard was. “Ah, ok”. He slowed down and was unexpectedly quiet while describing the problem. And that was it, I opened a ticket thanked him, he thanked me back and I closed the call. 

So, one direct and honest reply made the trick.

After that, the day went on more quietly, it seemed the customers were all more cooperative and polite, as if they knew about the previous experience. Probably I had been loud enough. Who knows.

At the end of the working day, I was feeling like a zombie.

6 hours of continuous calls in English had been too much for me. I could only think of my sofa. I went back home, saw it in the middle of the room and went immediately to sleep. 

It was 5PM but it felt like midnight to me.
