Seriously, I need to get up there?

Standing at the bottom of a staircase feels weird

You can barely see the top, but that’s where you need to go. 

Because you know your reward sits up there

So, you want to climb as quickly as possible
Which is dangerous and tiring (come on, running up a stair… who does that?) 🏃‍♀️‍➡️ 

And that’s when you get stuck.

The thought of climbing it all (fast) at once feels daunting, almost impossible.

The only way? 𝗢𝗻𝗲 𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗽 𝗮𝘁 𝗮 𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗲. 

Focus not on the top, but on the next step right in front of you.
And the next
And the next…

Improving your English works the same way.

If you only think about the big goal — speaking fluently and confidently in meetings — it feels overwhelming.

But if you break it down into small, consistent steps, progress suddenly feels achievable.

So, I believe we all need a 𝗦𝗧𝗔𝗜𝗥:

𝗦tart: write down one or two ideas (what you see around you, your day, your job...). 3 or 4 bullet points.

𝗧alk: use your ideas (bullets) to start speaking. Don't read but try to build a little speech. Give yourself 1 or 2 minutes, no more.

𝗔dd: details, examples, longer descriptions, longer sentences. Gradually expand from each bullet using linking words (so, because, for example…).

𝗜mprove: remove filler words (uhm, er....), stay within the 1-2 minutes, make sure you sound clear.

𝗥ecord yourself: it’s like speaking in front of an audience and it helps monitor your progress.

Each step may feel small on its own, but together they build fluency, confidence, and consistency… 

Until one day, you look back and realize you’ve reached the top.
