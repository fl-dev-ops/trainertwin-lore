When the person in charge of the training says: 
“You need to work with him”
That’s when you know you delivered real value.

That’s exactly what happened at the end of a recent project with BayWa r.e. EMEA, a company doing important work on a truly global scale.

🌍 𝗕𝗮𝘆𝗪𝗮 𝗿.𝗲. 𝗘𝗠𝗘𝗔 is a large company based in 33 countries.

They think energy, how it is produced, stored, and can be best used to enable the global renewable energy transition that is essential to the future of our planet.

They are a leading global developer, service supplier, distributor and solutions provider and have brought over 6 GW of energy online and manage over 10.5 GW of assets.

They are also an Independent Power Producer with an expanding energy trading business.

🌐 With offices in Germany, Italy, UK, Ireland, Sweden, Thailand… they really act on a truly global stage.

When you operate across borders like this, clear, confident English communication isn’t optional. It’s essential.

So how did we approach it?

🔹 We started by understanding who the participants talk to, how they use English, and where they felt stuck.
🔹 Then we built a program around real work tasks like presentations, negotiations, meetings, internal communication.
🔹 Sessions were interactive, relevant, and safe, so people could actually practice the skills they needed to grow.
🔹 Between sessions, they recorded themselves and received personalized feedback to gradually build confidence under pressure.

The result?

Here’s what participants shared:
💬 “A 𝗰𝗮𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 and 𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗴 teacher, giving everyone the chance to truly take part in the course.”
💬 “His 𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗱𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 and availability create a positive and motivating learning environment.”
💬 “After starting a collaboration with him, I had the pleasure of recommending him not only to other colleagues, but also to private individuals.”

That last one?

When the person managing the program personally recommends you — not just inside the company but outside of it — that’s the kind of impact I aim for.

Because real transformation doesn’t happen through generic lessons.

It happens when training is relevant, human, and built around how people actually work.
