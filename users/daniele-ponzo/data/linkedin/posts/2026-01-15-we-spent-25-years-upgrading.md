We spent 25 years upgrading education technology.
But not how people actually learn.

That’s the uncomfortable insight I arrived at after working across IT, education, and international environments since the early 2000s.

Back then, learning was about information:
content
procedures
knowing more

Today, we have unlimited access to knowledge.

And yet, I keep seeing the same paradox:
highly skilled professionals and learners
who know a lot…
but struggle to communicate, connect, and feel confident.

That’s when it became clear to me:

Knowing is not the same as doing.
Access is not the same as understanding.

Working in global tech environments and later in education, I saw this pattern repeat itself across roles, countries, and classrooms.

Which is why I wrote my latest article for the special New year edition of the 𝗘𝗱𝘂𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗲 𝗻𝗲𝘄𝘀𝗹𝗲𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿, curated by Pro.Ed Education Solutions

In it, I reflect on:
🔹 why access without context creates noise
🔹 why confidence is a skill that can be trained
🔹 why language is no longer decoration, but leadership
🔹 and why the future of education depends on hybrid intelligence: human + technology

The next phase of education is going to bring more platforms and faster tools.
But we need to focus on something different:
𝗛𝘂𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗶𝘇𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴.

Technology can amplify education.
But communication is what gives it meaning.

After 25 years, this is the lesson that matters most to me:
👉 Technology evolves fast.
👉 People evolve through communication.
