Yesterday I shared some of the conversations I had with founders, engineers, and business owners at We Make Future.

Here are four conversations that stayed with me...
and the advice I gave.

1️⃣ "𝗜'𝗺 𝗮𝗱𝘃𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲𝗱 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝗜 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗱 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗻. 𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝗜 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸 𝗶𝗻 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗻𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲, 𝗜 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗴𝗴𝗹𝗲"

Reading gives you time.

Speaking doesn't.

Your brain is thinking, structuring, choosing words, and reading the room all at once.

Instead of simply "speaking more," I suggested this:

Before every meeting, write one sentence:

"The one thing I want people to remember is..."

Then structure your explanation like this:
• What it does
• Why it matters
• One example

The goal isn't saying everything.
It's making sure people remember the right thing.

2️⃣ "𝗜'𝗺 𝗳𝗶𝗻𝗲 𝗮𝗻𝘀𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗾𝘂𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀 𝗮𝘁 𝗺𝘆 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗻𝗱. 𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝘀𝗲𝗹𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮𝗯𝗿𝗼𝗮𝗱 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗹𝘀 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗹𝘆 𝗱𝗶𝗳𝗳𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗻𝘁."

Because it is.

Answering questions is reactive.

Selling is proactive.

So prepare three anchor sentences:

• The problem we solve is...
• What makes us different is...
• The next step would be...

Now you aren't inventing the conversation as you go.
You're leading it.

3️⃣ "𝗜 𝗺𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗹𝘆 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸 𝗶𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗜𝘁𝗮𝗹𝗶𝗮𝗻 𝗺𝗮𝗿𝗸𝗲𝘁. 𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝗜 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗹 𝗹𝗶𝗺𝗶𝘁𝗲𝗱."

That word stayed with me.

𝗟𝗶𝗺𝗶𝘁𝗲𝗱.

Many professionals translate their Italian pitch into English.

The result?

Longer.
Heavier.
Harder to follow.

Instead, build your English message from scratch:

We help [customer]
solve [problem]
so they can [business result].

Not perfect.
Usable.

4️⃣ "𝗠𝘆 𝗱𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗺 𝗶𝘀 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗽 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝗺𝘆 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱."

The goal isn't translating faster.
It's thinking with less effort.

So don't memorise scripts.

Build repeatable structures for the conversations you have every week.

For example:

Short answer → Reason → Example → Check understanding

Simple structures reduce cognitive load.

When your brain spends less energy building the sentence...
it has more energy for the conversation.

That's when English starts feeling lighter.

Because you're no longer starting from zero every time you speak.
