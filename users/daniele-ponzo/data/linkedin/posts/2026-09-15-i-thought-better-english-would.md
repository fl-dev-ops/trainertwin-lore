I thought better English would get me promoted.
  
It didn't.
  
Many years ago, I joined an American multinational company. 

I had a simple plan.
  
Become technically excellent.
  
Improve my English.
  
Work hard.
  
The opportunities would come.
  
Except...
  
they didn't 😱 .
  
Instead, I watched colleagues around me receive the projects I wanted.
  
The international meetings.
  
The client visits.
  
The leadership opportunities.
  
Meanwhile, I stayed exactly where I was.
  
For a while, I blamed everything except myself.
  
My manager.
  
The company.
  
The system.
  
Eventually I realized something uncomfortable.
  
The technical skills weren't the problem.
  
My English wasn't the main problem either.
  
𝗩𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝘄𝗮𝘀.
  
So I stopped studying English.
  
I started studying communicators.
  
I watched how experienced managers explained complex ideas.
  
How they handled difficult questions.
  
How they influenced meetings.
  
How they made people trust them.
  
I found mentors.
  
Asked questions.
  
Practiced.
  
Made mistakes.
  
Little by little, I stopped trying to sound like a better English speaker.
  
I started becoming a better communicator.
  
That changed everything.
  
I eventually became a team leader.
  
Then a project leader.
  
And years later...
  
it became the foundation of the work I do today.
  
Looking back, I think that lesson is even more relevant now.
  
AI is helping us write emails.
  
Generate code.
  
Summarize meetings.
  
But it still can't build trust for you.
  
It can't explain your thinking.
  
It can't represent you in a room full of stakeholders.
  
Those skills are becoming more valuable, not less.
