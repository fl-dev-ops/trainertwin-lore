Heylistentomegotsmtnimportanttosay, you know?

I. Am. Pondering. Which. Words. I. should. Use. To. Entice. My. Audience.

These two sentences have something in common.

Wondering what that is 🤔 ?

Let me tell you: they are both ineffective!

❌ One is too fast, shows anxiety and nervousness. It communicates we are not in control and we want rush to the end.
-> When we try to prove that we’re fluent, we sacrifice precision.

❌ Two is too slow, shows insecurity and lack of preparation. It bores our audience and sounds monotone.
-> When we try to be too precise, we sacrifice fluency.

So, how should we speak, then?

There isn’t a magic formula, but the easiest answer would be: 

"somewhere in the middle".

When Leaders, Senior Managers, Executives, CEO’s speak, they have one thing in common:

They aim for CLARITY.

A mix of fluency and accuracy with a topping of intonation and pronunciation all served at the right rhythm and pace🍽️:

✅ Speed:
Keep a comfortable pace, not too fast to confuse the listener, nor too slow to lose engagement. 
-> Example: Angela Merkel (Former Chancellor of Germany) – Merkel is known for her calm, measured, and deliberate speaking style in English.

✅ Fluency:
A natural flow of words and ideas. Fluent speakers rarely pause excessively or stumble over words.
-> Example: Ban Ki-moon (Former UN Secretary-General from South Korea) – He often delivered speeches and engaged in diplomatic discussions without significant pauses.

✅ Accuracy:
the correct use of grammar, vocabulary, and syntax. 
-> Example: Christine Lagarde (President of the European Central Bank, from France) – She speaks English with great grammatical accuracy, delivering complex financial concepts with precision.

✅ Intonation:
the rise and fall of pitch in speech, which adds emotional nuance and keeps listeners engaged. 
-> Example: Nelson Mandela (Former President of South Africa) – His command of intonation added emotion to his words, especially in his speeches on peace and reconciliation.

✅ Pronunciation:
A confident speaker, even in a non-native language, focuses on clarity and consistency in pronunciation.
-> Example: Pope Francis (From Argentina) – As a non-native English speaker, Pope Francis enunciates clearly, ensuring that his words are easily understood by a global audience. 

➡ Remember: CLARITY should be our goal. If you’re too fast, slow down; if you’re too slow, speed up a little.

Tell me in the comments: have you found your ideal rhythm when dancing with words 💃 ?
