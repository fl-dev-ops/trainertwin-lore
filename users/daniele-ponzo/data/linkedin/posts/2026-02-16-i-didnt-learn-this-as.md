I didn’t learn this as an English teacher.

I learned it as an IT leader.

When you’re responsible for delivery, timelines, budgets, and people, communication changes.

You don’t speak to “express yourself”.
You speak to:

• move decisions forward
• reduce ambiguity
• create alignment
• manage risk

In those moments, perfect English doesn’t matter.

Clear thinking does.
Clear structure does.
Clear positioning does.

And here’s the part that matters most.

I know what it feels like to sit in a room where:

– stakeholders are impatient
– timelines are tight
– someone challenges your plan
– you need to push back
– you need to make a call

And you’re doing it in English.

That’s why empathy matters in my work.

I’m not teaching theory.
I’m not teaching textbook dialogues.
I understand the pressure.

I’ve been the one presenting to clients.
The one managing international teams.
The one handling difficult calls.
The one accountable when things didn’t go as planned.

This is what many senior IT & Tech professionals miss.

They focus on improving their English.

But the real shift happens when they start communicating like leaders
in English.

That’s a different skill.

And it’s the one that changes how others perceive you.

Because in high-stakes moments,
English isn’t the test.
Leadership-level communication is.

And that’s the skill that determines who gets trusted and who gets questioned.

👉 When the pressure is on, do you sound like a language learner or a leader?
