Trust doesn’t travel at the same speed everywhere.

In Episode 28 of The Non-Native Leader - Podcast, I sat down with Thomas Hoon, a Singaporean entrepreneur, CEO, mentor and serial team builder across Asia and beyond.

We talked about international business, yes.

But the heart of the conversation?
🌍 Learning to slow down.
🌍 Letting go of your “default” way of working.
🌍 Listening first, speaking second.

Because when you manage people across cultures — from the Philippines to Finland — speed, clarity, and results don’t mean the same thing everywhere.

At one point, Thomas said something that really hit home:
“Moving fast isn’t the most effective way in some markets. You have to adapt to pace, hierarchy, even lunch habits.”

Whether it's building trust in Vietnam, handling layered decision-making in China, or decoding feedback from a European client, every situation teaches you something new.

What I loved most about our chat?

Thomas doesn’t present a polished formula.
He shares the real experience:
👉 Frustration
👉 Self-doubt
👉 Emotional burnout

 …and how humility, curiosity, and cultural intelligence helped him grow into a more effective and human leader.

If you’re working internationally (or want to) strong English isn’t enough.

You need to learn to read a room in three languages at once: spoken, cultural, and emotional.
And that’s something no textbook can teach you.
