You don’t have an English problem.

You have a hesitation problem.

You see it in meetings all the time.

You have something to say…
but your brain hits pause.

And it shows.

Here are the most common hesitation signals 👇

𝟭. 𝗙𝗶𝗹𝗹𝗲𝗿𝘀 𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿
“Uhm… ehm… aaaah…”

👉 Why: you’re buying time
👉 Fix: Replace fillers with pause. Literally stop. Silence sounds confident.
  
𝟮. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗮𝗽𝗼𝗹𝗼𝗴𝗶𝘇𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴
“Sorry for my English…”
“Maybe this is wrong, but…”

👉 Why: lowering expectations
👉 Fix: start with the message

❌ “Sorry, maybe I’m wrong but…”
✅ “From what I see, the issue is…”
  
𝟯. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝘀𝗼𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗻 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴
“Maybe… perhaps… I think…”

👉 Why: avoiding sounding too strong
👉 Fix: be direct, then support your point

❌ “I’m not sure but maybe we could consider…”
✅ “One option is to…”

𝟰. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗿𝘂𝘀𝗵 𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗲𝗻𝗱
Words blur. Clarity drops.

👉 Why: you want to get it over with
👉 Fix: slow down on purpose

Clarity > speed
  
𝟱. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁 𝗺𝗶𝗱-𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲
“So basically… no wait… actually…”

👉 Why: building while speaking
👉 Fix: use simple structures

→ “The issue is…”
→ “What I suggest is…”
  
𝟲. 𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿-𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻
Too many details. No clear direction.

👉 Why: translating your thinking
👉 Fix: say less, structure more

→ Point → Explanation → Impact
  
Here’s the shift most people miss:

Confidence doesn’t come from better English.

It comes from removing hesitation signals.

Next time you speak, focus on this:

👉 Pause instead of filling
👉 Start without apologising
👉 Say one clear sentence

That’s already a different version of you.
  
Which one do you recognise the most in yourself?
