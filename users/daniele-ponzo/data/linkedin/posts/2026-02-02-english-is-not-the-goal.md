English is not the goal. Communication is.

We can learn a language for many reasons.

• We love its literature
• We’re fascinated by its grammar
• We enjoy its sound and rhythm
• Or… we need it to 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗲

And that distinction matters more than people think.

Today, around 1.53 billion people worldwide speak English.
Only about 25% are native speakers.

Which means the majority of English conversations in the world
happen between non-native speakers.

So before asking “𝘏𝘰𝘸 𝘤𝘢𝘯 𝘐 𝘪𝘮𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘷𝘦 𝘮𝘺 𝘌𝘯𝘨𝘭𝘪𝘴𝘩?”
there’s a more important question:

👉 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗱𝗼 𝗜 𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗱 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗶𝘀𝗵 𝗳𝗼𝗿?

If your goal is to analyze Shakespeare,
you’ll need one kind of English.

If you’re fascinated by linguistic structure,
you’ll need another.

But if your goal is to:
• work internationally
• lead meetings
• influence clients and stakeholders
• progress in your career

Then English becomes something else entirely:

👉 𝗮 𝘁𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻.

That’s why new concepts are emerging.

You may have heard of Globish
a simplified form of English used in international business.

It’s an extreme idea, but a powerful one.

The rise of business English is less about correctness and more about 𝗯𝗲𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘂𝗻𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗼𝗱 in global contexts.

Globish (and similar ideas like “Panglish”) strips language down to what really matters:
clarity, intention, effectiveness.

Not perfect grammar.
Not fancy vocabulary.
Not sounding native.

And this is where many professionals get stuck.

They try to improve everything at once.
They chase “correct English”.
They prepare endlessly… and still freeze in meetings.

Because they never defined the purpose.

The bottom line?

Before you decide to improve your English, decide why.

• What situations do you need it for?
• Who do you need to speak with?
• What outcomes do you want?

Once the purpose is clear,
the path becomes much simpler — and far more effective.

👉 So let me ask you:
 Are you learning English to know it…
 or to use it?
