English and Latin. Friends or foes? 🤝😡

We all know that English is a Germanic language.

But even in old times, people didn’t live in a bubble.🫧

The Romans were in Britannia.🏺

Then the monks arrive, with their Latin.

And, after that, the French Normans conquered the island.

So, with all this exposure to Latin and French, it is no surprise that more than 50% of English vocabulary is made of Latin-based words.

I'm passionate about etymology and the origin of words. And it always amazes me how languages are interrelated.

And I believe it is quite ironic that some people are seeking language purism. 

Think about the 𝘈𝘯𝘨𝘭𝘪𝘴𝘩 and the “𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘌𝘯𝘨𝘭𝘪𝘴𝘩 𝘵𝘶𝘯𝘨”, for example. 

Or all these people who want to preserve the “true” Italian (or Spanish, or French) from foreign influences.

Today more than ever, we live in a global World, full of quick connections. So, words spread faster and faster. 🌐

Anyway, to get back to Latin in English, here are some common words we use in business which come from Latin: 

🔵 acumen - ability to make good judgments.
🔵agenda - list of things to be done.
🔵conglomerate - parts put together to form a unit while remaining separate identities.
🔵facsimile - exact copy.
🔵incalculable - too great to be counted.
🔵obvious - clear (from the Latin for "in the way").
🔵perpetuate - preserve.
🔵plausible – probable.
🔵rapport - close relationship.
🔵reprisal - retaliation.
🔵stipend - fixed allowance.
🔵tentative - provisional.
