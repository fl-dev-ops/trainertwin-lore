Engineers spend years learning how to design predictable, scalable REST APIs.

Then, in international meetings, we often ignore those same design principles when we communicate.

And I think that's one reason so many technical professionals freeze under pressure.

Because great communication isn't that different from great system design.

Here's how I'd design a Communication API instead.

1️⃣ 𝗖𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿 𝗘𝗻𝗱𝗽𝗼𝗶𝗻𝘁𝘀 → 𝗖𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿 𝗢𝗽𝗲𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀

A good API makes it obvious where to start.

Your communication should do the same.

Instead of:
"Umm... I was thinking that maybe..."

Start with:
"My recommendation is..."

or

"The issue is..."

People shouldn't have to guess where you're going.
  
2️⃣ 𝗣𝗿𝗲𝗱𝗶𝗰𝘁𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗣𝗮𝘆𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱𝘀 → 𝗣𝗿𝗲𝗱𝗶𝗰𝘁𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗦𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲

Developers love predictable JSON.

Listeners do too.

When your message follows a familiar structure, people stop working to understand you and start listening to your ideas.

One of my favourites:

Issue → Impact → Recommendation

Simple.
Repeatable.
Easy to follow.
  
3️⃣ 𝗣𝗮𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 → 𝗗𝗼𝗻'𝘁 𝗣𝗮𝗻𝗶𝗰 𝗘𝘅𝗽𝗹𝗮𝗶𝗻

No API sends an entire database in one response.

Yet that's exactly what many engineers do when they're under pressure.

Every edge case.
Every dependency.
Every technical detail.

Instead:

Prepare one idea.
Deliver it in 1 sentence
Pause.
Then build from there.

Don't overload your audience's processing capacity.
  
4️⃣ 𝗚𝗿𝗮𝗰𝗲𝗳𝘂𝗹 𝗘𝗿𝗿𝗼𝗿 𝗛𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗹𝗶𝗻𝗴 → 𝗥𝗲𝗰𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝗪𝗶𝘁𝗵𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝗣𝗮𝗻𝗶𝗰

Good APIs don't crash because of one exception.

Neither should you.

If you lose your train of thought, don't apologise.

Recover.

A simple:

"Let me rephrase that."

is often all you need.

People remember how confidently you recover, not whether you made a small mistake.
  
5️⃣ 𝗟𝗼𝘄 𝗟𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗻𝗰𝘆 → 𝗟𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿 𝗖𝗼𝗴𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗟𝗼𝗮𝗱

This is the one that matters most.

When your brain is translating, checking grammar, searching for vocabulary, and reading the room at the same time...

communication latency increases.

hesitation appears.
you start running around in circles
panic explaining starts.
expertise becomes harder to hear.

The goal isn't perfect English.
It's reducing cognitive load.

Because when your brain has fewer processes running...

your communication becomes faster,
clearer,
and far more confident.

That's why I rarely coach grammar first.
I coach communication architecture.

Designing a system that still works when the pressure is on.

👇 If you had to debug one part of your communication today...
Which endpoint would you optimise first?

Clear openings? 
Structure? 
Information overload? 
Recovery? 
Communication latency?
