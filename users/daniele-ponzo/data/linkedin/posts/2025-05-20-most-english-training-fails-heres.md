Most English training fails. Here’s why.

Companies invest in English courses all the time

But when the meetings start… silence.

The training didn’t stick.

Why?

Because most corporate English programs aren’t designed for the real world.

They teach:
❌ Irrelevant vocabulary
❌ Random grammar drills
❌ Zero connection to how your team actually works

Meanwhile, your team is expected to:
✔ Present ideas clearly
✔ Collaborate with colleagues abroad
✔ Respond to emails fast
✔ Speak up in client calls
✔ Handle conflict with diplomacy

The gap is massive — and your people feel it.

Here’s the pattern I see over and over, and how effective training flips it:

1️⃣ Endless grammar exercises ➝ 𝗣𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗮𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗮𝘀𝗸𝘀
So that they stop memorizing rules and start using English in real conversations. Fast.

2️⃣ Generic topics ➝ 𝗜𝗻𝗱𝘂𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘆-𝘀𝗽𝗲𝗰𝗶𝗳𝗶𝗰 𝘀𝗰𝗲𝗻𝗮𝗿𝗶𝗼𝘀
So that they feel prepared and confident discussing what they actually do, not abstract textbook subjects.

3️⃣ Passive learning ➝ 𝗔𝗰𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗿𝗼𝗹𝗲-𝗽𝗹𝗮𝘆 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗱𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸
So that they don’t just “know” the language — they can apply it under pressure, in meetings and calls.

4️⃣ Fear of mistakes ➝ 𝗦𝗮𝗳𝗲, 𝘀𝘂𝗽𝗽𝗼𝗿𝘁𝗶𝘃𝗲 𝗲𝗻𝘃𝗶𝗿𝗼𝗻𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁
So that they build real confidence, speak up more, and stop holding themselves back.

5️⃣ Minimal impact ➝ 𝗧𝗿𝗮𝗰𝗸𝗲𝗱 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗴𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗼𝗻 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝘂𝗻𝗶𝗰𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗴𝗼𝗮𝗹𝘀
So that HR and L&D teams can actually see the return — in clarity, confidence, and participation.

These are changes in method.
And they lead to changes in performance.

If your team knows what to say, but can’t say it confidently in English, it’s not their fault.
It’s the format.
