A few days ago, one of the best moments at WMF - We Make Future had nothing to do with my talk.

It was catching up with Michel Murabito 🚀.

When we first started working together, he told me something I'll never forget.

"English has always been a struggle."

Not because he couldn't understand it.
He could.

Like many technical professionals, he could read.
Listen.
Follow conversations.

The real challenge appeared when it was his turn to speak.

Especially when the stakes were high.

He had already tried different English courses over the years.

But something still wasn't clicking.

Eventually he realized the problem wasn't grammar.

It was communication under pressure.

He didn't need another teacher.

He needed someone who could help him communicate with the same confidence and clarity he already had in his technical work.

So that's what we focused on.

➡️ We worked on how to structure ideas.
➡️ How to present them clearly.
➡️ How to handle unexpected questions without freezing.
➡️ How to reduce the cognitive load that so many international professionals experience in meetings.

Months later, Mich wrote something that perfectly captured the shift:

"For the first time, I feel I have taken a giant step forward."

Then he added something that meant even more to me:

"I haven't just upgraded my English skills, I have significantly improved my way of presenting and my confidence. Daniele didn't just act as a teacher, but as a true coach."

That sentence perfectly describes the work I care about.

Because my goal has never been to help people sound "more native."

It's to help them sound like the professionals they already are.

Seeing Mich again in Bologna reminded me why I love this work.

Because it's one thing to receive a message saying someone feels more confident.
It's another to meet them months later and see that confidence in person.

That's the real reward.

If you're a software engineer, architect, tech lead, consultant, or founder working internationally...
and you feel like your communication doesn't yet reflect your expertise...

know that it doesn't have to stay that way.

You don't need perfect English.

You need communication that still works when the pressure is on.

Have you ever realized that what you thought was an English problem... was actually a communication problem?
