Is being willing to compromise an important aspect of getting things done in a group?

Should people be willing to compromise on their principles for the greater good?

Those who hold onto their principles are easier to trust because if we know their principles, we can predict what they will do in any given situation.

Are principles malleable?

I'm fond of saying, "Truth is, truth is what you think it is." If you think something is true, it is to you, until you accept some other truth.

Facts are not the definitive truth. They often change over time or yield to a new perspective.

There are many well-known examples:

In 1984, Australian doctor Barry Marshall deliberately drank a laboratory culture containing the bacterium Helicobacter pylori. He did this to help prove that the organism could cause gastritis and ulcers. At the time, the medical consensus was that ulcers were mainly caused by stress, lifestyle, or excess acid, and many senior clinicians dismissed the bacterial theory as implausible.

A personal example. I used to deny the anti smoking evidence. Yes, I was a smoker. Even accepting the evidence wasn't enough to cause me to stop. I had linked smoking to freedom of choice, freedom being something I value highly. It wasn't until I flipped a belief, that choosing to smoke was exercising my freedom, that I managed to give up. I decided instead that smoking was curtailing my freedom. Then giving up became something I stuck with.

Beliefs aren't easy to change, however, changing them or at least adjusting them is often necessary to effect a behavioural change.

Back to my main question, should we be prepared to compromise in-order to get things done in a group setting?

It is a quandary that faces politicians continuously.

I'm interested to know what people think.

If truth is what you think it is, your truth is as good as anyone else's.
