How many small B2B businesses are there in the UK?
 
Let’s be more specific. I’m thinking of solopreneurs, one-person businesses, partnerships without employees, and small businesses with fewer than five employees.
 
After a few minutes' research via Google and ChatGPT, I arrived at the number, 345,000.
 
Are you a business expert or part of a small business selling to other businesses?
 
If you are in a narrow enough niche and have established a prominent reputation, perhaps business flows to you without effort.
 
Intuition suggests that the broader a niche, the bigger the market. For instance, everyone needs an accountant. Yet the opposite is true. The broader your appeal, the harder it is to stand out in your niche.
 
There are probably five or ten businesses in the UK that have an immediate need for exactly what you sell, who are aligned with your ethos and values, and who would instantly want you to do work for them, if they knew of you.
 
If you could get your unique message in front of them at the moment they recognise their need, you would win their business.
 
So, less is more.
 
To secure a steady stream of new business, identify those with an immediate need for what you sell and reach out directly or be where they look.
 
That’s what we are all trying to do on social media – be seen by those with an immediate need for what we are selling.
 
It was the promise of building a website. Build it and they will come.
 
It is why so many of us pitch up at networking events.
 
These three things are relatively inexpensive but time-intensive.
 
You can live your life on LinkedIn and get nothing unless those who need what you sell are there at roughly the same time.
 
The dream of having potential customers find you easily via your website is quickly shattered. Competing for page one listings is time-consuming, expensive, or both.
 
Networking events can be very productive if what you have to sell is desperately needed by the other networking event participants when you happen to interact.
 
I once asked a room full of consultants how much time they spent on marketing. The average seemed to be between 30% and 40% of their working hours. What could you earn if that time were spent delivering what you sell?
 
What if you spent that money, or some of it, on marketing?
 
It is a scary commitment. It might not work.
 
Perhaps you have tried this and got a metaphorical bloody nose for your trouble. There are endless schemes that don’t work or won’t work for you.
