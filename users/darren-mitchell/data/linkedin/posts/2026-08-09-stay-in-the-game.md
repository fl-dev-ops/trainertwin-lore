***Stay in the Game - the Power of the Follow Up***

Most salespeople don’t lose the deal when they hear “no.”

They lose it because they stop showing up.

Research has consistently found that the majority of salespeople give up after just one or two follow-ups, despite a large proportion of sales requiring multiple touchpoints before a prospect buys.

So here’s the uncomfortable question for every CRO and frontline sales manager:

➤ Are your salespeople addicted to instant gratification?

We live in a world of instant replies, instant results and instant feedback.

But B2B sales rarely works that way.

I was reminded of this recently.

I contacted a dealership because I was interested in testing an electric vehicle.

✅ I booked the test drive.
✅ I turned up.
✅ I drove the car.

The test drive is now over.
And since then?

Nothing.

❓Not a call.
❓Not an email.
❓Not even a simple:
“Thanks for coming in. What did you think?”

Why?

Did the salesperson decide I wasn't serious?

Did they assume I'd contact them if I wanted to buy?

Or have they simply moved on to the next lead because they need an immediate win?

This is where great sales professionals separate themselves.

They stay in the game.

But staying in the game doesn't mean pestering.

It means creating value-based contact.

➤ A useful insight.
➤ A relevant case study.
➤ A new piece of information.
➤ A different way of looking at the problem.
➤ A reason for the prospect to engage again.

The best salespeople understand something average salespeople don't:

The absence of an immediate “yes” isn't rejection.

Sometimes it's timing.
Sometimes it's competing priorities.
Sometimes it's uncertainty.
Sometimes the buyer simply isn't ready, yet.

And if you disappear, someone else will be there when they are.

So CROs and sales leaders should ask their teams:

➤ How many opportunities are we losing because we stopped following up before the buyer stopped considering us?

Don't just measure activity.

Coach the quality, consistency and value of the follow-up.

Because the sales professional who is prepared to stay the course, without becoming a nuisance, will consistently outperform the salesperson waiting for instant gratification.

Don't chase the prospect. Earn the next conversation.

👉 CROs & Sales Leaders: audit your team's open opportunities this week.

Find the prospects who haven't heard from your team recently.

Ask one question:
➤ “What value can we bring them next?”

Then follow up.
Stay relevant.
Stay useful.
Stay in the game.

What would happen to your sales numbers if your team simply became better at staying in the game? 👇
