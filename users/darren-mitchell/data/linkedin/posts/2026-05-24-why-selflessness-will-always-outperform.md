***Why Selflessness Will Always Outperform Selfishness***

The most dangerous person in sales leadership isn’t the weak manager.

It’s the selfish one.

Years ago, I watched a sales leader almost destroy a high-performing team in less than 12 months.

On paper, he looked elite.

Top-performing region.
Consistently hit target.
Sharp in every executive meeting.

But behind the scenes?

He hoarded opportunities.
Took credit for team wins.
Publicly criticised underperformers.
Refused to coach reps unless a deal directly benefited him.

At first, leadership loved the results.

Then the cracks started showing.

Top performers stopped collaborating.
New reps failed faster.
People became territorial.
Meetings turned silent.
Trust disappeared.

Eventually, the best people left.

And once they left, the numbers collapsed too.

Because selfish leadership creates fragile teams.

The best sales organisations don’t win because they hire the loudest closers or the most political leaders.

They win because they build cultures where:
✅ knowledge is shared freely
✅ top performers elevate others
✅ managers remove fear instead of creating it
✅ leaders care more about team success than personal spotlight

Selfless sales leadership scales.

Selfish leadership burns people out.

And here’s the irony:
The leaders obsessed with their own success often sabotage it.

The leaders obsessed with helping others succeed?
They build organisations people never want to leave.

In sales leadership, selfishness may deliver a quarter.

Selflessness builds a legacy.
