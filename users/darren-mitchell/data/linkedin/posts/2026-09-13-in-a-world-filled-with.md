***In a World Filled With AI, Are You The Differentiator?***

AI might help you create the proposal.
But can you defend it in the room?

I’m seeing far too many sales professionals leaning heavily on AI to create proposals, account strategies and execution plans.

And I worry we’re forgetting something fundamental:

The salesperson is supposed to be the differentiator.

Think about what happens when AI creates a complex, highly polished proposal.
The prospect asks you to present it in person.

Then comes the question:

➤ “Why did you recommend this approach?”
➤ “Walk me through this commercial model.”
➤ “Why is this the right solution for us?”
➤ “What would you do differently if you were in our position?”

Suddenly, the quality of the AI-generated document matters far less.

Your credibility is on trial.

➤ Can you explain the thinking behind the recommendation?
➤ Can you challenge the customer's assumptions?
➤ Can you connect the dots that aren't explicitly written down?
➤ Can you read the room and adapt?
➤ Can you bring a perspective that the customer hasn't considered?

That's where the human advantage lives.

AI should absolutely be used.

✅ Use it to research faster.
✅ Use it to interrogate your thinking.
✅ Use it to identify gaps.
✅ Use it to challenge assumptions.
✅ Use it to improve your execution.

But don't outsource the thinking.

Because if your customer can't tell the difference between your proposal and something any competent salesperson could generate with AI…

you've just commoditised yourself.

The best sales professionals of the AI era won't be those who use AI the most.

They'll be those who use AI to amplify their thinking while making their own perspective impossible to ignore.

For Sales Leaders and CROs, this should be a leadership priority:

Don't just teach your teams how to use AI.
Teach them how to think beyond it.

Because technology can generate an answer.

Trust is earned by the human who can stand behind it.

What are you doing to ensure AI is making your salespeople more valuable, rather than simply making them more efficient?
