***But It Isn’t My Fault!!***

One of the most frustrating statements I have ever heard is this one, ‘But it isn’t my fault!’ Unfortunately I often heard it from sales people, especially when they were not delivering the outcomes we expected. And interestingly, these were the same people who constantly delivered excuses for literally everything that happened in their role. Whether it be internal obstacles or customers not ‘playing the game’, it was excuse after excuse. And their sales performance reflected this.

Today, unfortunately not much has changed - in speaking with Sales Leaders, this remains a frustration for many of them. Is it because their team members are afraid of criticism or trying to avoid mistakes? Or is it because the expectations are so high, they feel as though they must literally convert every single conversation into a sale? Either way, it does not build resilience and certainly does not lead to exceptional results.

As leaders, one of the first key principles we must embrace is this one - despite what is happening, we must take full, 100% responsibility, for everything that happens. Which means excuses are unacceptable. Things may happen that are out of our control, but the principle still remains - take 100% responsibility.

I have lived this approach for many years throughout my sales leadership career, and certainly over the last 9 years where I have been working with sales leaders and their teams to help them extract their maximum potential.

Has it been easy? Absolutely not. There have been many times where I have had no idea where my next client or next engagement was coming from, and it would have been easy to fall into the trap of blaming external circumstances or looking for excuses. But that serves no one. 

Whilst the current results may not be where you want them to be, please resist the temptation to say ‘but it isn’t my fault’. This only serves to give the power to others (people or circumstances), and limits the ability to work through the challenge, analyse and determine the best course of action.

Here is one thing I have learned - the odds tend to be stacked towards the person who leans into challenges and takes full responsibility for the situation and stacked against the person making excuses.

As we begin the new week, please remove, or at least, reduce the excuses you are making and your team are making, and replace them with 100% responsibility.

Not only will this develop confidence, it will be empowering and you may just be surprised as to what is possible when the excuses are no longer there.


Here’s to a massive week.👊
