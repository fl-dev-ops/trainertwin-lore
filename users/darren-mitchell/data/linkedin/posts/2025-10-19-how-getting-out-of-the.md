***How Getting Out Of The Way Is The Key To Exceptional Sales Leadership***

Here’s a hard truth I had to learn as a Sales Manager:

Sometimes the best thing you can do for your team… is get out of their way.

My sales team only started winning when I stopped telling them what to do.

The moment I got out of the way, everything changed, for them and for me.

For months, I believed leadership was about being the one with all the answers.

If a deal was stuck, I’d step in.

If messaging felt off, I’d rewrite it.

If a rep was unsure, I’d tell them exactly what to do next.

I thought I was adding value.

But in hindsight, I was creating co-dependency.

Every decision, every approval, every next step came through me.

And the unintended message I was sending was clear:

“I don’t trust you to figure it out.”

The irony?

The harder I tried to “lead,” the less ownership my team took.

They waited for direction instead of using their own judgment.

They stopped taking risks because I was always there to step in.

Then one quarter, everything broke.

I was stretched too thin. I physically couldn’t be in every meeting or on every call.

And something surprising happened:

They started solving problems without me.

It wasn’t perfect. There were mistakes, missed opportunities, awkward calls.

But it was also the start of real growth.

I shifted my approach.

Instead of giving the answer, I asked:

➡️ “What’s your take?”

➡️ “What’s the best next step?”

➡️ “What would you do if I wasn’t here?”

Slowly, they built confidence.

They learned to navigate complexity.

They became resourceful, not reliant.

And when that happened, performance skyrocketed.

Deals closed faster.

Collaboration improved.

And the team started winning without me pulling the strings.

Exceptional Sales Leadership isn’t about being needed. It’s about building people who no longer need you.

How do you help your team develop independence without feeling like you’re “letting go” too much?
