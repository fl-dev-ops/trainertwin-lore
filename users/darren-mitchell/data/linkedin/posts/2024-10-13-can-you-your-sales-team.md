***Can You & Your Sales Team Resist The Urge?***

Over the last 9 years, I have thoroughly enjoyed working with sales leaders & their teams to help them unleash their sales potential, engaging with sales teams across a very wide spectrum of industries. The vast majority have their customers best interests at heart.

The one consistent element though through just about every single sales team is the propensity to introduce & talk about their product or service too early in the buyers purchasing cycle. And sales leaders, we are often the biggest culprits (I have both of my hands in the air right now!!) 

It is absolutely essential that we believe in our product or service, but we must recognise one very important point - customers do not want your product & they do not want your service. That may sound controversial but it is true. What customers want is a solution to either a known problem or a problem that has been identified through having a conversation with you. 

So why is it that 99.99% of sales people & sales leaders get to the product or service discussion too early in the conversation? Is it because they have done very little preparation on the customer, the competition or the industry? Or is it because they are conditioned to focus so heavily on the sales number & are heavily brainwashed to believe that their product or service is the elixir to solve all of the customer’s problems? I’m guilty on all charges because this is what I used to do for many years in my sales & sales leadership career, until I realised that it was not about the product, & I needed to find a way to differentiate myself & the team.

So what did I start doing? I started to not talk about the product or service, & instead started thinking about what business based questions that I could ask to understand more about the customer’s situation & how that situation was having an impact on them. The urge though to jump in & talk about products & services was intense, but a funny thing happened-the more I resisted the temptation, the more information I was able to obtain from the customer. And over time, something else intriguing started to happen - the customer started asking me questions about possible solutions. In other words, they started to lean in & generate genuine buying signals, often leading to a valuable business transaction.

So here is a challenge I would like to issue this week. For conversations you’ve planned with potential customers who are early in the buying cycle, are you able to resist the urge to talk about your product or service? Or to be more specific, can you conduct a business conversation without defaulting to your product or service, instead asking curious based questions to understand more, & to qualify better? You just may be surprised at what you discover & where the conversation may lead.

Can you resist the urge?

Here’s to a massive week 👊
