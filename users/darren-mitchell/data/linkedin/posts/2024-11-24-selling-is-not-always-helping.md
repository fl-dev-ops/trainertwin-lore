***Selling Is Not Always Helping, But Helping Sells***

Working with sales leaders & their teams for many years now, it’s fascinating to observe the difference between the sales teams which create sustainable success, & those who generate short term results, but find it challenging to replicate. 

Many sales leaders create an environment which is all about the number, selling a product or service-the approach is very much push - retrofitting a product into a perceived customer need. This may result in short term wins, but the teams which approach their craft with a pull approach, focusing on problems and customer challenges, significantly outsell their competition over the long term.

One of the best descriptions to describe this comes from an exceptional sales leader, Alan Versteeg. His approach is simple, yet profound - ‘Selling doesn’t always help, but helping sells’. This concept is founded on the pre-supposition that providing genuine value to customers fosters trust & long-term relationships, which ultimately leads to sales.

Here are 5 reasons to consider why selling is not helping, but helping sells:

1. Trust vs. Transaction
	❌	Selling is not helping: A purely sales-focused approach often feels transactional & manipulative, leaving customers skeptical.
	✅	Helping sells: When you prioritise solving the customer’s problems, you build trust, which encourages them to buy without feeling pressured.

2. Customer-Centric Value
	❌	Selling is not helping: A focus on closing the deal may overlook the customer’s actual needs, resulting in dissatisfaction.
	✅	Helping sells: By understanding & addressing the customer’s pain points, you provide tailored solutions that resonate, making the purchase a natural next step.

3. Long-Term Relationships
	❌	Selling is not helping: Aggressive sales tactics can damage relationships, leading to one-time transactions or lost opportunities.
	✅	Helping sells: Providing meaningful assistance builds loyalty & encourages repeat business, referrals, & positive word-of-mouth.

4. Perceived Expertise
	❌	Selling is not helping: Over-emphasising the product or service can make you seem pushy rather than knowledgeable.
	✅	Helping sells: When you educate & assist, you position yourself as an expert, increasing the likelihood of customers turning to you when they’re ready to buy.

5. Emotional Connection
	❌	Selling is not helping: Customers may feel undervalued or pressured when the focus is solely on the sale.
	✅	Helping sells: Genuine care & empathy creates an emotional bond, making customers feel appreciated & more inclined to support your business.

By shifting the focus from selling to helping, businesses can create genuine value-driven relationships that naturally lead to increased sales & customer satisfaction.
