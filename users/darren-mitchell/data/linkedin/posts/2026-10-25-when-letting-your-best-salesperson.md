***When Letting Your Best Salesperson Go Is The Best Thing To Do***

Sometimes, your best salesperson is your team’s biggest liability.

And the toughest leadership decision you’ll ever make might be letting them go.

As a frontline sales leader, you’re under pressure to hit the number. Every deal matters. Every percentage point counts.

So when your top performer delivers the results, it’s tempting to overlook the behaviour.

➤ The arrogance.
➤ The blame-shifting.
➤ The constant negativity.
➤ The way they undermine their peers, dismiss feedback, or take credit for someone else’s work.

You tell yourself, “They’re difficult, but they bring in the revenue.”

Here’s the often brutal truth:

Revenue can be replaced. A broken culture is much harder to rebuild.

When you tolerate toxic behaviour from your top performer, your team notices.

Your consistent performers start questioning why they should do the right thing.

Your emerging talent stops speaking up.

Collaboration gives way to competition. Trust erodes. And the culture you’re trying to build becomes the culture you’re willing to accept.

That’s not high performance. That’s a hidden cost of success.

Great sales leadership isn’t about protecting your biggest revenue generator at all costs.

It’s about building a team where people can perform, grow, challenge each other, and win together.

Sometimes, that means making the hardest call in the room.

Not because results don’t matter.

But because how you achieve those results matters just as much as the results themselves.

And when you make that decision with fairness, clarity, and conviction, you send a powerful message to everyone who stays:

Who you are matters just as much as what you deliver.

Your challenge this week:

Take an honest look at your team.

Are you rewarding the behaviours that build a winning culture, or excusing the behaviours of the person who hits the biggest number?

👇 Frontline sales leaders: Have you ever had to choose between protecting a top performer and protecting your team’s culture? What did you learn?
