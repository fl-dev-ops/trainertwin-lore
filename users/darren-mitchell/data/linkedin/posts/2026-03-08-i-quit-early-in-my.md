***I Quit***

Early in my career, I believed becoming an exceptional sales leader meant adding more.

➤ More pressure.
➤ More activity.
➤ More focus on the number.
➤ More control over the team.

But over time I discovered something uncomfortable.

Becoming a better sales leader wasn’t about what I needed to add.

It was about what I needed to quit.

1️⃣ I had to quit comparing myself to other leaders.
Comparison is a dangerous habit in leadership. It slowly replaces authenticity with imitation. You stop leading in a way that fits you and start copying what appears to work for someone else.

2️⃣ I had to quit perfectionism.
Sales is messy. People are messy. Customers are unpredictable.
If a leader is chasing perfection, the team stops experimenting, stops learning, and starts protecting themselves from mistakes.

3️⃣ I had to quit blind compliance to the number.
Of course the number matters. We are in sales.
But when the number becomes the only conversation, leadership quietly turns into pressure management.

4️⃣ I had to quit trying to be the smartest person in the room.
Exceptional sales leaders don’t dominate conversations, they create space for better thinking.

5️⃣ I had to quit fixing every problem for my team.
It felt helpful at the time.
But every time I jumped in with the answer, I unintentionally trained my team to depend on me instead of developing their own judgment. This created co-dependency.

6️⃣ I had to quit confusing activity with progress.
More calls, more meetings, more CRM updates don’t automatically mean better selling.

7️⃣ I had to quit avoiding difficult conversations.
Great cultures are not built on comfort.
They are built on honest conversations handled with respect.

8️⃣ I had to quit believing motivation was my job.
People are not “motivated” by speeches or slogans.
They are motivated when they feel trusted, capable, and challenged.

And perhaps the hardest one…

9️⃣ I had to quit thinking leadership meant having the answers.

➤ Real leadership started when I became more curious.
➤ When I started asking better questions.
➤ When I focused more on developing people than controlling outcomes.

Ironically, once I quit these things, something interesting happened.

✅ The team performed better.
✅ The culture improved.
✅ And the numbers followed.

Because exceptional sales leadership is not about driving results harder.

It’s about building an environment where results become inevitable.

So here’s a question for the sales leaders in my network:

What’s something you had to quit in order to become a better sales leader?

Or something you know you probably should.

I’m curious to hear your perspective. 👇
