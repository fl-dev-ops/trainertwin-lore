***Your Ultimate Purpose as a Sales Leader is to Become Redundant***

If your team can’t win without you… you’re not leading - you’re limiting.

Early in my career as a sales leader, I thought my job was to have all the answers.

If there was a deal at risk, I’d step in.
If there was a process breakdown, I’d fix it.
If someone was struggling, I’d map out exactly what they needed to do next.

I felt useful.
I felt important.
And I thought that was leadership.

But here’s the uncomfortable truth I learned over time:

The more my team needed me, the more I was failing them.

Because dependency is not a sign of great leadership - it’s a sign of a bottleneck.

And in sales, bottlenecks kill growth.

The real shift happened when I stopped seeing my role as “the answer person” and started seeing it as “the capability builder.”

I made a conscious decision to stop just giving my team resources - the right deck, the budget approval, the battle card - and instead teach them resourcefulness.

I wanted them to know how to find answers when I wasn’t available.

How to navigate a complex deal without me stepping in.

How to think like a sales leader, not just act like a sales rep following orders.

At first, it was uncomfortable — for them and for me.
I had to resist the urge to swoop in.
They had to resist the urge to ask for step-by-step instructions.

But then something amazing happened:

✅  The team started solving problems faster than I could.
✅  They started making better calls in the moment.
✅  And they started winning deals I didn’t even know were in play.

That’s when I realised:

The ultimate purpose of being a leader is to become redundant.
Not irrelevant - redundant.

Because when your team can operate at a high level without you, it means you’ve truly led.

Your greatest legacy isn’t in the wins you were part of — it’s in the wins they create long after you’re gone.

If you left your team tomorrow, would they still thrive?
