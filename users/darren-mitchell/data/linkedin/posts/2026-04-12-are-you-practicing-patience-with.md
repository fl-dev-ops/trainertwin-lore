***Are You Practicing Patience with Presence?***

A sales leader I worked with once shared a deal they thought they had “lost.”

After a handful of conversations, nothing materialised.
No clear opportunity. No defined next step.

So the team moved on.

Six months later, the prospect signed with a competitor.

Not because they were better.
Because they were still present.

It’s a scenario most sales leaders will recognise.

In the drive for pipeline velocity and near-term results, teams are often encouraged, explicitly or otherwise, to prioritise what’s visible and actionable now.

But that approach doesn’t always reflect how buying actually happens.

Gartner’s research highlights that B2B decisions typically involve 6–10 stakeholders, each working through their own perspectives and priorities. Alignment takes time. And importantly, it often happens outside of the supplier’s view. 

Which means periods of silence or ambiguity are not necessarily negative signals.

They are often part of the process.

The organisations that navigate this well take a different approach.

They remain engaged.

Not in a way that is intrusive or transactional, but in a way that is consistent and valuable.

They:
✅ Stay connected to accounts beyond immediate opportunities
✅ Contribute insight without forcing progression
✅ Build credibility early, before urgency exists
✅ Encourage patience, while maintaining a clear sense of purpose

Because when the customer is ready to move…

They tend to engage those who have been most helpful along the way.

For sales leaders, this is less about slowing down and more about staying the course with intent.

Patience matters.

But so does presence & urgency.

The question is:

Are your teams stepping away too early…
Or are they equipped to stay in the game long enough to matter?
