***Results Are On The Other Side Of Resistance***

The results you want are on the other side of resistance.

And in sales, that resistance often shows up as silence.

No reply.
“Not right now.”
“We’ll get back to you.”

For too many salespeople, that’s where the pursuit ends.

But for exceptional sales leaders, that’s where the opportunity to create value begins.

We’ve all heard the statistic that 80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th plus contact.

While the original research behind that number is dated and limited, the principle remains relevant: meaningful B2B decisions rarely happen after a single conversation.

Persistence matters. But value-based persistence changes everything.

There’s a fundamental difference between following up to serve yourself and following up to serve your customer.

❌ “Just checking in.”
❌ “Have you had a chance to review my proposal?”
❌ “Wanted to see if you’ve made a decision.”

These messages are about us.

Instead, ask yourself before every follow-up:

➤ What have I learned about the customer's priorities?
➤ What insight could help them make a better decision?
➤ What risk could I help them reduce?
➤ What perspective, idea or resource could move their thinking forward?

Every interaction should earn the right to the next one.

✅ Share a relevant industry insight.
✅ Connect them with a customer success story.
✅ Challenge an assumption that might be holding them back.
✅ Help them build the business case for change.

The goal isn't to be persistent for persistence's sake. It's to be persistently valuable.

For CROs and frontline sales leaders, this is more than a messaging tactic. It's a coaching discipline.

Don't just measure how many times your team follows up.

Coach them on the value they create with every touchpoint.

Because when your team shifts from chasing a response to helping a customer solve a problem, follow-up stops feeling like a sales activity and starts becoming part of the buying experience.

And that's where trust is built. Relationships deepen. Opportunities move forward.

The next breakthrough in your pipeline might be on the other side of one more valuable conversation.

CROs and sales leaders: Challenge your team this week. Before their next follow-up, ask them one question:

“What value are we bringing to the customer that they didn't have before?”

Let's make every follow-up count.
