***Don’t Fall For The Curse of The Comparison Game***

Stop comparing yourself to other Sales Leaders. It could be the very thing holding you back. 

Welcome to the curse of the comparison game. 

You’re a new Sales Leader. 

You look around and see other leaders hitting their numbers, building high-performing teams, winning awards and seemingly making it all look effortless. 

And suddenly, you start questioning yourself. 
➤ “Am I good enough?” 
➤ “Why aren’t my team performing like theirs?” 
➤ “What am I doing wrong?” 

Sound familiar?

Here’s the sometimes uncomfortable truth:

You’re comparing your chapter one to someone else’s chapter twenty.

➤ You don’t see the years of experience. 
➤ The mistakes they’ve made. 
➤ The setbacks they’ve overcome. 
➤ The lessons they’ve learned.

You see the results. Then judge yourself against them.

And that’s a dangerous game to play.

Because comparison doesn’t just steal your confidence. It distracts you from the one thing that actually matters: your own growth.

Let me be clear. 

By all means, study successful Sales Leaders. 

✅ Ask questions. 
✅ Understand their strategies. 
✅ Model their best practices. 
✅ Learn from their mistakes.

Model excellence. Don’t measure your worth against it.

Because the only Sales Leader you should be comparing yourself to is the person staring back at you in the mirror. 

➤ Are you better than you were last month? 
➤ Are you having the difficult conversations you once avoided? 
➤ Are you coaching your people more effectively? 
➤ Are you making better decisions? 
➤ Are you becoming the leader your team deserves?

That’s the comparison that counts. 

Leadership isn’t about becoming a carbon copy of someone else. 

It’s about becoming the best version of YOU. 

So, the next time you catch yourself comparing your results, your team or your leadership style to someone else’s, stop. Take a breath.

And ask yourself one question: 

“Am I better than the leader I was yesterday?” 

That’s the benchmark. 
That’s the challenge. 
That’s where real growth begins.
