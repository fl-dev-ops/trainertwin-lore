***When Quitting is Winning***

Sometimes, quitting is winning.

I recently walked away from something I had dedicated more than 13 years to.

A female sporting program I had been running as a volunteer.

13+ years of time, energy, relationships, memories and commitment.

Walking away wasn't easy.

But there comes a point where staying means compromising something far more important:

Your values.

There was a serious misalignment between what I believed was right and what was happening.

And I realised that no amount of history, loyalty or commitment was worth compromising my integrity.

So I quit.

And here's the lesson I keep coming back to, particularly in sales:

Quitting isn't always failure.

Sometimes quitting means:

➤ Walking away from a deal that isn't right for your business.
➤ Saying no to a customer who doesn't respect your boundaries.
➤ Leaving a culture that asks you to compromise your values.
➤ Stopping the pursuit of something that no longer aligns with where you want to go.

Sales teaches us to persevere.

➤ To follow up.
➤ To handle objections.
➤ To never give up.

And those are valuable traits.

But there is a difference between persistence and refusing to let go.

Great salespeople know when to keep pushing.

Great sales leaders also know when to walk away.

Because there is always another opportunity.

And high-quality people will always endure.

Your reputation, your integrity and the standards you set will stay with you long after any single deal, role or organisation is gone.

Sometimes the biggest win isn't closing the deal.

It's having the courage to walk away from the wrong one.

Where in sales have you seen quitting, or walking away, actually become the best decision?
