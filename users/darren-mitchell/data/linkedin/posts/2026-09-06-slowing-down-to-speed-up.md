***Slowing Down to Speed Up***

Your sales team may not need more urgency.
They may need less.

We’ve conditioned sales leaders to believe that speed is a competitive advantage.

➤ Move faster.
➤ Call more.
➤ Follow up sooner.
➤ Add another pipeline review.
➤ Push harder.
➤ Do more.

But sometimes, the fastest way to improve sales performance is to slow the hell down.

Slow down long enough to ask:
➤ Are we pursuing the right opportunities, or just more opportunities?
➤ Are our reps actually listening to buyers, or racing to the next question?
➤ Are we coaching the root cause or reacting to the latest symptom?
➤ Are we creating clarity or simply creating more activity?
➤ Are we giving our best people space to think, or filling every hour with “sales productivity”?

Because activity without thought creates motion.

Not momentum.

The irony is that slowing down often creates the conditions for speed:

✅ Better decisions.
✅ Sharper prioritisation.
✅ Stronger customer conversations.
✅ Cleaner execution.
✅ Fewer mistakes.
✅ Less wasted effort.

The best sales leaders I know don't confuse urgency with effectiveness.

They know when to push.

And, more importantly, they know when to pause.

Sometimes the most strategic thing a sales leader can do is stop the team from doing more and help them do what actually matters.

So here's the uncomfortable question:

Where might your team be moving faster today… only to arrive at the wrong destination sooner?

I'd love to hear from CROs and sales leaders: what's one thing you've deliberately slowed down that ultimately helped you move faster?
