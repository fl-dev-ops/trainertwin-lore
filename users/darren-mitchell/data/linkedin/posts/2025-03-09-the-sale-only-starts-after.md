***The Sale Only Starts After The Sale Is Made***

I love asking teams, especially sales teams about their perceptions of sales. Many of them, especially those not directly involved in a sales role, often have less than savoury things to say about sales - perceptions such as pushy, sleazy, untrustworthy, inauthentic. Very rarely do I hear positive perceptions, which is disappointing.

I often share with teams that I truly believe that sales is the ultimate form of service, and can be not only very lucrative, it can also be a profession of high integrity, authenticity and trust. And one of the key pillars around this is understanding that the sale actually doesn’t start until after the sale is made.

Many years ago, I purchased a brand new car, and leading up to the final purchasing decision, the sales person and the sales manager were ‘all over’ me, contacting me every couple of days to check in, hoping that I was creeping closer to a purchasing decision. Once I did make the buying decision (despite their constant ‘check in’s’) they were obviously ecstatic, however I never heard from them again. Only once did I bump into the sales person a few years later when getting my car serviced, and he didn’t even acknowledge me. Obviously to him, I was a transaction to reach his monthly sales quota. Some would say a ‘typical’ sales person!

Unfortunately what this sales person either didn’t know, or choose to ignore, was that post the sale presented a massive opportunity for the sale to be made. If he had have matched his pre-sale enthusiasm with a level of post sale attention, he may have discovered that within my network were at least 5 people who were in the buying cycle for a new vehicle, not to mention that within 3-5 years, I was probably in a new buying cycle. Instead, I got crickets. And he had to repeat the same behaviour in order to hit his sales target the next month.

So why is this such an important concept to consider? Because as a sales person, we must understand that people like to do business with people who they know, they like and they trust and if we look at a potential customer as a transaction, whilst we may win the short term deal, if we do not continue to invest in a value based relationship post the sale, we will miss out on significant future opportunities.

So this week, consider your current approach to sales. Are you looking at your customer as a transaction, or are you considering the lifetime value of your customer? If you focus on the latter, you will begin to appreciate that the sale actually doesn’t start until after the sale is made. And this approach leads to exceptional and sustainable results.

Here’s to a massive week in sales 👊
