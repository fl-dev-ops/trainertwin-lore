***Why Are You Avoiding Coaching?***

Why are you avoiding coaching?

Sales leaders say coaching is critical.
But their calendars suggest it’s optional.

Let’s be honest about something happening in sales teams everywhere.

➤ Everyone agrees coaching matters.
➤ Every sales methodology talks about it.
➤ Every leadership conference preaches it.
➤ Every CRO says it’s essential.

Yet in the real world…

Coaching is the first thing to be rescheduled.

Quarter running hot?
Coaching gets pushed.

Quarter behind?
Coaching gets pushed.

Pipeline review runs long?
Coaching gets pushed.

Executive meeting added?
Coaching gets cancelled.

The activity that drives the biggest improvement in sales performance is somehow treated like the least urgent.

And that’s the paradox.

Because coaching is the #1 driver of exceptional performance.

❌ Not more dashboards.
❌ Not more reporting.
❌ Not more forecasting calls.

✅ Coaching.

✅ Consistent. Focused. Intentional coaching.

The best sales leaders understand something many others miss:
Sales growth doesn’t come from inspecting deals.

It comes from improving sellers.

Deal reviews help this quarter.

Coaching builds performance for every quarter after that.

But coaching requires something uncomfortable for many leaders:

➤ Time.
➤ Consistency.
➤ Focus on development instead of control.

And it forces a tough question:

Are you trying to manage activity, or are you trying to build elite sellers?

Because the data is clear.

Sales leaders who coach consistently outperform those who coach sporadically.

And they dramatically outperform leaders who only coach when something goes wrong.

Not because they have better territories.
Not because they hire better reps.
Not because they run better forecasts.

Because they build better people.

And better people produce better results.

Every time.

If coaching keeps getting bumped from the calendar, it’s not a time problem.
It’s a priority problem.

Be honest: what usually replaces coaching time on your calendar?
