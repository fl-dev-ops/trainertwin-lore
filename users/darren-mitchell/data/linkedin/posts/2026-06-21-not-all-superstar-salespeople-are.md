***Not All Superstar Salespeople Are Good For Business***

Your biggest sales problem might not be pipeline.

It might be the person everyone is afraid to confront.

The "superstar" rep who crushes quota, ignores process, undermines colleagues, and creates friction everywhere they go.

And leadership keeps looking the other way because the numbers are good.

Until they're not.

Patrick Lencioni's 5 Dysfunctions of a Team highlights a truth many sales organisations learn too late:

🚫 Absence of Trust
🚫 Fear of Conflict
🚫 Lack of Commitment
🚫 Avoidance of Accountability
🚫 Inattention to Results

When one high performer operates outside the team's values, every one of these dysfunctions begins to spread.

People stop speaking up.

Managers avoid difficult conversations.

Accountability becomes selective.

Collaboration disappears.

And "team results" become secondary to individual success.

The cost?

A toxic culture that silently drives away your most valuable people.

The irony is that many sales leaders tolerate behaviour from top performers that they would never accept from average performers.

But culture doesn't care about quota attainment.

Every behaviour you tolerate becomes a standard you endorse.

The highest-performing sales teams aren't built around individual heroes.

They're built around trust, accountability, and a shared commitment to winning together.

Because one dysfunctional superstar can do more damage to long-term performance than a dozen missed deals.

As a leader, your job isn't just to manage performance.

It's to protect the culture that makes sustainable performance possible.

What's your view: Have you ever seen a "star performer" become a net negative for a sales team despite delivering great numbers? 👇
