On a recent conversation with John Golden on The Exceptional Sales Leader Podcast, one idea stood out:

When leaders step in too quickly, they unintentionally undermine their own people.

If you override your salesperson in front of a prospect, what are you really communicating?

You’re saying:

➤ “Don’t fully trust them.”
➤ “If you want the real answer, talk to me.”
➤ “I don’t have confidence in my own team.”
And prospects & customers notice.

Sales leadership isn’t about disintermediating your reps.
It’s about empowering them.

Preparation is critical. Know your role before you enter the conversation. Align in advance. Agree on signals. Support with intention.

Yes, things will go off-script.
Yes, there will be moments to step in.

But there’s a difference between supporting and supplanting.

The best leaders interject elegantly, reinforcing credibility, not replacing it.

Because when you empower your people publicly, you strengthen trust privately.

Sales leaders:
Are you stepping in to add value or stepping in because you don’t trust your team?

If this resonates, share it with a fellow sales leader who needs to hear it.
