***How I Overcame the Fear of Selling***

I used to be terrified of selling.

Not because I didn’t believe in my product, but because I didn’t fully believe in myself.

Every sales conversation felt like walking a tightrope.

One wrong word, and I thought I’d lose my balance.

I’d overthink every email.

Rehearse every pitch until it didn’t even sound like me.

And after every “no,” I’d replay the moment in my head for hours, analysing, overanalysing, and quietly doubting whether I was really cut out for sales.

What I didn’t realise at the time was this:

My fear wasn’t really about selling.

It was about being seen.

I was scared of being rejected, because deep down, I was already rejecting myself.

I was scared of judgment, because I was already judging myself.

And the loudest voice of all?
Imposter syndrome.

That constant whisper saying:

“You just got lucky on that last deal.”

“Sooner or later, they’ll find out you’re not that good.”

“You don’t sound like a ‘real’ salesperson.”

It’s amazing how heavy invisible fear can be.

For a while, I tried to fight it by faking confidence.

Smile more. Speak louder. Push harder.

But here’s what I learned the hard way; fear doesn’t disappear through force.
It dissolves through understanding.

The turning point came when I stopped seeing selling as convincing and started seeing it as connecting.

When I made it less about closing and more about contributing.

When I realised that my job wasn’t to be perfect. It was to be present.

That’s when things began to change.

Rejection stopped feeling personal, because I wasn’t selling myself. I was serving someone else.

Judgment lost its power, because I wasn’t performing, I was partnering.

And imposter syndrome?
It still shows up from time to time…

But now, I take it as a sign I’m stretching beyond my comfort zone. Not proof that I don’t belong.

Today, selling feels completely different.

It’s no longer about performance. It’s about purpose.

And that mindset shift didn’t just change my numbers… it changed me.

Fear doesn’t vanish when you succeed, it fades when you decide to keep showing up, even when that little voice says you shouldn’t.

Sales leaders, how do you help your teams move beyond fear and imposter syndrome, and sell with true confidence and purpose? 👇
