***Why Curiosity in Sales Leadership Can Be a Superpower***

Curiosity might be the most underrated superpower in sales leadership.

When the pressure to hit the number intensifies, curiosity is often the first thing to disappear.

➤ Forecasts are under scrutiny.
➤ Targets are looming.
➤ Boards want answers.

And leaders can quickly default to command and control.

➤ More questions about the number.
➤ More pressure on the team.
➤ More direction. Less discovery.

But exceptional sales leaders understand something different:

You don't always unlock better performance by telling people what to do. Sometimes, you unlock it by getting curious about what you're missing.

Curiosity changes the conversation.

Instead of asking:
→ "Why are we behind target?"
Ask: "What are we learning from the deals we're losing?"

Instead of:
→ "Why isn't the team executing?"
Ask: "What's getting in the way of their best performance?"

Instead of:
→ "When will this deal close?"
Ask: "What might the customer not be telling us yet?"

These aren't just better questions. They create space for better answers.

And those answers can uncover opportunities that command-and-control leadership often misses:

🔹 A deal that's stalled because we haven't understood the real decision-maker.
🔹 A high-performing rep who's quietly struggling with a skill gap.
🔹 A customer need that opens the door to a much bigger opportunity.
🔹 A process that's creating friction rather than driving results.

Curiosity isn't about being soft on accountability.

It's about being rigorous enough to challenge your own assumptions before jumping to conclusions.

For CROs and frontline sales leaders, the challenge is clear:

Maintain the discipline to deliver the number, without losing the curiosity to discover what could make the number possible.

Because the best sales leaders don't just have the answers.

They know how to ask the questions that reveal what everyone else is missing.

👇 A question for sales leaders:

When the pressure is on, do you find yourself asking more questions, or giving more instructions?

Share your perspective in the comments.
