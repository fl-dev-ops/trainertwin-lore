Promote your best salesperson and you'll often lose two people: a great salesperson, and the manager they never became.

A few years ago I watched a client company in Singapore do exactly that.

He was brilliant. Could close anything, charm anyone, hit a number that made the rest of the team look ordinary. Promoting him felt obvious.

Six months later he was miserable, his team was underperforming, and the CEO was on the phone asking me what had gone wrong.

The problem was they promoted him for his performance in the job he'd just left, not his potential for the one they needed him to do. 

And everybody has an emotional attachment to the reason for their success. So he did what got him promoted. 

When one of the team came to him with a problem, he solved it. 

When a rep hit a tricky deal, he took it over and closed it himself. 

It felt like leadership, but it was actually the opposite. Every time he solved a problem for them, he was training them to bring him the next one. Within months he'd become the bottleneck he used to bypass. His reps stopped thinking, because he'd trained them not to. 

His attachment to the reason he got promoted was the exact reason he was drowning.

When the best person on the team gets promoted, don't forget to tell them that their job is now to replicate themselves.
