Recruitment agencies frequently offer a 90-day replacement guarantee. That's not generosity. That's a calculated bet.

The bet is this: they know, from long experience, that nothing decisive is going to surface in the first 90 days of a new CEO's tenure. The organisation is still in good-faith mode. Nobody is scrutinising performance data yet. The narrative of the appointment is still warm. Direct reports are still forming their views. The board is still optimistic.

So the agency offers a guarantee that costs them very little — because the window in which it could be triggered is also the window in which the organisation isn't yet looking.

Around day 90, two things happen simultaneously. Inside the organisation, the informal networks (always more powerful than the formal hierarchy) have already formed a settled view on whether this person has what the role requires. That verdict has been building quietly from week one. Above the CEO, however, it is only now that the board begins to look. The first ninety days are the period during which nobody upstairs is looking closely at how the new appointment is doing. Once that window closes, they start asking.

By the time that verdict is reached, the replacement guarantee has already expired.

This is the dynamic that determines the entire logic of first-year support. The 90 days before scrutiny begins is not a settling-in period. It is the most valuable runway a new CEO has — the window in which they can build the foundations of real performance, establish credibility at the right level of the role, and shape the organisation's informal verdict before it becomes a fixed one.

A well-designed coaching plan treats those 90 days accordingly. Not as a grace period to be waited out, but as the period that determines everything that follows.
