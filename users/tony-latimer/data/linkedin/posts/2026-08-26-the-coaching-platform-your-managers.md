The coaching platform your managers are using is cheaper than me.

It will stay cheaper. That is not the number that matters.

Only 7% of companies formally assign a coach or mentor to their most senior appointments. The other 93% send people into the hardest transition of their career without structured support.

At the very top, 70% of new CEO appointments fail to meet performance expectations within eighteen months. Gartner, Heidrick & Struggles, the Stanford Rock Centre, The Conference Board and the Institute of Executive Development all point the same way.

That tier gets watched. One level down, the same transition happens without the same scrutiny.

Here is the number that stays out of the business case.

A manager stuck on something is not stuck for free. If it takes six sessions across three months to work through, the situation is live in your business for three months. Ask what a quarter of underperformance in that function is worth, then set it against the difference in coaching fees.

Two delays do the damage, and they compound.

1. Waiting for access: budget approval, a panel process, or next quarter's cycle, while the problem sits there through all of it.

2. The pace of the work itself: six sessions spread across three months is a schedule rather than a solution, and that delay is set by how the coaching is done, not by how hard the problem is.

My sessions are not time bound. They run from 30 to 90 minutes as the situation requires, and when the breakthrough has been reached it is time to get back to work. At this level a single session frequently resolves several issues rather than working through one.

How to tell what you need. 
- A transition is a period, so it takes a programme: a CEO gets twelve months because that is one business cycle, and what they face in the first quarter is not what they face in the fourth. 
- An issue is an event, so it takes one or more sessions and then it is finished.

Coaching as a Service covers the events. Hours are held across the whole management population each month and directed to whoever needs them, so nobody funds a seat for every individual, and when someone hits a wall they get senior help that week.

The ROI of high value coaching in organisations comes down to three things: how quickly help arrives, how quickly the problem is resolved, and what it is costing you while it is still there.
