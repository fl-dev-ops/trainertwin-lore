Five coaching sessions a day isn’t exhausting.
Thinking hard during five coaching sessions a day is.

That was the real insight that landed in this month’s Foundations Tutorial.

One of the members shared how doing five sessions in a single day left them depleted. Not dissatisfied. Not ineffective. Just drained. The natural assumption is volume. “I’m doing too many sessions.” And yes, there was a pragmatic conversation about limits. For most coaches, three deep sessions a day is a sensible upper boundary.

But that wasn’t the interesting part.

What really shifted the conversation was why some days feel exhausting and others don’t, even with the same number of clients.

When a coach is up in their conscious awareness, analysing the session while it’s happening, planning the next question, monitoring whether they’re “doing it right”, the brain is working very hard. That kind of coaching burns energy fast. It feels effortful because it is.

By contrast, when you are listening from the subconscious, using empty-minded listening, something changes. You are no longer pushing the session forward. You’re receiving it. Responses arise rather than being manufactured. The thinking happens, but not by you.

Ironically, this is when coaching becomes both deeper and less tiring.

The work doesn’t disappear. The responsibility doesn’t disappear. But the strain does.

This is one of the least talked about aspects of mastery. Not how to coach better, but how to coach without exhausting yourself in the process.

If you find yourself wiped out after sessions, it’s rarely because you care too much.

It’s usually because you’re thinking too hard.

And that’s a skill you can unlearn.
