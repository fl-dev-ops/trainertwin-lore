Most coaches are trying to win a game that no longer pays.
The smart ones are quietly changing the rules.

In the December 16 USA/EMEA tutorial at Simplicity of Mastery, something interesting happened.

Nobody asked how to get more clients.
Nobody asked how to tick more competency boxes.

Instead, the conversation kept circling one deeper question:

What does it actually take to play a bigger game in 2026?

As Georgy put it, “The purpose stays. What’s missing is space to express what’s deeper.”
Others spoke about stopping playing small, executive presence, self-compassion, and the energetic shift that happens when you become fully comfortable with who you are.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth most coaches sense but rarely say out loud.

Average coaching is becoming cheap.
Platform coaching is becoming scalable.
And credential-only differentiation is disappearing fast.

The coaches who will thrive are doing two things at once:

1️⃣ Developing deeper coaching capability
Not better questions. Not shinier models.
But the ability to work at the level of presence, energy, coherence, and real thinking.
That’s the work we do inside Simplicity of Mastery.

2️⃣ Learning how to access higher-value conversations
Not selling “coaching hours”.
But positioning learning, insight, and implementation in ways organisations are willing to fund properly.

That second piece is what I’m revealing in my first book, launching January 8.
Not theory. Not hype.
But how coaches actually move into well-paid corporate work without selling their soul, or sounding like a consultant they promised themselves they’d never become.

If 2026 is the year you stop playing small,
and start playing a game worth winning,
this is where the thinking begins.
