“ICF has ruined coaching!” Really?

There’s been a lot of noise lately about the ICF’s latest competency update — particularly the change to Competency 7: Evokes Awareness.

One little word has sent coaches into a spin.
The word? Knowledge.

“Why did they replace insight with knowledge?”
“Are we all going to turn into mentors?”
“Isn’t coaching supposed to be purely non-directive?”

Let’s take a breath.

Here’s the thing: this isn’t a step backwards — it’s the ICF finally acknowledging what mastery-level coaches have known for years.

You can’t coach across a knowledge gap.

Coaching is the process of making people think.

Thinking, innovation, creativity, problem solving; is all just connecting previously unconnected data (in the brain or body).

If a piece of data is truly missing, they need to find the data.

When I was doing MCC Assessor training back in 2007/8 it was made very clear by my teachers (the experienced coaches who devised the core competencies) that it is a responsibility of the coach to help a client discover sources of missing knowledge that may be useful.

Real coaching still begins with evoking fresh thinking. But if the client simply doesn’t have the data to connect, it’s not “directive” to share or help them find knowledge — it’s responsible.

I caveat and say you need to put a lot of effort into be absolutely sure the data is missing, and then:

The distinction is simple:
You may share knowledge, but never tell them what to do with it.

Recognise that nothing is true. What you hold as knowledge is simply something you have learned, been taught, figured out, AND may only be true within the limits of what humans currently know; and that may change. This is why you must have no attachment to anything you say or share.

When done correctly, it deepens the client’s thinking — it doesn’t replace it.

Soon you'll be able to validate the mastery of your coaching against the Three Laws of Coachingᵀᴹ.

It’s why our coaches aren’t rattled by this change… they understand what true mastery looks like.
