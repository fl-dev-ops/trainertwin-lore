Most leadership teams aren't teams. They're a group of people who happen to share a CEO.

Every new CEO Ive worked with refers to their leadership team, and almost without exception, what they've actually got is a group of functional heads who report to them. 

The clue is in the name. Although they dont behave in any way as a team, the fact that we instinctively call them a team is the clue. Almost everything that has to cross from one function to another requires the CEO to get involved. 

I was in conversation with a few people at a gathering of the Singapore Institute of Directors a couple of weeks ago (interesting session on HR perspectives in the age of AI), when somebody actually said, "But isn't that the CEOs job?" 

No, it isn't. The CEOs job covers three areas: Guide and develop their direct reports. Connect their organisation to the rest of the world, and be strategic. In other words, have time to smell the horizon and see what's coming; have time to think about, and work with their direct reports, to see what needs to be done to equip the organisation to face it and thrive through it. 

The leadership team has to learn the big secret that when you're promoted to functional head, you now have two roles. The obvious one is the head of your function, and the one nobody talks about is being part of the team that runs the business. 

If you are a CEO who is constantly being pulled down from the strategy level to the execution level, we should talk.
