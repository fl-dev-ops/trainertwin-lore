Tariff Chaos: Leadership Lessons from the Lehman Collapse

We’ve been here before.

I remember exactly where I was on September 15, 2008, when the BBC World Service announced that Lehman Brothers had collapsed. That moment changed everything. My business partner and I stopped what we were doing and spent several days thinking deeply, talking constantly, and strategising around what was likely to come.

We got it right.

We predicted that companies would freeze hiring, reduce headcount, slash budgets, and halt spending across the board. But we also saw a deeper consequence: the unrelenting pressure that would fall on leaders at every level to deliver results with fewer resources.

The unexpected shift for our business came when the financial sector—an industry that had never worked with us before—began calling. One HR Director put it bluntly:

“For years, business flowed. Profits rolled in. We never really had to look at productivity. To be honest, we’ve had ten people doing the work of seven. Now we’re lucky if we have seven people… and they still have to deliver. The problem? Our leaders don’t know how to manage performance. They don’t know how to have the conversations we now desperately need.”

Here we are again—17 years later—with a whole generation of senior and mid-level leaders who never lived through that storm.

Since 2008, many organisations have slipped back into a rhythm: set annual goals, check in at mid-year, then run end-of-year appraisals. But in times like these—amid the tariff chaos now disrupting global business—that approach simply won’t cut it.

Companies are under immense pressure to deliver with tight budgets, constrained headcount, and paused training programmes. There’s no time for lengthy development plans. You need fast, effective change.

That’s where we come in.

We’ve helped leadership teams navigate this before. Using our Voyage Planning concept and our Profitable Leadership Performance Coaching approach, we work intensively with the top team to help them lead effectively—together.

We help them:
• Reclaim clarity, focus, and alignment.
• Replace outdated performance cycles with real-time, no-fail performance conversations.
• Equip their direct reports with the skills to do the same—creating a ripple of high performance through the organisation.

This is what performance leadership looks like in chaotic times.
