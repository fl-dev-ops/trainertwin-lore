Most coach training programmes teach you how to coach.
Almost none teach you how to survive as a coach.

Which is odd, when you think about it.

In last week’s Foundations tutorial, we didn’t talk about competencies, markers, or how to “sound like a coach”.

We talked about the thing almost nobody names out loud:

- Why so many good coaches quietly disappear
- Why selling “coaching” is the weakest possible commercial position
- Why platforms are winning on price, and humans are losing on value
- And why the future belongs to coaches who know something and can help others apply it

Here’s the paradox.

Companies will happily spend serious money on learning.
They will not spend serious money on “coaching hours”.

But combine knowledge, context, and implementation, and coaching suddenly becomes essential, not optional.

That’s what Simplicity of Mastery Foundations is for.

Not just to make you a better technician.
But to help you think like a professional.

How to articulate what you know.
How to position yourself without feeling “salesy”.
How to stop chasing hours and start building work that pays properly.
And how to develop the kind of presence AI will never replicate.

If you’re a coach who suspects that the problem isn’t your skill, but the way the profession talks about itself, you’ll feel at home here.

Simplicity of Mastery Foundations is where that conversation starts.
