The Question Every Credentialed Coach Should Be Asking — But Usually Isn't

You passed your PCC. You've logged your hours. You've done the training, the practice recordings, the mentor coaching. You know the competencies.

And somewhere in your coaching sessions, you notice a gap.

Not every session. Not even most of them. But often enough to register; a moment where you're not quite sure what you said that made a difference, or you said something that felt clever but didn't move the client anywhere. The conversation happened. Something shifted, maybe. Or maybe it just continued.

The question most experienced coaches never formally ask themselves is: where, precisely, am I on the scale between competent and mastery? Not in terms of hours or credentials. In terms of what actually happens in the room.

This matters more than it sounds. Because the difference between a competent coach and a mastery-level coach isn't just knowledge. It's not just technique. It's something harder to name and therefore harder to develop deliberately. 

It shows up in things like whether you're genuinely free from needing the client to reach a particular outcome. Whether you can stay fully present when something emotionally charged comes into the session without it pulling you off centre. Whether everything you say is cleanly grounded in what the client actually brought, or whether something of yours is quietly in there too.

These are not things that coach training programmes typically help you measure. And they're not things a performance review or a credential renewal tracks either.

I've spent the last few years developing a way to map exactly this — six behavioural categories that sit above any specific training methodology or credentialing framework, which then align to what I've come to call the Three Laws of Coaching™. 

You don't need to know what those laws are to find the assessment useful. You just need to be willing to answer honestly. The Coaching Mastery Profile takes about twelve minutes. The report it generates is specific to your coaching behaviour; not generic encouragement, but a direct read of where your natural tendencies are taking you, and what that means for the level of coaching you're currently delivering.

If you're a credentialed coach who takes their development seriously, it's worth knowing where you actually are.
