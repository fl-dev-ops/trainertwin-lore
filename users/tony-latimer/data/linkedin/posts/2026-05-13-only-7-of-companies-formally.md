Only 7% of companies formally provide coaching support to their C-suite executives. The other 93% are betting that talent alone is enough. The failure rate tells you how that bet is going.

Seven percent is the figure that stops me every time I reference it. It comes from research across the Rock Centre at Stanford University, the Conference Board, and the Institute of Executive Development. Many organisations have leadership development policies in place, and most of them struggle to execute at the critical moment — when a new general management appointment has just been made and the clock has started running.

The consequence is predictable. A newly appointed CEO is navigating the most complex leadership transition of their career, without structured support, while simultaneously being held accountable for delivering the strategic mandate that justified the appointment in the first place. Seven in ten fail within 18 months. That is not an anomaly. It is the natural output of a system that invests heavily in finding the right person and almost nothing in helping them succeed.

There's a question I put to every CHRO and board member I work with: "if you knew before you made the appointment that the odds were 70/30 against, what would you do differently?" The answer is usually some version of — we would be far more deliberate about the support structure around the new leader, and far less confident that capability alone would carry the day.

The first six months of a new CEO's tenure is when everything is decided. It's when the 90-day window of good faith either converts into genuine credibility or gently expires. It's when the leadership team either starts to function as a real collective or settles back into the patterns the new CEO was brought in to disrupt. It's when the transformation either takes root or doesn't.

Getting that support in place, specialist, structured, starting on day one, is not a luxury. It is the most cost-effective risk management decision available to any organisation making a senior appointment.
