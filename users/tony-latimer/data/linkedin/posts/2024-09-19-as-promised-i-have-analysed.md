As promised I have analysed the academic paper about the coming of the Daleks; and the conclusion, translated into simple english, is . . .

1. The study discovered coaches from 50 different countries felt threatened or worried about AI coaching. 

2. As a result, they were less interested in learning about it;  

3. and therefore they had a more negative opinion of AI coaching.

Or as Simon Sinek put it: "The cynical majority said, "We don't believe you. We don't need it. We don't like it. You're scaring us."

But there is a lot more to be discussed around ai coaching.

The author's recommendations to overcome the problem was:

Do some deep breathing or meditation so you won't be so scared; then learn more about ai and get used to it.

Not exactly reassuring or helpful.

What does need to be recognised is that there is a huge difference between adopting ai as a supportive learning and reflection tool (for example using it to analyse a transcript for faster and deeper mentoring), and ai being used to actually coach someone.

One cannot help but feel that those who may bring ai coaching to the mass market are gently blurring the lines.

The simple truth is that ai coaching tools are getting better, and will continue to. If you only coach at a level that will pass a PCC or equivalent credential, you are under threat in the medium term. 

I have tested as many of the ai coaching bots as I can get my hands on.

Some are rubbish, and trip very easily into giving advice and asking stacked questions; things that would be a fail at the first level credential; some like Prof. Nicky Terblanche (PhD)'s work well in a restricted focus with willing users;   

but they are all getting better - and some are rather good. (Sam Isaacson's is the best I have tried so far; really had to work hard to trick it into giving advice)

The reality is that coaching at the "Newtonian Physics" level; what you can see, hear, say, can/will eventually be able to be programmed to be done by an ai. 

One of the limitations is that GPTs are designed to ANSWER questions from a knowledgeable, whereas coaching is designed to Ask questions (from a knowledge/experience base). This can be overcome by fundamental redesign, or more likely better programming.

Second limitation is the lack of ability to sense silence or detect thought.

This gets easily overcome by embedding sensors. This has already been demonstrated this year with robots.

So we are left with a reality that you need to coach at the level beyond Newtonian physics; at the energetic level, "quantum physics" if you will.
