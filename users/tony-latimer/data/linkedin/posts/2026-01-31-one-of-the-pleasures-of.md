One of the pleasures of this story is that Arthur doesn’t become a different person.

He doesn’t turn into an extrovert. He doesn’t suddenly love selling. He simply stops flailing, because he starts to see patterns. He starts to ask better questions, and he starts to notice what people respond to when he’s actually paying attention.

That’s the invitation for the reader too. You don’t have to become a salesperson. You have to stop abandoning the skills you already rely on in coaching.
