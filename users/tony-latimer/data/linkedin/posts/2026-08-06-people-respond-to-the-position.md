People respond to the Position, not the Person.

The minute you step into the top job, there is an immediate change.

The colleagues who used to push back, challenge ideas, and tell you when something wasn't working start agreeing with you. Not because you suddenly got smarter, but because you're now the CEO.
 
Which means nobody tells you the whole truth anymore. 

You have to work your way through that and get comfortable with the discomfort of it, because it doesn't go away.
 
The second thing is you don't have anybody to talk to. You can't sit down with your direct reports and brainstorm your uncertainties, because the moment you do, they're going to tell you what they think you want to hear. 

It's like thinking through a problem with an Al chatbot. It's always going to try and figure out what you really want and then just agree with you.
 
You need someone external; a thinking partner whose job is to challenge you to think deeper, not to make you feel better about what you've already decided.
 
That's what a good executive coach does in those first six months. An honest thinking partnership that the role itself makes almost impossible to find inside the organisation.
