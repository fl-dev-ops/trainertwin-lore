Everyone knows what it costs to hire a CEO. Very few ask what it costs to mako them successful.

The hiring number is visible and gets scrutinised. A retained search firm charges 25–40% of first-year total compensation for a C-suite appointment. Add relocation, sign-on, legal, and onboarding costs, and before the new CEO has attended their first leadership team meeting, the organisation has committed somewhere between $300,000 and $345,000. That figure goes through a budget approval process. Someone owns it. It gets discussed.

The cost of making them successful; of giving them the structured support to navigate the most demanding leadership transition of their career; rarely gets asked about at all. And because it doesn't get asked about, it doesn't get funded. And because it doesn't get funded, seven in ten new CEO appointments fail within 18 months.

The irony is that the cost of that failure dwarfs both numbers. Severance runs to 12–24 months' base salary. Interim leadership adds more. Then comes a full replacement search at the same fee level, another onboarding cycle, and the indirect damage: direct reports who leave, projects that stall, revenue that doesn't materialise. The research puts the total cost of a single failed senior appointment at $1.5 million to $5 million or more — for one hire, at one salary level, in one appointment cycle.

The entry fee is visible. The exit cost is modelled. The investment in the middle — the piece that stops the exit cost from being triggered — is the one that gets questioned.

That logic is exactly backwards. I've written about why, and what the numbers look like when you put them all on the same table.
