The ICF Hours Trap: Why Logging 500 Hours Isn’t Earning You Corporate Contracts.

In this latest edition of Profitable Leadership, I’m taking a hard look at a structural flaw that’s holding back thousands of talented coaches: the way the International Coaching Federation (ICF) measures and rewards coach development.

My article exposes how the current emphasis on logging raw hours—specifically requiring a high percentage of "paid" hours for early credentials—forces new coaches into a commercial trap.

To hit these quotas without access to real corporate buyers, coaches turn to reciprocal peer coaching. But coaching another coach on rehashed or theoretical problems doesn't build real-world capability. 

The result? A market flooded with credentialed coaches who lack genuine workplace practice, while corporate buyers remain skeptical of what an ACC or PCC credential actually guarantees.

In this piece, I cover:
The Market Disconnect: Why so many coaches are earning less per session than their hairdresser, and where the real untapped corporate opportunities lie (hint: it’s not just tier-1 multinationals).

The Perverse Incentive: How counting peer coaching as "paid" work creates a performative echo chamber rather than real coaching capability.

The Buyer's Perspective: Why HR directors are struggling to trust credentials—and what evidence they are actually looking for.

The 3-Step Reform: A simple policy shift that would deliver hundreds of thousands of coaching hours to underfunded causes (healthcare, education, charities) while giving new coaches the real-world experience and portfolio evidence corporate buyers demand.

It’s time to stop counting hours and start building real coaching capability.
