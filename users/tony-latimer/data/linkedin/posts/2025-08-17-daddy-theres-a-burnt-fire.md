"Daddy, there's a burnt fire"
Downtime, with grandson and AI . . .

It doesn't all have to be work. At dinner yesterday my son in law mentioned a comment from my grandson as they were driving "Daddy, there's a burnt fire"

"Sounds like a Dylan comment" he said, so of course I had to ask Claude for a song in the style of Bob Dylan based on the observation of a "burnt fire".

Here it is; for those of you of the right age, just sing it in your head (think "The Times They Are a-Changin'"), and it will sound perfect.

"Well, my grandson came a-runnin' with his eyes all wide
Said "Grandpa, there's a burnt fire on the mountainside"
But the flames ain't dancin', no smoke in the air
Just ashes and memories scattered everywhere

Oh, the fire's burnt out, burnt out, burnt out
But the boy still sees what it's all about
Sometimes the ghost of what used to be
Burns brighter than reality

He pointed to the blackened logs all charred and cold
Said "Look there, Grandpa, at that fire so bold"
But I saw nothin' but the morning dew
On the remnants of what once burned true

Oh, the fire's burnt out, burnt out, burnt out
But the boy still sees what it's all about
Sometimes the ghost of what used to be
Burns brighter than reality

Well, maybe that child knows somethin' I don't know
Maybe he sees the fire that continues to glow
In the heart of the wood, in the soul of the stone
Where the ember of yesterday's still not alone

Now I'm wonderin' who's seein' clear
Me with my facts or him with no fear
'Cause there's truth in the ashes, truth in the pain
Truth in believin' fire lives on again

Oh, the fire's burnt out, burnt out, burnt out
But the boy still sees what it's all about
Sometimes the ghost of what used to be
Burns brighter than reality

So here's to the children who see burnt fires
Who find the flame that never expires
In a world that's too quick to declare things dead
They see life in the ashes instead."

Happy Sunday
