Code is cheap now. Attention and coordination aren't.

Most engineers I know are running three or four coding agents at once. Generating code is no longer the hard part. The hard part is deciding what to build: and keeping everyone, people and agents, pointed at it.

Decisions get made in threads, in calls, in someone's head. Agents keep building from last week's plan. 

You spend the day re-explaining context instead of deciding anything.

Today we're launching 𝗟𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗙𝗼𝗿𝗰𝗲 𝗪𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀𝗽𝗮𝗰𝗲, a multiplayer workspace where every decision outlives the conversation it came from.

→ Every piece of work gets a topic. Your team talks it through there: messages, call transcripts, notes.

→ The workspace writes the record itself: a wiki of what's decided and what's still open, and a to-do list of who has what. Every line cites the exact message it came from. No summaries you have to take on faith.

→ When someone quietly contradicts a decision three weeks later, it says so. Out loud, in the thread.

→ Hand any to-do to your coding agent over MCP -Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot. It starts knowing what your team decided, and its progress comes back to the topic.
Your people decide. Your agents build.

If you're running agents on a team: where does the plan actually live right now?
