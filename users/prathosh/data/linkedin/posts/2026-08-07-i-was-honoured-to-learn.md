I was honoured to learn that I've been selected for the IISc Award for Excellence in Teaching 2026. This recognition made me reflect on how much teaching has changed over the past decade.

Over the last 10 years, I've had the privilege of teaching at IIT Delhi Indian Institute of Technology, Delhi, IISc Indian Institute of Science (IISc), and through NPTEL NPTEL, reaching more than 15,000 learners. That journey has been every bit as educational for me as I hope it has been for my students.

When I started teaching, the challenge was helping students access knowledge.

Today, knowledge is abundant. AI can explain concepts, write code, summarize papers, and even complete assignments. The bottleneck is no longer access to information; it's developing judgment.

The role of a teacher is therefore changing. It's becoming less about delivering content and more about helping students ask better questions, reason from evidence, design meaningful experiments, evaluate AI-generated outputs critically, and cultivate the curiosity to keep learning long after a course ends.
Ironically, the more capable AI becomes, the more important these human abilities become.

I'm grateful to the thousands of students and learners I've had the opportunity to teach over the years. Every thoughtful question, every disagreement, and every success story has shaped the way I think about education.

This award is a wonderful encouragement to keep experimenting, not only in AI research, but also in how we teach and learn in the age of AI.
