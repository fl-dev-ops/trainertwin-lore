speaker_2: You worked for Pekka Viljelkäinen at Teater Corporation as a facilitator around the time when a lot of the material for the No Fear book was developed Right How has the world changed since then because of the digital cowboys and their arrival

speaker_3: How has it changed It was an interesting question I thought about this for quite a while I think whats changed is number one the appreciation of the fact that there are differences I think the internet has in many ways created a great equalization platform that allows the differences not only to be more visible to other people because You know before we worked in our little silo We worked you know whether we were in Finland we were in Sweden we were in Denmark or the Netherlands or Germany Our world was largely limited to what we could see and what we could travel to And today just a click of a mouse takes you to I mean it can take you to Malaysia to to Argentina and everywhere in between So I think the awareness of the differences has actually heightened tremendously But at the same time The internet has also created if you will a bit of an equalization in terms of these differences mean less because the communication thats facilitated by the internet allows people in these often very disconnected and different places to be able to link to each other and to share information in ways that they couldnt before Youre carrying an iPad for example so what that means is even without the the need to sit down at a PC and open and do all of this stuff Wandering wherever you are you pick up a network or a 3G and you have access to information that you didnt have I mean even 5 or 10 years ago And that I think changes the perspective and it changes the conversation in the way people interact with each other So that to me is the biggest difference the awareness as well as the way in which that awareness is used to change the conversation

speaker_2: One of the points in No Fear is that cultural and national differences mean less for the kids of today the digital cowboys Hows that changing corporate leadership or how should it change it

speaker_3: Not as quickly as I think it should because I think much of the corporate leadership that you see today are the same ones that were in place 5 years ago Okay in the case of PECO thats changed specifically but in many of these organizations that I work with today particularly in the industrial sectors and consulting businesses and things like that the leadership does recognize the need to integrate this widening playing field if you will I wouldnt say its a leveling one but its a widening playing field and that more players are acting upon it in different ways Social media for example the place where youre involved is one of the things that I think has changed the way in which organizations communicate with each other Also its brought in a bit of a watchdog if you will in terms of the way in which any message if its poorly presented will very quickly make the rounds of the internet and come back to bite you essentially in a very very short time So the information out to information back that timeframe has shortened dramatically So thats one of the things I think has changed quite dramatically in the last few years

speaker_2: How should leadership education prepare leaders and companies to better work with Well

speaker_3: first of all its a damn good question actually Its one of the things that I notice For example if you take an example from my country where I was raised in the US you look at the 2 recent presidents Bush and Obama and you look at the one who was more or less the technophobe and one who embraces technology The internet and the website and the way he leveraged for example social media was credited largely with the way in which Obama actually won the election So I think he is an example of a leader who is more in touch with not only the people to whom hes talking but the way in which they talk That link I think is one of the essential things that leadership schools were sitting at IMD so this is one of the things I think that IMD has to do as well as other organizations is to bring together this mesh And its not Its not just about the technology Its the way in which people use the technology to communicate and how to leverage that to actually accomplish their business goals Because the business objectives and the challenges they face those dont change Those are exactly the same We need to enter a new market We have to launch a new product Those things are exactly identical What changes is how do we do it And the way to reach your target market Thats changing quite dramatically in the last few years and thats where I think the old practices of leadership schools need to catch up a little bit with whats going on today

speaker_2: So what youre saying is that cultural differences actually mean less than they used to

speaker_3: Yes I believe that cultural differences although they will never go away and I hope they dont because its part of what makes the world rich in terms of the human interaction in terms of business environment I do think that they are to some extent being overtaken by the need for greater communication and that the technology has enabled communication in a way that some of the cultural differences Are in fact less significant Yes absolutely I mean I led a team of people that spanned 7 different offices across well actually most of the world between Asia and Europe And in that group of people there were about 23 or 24 different nationalities And yet through our ability to communicate with each other we still served a common purpose We served our customers We helped them to accomplish the things that we wanted to do And that was actually the most important thing It wasnt about are you Spanish or are you Malaysian It was more about whats our common purpose And I think that the digital age has evolved in a way thats allowed us to focus more on that to focus on the critical elements
