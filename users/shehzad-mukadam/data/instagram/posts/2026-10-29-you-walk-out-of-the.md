You walk out of the board meeting. Your team is waiting.
What you say next decides whether they trust you.

This quarter, your team gave you their best ideas.

Upstairs, there's pressure to look sharp. Part of you wonders: if I give the credit away, will anyone see my value?

That's a normal fear. It doesn't make you a bad leader.

I train sales teams and managers, and I've watched two kinds of leaders walk into those rooms.

One presented the team's idea as his own.

The other took the blame himself, and never told the team what he'd absorbed. He just kept the pressure away from the people doing the work.

Only one team felt safe.

Simon Sinek wrote about this in Leaders Eat Last. In a Marine chow hall, the most junior Marines eat first and the most senior go to the back of the line. He calls what the best leaders build a "Circle of Safety." 

Three habits before your next board meeting:
➡️ Credit goes down. Name who had the idea, in the room.
➡️ Blame comes up. Take the hit upstairs first.
➡️ Pressure stops with you. Your team executes. You absorb.

Skip this, and good people stop bringing you their best ideas.

Do it, and your team tells you the truth early and works at its best.

Tomorrow, name one person on your team whose idea you'll credit out loud.
Then send this to a leader who once protected you. 💬
