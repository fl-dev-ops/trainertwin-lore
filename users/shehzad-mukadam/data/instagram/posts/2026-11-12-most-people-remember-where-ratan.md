Most people remember where ratan Tata stood in 2008.
Fewer talk about what he did next.

In November 2008, the Taj Mahal Palace in Mumbai was attacked. Reports say Tata, then 70, stood in solidarity with the hotel’s management and staff outside the Taj.

Inside, many staff refused to leave until they had made sure their guests were safe. 

In the weeks that followed, he visited injured employees in hospitals and consoled those who had lost family members. Reports say the families of employees who died received financial support ranging from Rs 36 lakh to Rs 85 lakh, were paid full salary until the employee’s date of retirement, and had their children’s education supported. 

The employees stayed for the guests. The company stayed for their families.

I started my career in hotels, and this is the standard I hold leaders to.

Your team won’t judge you on a good quarter. They’ll judge you on the worst day of someone’s life, and on every day after it.

You may never face anything like this. But a small version arrives in every team: a bereavement, an illness, a burned-out colleague, a mistake that exposes someone.

They watch for three things:
➡️ Do you show up in person?
➡️ Do you support before you’re asked?
➡️ Do you keep the promise when the news moves on?

What’s one thing a leader did for you on a hard day that you’ve never forgotten?
