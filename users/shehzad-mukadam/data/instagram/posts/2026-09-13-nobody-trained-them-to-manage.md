Nobody trained them to manage. They just got promoted for being good at their old job.

Great salesperson → mediocre sales manager.
 
Great engineer → poor people manager.
 
Great employee → reluctant leader.
 
We keep making the same mistake: promoting people for what they were good at, not for what the new role actually needs.
 
Nobody sat them down and taught them:
 
→ How to give feedback that doesn't sound like criticism 
→ How to delegate without micromanaging 
→ How to have the conversation nobody wants to have 
→ How to coach instead of just doing the task themselves
 
So they default to what they know - working harder, not leading better.
 
Then leadership wonders why engagement drops the moment someone gets promoted.
 
It's not the person. It's the gap nobody filled.
 
If you're a manager who was never actually taught how to manage - you're not alone, and it's not too late to close that gap.
