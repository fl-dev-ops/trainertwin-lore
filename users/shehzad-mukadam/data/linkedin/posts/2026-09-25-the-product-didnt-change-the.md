The PRODUCT didn't change, the PRICE did. 

In Big Miracle, a young boy notices reporters struggling to stand on the freezing ice.

So he finds a simple solution:

Cardboard for their feet.

One reporter refuses to pay $20.

But when the cold becomes unbearable and the supply runs out, she comes back ready to buy.

The price?

$40.

Why?

Because the problem became more urgent.

The supply became limited.

And suddenly, the same cardboard had much more perceived value.

That's the lesson:

Customers don't always pay for the product. They pay for the value of solving their problem.

Great entrepreneurs don't just ask:

What can I sell?

They ask:

What problem can I solve, and how valuable is that solution right now?
