Two top sales performers got promoted to manager.
One told me he misses his old job. The other quit.

Both were great at selling. Both were promoted for it.

The first, a former top closer, sat down with me after a training and talked about his team's results. Then he admitted he misses being on the floor, because his numbers now depend on people who can't all do what he did.
The second had been a manager for only two months when I trained his team. Months later I met him again. He'd left.

He told me the job was overwhelming. The process wasn't structured, and he didn't know how to fix it. When he raised problems, he was told to come back with solutions.

Then he said: I know how to sell. I was never trained to think this way.
Different industries. Same gap.

Here's what I took from it:
A promotion changes the job from doing to enabling. Most companies reward the first skill and assume the second will appear.

Before a top performer gets the title, they need three things:
➡️ How to set goals for a team, not for themselves
➡️ How to lead people who sell differently from them
➡️ How to think in strategy, not only in targets

That's training, and it has to come before the announcement, not after.
I'm not saying promotions are wrong. I'm saying the training should come with them.

HR leaders and founders: what does your company give a top performer between "congratulations" and their first team meeting?
