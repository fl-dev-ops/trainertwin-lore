Most sales teams think they lose deals because they can’t close.
The real reason is simpler… they fail before the conversation even begins.

Yesterday, I spent 6 hours in a room that proved this in real time.
At The Ritz-Carlton DIFC, I facilitated a Sales & Communication Fundamentals workshop for a client, in collaboration with Dynamo Selling.

➡️ 40+ professionals.
➡️ One room.
➡️ One focus: how people actually make decisions in real conversations.

We didn’t talk about “selling techniques.”
We talked about people.
How they think.
How they react.
How trust is formed in seconds… and lost just as fast.

We explored human behaviour, buyer psychology, communication cues, confidence, objection handling, DISC behavioural styles, and the real structure behind effective sales conversations.

But none of that is what I’ll remember most.

Because frameworks don’t change people.
Moments do.

During a discussion on DISC behavioural styles, something shifted.
One participant paused and said:
“I can now clearly see my clients and colleagues differently. I understand their behavioural patterns… and I know how to adjust my communication with them.”

That wasn’t a learning outcome.
That was awareness becoming action.

A second moment followed soon after.
Another participant shared how he often faces resistance from store managers when introducing premium products. The usual response: “This won’t sell in my store.”
After the session, he said something different:
“I now understand how to handle that conversation with confidence. Not by pushing the product… but by reframing the opportunity.”

That is the real transformation.
Not learning scripts.
But changing how you think under pressure.

And then I noticed something even more powerful.
The leadership team didn’t step in, open the session, and leave.
They stayed for all six hours.
Present. Engaged. Fully invested in their people.
That single decision speaks louder than any strategy deck ever could.
Because culture is not what leaders say.
It is what they are willing to participate in.

By the end of the day, something was clear in the room.
People weren’t just leaving with tools.
They were leaving with clarity.
Less hesitation.
More awareness.
More confidence in real conversations.

And that’s when training stops being training.
It becomes transformation.

To Mohammed Hegazi and Meenu, thank you for orchestrating this with precision and intent.

To every participant, thank you for your openness, energy, and willingness to challenge how you think and sell.

And to every leader reading this:
Your team doesn’t need more information.
They need better understanding of people.
Because performance doesn’t change when knowledge increases.
It changes when behaviour does.
