He isn’t selling a camera to her. He’s selling what the camera does to the three the shop already owns.

That’s the reframe. The owner’s objection is that he has three identical cameras and doesn’t need a fourth. Huck’s answer isn’t that the fourth is worth buying. It’s that the fourth makes the other three easier to sell. One priced high, one priced low, two in the middle. The cheap one reads as broken and pushes people up. The expensive one makes the middle look like a deal. Nobody needs to buy the decoy for the decoy to earn its keep.

This is a real effect and it’s been measured. Dan Ariely tested The Economist’s subscription page on 100 MIT students. With a pointless print-only option sitting on the page, 84 chose the expensive bundle. Take that option away and only 32 did.

The best thing you can sell someone is rarely the thing itself.

What are you selling as a product when it’s really a lever on something else?
