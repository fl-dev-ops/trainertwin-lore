My manager from Germany mentored me for a year. She gave me advice about work that I didn't fully believe at the time.

In 2008, she taught me how to be emotionally strong at work.

Her rules:
➡️ Be familiar with everyone. Don't turn colleagues into family.
➡️ Be empathetic, but don't be too emotional.
➡️ Care about people, and show it, but don't over-care.

Her reasoning: family at work pushes you to make decisions out of emotion that you wouldn't make otherwise. Over-caring raises expectations that you'll always be available.

I thought she was being cold.

I was wrong.

"We're a family" sounds warm. For a manager, it can make a hard conversation almost impossible: an underperformer, a missed target, a difficult decision.

Warmth isn't the problem. Blurred boundaries are.

Do you think "we're a family" helps or hurts managers? I'd like to hear from people who lead teams.

I thought she was wrong. It took me about 15 years to learn how right she was.

Part2 on Thursday: what happened when I ignored her advice.
