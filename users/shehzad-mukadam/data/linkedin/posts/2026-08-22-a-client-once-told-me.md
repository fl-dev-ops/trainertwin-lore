A client once told me,
“I don’t understand why I still miss someone who broke my trust.”

I asked him, “Do you miss them, or do you miss who you thought they were?”

He went quiet.

Then he said, “I think I miss the person I believed they were.”

That was the real breakthrough.
Sometimes, we’re not holding on to the person.
We’re holding on to the memories, the good moments, and the version of them we created in our minds.

As a coach, I told him:
“You don’t have to hate someone to let them go. You just have to stop confusing who they were with who you hoped they would be.”

Healing isn’t always about forgetting someone.

Sometimes, it’s about accepting that the person you miss was partly a version you created.

And once you accept that, letting go becomes a little easier.

Have you ever realised that you weren’t missing the person, but the version of them you believed in?

If this resonates with you, take a moment to reflect: What are you really holding on to?
