Monday: best training session your team has ever had.
Wednesday: everyone's back to their old habits.

I've watched this happen in company after company, and it's not because the training was bad. Most of the time, the content was solid, the trainer was engaging, and the room was fully bought in by the end of the day.

The problem isn't the training. It's what happens after it.

Here's the sequence most companies skip:
Training room → (nothing) → Real-world application

Here's the sequence that actually changes behaviour:
Training room → Practice → Manager reinforcement → Real-world application → Feedback → Measurement

Notice what's missing in most L&D budgets: everything after the training room.

Companies will spend real money on a two-day workshop, and zero money on:
→ A manager checking in on what was learned → A chance to actually practice the skill before using it live → Feedback on how it went the first time they tried it → Any way to measure if behaviour actually changed

Without those four things, training isn't a waste - it's just entertainment with a certificate.

If you're a leader reading this: the real question isn't 𝗱𝗶𝗱 𝘄𝗲 𝗱𝗲𝗹𝗶𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗶𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴. It's 𝗱𝗶𝗱 𝗮𝗻𝘆𝗼𝗻𝗲'𝘀 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗿 𝗮𝘀𝗸 𝗮𝗯𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝗶𝘁 𝗮 𝘄𝗲𝗲𝗸 𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗿?

Most of the time, the honest answer is no. And that's the actual reason it didn't stick - not the trainer, not the content, not the employees.

What does your organisation do in the two weeks after a training session ends?
