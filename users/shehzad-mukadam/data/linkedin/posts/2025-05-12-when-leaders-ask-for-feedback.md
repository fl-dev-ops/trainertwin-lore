When leaders ask for feedback but punish those who give it, they don’t just lose trust - they damage the entire workplace.

I was speaking with a client over the weekend. He paused for a moment and said something that really stuck with me:

"𝗜’𝘃𝗲 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗱 𝘄𝗮𝘆… 𝘀𝗼𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗶𝗺𝗲𝘀 𝘀𝗶𝗹𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗳𝗲𝗲𝗹𝘀 𝘀𝗮𝗳𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝗻 𝗵𝗼𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘁𝘆."

That hit me.

We talked about how when employees stop speaking up, it’s rarely because everything’s fine - it’s often because they’ve given up. And that’s when the real damage begins.

“𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗳𝗮𝘀𝘁𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝘁𝗼 𝗸𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝗮 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗻𝘆’𝘀 𝗰𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲,” 𝗵𝗲 𝗮𝗱𝗱𝗲𝗱, “𝗶𝘀 𝗯𝘆 𝘀𝗵𝘂𝘁𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗼𝗼𝗿 𝗼𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗲𝗼𝗽𝗹𝗲 𝘄𝗵𝗼 𝗰𝗮𝗿𝗲.”

I couldn’t agree more. I’ve seen it firsthand in my career - managers failing not because they lacked skills, but because they refused to listen. Leaders staying in their bubble… until it bursts.

Just because your team is quiet doesn’t mean they’re on board.
Sometimes, silence is just survival.
