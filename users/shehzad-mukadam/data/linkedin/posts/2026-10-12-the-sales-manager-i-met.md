The sales manager I met at a training had quit. His reason wasn't what I expected.

A few months ago, I trained a sales team. Great feedback. Everyone left happy.

One of the managers had been promoted only two months earlier.

Last week I met him again. He'd left the company.

I asked why. He said the role was overwhelming. As a salesperson, he was fine. As a manager, he saw how unstructured the process was, and he didn't know how to fix it.

When he took the problems to management, he was told: "Come back with solutions, not problems."

Then he told me something I haven't forgotten: "I know how to sell. I was never trained to think like this."

He didn't fail. We promoted the skill he had and asked for a skill nobody had given him.

A new sales manager needs 3 things before the title:
➡️ How to set goals for a team, not just for himself
➡️ How to lead people who sell worse than he does
➡️ How to think in strategy, not only in targets

"Solutions, not problems" is fair advice for someone who was trained to find solutions.

HR leaders and founders: what are you training your next promoted performer to do on day one?
