I spent 10+ years climbing the ladder, only to realize I was leaning it against the wrong wall.

Seven years ago, I didn't just fail as a manager. I failed as a human being.

I had the title. I had the corner office. I had the authority.
And yet, my best talent was walking out the door. 

The feedback during an exit interview hit me like a physical blow:
You don't lead people. You manage spreadsheets. And we aren't numbers.

That night, I didn't sleep. I looked in the mirror and realized I had become the "boss" I used to complain about early in my career. I was obsessed with control, terrified of mistakes, and completely deaf to my team’s needs.

So, I did the only thing that made sense:
I unlearned everything.

I stripped away the ego. I stopped managing and started listening. 

Here is what I learned when I started fresh:

➡️ Trust is a gift, not a mandate: You don't get it because of your title; you earn it by being in the trenches.

➡️ Silence is a leadership tool: The best leaders speak last. They create a space where the quietest voice feels safe to share the loudest idea.

➡️ Accountability starts at the top: When the team fails, it’s my fault. When the team wins, it’s their glory. 

True leadership isn't about being in charge. It’s about taking care of those in your charge.

If you’re feeling stuck today, don’t be afraid to burn your old playbook. Sometimes you have to unlearn the #rules to finally understand the people.
