Last week, I was facilitating a leadership workshop for a corporate team here in the UAE.

Everything looked fine on the surface.

Targets were being hit.
Meetings were happening.
Reports were being submitted on time.

But within 20 minutes of the session, something became obvious.

The room was full of capable people…

…who had slowly stopped speaking up.

Not because they lacked ideas.

Because somewhere along the way, they learned that silence was safer than honesty.

That’s when I asked one question:

When was the last time your team felt safe enough to challenge your thinking?

The room went quiet.

That silence said more than any KPI dashboard ever could.

Here’s what years of training leaders has taught me:

Most performance issues are rarely skill issues.

They are leadership behaviour issues.

Teams don’t disengage because they suddenly become lazy.

They disengage when:

👉 Feedback feels unsafe
👉 Ideas get dismissed
👉 Presence is rewarded more than contribution
👉 Leaders manage tasks but neglect trust

Soft skills are often treated like optional extras.

They’re not.

They are the operating system of high-performing teams.

You can invest in systems, strategy, and technology.

But if your leaders cannot communicate, listen, coach, and build trust…

performance will always hit a ceiling.

The best leaders I’ve worked with in the UAE all have one thing in common:

They make people feel seen, heard, and challenged to grow.

That is not charisma.

That is leadership.

What’s one leadership behaviour you believe organizations need more of today?
