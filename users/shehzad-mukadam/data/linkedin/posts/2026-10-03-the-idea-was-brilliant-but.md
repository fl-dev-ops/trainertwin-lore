The idea was brilliant, but it only took few conversations with the people he looked up to for him to throw it all away... 

A friend once called me excited about an idea.

He couldn't stop talking about it.

He had already imagined the customer, the experience, the business model and where it could go.

Then he made what seemed like a sensible decision.

He told a few people.

➡️ The first person said: I don't think it will work.
➡️ The second asked: Has anyone done this before?
➡️ The third pointed out all the things that could go wrong.

By the fifth conversation, my friend wasn't excited anymore.

He was confused.

The idea hadn't changed.

But his belief in it had.

And this taught me something I still think about.

When an idea strikes you, you are seeing it through a lens that others don't have.

❌ They don't have your experiences.
❌ They don't have your intuition.
❌ They don't have the picture that appeared in your mind.

And sometimes, they simply cannot see what you can see.

I believe that some of those moments are gifts from God.

Not necessarily because every idea will succeed.

But because you were given the ability to see a possibility before it became obvious to everyone else.

That doesn't mean you should ignore advice.

It means you should know the difference between seeking wisdom and seeking permission.

✔️ Get feedback.
✔️ Challenge your assumptions.
✔️ Test the idea.
✔️ Listen carefully.

🚫 But don't hand over ownership of your vision to people who were never able to see it in the first place.

Because sometimes the world doesn't reject your idea because it's bad.

It rejects it because it hasn't seen what you have seen yet.

So the next time an idea keeps coming back to you...

Don't immediately ask:
What will everyone think?

Ask:
Is there something here that I haven't explored yet?

Maybe that idea deserves a chance.

What is an idea you've had that you still believe in, even though others didn't see it?
