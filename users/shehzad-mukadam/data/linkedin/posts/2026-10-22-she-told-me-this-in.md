She told me this in 2008. I only understood it about 15 years later. 

In my career, I forgot her advice.
I cared too much. I was always there, always available, always saying yes.
People didn't see a caring leader. They saw someone they could rely on for anything, at any time. And I was taken advantage of.

That's when I remembered what she told me. She'd been right the whole time.

Caring for people isn't the same as carrying them.

If you're a new manager, here's what I'd tell you:
➡️ Care about the person, not about being liked
➡️ Say no without guilt, and explain why
➡️ Keep one clear line between support and rescue

A lot of managers don't struggle with the technical side of the job. They struggle with how much of themselves to give.

I wish someone had trained me on this before I needed it.

What's one boundary you learned late?
