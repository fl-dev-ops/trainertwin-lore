I trained three salespeople with the exact same script. Only one of them used it.

Same product. Same pitch. Same objection-handling framework.
I watched all three try to apply it in the field.

The first followed it word for word and sounded like a robot reading a script. Deals stalled.

The second threw the script out completely, went full instinct and lost the client halfway through because there was no structure to fall back on.

The third did something different. She used the framework but said it in her own words, at her own pace, in her own style.
She closed the deal.

Same training. Same trainer. Three different outcomes.

That day I stopped believing in "one-size-fits-all" sales training.
Because here's the truth most companies get wrong: they think training is about giving people a script to follow.
It's not.
Training isn't about teaching people what to say.
It's about teaching people how to think - so they can say it their way, under pressure, in real conversations, with real clients.

The problem isn't effort. It's design.

Good sales training isn't a script handed down.
It's a system built around how your team actually thinks, sells, and connects with your specific customers.
