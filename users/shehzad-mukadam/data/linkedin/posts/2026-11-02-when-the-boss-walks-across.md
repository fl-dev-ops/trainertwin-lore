When the boss walks across the floor, everyone suddenly looks busy.
The moment they leave, everyone exhales.

I've noticed this personally in my 18 years of career.

When a manager, director or VP passes through the floor, most of the team pretends to be working. The moment they're gone, people look at each other and smile, as if the ghost has left.

I've seen it in India, in the Gulf and in Europe.

That's not respect. The pretending was out of fear.

Respect sounds like:
👉 People bring you bad news early
👉 They disagree in the meeting, not after it
👉 Work keeps moving when you're away

Fear sounds like:
👉 The floor changes the moment you walk in
👉 You hear about problems late
👉 People go quiet when you arrive

If your team has to perform for you, you're not leading. You're being watched.

Three things to change it:
➡️ Walk the floor to ask, not to check
➡️ Thank someone by name before you ask for anything
➡️ React calmly to the first bad news you hear this week

Have you ever seen the "ghost has gone" moment on your floor?
Send this to a leader who'd want to know.
