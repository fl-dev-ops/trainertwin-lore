A delayed flight, a chauffeur on leave, and a guest landing at dawn. My manager asked me, a fresh graduate, what we should do. #MyStory

I was on the night shift at a hotel in Juhu, Mumbai. Our chauffeur wanted leave, and a guest had an airport pickup booked for the morning.

My manager asked me: What should we do?

I said: if we can make other arrangements, let him go.

Then the flight showed as delayed. Landing was now around 7:30am, and the morning chauffeur's shift started at 7am.

So we made a plan together:
✔️ Ask the morning chauffeur to come in 30 minutes early. The airport was only 20 minutes away.
✔️ If that failed, one of our three security guards would change into uniform and do the pickup.

The morning chauffeur came in early. The backup was never needed.

The guest never knew there had been a problem.

That's the thing about planning for the bad outcome. When it works, nobody sees it.

My manager told me that night: "Every decision has two possible outcomes. Stay positive about the good one. Plan for the bad one."

He could have just decided for me. Instead, he made me think first, then gave me a rule to think with.

Good managers don't hand over answers. They teach people how to decide.

Next time you face a decision, ask:
➡️ What's the best outcome?
➡️ What's the worst realistic outcome?
➡️ What's my backup if it happens?

If you manage people, how often do you ask instead of tell?
