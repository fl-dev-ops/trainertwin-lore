He Read The Room Like A Poker Player And Made $725 Million

In The Change-Up, Mitch finds himself in a position he has no business being in, sitting in a high-stakes merger meeting worth hundreds of millions of dollars, surrounded by experienced lawyers and executives while he has zero background in business.

The opposing side opens with $625 million, and everyone on Mitch’s side is ready to accept it, seeing it as a solid deal and not wanting to risk losing it by pushing further.

But Mitch pauses the room.

He notices something no one else is paying attention to, that the other side isn’t acting like a deal is done, because no one is packing up, no one is shifting in their seat, and no one is preparing to leave.

Instead of focusing on the number, he focuses on their behavior.

And that’s where everything changes.

He points out that if $625 million was truly their final offer, they would already be closing the meeting and heading out, but the fact that they’re still sitting there means there’s more room to move.

So he takes control of the moment and raises the number to $725 million, then tells his team to start packing up and walking away, forcing the other side to react.

They panic.

They accept.

Deal closed.

That’s what makes this scene so sharp.

Because someone with no formal training outperformed a room full of professionals by reading the situation instead of just listening to the words being said.

Everyone else negotiated the number.

He negotiated the behavior behind it.
