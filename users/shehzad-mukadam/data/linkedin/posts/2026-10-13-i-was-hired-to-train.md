I was hired to train a sales team. The most useful conversation happened with their manager.

Training went well. The team was engaged, and the feedback was good.

Then their manager, the company's former top closer, sat down with me. We talked about the team, the training plan and the results. Then he told me he misses being on the floor, because his numbers now depend on people who can't all perform the way he did.

That's when I saw the gap.

The team had a training plan. He didn't.

Everyone on the floor was getting new skills. The one person responsible for making them stick every Monday got none.

I've now seen this pattern in more than one industry. 

Without a manager who knows how to coach, training tends to fade. Then the company concludes "training doesn't work."

It does. But training the team without the manager is a gym membership for everyone except the coach.

Before the next training budget is approved, ask one question: who is reinforcing this after the trainer leaves?

Founders: when did your sales managers last get trained separately from their teams?
