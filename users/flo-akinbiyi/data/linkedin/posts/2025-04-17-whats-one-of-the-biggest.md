What’s one of the biggest mistakes you can make when presenting?

Speaking to the audience with your hands in your pockets.

A few years ago, I spoke to an ambassador, and we talked about the importance of your delivery and body language.

And then, out of the blue, he said something I will never forget:

“You know, Flo, I personally find it rude when someone speaks to me with their hands in their pockets.”

That statement says it all.

It may feel good to you to keep your hands in your pockets - and it can help calm your nerves - but this posture is not a clear signal for the audience.

It’s an open invitation for them to interpret your body language, and the results of their analysis can vary widely:

Casual. Nervous. Insecure. Arrogant. Rude. Disconnected. Unprepared.

Instead of having a strong, confident presence, you create a barrier.

Instead of inviting connection, you signal distance.

Instead of being clear, you become vague.

Winning communicators don’t just focus on content - they focus on their delivery, their posture, presence, and openness.

So the next time you catch yourself in front of an audience with your hands in your pockets…

Simply take them out - and communicate better!
