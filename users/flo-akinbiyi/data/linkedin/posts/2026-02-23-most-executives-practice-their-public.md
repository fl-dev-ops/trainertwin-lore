Most executives practice their public speaking skills at the office.
I practice mine at bedtime.

Every evening, my son asks me to read him a bedtime story. And I’m going to be honest, yes, sometimes I am tired, and it takes a lot of effort to get started. But as soon as I read the first line, it’s game on.

Every single word is there for his enjoyment and, at the same time, for my training. 
Over the last seven years of his life, I have consciously played around with voices, different aspects of voice modulation, and varying levels of intensity to refine my speaking voice.

One of the most wonderful aspects of training your voice with a child is the instant feedback. If my intensity is too much, he’ll tell me to calm down. If I’m too boring, he’ll tell me it’s not exciting enough. There’s no polite audience applause, just honest, real-time direction.

And it shows. If there’s one thing he appreciates in his life, it’s me reading him bedtime stories. And if there’s one thing I appreciate, it’s him giving me the opportunity to practice.

Are you practicing your skills while reading bedtime stories?
