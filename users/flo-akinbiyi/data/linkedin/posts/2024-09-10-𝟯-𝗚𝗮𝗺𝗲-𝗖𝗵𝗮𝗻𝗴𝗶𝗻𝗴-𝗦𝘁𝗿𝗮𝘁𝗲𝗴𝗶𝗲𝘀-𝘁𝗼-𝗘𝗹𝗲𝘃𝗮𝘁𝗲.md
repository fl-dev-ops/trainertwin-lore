What if I told you there’s a better way to prepare than what you are most likely doing right now? 

In many of the #workshops I deliver for corporates, we dive into how participants prepare their #presentations and #speeches. One thing that stands out and is widely known—is that most people spend a lot of time on their PowerPoint slides, but very little time planning and thinking about the words they will actually say. 

This is quite surprising when you’re about to deliver a verbal #presentation and haven’t invested enough time thinking about your #communication. 

Here are three game-changing strategies that I’ve learned over the years, which will have a major impact on your delivery:  

1. Write a script, but don’t memorize it. 🎯
Write, edit, revise, and familiarize yourself with your script, but avoid learning it word-for-word. Before delivering, break it down into #bulletpoints with just a few words per point and add those to your speaker or #presenternotes. It’s crucial not to memorize your entire script unless you can remember it effortlessly. Otherwise, your brain will be preoccupied with recalling content and thinking about what comes next—which negatively impacts your #delivery. The audience can sense this. Also, avoid reading directly from a script. The audience can easily tell if someone is reading to them or truly speaking with them. 

 2. Prepare your presentation with ChatGPT. 🤖
#ChatGPT can become your presentation assistant by asking you insightful questions about your #topic. Feed your script or presentation into ChatGPT and say: “I’m going to practice section by section. Let me know what content I missed, suggest better ways I can phrase things, and ask me tough questions at the end so I’m fully prepared.” This approach helps refine your #message and prepares you for tough audience questions. #AIForPresentations #PublicSpeaking 

 3. Dictate your script. Speak, don’t write.🎙
This tip is an absolute #gamechanger. Instead of writing down your words, speak them. Dictate your #script aloud. This not only helps you rehearse but also ensures that your language is natural and flows as it would in a real presentation. Once you’ve dictated your script, follow the same process as in tip one and break it down into bullet points. This way, you’re not only practicing but also crafting your #presentation in a more organic, engaging manner. #PresentationSkills #Dictation. 

We’re all used to doing things in certain ways that have served us well. But there’s always another step—another opportunity to improve. Challenge yourself during your next #preparation and see if you can take it to the next level. Thank you. 🙏
