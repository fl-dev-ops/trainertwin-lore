Last Friday I ran a sold-out workshop with German Emirati Joint Council for Industry & Commerce (AHK) Dubai  on how to communicate in 2026, at a time where AI tools are everywhere and expectations are rising.

Everyone asks: “Which AI tool should I use?”

But the better question is: “What do I want my audience to feel and take away?”

Here are six observations we surfaced together in the room: 

- AI savviness is not evenly distributed. Some professionals are already deep into tools and workflows. Others are experimenting. Others are just starting. And that’s okay, but the spread is real.

- AI is not replacing communication. It’s raising the standard for it. When everyone has access to the same tools, what differentiates you is clarity, tone, judgment, narrative, and presence. 

- There is no single “perfect tool.” If you rely on one platform to do everything, you’ll end up disappointed.

- The winning setup is multiple tools, human intention, and human review. AI alone won’t make you a better communicator. It will just make you faster at sounding generic. The magic still comes from humans.

- People remember stories, moments, and how you make them feel, not the prompt you used. The future belongs to the hybrid communicator.

- In 2026, communication splits into two camps:
1. Tech-assisted
2. Human-led.

The winners combine both.

Throughout the session we tested prompts, compared outputs, challenged assumptions, and I shared stories and examples from the field on why human communication is becoming a key skill for the future of work.

And the best part: the audience contributed as much insight as the slides.
Thank you to Nadine Arenja-Ziegler and German Emirati Joint Council for Industry & Commerce (AHK) for bringing together such an engaged group. 

Great energy in the room.

Which side are you currently on: tech-assisted, human-first, or both?
