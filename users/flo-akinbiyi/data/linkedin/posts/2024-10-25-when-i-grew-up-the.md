When I grew up, the world was mostly analog. I saw the rise of mobile phones, the arrival of computers, and the emergence of YouTube, which changed everything. Fast forward to today, and there’s an overwhelming amount of information out there. The digital landscape has completely transformed.

Now, we live in a time when AI-generated content and #deepfake technology are challenging what’s real and what isn’t. Imagine a video that looks convincing but never actually happened—it’s fascinating, yet unsettling.

As a presenter, this shift can be scary. When I host events, conferences, and virtual formats, there’s always the risk that someone could take a video of me and manipulate it. Imagine an introduction I give for a minister being edited into something I’d never say—completely out of context or even harmful. That’s the kind of reality we’re facing now.

With these advancements come opportunities—new ways to share knowledge and amplify diverse voices. But with that, there are also risks. How do we ensure that, in this era of deepfakes, information still serves the public interest?

This is why Global Media and Information Literacy Week 2024 matters. It’s a time to explore “The New Digital Frontiers”, focusing on how technologies like generative AI are shaping the way information spreads.

It’s crucial for all of us—whether creating content or just consuming it—to think about how we can stay informed and adapt in this evolving digital world. How do you think we can keep innovation in check while maintaining trust?
