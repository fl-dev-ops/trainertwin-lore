Using notes in a presentation or on stage is not professional. 

I hear this misconception all the time in my communication workshops. And I'm not surprised because most people have never been taught how to use notes properly. If your notes are nothing more than a script that you stare at and read from, then yes, you're hurting your presentation. That's not what great notes look like. 

Great notes are a few keywords, numbers, short bullet points, or a simple outline. They don't replace your presentation. They simply trigger your memory and keep you on track. Last week, I used my most creative notes ever. I taped a few keywords on the back of a bag that I was using as a prop on stage. The audience could not only see the front. Nobody noticed that I had notes at all.

That is the goal. The best notes don't compete with your audience. They quietly support you while your attention stays exactly where it belongs, on the audience in front of you. 

Using notes isn't unprofessional. Using them poorly is. Do you use notes when you're presenting or do you prefer speaking completely without them?
