Yesterday at Milan Malpensa Airport on my way home from the Intercultural Innovation Hub ceremony, I stopped in front of a screen above the security line.

"When you smile, we smile. Kindness is good for everyone. In case of difficulties, do not raise your voice. Kindly ask us what you need and we will be happy to help you."

An airport has every excuse to be harsh. The staff need authority. They need to be direct. There is no time for a long human connection, every interaction has to be quick so that thousands of people get through.

And still, Milan put kindness on the screen. Especially for the moment when somebody has a problem.

I am a firm advocate of positive communication. I believe we as human beings can overcome our differences without getting loud, without disrespect, without shouting at each other.

If it works at an airport, under time pressure, with tired people and lost luggage, it works in a business meeting. It works in a negotiation. It works at home.

There is always a better way to say it than saying it loud.

Where did you last see kindness in a place you didn't expect it?
