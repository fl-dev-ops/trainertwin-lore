Most leaders aim for respect. The best ones aim for something stronger: love.
Last year I had the opportunity to spend some time with the one and only Pep Guardiola on stage, and he left a strong impression on me.

Over the past few weeks and months, I reflected, researched, and studied leadership in times of uncertainty, and Pep came to my mind again and again.

During the interview, I asked him what is his leadership philosophy and he said something truly powerful.

But let me first explain why I compare soccer to these times of uncertainty. Professional athletes and sports teams have to deal with the highest highs when they are winning, but also with the lowest lows when they are losing.

And Pep’s philosophy is one I have seen only a few times, from leaders in the private and public sectors.

“My players have to love me.”

Wow, that’s a statement. But is that really possible?

These were my exact thoughts when Pep said these words. He went on to explain that it’s not really an easy task for him because he is also the one who decides the fate of the players.

He decides whether a player sits on the bench or gets to play, and every player wants to play, not sit on the bench.

Imagine if we found this leadership mindset more in the business world: that teams love their leaders.

In business, love translates into trust, respect, psychological safety, and human connection.

For Pep this love goes beyond the game. 
He said that he wants his players to come to his house when he is retired and that they can remember the journey they had together.

Getting there is not easy.
A good start is to reflect on how large the portion of our lifetime spent at work is.
Shouldn’t we make this time count on a human level as well?

Would companies be more successful?

The leaders I know who are loved by their teams are the proof because they are really successful.

Coincidence? Absolutely not!

So the question is: does your team love you?
And if not… why not? 

We are human after all!
