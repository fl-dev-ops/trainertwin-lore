Three minutes. That was all a truly memorable communication needed. 

On yesterday's flight with Lufthansa from Athens to Munich, I spotted a note. 

A note on a napkin stuck on the oven. I noticed it from my seat and when I read it, I was blown away. 

“Hello, dear crew, your meals are already in the oven and the bread too. Have a great flight, Love, Anne.”  

Completed with a hand-drawn sun.

I'm not sure I've ever seen such a kind gesture in communication from a colleague in the aviation industry. 

I don't know the process of stocking the plane with food, but if Anne had already put the food in the oven, she went the extra mile. And then she took a moment to write a note on a napkin, stick it to the oven, and wish them a good flight with a little drawing that just warms your heart. 

It was a small plane. Filling the food order, putting the food in, is maybe two additional minutes, writing on the napkin another minute. But these three minutes that she invested truly made the crew feel appreciated and make you realize what great people you're working with. 

Going the extra mile is a wonderful communication technique. 

And when it is applied, it can create powerful moments and emotions that the receiver might remember for a lifetime. 

Dear Lufthansa, I really hope that you will find out who Anne is and give her a massive high five for being such an incredible colleague and employee.
