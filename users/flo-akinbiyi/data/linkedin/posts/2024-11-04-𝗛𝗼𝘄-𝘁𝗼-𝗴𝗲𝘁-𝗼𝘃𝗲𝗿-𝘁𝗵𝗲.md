𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝘁𝗼 𝗴𝗲𝘁 𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗮𝗹 #𝗻𝗲𝗿𝘃𝗼𝘂𝘀𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝘄𝗵𝗲𝗻 𝗱𝗲𝗹𝗶𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮 #𝗽𝗿𝗲𝘀𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻

For so many #professionals, the first moments are the most nerve-wracking. Once you get going, you’re fine, but those first few seconds can feel like the toughest hurdle. It doesn’t have to be that way. 

Knowing these initial moments are challenging means we can take steps to ease that discomfort. 

This picture definitely shows a moment where nerves got the best of me. It was just the beginning, though—I ended up receiving great feedback from that event, even though it was years ago. One of those moments!

𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗳𝗶𝘃𝗲 #𝘁𝗶𝗽𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝘀𝗺𝗼𝗼𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗿:

	1.	✅ 𝗣𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗲 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗣𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗢𝗽𝗲𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴
Know the first few sentences inside out, so no matter what happens, you won’t forget them. This lets you focus less on the content and more on connecting with your #audience.

	2.	✅ 𝗖𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝗮𝗻 𝗘𝘅𝗰𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗿𝘁
Think of something that energizes you—an #audience interaction, a compelling visual, or a powerful sentence. When you look forward to delivering the opening, it sets a positive tone.

	3.	✅ 𝗢𝗯𝘀𝗲𝗿𝘃𝗲 𝗬𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗕𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴
Nerves often make your breathing shallow. Practice #breathing techniques before your presentation, like six-four breathing or box breathing. As you start, focus on #belly breathing to stay calm. Diaphragmatic breathing helps you stay composed in those crucial moments.

	4.	✅ 𝗧𝘂𝗿𝗻 𝗦𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗻𝗴𝗲𝗿𝘀 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝗙𝗿𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗱𝗹𝘆 𝗙𝗮𝗰𝗲𝘀
Take time before your presentation to engage with a few audience members, turning strangers into friendly faces. Create small, genuine connections by introducing yourself, asking questions, or sharing a lighthearted moment. These interactions build rapport, ease your nerves, and make you feel like you’re presenting to people you already know. By stepping out of the presenter zone and into a conversational mindset, you’ll feel more relaxed and confident when it’s time to start.

	5.	✅ 𝗘𝗻𝗴𝗮𝗴𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗔𝘂𝗱𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗘𝗮𝗿𝗹𝘆
Start with an icebreaker, a question, or even a quick show of hands. Early engagement brings the audience into your world, making the setting feel less intimidating and turning the presentation into a conversation rather than a performance. This connection helps shift focus away from nerves and toward a shared experience.

These are just a few of the many #tools and strategies that can help you manage initial nervousness.
