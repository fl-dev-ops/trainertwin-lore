𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗱𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘀𝗲 𝟰 𝗽𝗶𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲𝘀 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝗶𝗻 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗺𝗼𝗻?

3 letters: "𝗬𝗘𝗦."

1996: I said yes to someone taking a photo of me.  

It was my first real encounter with a professional camera and I was fascinated. 
Not just because, okay yes, I thought I looked pretty good back then.  
But because I saw what a real camera can pull out of someone when the moment is captured right. 

2006: I said yes again, this time to hosting my first ever event.  

A surf competition at the Alpamare in Bad Tölz. 
My own event. My first time behind the mic.  
And I still remember the feeling: the energy I put into the microphone and the energy that came back from the audience.  
I was nervous, but I raised my hand anyway. I said yes.

2016: I had just quit my job.  

I was freelancing, trying to find my footing, chasing speaking opportunities, doing event work, figuring it out day by day.  
Then a friend asked if I could do a photoshoot for a publication he was contributing to.

And here’s the funny part: the photoshoot was the morning after a big celebration.  
Not my best “fresh and rested” moment.  
But I said yes anyway.

The outcome? Spectacular.  
And that was a turning point for me. I realized: maybe it’s time to stop hiding and start building my personal brand while I build my career. 

And now, 2026.  
I’m still saying yes, just on bigger stages.

I’ve embraced my superpowers. I get to speak globally, work with multinationals and government entities, and empower teams and leaders. And honestly? I’m grateful. Deeply grateful.

But let me tell you something these photos don’t show:

They don’t show the imposter syndrome.  
For many years I genuinely believed I wasn’t good enough.

They don’t show the blood, sweat and tears.  
The financial stress.  
The stage fright.  
The nights that turned into mornings.  
COVID moments that shook everything.  
The days where the “no” would have been easier.

And still, I kept saying yes.  
Even when the negative voice was nagging in the background.

Because that’s what I’ve learned:  
Magic happens when you say yes.  
Every yes made me grow.  
Every opportunity changed me for the better.

And maybe that’s the real lesson behind these pictures:

Anyone can look great with the right camera, the right lighting, and the right photographer.  

Anyone can become a great speaker with time, effort, and the right guidance.  

And any audience can be a great audience when you show up for them and do the work.

I wonder what the next photo series will look like a couple of decades from now.

When was the last time you said YES... when you really wanted to say NO?
