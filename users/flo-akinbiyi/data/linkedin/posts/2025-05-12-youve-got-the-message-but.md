You’ve got the message. But can they hear you?

You can have the best story, the strongest data, or the most inspiring message… But if people can’t hear you clearly, none of it will land.

One of the most common and overlooked presentation mistakes? Holding the mic incorrectly.

While hosting events, I see this all the time:
Some hold it way too low - the mic’s near their chest.
Others wave it around like it’s part of their body language.
And a few go full Snoop Dogg mode (you know the grip I mean).

It’s awkward to watch. 

And honestly, I hate having to interrupt a great flow to say: “Please hold the mic closer to your mouth.”

So let’s fix this once and for all.

✅ This is how you do it: Hold the mic at the same angle, keep a steady distance - about 5 to 10 cm from your mouth. That’s it. That’s the magic.

Want to practice?
✖️ Don’t use the mic as a wand. 
✖️ Don’t drop it down by your belly. 
✖️ Don’t switch hands mid-sentence.

✅ Instead: Grab a knife, a stick, a candle - anything mic-shaped.  
Stand in front of a mirror, practice your lines, and lock in the muscle memory.

It’s a tiny adjustment that makes a huge difference. And it makes you instantly more confident, more credible, and more professional.
