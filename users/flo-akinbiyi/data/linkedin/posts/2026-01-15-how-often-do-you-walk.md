How often do you walk into a café or restaurant and you’re not greeted nicely?

For me, that’s such a turn-off. I’m coming to your place, I want to have a good time, I’m happy to spend money and in return, the first thing I get is grumpiness and negativity from the team.

A few months ago, I was at Café Buur in Munich with my brother. And when I walked in, a gentleman came straight to me and said:

“Hi, it’s wonderful that you’re here. Welcome!”

That’s how our experience started.

Warm. Sincere. Memorable.

Here’s the sharp insight:
Being “nice” is not just politeness, it’s a micro-moment of human connection delivered intentionally to make people feel welcome.

And those micro-moments matter more than most businesses realize.

Combined with great food and a good experience, this one interaction turned Café Buur into one of my favorite places in Munich. Because I now know I’ll be welcomed nicely, treated nicely, and have a great experience when I go back.

If you own a restaurant, a café, or any business with a customer-facing function, train your team not just to be nice but to host.

And here’s where it gets practical:

Empathic language, positive delivery, the right body language, and the right tone of voice can change an entire interaction in seconds. People don’t just see it, they feel it. That’s hospitality. That’s human connection. And that’s what drives loyalty.

The truth is, it costs your team nothing to smile, to greet, to use warm language, and to show genuine interest, but the return on that tiny investment is massive.

That’s how people remember you.
That’s how people talk about you.
That’s how people come back.

And this is exactly what I coach organizations and leaders on: how to use human-first communication so interactions become warmer, experiences become stronger, and businesses become more impactful.

Because products can be copied. Menus can be copied. Pricing can be copied.
But the way you make people feel is and always will be special!
