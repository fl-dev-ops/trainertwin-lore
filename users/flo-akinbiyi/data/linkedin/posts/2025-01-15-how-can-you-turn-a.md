How can you turn a NO into a YES?

Last weekend at the Mastermind by Cope, I had the privilege of hosting a fireside chat and learning from world-class negotiation expert, author, speaker, and lecturer Jack Nasher.

Not only has Jack written bestselling books on negotiation and influence, but he has also lectured at some of the world’s leading institutions, including Oxford University, Stanford University, and the Munich Business School. His expertise is recognized globally, and his insights are as engaging as they are transformative.

Oh, and by the way, Jack isn’t just a negotiation superstar - he’s also a professional magician who performs regularly at the iconic Magic Castle in Hollywood. Talk about a man of many talents!

Here are the five negotiation techniques from Jack that really stuck with me:

✅ Have alternatives to your preferred option
If you have alternatives, you can approach a negotiation with a completely different level of confidence. When you only have one option, your leverage decreases, and so does your ability to negotiate effectively.

✅ Be ready to walk away
You must be willing to walk away from a negotiation. Only then will you have the strength to convince the other side and hold your ground.

✅ Don’t be the final decision-maker
Whether or not you are the final decision-maker, involve someone else in the process - your partner, CEO, manager, or even the bank. By doing this, you position yourself as a mediator rather than the person confronting the other side directly.

✅ See negotiations as a game
Negotiations are a dance, a game. Sometimes you win, and sometimes you lose, but the key is to enjoy playing. This mindset will help you approach negotiations with less pressure and more confidence.

✅ Remember you always have power
No matter what someone says, they need you just as much as you need them. Whether their organization is big or small, there is mutual dependency. Recognizing your own power in a negotiation can change how you approach it.

Jack inspired me to delve deeper into the art of negotiation because it’s something we engage in every day whether in business or personal life.

So, to all my clients and suppliers, get ready for some tough (but fun!) negotiations in the months to come!
