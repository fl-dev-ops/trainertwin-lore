October 10th is World Mental Health Day, and as a coach, I reflect on how important our communication and actions in the workplace are when it comes to mental health.

We live in a world with rapid changes, uncertainty, continuous business growth, technology shifts, emails, messages, and many other challenges.

For leaders as well as employees, it is overwhelming, and it's always easier to say, it's just business, it's just work, and allow yourself to be tougher, and less human.

What would happen if we shifted that thinking and we look at the greatest business leaders that we can find?

I have seen a number of incredible leaders who communicate with kindness.
And yes, they can also be firm if needed. 

I've seen leaders who know the name of every single employee, and they have hundreds of them.

I know leaders that once a week will go around the entire organization, shake everyone's hand, interact with everyone.

I've seen leaders who sit in the canteen next to somebody from every part of the business, including those whose work is often overlooked, and take the time to have regular conversations.

There are great leaders out there who do an exceptional job.

Why don't we make this more the norm, whether it's through communication or through actions?

Great leadership can support better mental health in the workplace, and wouldn't this be a beautiful step in the right direction to make this world a better place?

This is why I post this slightly older photo of Matthias Kasprowicz, who is one of those leaders that I described.

Great leadership cannot solve every mental health challenge. 

But the way we lead, communicate, and treat people can make a meaningful difference.
