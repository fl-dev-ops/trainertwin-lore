This year I picked up climbing.

And I truly enjoy it, not just for the physical aspects, but also for the mental ones.

Almost every city has an indoor climbing gym, which makes it easy to get a workout while traveling or to fit it into your day. What I love about this sport is that it constantly challenges me physically. To be honest, quite often I'm simply not strong enough yet to hold on to the wall or to pull myself up on these tiny little holds. That's how much of a newbie I still am.

But then there's also the mental side. When you're on the wall, there's just no time to think about emails, pressure, work, geopolitics, social media, meetings, or any other problems. Whether you like it or not, you have to be fully present.
Your body is working and your brain is solving the situation that you have put yourself into. And this makes your focus become incredibly sharp.

What is wonderful is that there is also a connection to my work as a communication coach and presenter, because climbing forces you to become mentally stronger and to work on your fear. For me, it's a fear of height, falling, letting go, the fear of trusting the rope, the harness, the auto-belay machine, or the partner that might be securing me. And every time you work through fear, you become a little braver, not just as a climber, but also as a human and as a speaker. Great communication does require trust, presence, commitment, and the courage to let go and fully step into the moment. 

I'm grateful for my brother Leonhard  " Leo " Akinbiyi for introducing me to this incredible sport.

And it's also perfect timing, because this weekend and next week, I'll be surrounded by the outdoor world at the European Outdoor Week in Riva del Garda. Can't wait.
