Today I sat with my son in school, and it gave me an incredible insight into human communication.

We had a parental engagement session where the parents sat next to their child and observed one entire class. 

I definitely have to say that the teacher did an excellent job, and I'm impressed with her teaching skills. Keeping the class engaged, entertained, making learning fun and at the same time also staying in control.

I have to be honest, even year three math at some point was almost overwhelming for me, but I got through the session well. 

I had an insight into communication.

A few children, including my own son, had the chance to answer a question, stand up and present their answer to the class, not at the front of the class, but standing. 

What I noticed was the body language of every child.

Every child displayed a different body language, and I also noticed that some of these postures they were showing were not necessarily comfortable postures: hands behind the back, hands together in the front, were two notable styles. 

And why is this such a relevant insight?

Do these kids have the right to feel uncomfortable?

Absolutely.

And the teacher is naturally focused on teaching them math or any other academic subject right now, not to focus on communication. 

So what happens next?

As a child, you're now used to speaking in a certain way, for example, with your hands behind your back when you are in front of an audience, in this case, your class.

And if you do that for many years, of course that has an impact on how you will speak in your professional life. 

It's quite incredible if you think that, although schools nowadays do teach public speaking and presentation skills, it is only one element of the curriculum, and communication is not a key skill that is taught as part of the school journey. 

So here I sit and wonder: what would happen if the school system and all of us placed a much larger focus on great communication?

How would we then communicate and interact with each other?
