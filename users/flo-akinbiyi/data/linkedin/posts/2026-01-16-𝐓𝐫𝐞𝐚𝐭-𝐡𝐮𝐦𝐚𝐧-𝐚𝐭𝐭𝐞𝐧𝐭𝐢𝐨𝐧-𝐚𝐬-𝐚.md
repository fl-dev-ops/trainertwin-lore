"𝐓𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐭 𝐡𝐮𝐦𝐚𝐧 𝐚𝐭𝐭𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐚𝐬 𝐚 𝐥𝐮𝐱𝐮𝐫𝐲."

These words came from Professor Gianluca Saviotti of SDA Bocconi during his keynote at yesterday’s Human 2 Human Summit, a full-day gathering I had the privilege to host, alongside an incredible coalition of organizers from DMT, Dubai World Trade Centre, Informa and many others pushing the exhibition ecosystem forward.

Professor Saviotti spoke about the evolving role of AI and how it can be integrated into in-person events and exhibitions. But this line hit me the most.

We often assume that because we’re speaking, the audience is listening.

Reality check: they’re not.

Their attention is being negotiated against notifications, tasks, deadlines, internal dialogues, and the entire digital universe in their pocket.

Which means if you choose to stand in front of an audience, whether it’s 10 people or 10,000, you don’t get attention by default. You earn it.

You earn it with relevance.
You earn it with clarity.
You earn it with value.
And you honor it by remembering that attention is not owed to you, it’s given to you.

And it’s something I felt firsthand during my own keynote. I was able to capture attention by designing for it: building in the 𝐖𝐎𝐖 factor, creating interaction moments, and giving people a reason to stay present rather than simply asking them to.

Because that’s what the Human 2 Human Summit was really about: not just delivering content, but creating more memorable moments with the audience, moments that cut through distraction and feel worth showing up for.

In a world full of noise, this message felt like the right reminder:

It’s not about the speaker.
It’s about the audience.

A powerful moment of reflection for me and hopefully a bit of inspiration for some of you too, especially as we turn our attention toward the weekend.
