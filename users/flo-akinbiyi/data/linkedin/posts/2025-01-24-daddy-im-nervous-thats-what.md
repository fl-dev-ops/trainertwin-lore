“Daddy, I’m nervous.” 

That’s what my six-year-old son told me as he prepared for his theater play. And you know what? That’s perfectly okay.

We’ve all been there. Nervousness before stepping in front of an audience, whether you’re a child on stage for the first time or a CEO addressing thousands, is normal.

I shared stories with him: 
•	About CEOs giving their first big speech and feeling their hands shake. 
•	About students so nervous before a performance they couldn’t sleep the night before. 
•	About myself, yes, even me, getting nervous before stepping on stage with someone like Michelle Obama. (How could I not?)

But here’s the truth I shared with him: The key to overcoming stage fright is great preparation.

When you: 
•	know your content inside and out, 
•	have crystal-clear clarity on your message, and 
•	have a plan to connect with your audience, 
…nervousness becomes manageable.

But if your content feels shaky or incomplete, that anxiety grows. Why? Because uncertainty fuels fear.

So, the first step is to prepare so well that, if someone wakes you at 3 a.m., you could deliver your talk, or, in my son’s case, his lines - flawlessly.

As the ancient Greek saying goes: “Sweat more in training, bleed less in war.”

I’d love to share more tools to help you overcome nervousness and stage fright in the coming weeks. Let me know if that’s something you’re interested in!
