𝗬𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗱𝗮𝘆, 𝗜 𝗷𝘂𝗺𝗽𝗲𝗱 𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝗼𝗳 𝗮 𝗱𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗨𝗯𝗲𝗿.

To be clear, we were rolling at 3km/h towards a red traffic light, but I had to get out.
I couldn’t stay a second longer in that car.

It’s rush hour. I’m on my phone working.

Suddenly, the Uber driver starts a conversation.
He tells me where he’s from, shares his challenges in life and business, goes on to his family and financial challenges.

I start to genuinely feel sorry for the gentleman and wonder what kind of a tip I should give him.

Then as we roll towards the red light, he reaches to the dashboard, grabs a folder, opens it, and shows me vivid and graphic medical images from a surgery he recently had.

And that’s the moment where I say “no, just no” and I jump out of the car.
Because emotional blackmail is where I draw the line.

This kind of begging has happened to me at least 4 times over the last 6 years. 
And from the second time I realized there is a pattern.
They follow a structure.

1. Initial innocent conversation
2. ⁠Sharing hardships and challenges
3. Shock moment (the wow factor)
4. Call to action

If it lands, they win big.
If it doesn’t, they lose.
And when you beg loosing is  anyways a reality you have to deal with.

Once I stripped away the sadness and disgust of the situation, I became fascinated by the wow factor.

Because if you translate that pattern into business communication, the wow factor is what the audience will remember.

It’s the moment that shifts the energy, attention and how the audience feels.
It’s the moment that stays with them.

It’s not just the words or the slides.
It can be a prop, an activity, an interaction, a surprise.

Over the years this insight has led me to create incredible moments.

• It inspired me to throw money around DXB Today’s studio (Watch the interview for the full story).
• It inspired me to use magic tricks on stage.
• It inspired me to interact more impactful with audiences and move them from listening to feeling.

Because the wow factor creates impact!

It goes without saying, but we should never use emotional blackmail with any audience, that’s not what we do.
