Great leaders know how to activate one of humanity’s superpowers: 
the ability to imagine a future that does not exist yet.

But most leaders do exactly the opposite.
They focus on the risks, the challenges, and the pressure.
Some focus on what feels realistic.
And only a few talk about the best-case scenario.

A few weeks ago, my psychologist introduced me to the three-case framework, which is pretty simple.

Instead of just imagining one outcome, imagine all three:
The best, the realistic, and the worst scenario.

When I reflected on this framework, I realized how powerful this approach is for leadership communication.
The way leaders communicate the future shapes people’s reality.

Here in the UAE, leaders communicate what is possible in the future rather than going over the challenges.

If your communication is dominated by fear, pressure, and risk, teams start operating from fear, pressure, and risk.

But when leaders also communicate possibility, vision, and opportunity, the mindset shifts.
You allow people to imagine what could actually be possible.

With advances in AI, some leaders often share their concerns with their teams:
“If we don’t move now, we will fall behind.”

But what if they communicated:
“Imagine what we can create together?”
“How can we transform our business?”

In times of massive change, human-first communication becomes even more important.

I’m curious: which scenario do you lean toward most?
