A dashboard full of green can hide a leader who's completely underwater.

Early in my career I ran a project to rewrite an order management system that was drowning in tech debt. Budgets were tight, every project needed the finance director's sign-off, and I was new, working with engineers I'd never worked with before.

About 2 months into a 6-month build, I could see it. Progress wasn't close to the estimates. It was going to cost far more and take far longer than we'd promised.

So I did what a lot of capable people do. I quietly held it together. I cut scope, found ways to keep the big wins cheaply, and kept the thing looking on track. And it worked, right up until there was nothing left to cut except the actual value of the project.

I was going to have to tell the finance director. He'd given me no safety to do that, and I dreaded it. In the end I got bailed out by luck - the company was acquired and the whole product team was made redundant before I ever had the conversation.

I never let it happen again. From then on I was honest about delivery velocity from week 1, not week 20.
If a project only looks on track because you're quietly holding it up, it isn't on track. It's on you.

What's one thing right now that only works because you're personally holding it together?
