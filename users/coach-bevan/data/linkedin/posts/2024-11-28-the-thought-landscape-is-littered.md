The thought landscape is littered with examples of successful companies. Innovating to beat competitors using a Product-led strategy. According to research from McKinsey, investors should look to companies that have mastered being Product-led, as they observed 2x growth.

So why do I see more and more companies reverting to Project-led strategies right now?

Making money is harder. Consumers are spending less. Times are tough. These tough times elicit a natural human reaction to act more defensively. Batten down the hatches. Park the bus. Do whatever is needed to survive! 

Project-led feels predictable. Safe. Seeing a nice glossy spec document. Understanding what you are going to get in a few months time. That beautiful warm snuggly feeling. That semblance of control. 

The problem is… The project is never on time. The scope is reduced. When launched, customers neither want nor need it. Seems no-one was really in control after all, but at least it felt nice for a while.

Agile was meant to fix all this right? Well, Agility is great as you know. I love it. It allows for quick manoeuvring to ensure product market fit. But this is useless if you don’t have real Product Managers that really know actual customers. You can pivot because of Agile but you will simply pivot from one misinformed Product Strategy to another. Who knows. Maybe you’ll get lucky. There is a reason investors target Product-led businesses. This is because they don’t guess. They aren’t lucky. They test.

What do your Product Managers spend the majority of their time doing? Are they really Project Managers? Or Delivery Leads? Maybe they are BAs? Are they stuck in the office presenting on delivery timelines? Maybe they are  managing backlogs. Or grooming requirements? Eugghhh, gross.

Product-led companies are bold. They are brave. They invest in good people and give them time. Their Product Managers engage full time in Product discovery to gain valuable insights. These insights are revolutionary. They share them with talented Engineers. They lead to the solving of real problems for customers, often in novel ways. The kind of solutions that offer serious value. The kind customers love. The kind I love. The kind customers pay to have solved. The kind customers will pay a lot of money to have solved.

If you want to win in these tough times. Reject your natural instinct to curl into a ball and hide. Don’t call it pragmatism. It is fear. Be bold. Be brave. Being brave is doing things even though you are scared. Trust good people to solve real problems. Be Product-led.
