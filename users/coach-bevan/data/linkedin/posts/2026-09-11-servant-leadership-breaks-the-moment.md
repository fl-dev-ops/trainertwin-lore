Servant leadership breaks the moment the people above you expect to be served but won't serve you.

For a long time I led the way I believed in. Coaching my team, giving them real autonomy, backing them when it mattered, trying to be the kind of manager I'd have wanted. Serving the people below me, in the proper sense of the word.

What I assumed was that the same thing flowed downward from above. It didn't. 

In one large, highly political organisation, I slowly realised how much of my time was going upward instead - building decks to make managers look informed, endless status updates, helping people win small political battles, producing information nobody actually needed. Work that did nothing for the product, the team, or the customer. It existed to manage egos and appearances.

So I was serving in both directions at once. Protecting and empowering my team below, while feeding politics and pressure to the hierarchy above. And that arrangement only runs one way for so long before it runs you into the ground. Eventually it broke me.

Here's what I took from it. Servant leadership isn't sustainable when it only flows downhill. You can't keep pouring trust and protection into your team while absorbing control and politics from above, and expect to have anything left. Something has to give, and too often it's the leader in the middle.

If you're that person right now - genuinely serving your team while being quietly milked from above - it's worth naming honestly, because it's not a personal failing. It's a structure that can't hold.

Have you ever been squeezed from both directions like that? What did you do about it?
