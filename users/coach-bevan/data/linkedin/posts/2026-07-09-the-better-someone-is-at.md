The better someone is at their job, the more they fear secretly being found out. 

The most capable leaders I coach share the same quiet secret. They think everyone else has it figured out, and they're the only one improvising.

They sit in meetings sounding calm and go home replaying every sentence. They lead teams who clearly respect them while privately wondering when they'll be found out. 
They deliver, again and again, and still that voice asks whether they're actually good enough for this.

Here is what I've learned watching it up close. The voice has almost nothing to do with ability. The people who hear it loudest are often the most capable in the room. It gets louder with responsibility, not with weakness.
The voice is lying. Most people just can't hear that yet, because it's using their own voice to speak.

If you're leading something right now and quietly carrying that doubt, you are not the exception in the room. You're most of the room.
