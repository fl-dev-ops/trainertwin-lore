He was paid above market rate and convinced he'd fooled everyone into it.

One of the most gifted engineers I ever managed didn't believe he was any good. He was out-performing the people around him and privately sure he'd tricked his way in. He had things he wanted to do with his life and had talked himself out of all of them, because the story in his head said someone like him would never earn his way back if he stepped off the track.

None of it was true. But he couldn't see that, because the belief was running underneath everything, quietly, where he couldn't argue with it.
We worked on it. Not pep talks. We went after the actual belief – where it came from, whether it held up, what became possible without it.

It held up nowhere. And once he could see that, everything changed. He made a bold decision he'd been too scared to make for years, came back stronger, and started choosing his work by what he valued rather than what he was afraid of losing.

Talent was never his problem. Believing he had it was.
I see this in senior people constantly, not just junior ones. The higher you climb, the better you get at hiding it.
