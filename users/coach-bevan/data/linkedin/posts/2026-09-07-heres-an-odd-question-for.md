Here's an odd question for you as a leader. 
What should you know less about each year?

Technical leaders usually get promoted for knowing things - every detail of the product, where the problems hide, how to make the thing succeed. It works brilliantly. 

You know one project inside out. Then three, and you still know everything. Then you're running ten, or fifteen, and knowing every detail becomes physically impossible.

This is exactly where a lot of brilliant technical people stall as leaders. The thing that made them successful - knowing everything - is the thing they now have to give up, and letting go feels like losing the very skill that got them here.

But here's what I eventually understood. The detail doesn't disappear when you become a leader. It moves. It goes from the work to the people.

I noticed it in myself. I slowly stopped caring about every detail of the product and the architecture, and got genuinely fascinated by my team instead. 

What motivated each of them? What they wanted to learn? Which work they'd actually enjoy? That human detail became the thing I wanted to know everything about.

You can't hold fifteen projects in full detail and know your people properly at the same time. One has to give. The leaders who grow are the ones who let the technical detail go and pour that same attention into the humans doing the work.

What are you still trying to know in full that your team should own by now?
