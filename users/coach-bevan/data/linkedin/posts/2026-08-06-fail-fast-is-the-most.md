"Fail fast" is the most agreed with idea in tech that almost nobody actually does.

We all nod at it. Make the call early, ship the thing before it's polished, learn from what breaks. It's how the best software gets built, and everyone knows it.
Then the moment arrives and we don't do it. We polish for another sprint, wait for more certainty and quietly delay the test that might tell us we're wrong.
And it’s not because we've stopped believing in it. Because of what happens if it fails in front of the stakeholders - the questions, the raised eyebrows, the sense that this was your call and it didn't work.

That's not a confidence problem. But a self-trust problem, and they're not the same thing.
Confidence is a feeling - walking in feeling sure. People chase it, wait for it, try to fake it. And it never quite arrives, because feelings are weather.
Self-trust is different. It isn't the feeling of being sure. It's knowing that if this goes badly, you'll handle it. That you can make a call, be wrong, and not fall apart.

Confidence waits for certainty. Self-trust acts without it. One keeps you polishing for another sprint. The other lets you ship and find out.
Stop trying to feel confident. Start building the evidence that you can trust yourself with a bad outcome. That's the thing you can actually construct.
How does that distinction land for you?
