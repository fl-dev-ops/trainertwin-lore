Software problems usually give you an answer.

The code works. Or it doesn’t.
There’s a bug. It crashes. You fix it and move on.

People are rarely that tidy.

A difficult conversation can go well and still leave you wondering whether you said the right thing. Someone can agree with a decision and remain unhappy about it.

You can support a person properly and still have no idea what they’ll do next. I think this is especially difficult for people who built their careers around technical work.

Software can feel a bit like maths.
You get the answer right or you get it wrong.

Human problems feel more like an essay exam.
You write a good answer. It gets 80%. You get an A*. And you’re left wondering what the missing 20% was.

There may be no clear answer. No obvious action plan. No moment where the problem announces that it’s finished.

That uncertainty is one of the biggest things managers take home.

They think about it in the shower. In the swimming pool. While having dinner with their wife.
While they’re meant to be playing with their children.

Their brain keeps working because the situation still feels unresolved. And honestly, that makes sense.

People change. New information appears.
Yesterday’s good decision can create tomorrow’s difficult conversation.

Leadership involves spending a lot of time in that grey space.
You may never get the clean ending your technical brain is waiting for.
