I spent most of my corporate career pretending to work at a startup.

It's called a skunk works. A weird, disruptive bit of the business that's meant to work like a startup. A small team, one product to deliver, and a lot of freedom in how you do it. Our fake startup, basically.

I'd worked in actual startups early on, so I sort of knew what we were pretending to be.

The trouble with a fake startup is what happens when it works. Ours did. And the reward was getting dragged into the rest of the business to help everyone else change how they worked... which is a very different job, and a story for another day.

When I started coaching, I tried to categorise the people I really enjoy working with. It kept coming back to the same answer, which in hindsight was a bit obvious. Founders. Small teams, up to about 20 people, building something new.

Which is probably why founders feel like my people. I've worked in the real thing, and in the pretend version.
