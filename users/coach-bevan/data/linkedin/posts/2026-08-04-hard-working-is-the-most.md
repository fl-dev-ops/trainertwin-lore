"Hard working" is the most overrated quality in a leader.
We treat it like the ultimate compliment. Puts in the hours. First in, last out. Grinds.

I tried that at the start of my career. Copied the hard work culture, filled every hour, and had no space left to make a single great decision. So I changed tactics.
I started leading more like a shepherd. Watching, mostly. Rarely intervening. Gently steering, and saving the decisive moves for the moments that genuinely needed one. It looks like doing less from the outside. It's actually doing the few things that matter, at the right time, with a clear head.

Here's what I see now, after 20 years. The hardest-working person in the room is very often the one who trusts their team least, can't prioritise, and mistakes motion for progress. They're not admirable. They're stuck, and exhausting themselves staying stuck.

The shepherds are the ones who actually move things forward. They've worked out what matters, handed the rest away, and protected their energy for the few calls that count.
Effort isn't the achievement. It's what you fall back on when you haven't figured out the real problem yet.
I suspect this one divides people. Where do you sit?
