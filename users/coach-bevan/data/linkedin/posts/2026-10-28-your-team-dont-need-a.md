Your team don't need a nanny.

That was one of my slides at Product Tank in Manchester. Most of the talk was about going gonzo, and there's a slide of Hunter S. Thompson that might or might not be how I hope to look in about 15 years. This bit was more about the powerful stuff that happens when you're not around mothering your team.

At one point in my career, it was me and 10 software engineers, and I was delivery, product and line management. Brevity was my goal. And when I first got the chance to manage, I didn't understand any management methodologies other than servant leadership and coaching. So I did those.

I think founders can slip into a version of the nanny thing. Writing everyone's tasks and keeping all the admin, because it's quicker to do it yourself. You tell yourself you're making the team more efficient. But really it's about trust.

It is quicker. It also means the team never has a reason to make any of it better.

What are you still doing yourself because it's quicker than handing it over?
