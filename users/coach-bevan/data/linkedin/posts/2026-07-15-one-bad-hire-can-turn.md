One bad hire can turn a good leader into someone who trusts no one.

"I can't delegate. My team just isn't ready." I hear this constantly from leaders drowning in work they shouldn't be touching. On the surface it sounds like the team. It usually isn't.

When you dig in, the leader who can't let go is rarely doubting the work. They're doubting their own read on people. Can I actually tell who's capable? Did I promote the right person? If I ask the team whether they've got it and they say yes, can I trust the answer?

Often there's a story underneath. They backed someone once who let them down. Hired someone who turned out to be coasting. And the lesson they quietly took from it wasn't "that hire was wrong." It was "my judgement can't be trusted." So now they check everything, quietly redo things, and become the bottleneck in their own team.

The team feels it too. People who aren't trusted stop taking initiative. They wait to be told. And then they look exactly as unready as you feared. The loop tightens.
The work rarely starts with the team. It starts with rebuilding the leader's trust in their own read, on people, on risk, on who can carry what. Once that's solid, letting go stops feeling like handing over a grenade. And the team, given room, tends to rise into it.

If you're carrying too much right now, the question isn't "why isn't my team ready." It's "what would I need to trust to let this go?"
