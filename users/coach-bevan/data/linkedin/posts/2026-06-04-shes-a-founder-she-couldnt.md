She's a founder.

She couldn't say it out loud - "founder"

In a session this week, a client was handed the mic to introduce herself. She froze. Couldn't describe what she'd built.

The word "founder" felt too big. Like it belonged to someone else. Someone further along. Someone who'd earned it.

Even though she'd already done the work. She's built multiple businesses. She's unquestionably earned it.

That's not imposter syndrome. That's a voice doing the job it was made for.

We didn't try to silence it. We got curious about it. What's its purpose? What is it here to say?

"I'm not good enough." "I'm not there yet." "I don't belong here." The purpose is to keep her safe. Keep her small enough that nothing bad can happen.

It's protective. On her side. But self-limiting.

Once she could see that... The voice lost its grip.

It's still there. But now. She knows what it's for.

The work isn't killing the voice. It's learning to move with it. Instead of being stopped by it.

If you've ever shrunk in a room full of people you think are more successful than you... that voice is running the show.

That pattern doesn't break in one session. But it does break.

The question is whether you're willing to look at it?
