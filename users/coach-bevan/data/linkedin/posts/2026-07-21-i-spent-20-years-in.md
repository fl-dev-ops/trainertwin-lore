I spent 20 years in product and technology before I became a coach. 
I mention it because it changes what I can offer the people I work with now.

I led product on a data platform connected to a million Jaguar Land Rover vehicles. The work drove around £200m in revenue and £400m in savings. Earlier, at a security company, I took a team that was certain six releases a year was their ceiling, and we reached ninety-six.

A clean hundred would have been nice. Still slightly annoyed about that.
I'm not listing this to impress anyone. I'm listing it because when a stressed product leader sits down with me, I'm not guessing at their world from the outside. I've been in the rooms. I know the pressure of owning a number, the politics, the particular loneliness of being the person expected to have every answer.

There are many good coaches who've never operated at that level, and plenty of them are excellent. But for the leaders I work with, it matters that I've stood where they're standing. The inner work is the same either way. It just lands differently coming from someone who has carried the same weight.

That's the part I care about most. Not the numbers. The fact that I remember exactly what they cost.
