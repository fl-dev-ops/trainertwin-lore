Some leaders aren't overloaded. Being needed has quietly become part of who they are.

I know it because I was one. At one company I was sharp, I knew the codebase better than anyone, I was working with contractors who didn't, and I was a bit arrogant about all three. 
I tried to empower the team. I genuinely thought I was.
Then I went on holiday for 2 weeks, and came back to find a single decision had sat frozen the entire time, waiting for me. Nobody had felt able to make it without me.

That was the moment. I wasn't a leader with a strong team. I was a bottleneck with a reputation, and I'd built it myself.

Here's the strange fix I landed on eventually. I stopped keeping up with exactly how everything worked under the hood. I let myself become a little technically incapable, on purpose. And it helped - because when I genuinely couldn't be the one with the answer, my team had to trust their own, and they were more than good enough.

I've told that story to a lot of technical product managers since, as a warning. The thing that makes you valuable as an expert is often the exact thing that makes you a bottleneck as a leader.
If you went away for 2 weeks, what would quietly stop moving until you got back?
