The wrong job is hardest to leave when it's going well.
And a lot of very successful people are quietly in it. 
Not a failing job. A successful one. Good title, good money, the kind of role other people would be glad to have. Which is exactly what makes it so hard to walk away from.

I've come to believe a surprising number of capable, accomplished people are running on a version of success they never actually chose. They picked the path that looked right, got good at it, got rewarded for it, and somewhere along the way stopped asking whether it was theirs. The rewards became the reason to stay. And the quiet sense that something was off got easier to ignore than to face.

I'm not saying everyone who's tired should quit. Most shouldn't. Often the role is right and the person has simply lost themselves inside it.
But some people are exhausted because they're pouring themselves into something that was never theirs to build. And no amount of resilience fixes being in the wrong place.

So it's worth asking honestly. If nobody was watching, and nobody was impressed, would you still choose this?
