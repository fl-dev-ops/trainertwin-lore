I was hitting every target and quietly coming apart.

For years I carried a low hum of anxiety I couldn't explain. On paper I was doing well. Leading product teams, hitting targets, getting promoted.
But promoted at a normal rate. And in my head, that was the problem. If I was really in the right place, wouldn't I be moving faster? One of those people who's a CTO at 30? I was sure I could do more, and quietly convinced nobody around me could see it.

So I did what ambitious people do. I pushed harder, certain that if I just gave a bit more I'd finally get on top of the feeling.
I never did get on top of it. Because the problem was never effort.

At one company it caught up with me. I hit a low I couldn't think my way out of. And in the middle of it, something I'd been avoiding for years became clear. I was in the wrong role. Not a bad company, not a bad job. Just built around someone else's idea of what success should look like, rather than mine.
When I found the thing I was actually made for, the anxiety lifted. Not overnight, but it lifted.

I coach tech and product leaders now, and a lot of them are carrying that same low hum. Successful, capable, and quietly wondering if they're good enough.
So I'll tell them what I wish someone had told me sooner. The feeling isn't proof you're failing. It's usually proof you've lost contact with what you actually want.

Have you ever felt that gap between looking fine and feeling fine?
