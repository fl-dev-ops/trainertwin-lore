Your roadmap got approved. Everyone nodded. You left thinking it went well. That should worry you.

The most dangerous meeting is often the one where nobody disagreed with you.
Here's the uncomfortable part though: People usually stop disagreeing for good reasons. You answer quickly because you're trying to be helpful. You explain because you want to be clear. None of it is ego. But the effect is the same, the conversation quietly closes, 
when what you actually needed was to keep it open. And a closed room has a cost. Without disagreement, the wrong thing gets built, and it gets built fast, because nothing slowed it down.

I had to notice this in myself. The quiet meetings weren't proof I was right. They were proof people had stopped telling me when I wasn't.

When did someone last properly disagree with you in a meeting? If it's been a while, that's worth sitting with.
