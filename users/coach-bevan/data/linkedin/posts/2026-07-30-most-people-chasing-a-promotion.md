Most people chasing a promotion don't actually want the job above them.

They want the proof. The signal that they're doing well, that they haven't stalled, that they're still the kind of person who gets promoted. The role itself is almost incidental.

I see it consistently. Brilliant engineers grinding toward a management role they'll quietly hate. Product people angling for a VP title so they can stop feeling behind, not because they want the job.
The tell is simple. Picture the promotion already yours, title, money, all of it. If the feeling is relief that you've proven something, rather than excitement about the actual work, you're chasing the signal, not the job.

There's nothing wrong with wanting to prove you're good. But building a career on it is how capable people end up successful and miserable, one well-earned step at a time.
What would you be doing if you had nothing left to prove?
