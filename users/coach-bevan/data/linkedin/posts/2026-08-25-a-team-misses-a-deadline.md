A team misses a deadline. 2 leaders respond, and the difference shapes everything that comes after.

One asks: "How did we let this slip?" The other asks: "What did we miss?" 
The first sounds reasonable, but people hear the blame underneath it. 
The second is genuinely looking for what went wrong, not who.

For a long time I thought the trick was asking the better question. It isn't.
You can ask the perfect question in a cold room and people will still tell you nothing. And you can ask a blunt one in a safe room and the truth comes out anyway. It was never really about the words. It's about whether the energy in the room makes it safe to be honest.

I learned this in one large, highly political organisation, where honest post-mortems got quietly used against me and my team. Team leads were competing for a job only one of them could get, and every admission became material. Root cause analysis never reached the root, because admitting fault was a weakness nobody could afford.

What it gave me was an ear for the room. I can feel the exact moment safety leaves - the shift where honesty stops being worth the risk - and that matters far more than any clever question.
Fix the safety and almost any question works. Miss it, and the best question in the world gets you nothing.

When was the last time it felt genuinely safe to admit a mistake at work?
