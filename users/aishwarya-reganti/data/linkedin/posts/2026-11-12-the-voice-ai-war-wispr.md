🫠 The voice AI war (Wispr, Granola, Fireflies etc.) is getting really interesting to watch. And I think it's a great example of why product moats don't exist anymore even for billion $ companies!

Wispr Flow has arguably been one of the most popular products for AI voice typing, growing almost virally this year and reaching a $2 billion valuation. 

I remember wondering why nobody else was going after this market. And then, look at what happened in just three months. 

- August 5: Wispr Flow launched its own AI meeting notetaker, entering an already crowded market with companies like Granola and Fireflies. 
- September 29: Fireflies launched Fireflies Talk, offering free, unlimited voice typing, essentially going after Wispr's market. 
- October 29: Granola launched its own voice typing feature, also free and unlimited. 

3 billion dollar companies, all trying to eat into each other's markets, with voice AI as the underlying technology. 

And if you think about it, voice typing as a standalone product is probably one feature release away from a frontier lab offering something similar. Which is probably why all these companies are trying to expand their offerings and make their products stickier. 

 😅 I actually ended up switching almost entirely from Wispr Flow to Granola in the middle of all this drama. 

I'd been noticing that Wispr's quality wasn't quite what it used to be after they moved to their own model.  Also, on Granola, there's a lot more history that I wouldn't want to lose or migrate. Wispr, on the other hand, was much easier to replace. 

I'm not saying the game is over for any of these companies. But it's fascinating how quickly the competitive dynamics are changing. And users seem increasingly comfortable switching products, esp. when they can consolidate subscriptions. 

What a time to be alive!
