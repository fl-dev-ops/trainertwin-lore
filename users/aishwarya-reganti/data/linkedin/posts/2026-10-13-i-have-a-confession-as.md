🫡 I have a confession: as an engineer, I never fully appreciated people who do sales well. Now I bow down to you.

2 years ago, when I was still in my job, hearing “sales” made me think of someone getting on a call and pushing me to buy something that wasn't worth my time. My entire understanding of sales was wrong.

Over the past year, learning to sell has changed that perspective completely. I'm not talking about sending 1000s of cold messages or promoting something at scale with slop. However much you automate, the work of earning someone's trust is deeply human.

People want help solving their problems. You need to understand what matters to them and whether what you're offering can actually help. Your passion for the product or service needs to come through in a way that helps them see why buying it would be a good decision for them.

That takes far more listening, judgment, and care than I'd ever given it credit for. I've had to do 200+ pre-sales and sales calls in the last year and man, there's still so much to learn!

If you're an engineer like me reading this and thinking "big deal", I challenge you to sell something people would pay for. A small app, a paid newsletter, a service. Anything you have to convince someone is worth their money.

It changes your perspective when something you've built has to earn its place in someone else's life and budget. 

You start to see how much of a craft it is to do sales well.
