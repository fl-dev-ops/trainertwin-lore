👀 Really raw and no BS article from McKinsey QuantumBlack on creating a future proof enterprise agentic platform architecture. It mirrors a lot of patterns we’re seeing on the ground too.

The article argues that most enterprises won’t get meaningful impact from agentic AI by running scattered pilots, buying copilots, or letting every team build their own agent stack.

Their proposed solution is a shared enterprise architecture that lets agentic systems plug into business workflows, internal tools, data systems, evaluation, observability, security, orchestration, and governance.

Some ideas:

⛳ Horizontal copilots are not enough: Employee copilots are easy to roll out, but they usually sit on top of work instead of changing how work actually gets done. The bigger value comes from vertical workflows where agents are embedded into real business processes.

⛳ Don’t build everything: Most companies should buy or partner for a lot of the stack, and only build where there’s real differentiation, control, or organizational specificity. 

⛳ Design for replacement from day one: Their phrase is “composable and compostable.” Composable means you can assemble the architecture from reusable pieces. Compostable means you can replace pieces later without rebuilding the entire system. I think this is my favorite mindset change given that the the agent stack is changing too fast for anyone to pretend they’ve picked the final architecture.

⛳ Production has to start on day one: A lot of agent work gets stuck at the proof of concept stage because evaluation, observability, security, memory, governance, and failure handling are treated as things to add later. If agents are going to touch enterprise workflows, the control system has to be designed from the beginning.

⛳ Autonomy needs structure.
One of the most practical ideas in the article is that workflow engines can make agents more useful and less risky. Instead of giving agents unlimited freedom, put them inside defined workflows with clear steps, validation, approvals, retries, audit logs, and access boundaries.
