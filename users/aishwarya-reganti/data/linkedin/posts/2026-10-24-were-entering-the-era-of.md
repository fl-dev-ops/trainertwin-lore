🤦‍♀️ We're entering the era of resume slop!! I've reviewed 100s of resumes over the past few months while hiring for LevelUp Labs, and it's becoming an incredibly painful experience.

A lot of applicants seem to be tailoring their resumes to match our job descriptions almost perfectly. Their experience somehow aligns with the exact systems we're building, the use cases we're working on, and even the terminology we use.

I've heard similar things from friends who are hiring as well.
- With AI, it's become incredibly easy to generate a resume that makes you look like the perfect candidate for pretty much any job.
- Some people are even taking this a step further. They're picking a cluster of jobs they want to apply for and updating their entire LinkedIn profiles, resumes, and everything else to align with those roles.
- Only for you to get on an interview and realize that they've never actually built most of these systems. Sometimes they can barely explain the projects they've listed. And in some cases, they're even using agents during interviews to read out answers.

I always thought AI slop was mostly limited to influencers putting out endless AI generated content about things they've never actually built or worked on.

At least with those influencers, a lot of people in the industry can tell who's actually done the work and who's just recycling information for engagement. Most of the time, you can just ignore them and move on.

But it seems like slop has made its way into hiring as well. And I think this creates a much bigger problem for recruiters and hiring managers.

This makes it super hard for freshers, because you can't rely on traditional signals like past experience, referrals, or career trajectory. You have to judge them by the projects they've built.

I've had great experiences hiring talented freshers with no prior experience, but it's becoming much harder to identify who's actually done the work.
