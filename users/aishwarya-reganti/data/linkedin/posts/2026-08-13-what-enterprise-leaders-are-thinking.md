💡 What enterprise leaders are thinking about in 2026 feels like a complete flip from 2025, at least based on the CXO conversations I've been having over the past few weeks, ofc, all AI related

2025:
- Where should we actually use AI across the company, and which use cases are worth investing in versus just being interesting demos?
- How do we prove ROI beyond productivity anecdotes, and how do we know whether these investments are actually creating business value?
- Is our data even ready for AI, and how do we deal with fragmented data, permissions, legacy systems, and knowledge sitting across different parts of the company?
- How do we get employees to actually use these tools instead of rolling out Copilot or ChatGPT licenses that barely change how people work?
- Which workflows are actually ready for agents, and how much autonomy should we give them?


2026:
- How do we prevent vendor lock in and manage token economics as usage starts getting much larger?
- Employees are building a lot of point solutions, but how do we set up defined processes so that what they build can actually be reused and scaled across the company?
- How do we build explainability and observability into systems from day one, given that it's table stakes that AI systems will make mistakes?
- How do we put guardrails in place so people aren't just producing AI slop, while still letting them move fast?
- For mature, high volume use cases, what does it cost to own more of the inference layer and self host?

TL;DR from my purview, 2025 was largely about AI adoption. Now that AI is there to some extent, 2026 is much more about AI operationalization.

I also think many leaders have accepted that proving clean, on paper ROI with AI isn't super easy. It's something they may have to live with while continuing to invest and looking for the gains to accrue over the next few years.
