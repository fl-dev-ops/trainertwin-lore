5 topics that keep coming up again and again in my discussions with enterprise AI leaders in Q4 2026. (And boy, have things changed SO MUCH since the beginning of the year!)

1. Token economics
Unfortunately, the era of token maxing was very short. Companies are now actively looking into model routing, figuring out cheaper ways to use tokens, and experimenting with different vendors to optimize costs. Even within products, there's a good amount of scrutiny around where tokens are being used and whether the ROI justifies the spend.

2. Voice and multimodal workflows
We've already seen this happen in consumer interfaces. They're becoming increasingly voice based. Now, enterprises are looking at how to move existing systems that already work well into smoother voice interfaces. This also extends to sales workflows, customer support, and building multimodal workflows that can work across voice, text, and visual inputs.

3. Inference engineering and sovereign AI
This ties back to token economics. Companies are increasingly thinking about whether running their own inference infrastructure or investing in sovereign AI makes economic sense.
The density of talent that can actually run and optimize inference systems is still very low. IMO, inference engineering will be one of the most valuable technical skills to have over the next 4 to 5 years. Abi Aryan is def getting rich 😛 

4. Agent harnesses
In Q2/Q3, there was a lot of discussion around building agents. Now, there's a lot more discussion around building agent harnesses. Can we build the orchestration and observability layers, execution state management, and failure recovery mechanisms needed to run agents reliably?
Trust has unfortunately become one of the biggest problems with production AI systems. Harness engineering is increasingly becoming the way companies think about improving quality, reliability, and trust (IFYDK Kiriti Badam has insane expertise in this space from his work on Codex)

5. Knowledge runtimes
This is a term we coined LevelUp Labs because we were seeing enterprise requirements move far beyond vanilla RAG use cases. Essentially, knowledge runtimes are agentic retrieval systems with permission aware access, claim level provenance, audit trails, conflict detection, and the surrounding infrastructure needed to make enterprise knowledge usable by agents.
The focus is shifting toward building the scaffolding around retrieval systems rather than obsessing over individual retrieval components.
