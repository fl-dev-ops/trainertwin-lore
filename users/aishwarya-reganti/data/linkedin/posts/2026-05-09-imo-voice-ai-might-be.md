😅 IMO, voice AI might be the most important AI trend to watch in 2026, yet there’s still surprisingly little good material on where the space actually stands.

We recently hosted a session on the state of voice agents and what the stack looks like in our Chai and AI community, curated by Jacob Chacko.

It was  packed with useful context on one of the fastest growing areas in agentic AI, but one that still feels under discussed. So we put an article together to share some of the key ideas from the session. 

h/t to Aiza Hasib for articulating the session so well!

Highlights from the article:
- The voice stack has three main layers: the model layer, the orchestration layer, and the infrastructure layer.
- Cascaded systems are what most teams are shipping today. Speech to text, LLM, and text to speech stitched together. Practical, modular, and enterprise friendly.
- End to end and full duplex systems are where things get really interesting, especially for more natural conversations and interruption handling, but most of this is still early for serious enterprise deployment.
- Latency isn't just a model problem. Infrastructure choices can easily make or break the user experience.
- The best teams aren't trying to automate everything (as usual, and how many times should we say this, hehe). They're deciding upfront which 30 percent of conversations should stay with humans.
- Voice doesn't magically solve hallucinations, jailbreaks, bad handoffs, or weak guardrails. It inherits the same AI system design problems, and in some cases, makes them harder.
