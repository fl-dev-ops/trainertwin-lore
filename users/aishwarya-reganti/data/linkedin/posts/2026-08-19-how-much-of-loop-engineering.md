🤓 How much of loop engineering is actually new? When is it actually useful? And what about token costs? If these questions are on your mind too, do check out our latest article!


We take a first principles look at the trend instead of assuming loops are the next thing every AI system needs. We trace where the idea came from, how it relates to prompt, context, and harness engineering, and where repeated autonomous execution actually earns its complexity.

A few things we cover:
• Where loop engineering came from, including Ralph loops, ReAct style agents, and why some of what feels new has existed in software automation for years.
• The different types of loops, from simple agent loops and verification loops to event driven systems and hill climbing loops that try to improve the system itself.
• How to design a loop properly, including stopping conditions, context management, tool access, subagents, verification, and escalation.
• The token and reliability tradeoffs, because an agent that can keep going can also keep wasting tokens, accumulating context, and producing work nobody has properly verified.
• When loops are actually worth it, and when a cron job, workflow, or single agent run is probably enough. The strongest cases tend to have repeated work and a clear signal for whether each iteration improved the result.

We also get into some of the less discussed costs, including verification debt, comprehension rot, and cognitive surrender as teams give increasingly autonomous systems more room to operate.
