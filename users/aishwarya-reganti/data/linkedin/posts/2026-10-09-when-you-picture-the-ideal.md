🫠 When you picture the ideal version of a successful person, what comes to mind? Someone quiet, humble, who puts others first and lets their work speak for itself?

😅 It's probably one of the biggest things I've had to unlearn.

Growing up in a middle class Indian family, that was pretty much the archetype of someone I  was taught to admire. Someone who stayed grounded, never felt the need to sell themselves, didn't talk too much about their accomplishments, and trusted that good work would eventually get noticed.

I owe a lot of who I've become to that upbringing, and I still deeply value those principles. But I think I took some of them a little too literally (and I know many people from similar backgrounds who have too).

It's not these principles themselves that hurt, but what they can end up manifesting into:
- Humility can become a reluctance to take up space.
- Putting others first can become a habit of putting yourself last. And the more you do it, the more it becomes an unconscious habit.
- Letting your work speak for itself can become an excuse to never learn how to speak for your work.

You start believing that if you're good enough, someone will eventually notice. That recognition is something you earn silently, rather than something you can actively pursue.

...And then you start climbing the corporate ladder or building something of your own, and realize the world doesn't quite work that way.

The people who shape decisions are often extremely comfortable doing *whatever* it takes to get attention, establish influence, and make sure their ideas are heard. 

...And then you start realizing that so many things don't have an objective right or wrong answer. A lot of it comes down to who can sell their vision better.

I know so many incredibly smart people who remain quiet simply because speaking up feels like stepping outside the archetype they were taught to admire. And that also means the world doesn't always get to benefit from the best ideas. (Read up on the "Babble hypothesis")

Anyway, if I could tell myself one thing 10 years ago, it'd be to come out of that bubble sooner.

You can be deeply grounded in who you are and still want the world to know what you're capable of  😁 

I had to be thrown into the trenches of building a company to really learn this, and I do think I learned it a little late. I hope you don't have to.
