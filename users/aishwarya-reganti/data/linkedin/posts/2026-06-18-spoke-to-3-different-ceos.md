👀 Spoke to 3 different CEOs last week, and they pretty much had the same thing on their mind about AI right now. 

One was the CEO of a public company. The other two were from upper mid market companies.

The idea: Build smarter model orchestration and routing pipelines internally. Stop depending blindly on frontier model providers, even if you don't fully eliminate them.


Given the latest updates, and the fact that the honeymoon period for most AI companies is probably over, token costs are likely going to remain high for the next few years. 

The biggest bottleneck is also the hardware, and it doesn't look like that clears up anytime soon.

More companies are now thinking about how they can build their own scaffolding, so they don't have to move away from frontier models completely, but can use them more intelligently.

Frontier models are incredibly capable. But not every part of every workflow needs the most expensive model. A lot of workflows can be routed to smaller models for simpler steps, while frontier models handle the harder reasoning, planning, synthesis, or review parts.

At the current stage, most models are not post trained to route by default. I think that can change pretty quickly.

Even if companies are not self hosting, they are going to look for ways to reduce cost and control token usage. Maybe frontier model companies do the routing internally to optimize cost. Or maybe they expose more routing controls to enterprises, so teams can decide when to use cheaper models and when to call the big ones.

Think about how coding agents like Claude Code or Codex already do subagent routing, but now applied at a much larger enterprise scale.

It is interesting that this was a 2024 trend that kind of died for a while. A lot of companies that were heavily betting on it got washed away.

But it seems to be coming back.
And this time, it may actually be here to stay.
