🫠 When companies hire for AI roles today, your GitHub contributions and projects might get you into the room. The problem is that almost everyone has them now. So what'd set you apart?

Over the past few weeks, I’ve been speaking with friends, CXOs, and leaders who are actively hiring. They’ve seen a sharp rise in resumes claiming experience across multiple AI projects, often backed by GitHub repositories and cool prototypes.

A few years ago, that alone would’ve been super impressive. Today, everyone knows much of this work is likely AI augmented.

I put together a builder’s loop based on the skills we increasingly look for during hiring. It might be helpful if you’re thinking through what defines a modern AI builder too.

1. Frame the right problem: Start with the business problem, the user, the constraints, and the desired outcome. Don’t start with a model, framework, or tool.

2. Design the system intentionally: Choose the right architecture, context, level of autonomy, and human involvement based on the problem.

3. Define what good looks like: Decide what quality means before building. Identify the behaviors, risks, and outcomes that need to be measured.

4. Build an improvement flywheel: Use evals, production signals, feedback, and error analysis to continuously improve the system.

5. Design AI augmented workflows: Decide where AI should support thinking, research, synthesis, decision making, and execution, and where human judgment should stay in control.

6. Build repeatable operating systems: Turn effective AI workflows into reusable processes, templates, automations, and systems that improve how work gets done over time.

In fact, hiring is increasingly moving toward this loop as well. Yes, play around with the tools and models and understand how they work, but don't become so obsessed with them that you lose sight of the bigger picture.
