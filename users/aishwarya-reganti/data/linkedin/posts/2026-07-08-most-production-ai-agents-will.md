🫡 Most production AI agents will either move to voice or have some form of voice modality in the future. And we’re already seeing this play out across a lot of companies.

But voice brings in a lot more complexities in production than chat-based interfaces and a lot of the solution lies in the scaffolding than the model itself 

We had a session with the Vapi's CTO Nikhil Gupta & FDE Dhruva Reddy for our internal community and AI Builder Pass members on how teams are productionizing voice AI, and there was so much gold in the session.

A few takeaways:
⛳ Voice agents are not chat agents with speech added on top. The user can’t scroll back, reread, or wait through awkward silence. The design has to be different.
⛳ Latency is UX. In voice, three seconds of silence doesn’t feel like processing. It feels broken.
⛳ Pick the right problems for voice. A phone based workflow doesn’t automatically become a good voice AI workflow.
⛳ Inbound and outbound agents need very different design. One responds to intent, the other has to earn the right to continue the conversation.
⛳ Persona matters more in voice than people expect. Warmth, pacing, initiative, and boundaries all show up immediately.
⛳ Tool use has to be much more directive. You can’t leave every decision to the model when a user is waiting live on the call.
⛳ Human handoff is part of the product. If escalation feels clunky, the whole agent feels unreliable.

Voice agents feel magical in demos because the interface is so natural. But in production, they’re a product problem, an infrastructure problem, a UX problem, a governance problem, and an observability problem.

All of these need to be designed for intentionally. Voice can’t just be treated as an interface layered on top of chat. It has to be treated as a first class modality.
