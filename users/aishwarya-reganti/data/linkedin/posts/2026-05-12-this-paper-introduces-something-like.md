🤩 This paper introduces something like a Claude Code for ML research.  It gives agents a structured harness to discover ideas, run experiments, review claims, write papers, and prepare rebuttals too!

The key idea is that autonomous research shouldn’t depend on one agent doing everything. ARIS uses structured workflows, persistent memory, and adversarial review between models to make AI assisted research more reliable.

Interesting facts from the paper:

- ARIS has more than 65 Markdown defined skills, five end to end workflows, six MCP model bridges, and a per project research wiki. The tested executor platforms include Claude Code, Codex CLI, and Cursor. 

- The system’s five workflows cover idea discovery, experiment bridge, auto review, paper writing, and rebuttal. These map roughly to discovery, experimentation, manuscript creation, and post submission response. 

- ARIS recommends using different model families for execution and review. The executor moves the research forward, while the reviewer critiques intermediate outputs and asks for revisions. Similar to how we treat LLM judges.

- The paper includes a claim audit process that checks whether experimental claims are actually supported by evidence. It has three stages: integrity verification, result to claim mapping, and auditing manuscript claims against the raw evidence and claim ledger. 

In the auto review loop, the paper shows an example where the reviewer score improves from 5.0 to 7.5 out of 10 after multiple rounds. Interesting idea of building harnesses for "X" based on niche and domain-specific workflows. Think it'll be pretty common in the next few months.
