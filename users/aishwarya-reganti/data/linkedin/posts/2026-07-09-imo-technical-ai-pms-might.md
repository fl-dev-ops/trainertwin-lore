😅 IMO, technical AI PMs might make for extremely good FDEs, sometimes even better than engineers... and before you come at me, hear me out:

After doing 50+ AI deployments across large companies, first at AWS, before being an FDE was cool, and now at LevelUp Labs, one thing has become very clear to me.

"Forward Deployed Engineering is barely about engineering."

A lot of it really boils down to people, process, management, negotiation, and meeting customers where they are. The engineering itself can be pretty straightforward if you have good design thinking and a clear understanding of the problem.

When we meet customers and start building for them, some of the biggest pain points we see are:
- Educating them on their existing infrastructure, what AI can do, and what AI can’t do.
- Boiling down outrageous expectations and bringing the conversation back to their actual processes. A lot of teams need to understand and document their workflows properly before they automate them.
- Explaining why AI implementations need time, space, and feedback to improve. Complete automation on day one is usually not a good idea.
- Meeting them where they are, so we build something they’re actually excited about without creating a useless build. For instance, many companies ask us to build custom tools that come with long term maintenance costs. Often, it’s better to work around existing tools and anticipate what frontier products might ship next, instead of building something that becomes dead weight.
- Staying obsessed with their problems and solving backwards from the customer, rather than introducing the fanciest thing. And yes, making sure token costs stay under control.

And so many other things like that.

I’ve started realizing that engineering takes up maybe 20% of the time, or even less. Around 80 to 90% of the work is really about understanding people, processes, constraints, incentives, and meeting teams where they are. 

💡 That's pretty much product management 101

So yep, I’m super bullish that technical product managers could be a great fit to lead forward deployed solution work, alongside FDEs, and in some cases, maybe even without them given where this is all heading 😅
