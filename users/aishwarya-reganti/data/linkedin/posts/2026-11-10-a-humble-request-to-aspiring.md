😅 A humble request to aspiring AI engineers: Please stop filling your GitHub with AI generated projects. Here are 5 things hiring managers would much rather see.

We interview quite a few engineers at LevelUp Labs, and I've always been a huge fan of hiring younger engineers because they challenge what's possible.

But one thing that really puts me off is seeing GitHub repos full of clearly AI generated projects with very little engineering thought behind them. And when we ask candidates about their implementation, quite often, they can't explain what they've built.

I'm all for using AI to write code. That's not the problem. Here's what I'd much rather see.

1. Build projects that solve problems companies actually have.
Stop building random tutorial projects that nobody would use. Read engineering blogs, research real problems companies are solving, and better yet, build something relevant to the company you're applying to. Your coding agents can help you research all of this. 

2. Use as much AI as you want, but understand your code.
I don't care how much of your code was written by AI. But if I ask why you chose a particular architecture, library, or approach, you should be able to explain it. You don't need to memorize every line, but you should understand the decisions behind it.

3. Show your engineering decisions, not just your final code.
A small, well documented project is far more impressive than a massive repository. Write a short Markdown file explaining your architecture, tradeoffs, what didn't work, and what you'd change. If you built multiple iterations, include a simple changelog. Let me see how you thought through the problem.

4. Make it ridiculously easy to run your project.
You'd be surprised how many repositories I've tried to run that simply don't work. Include clear setup instructions, working dependencies, and ideally a live demo or API. Assume the person interviewing you will actually clone and run your code. Because I often do.

5. Show me that you've actually tested what you built.
What edge cases did you test? What are the known limitations? Even a handful of meaningful tests tells me you've thought beyond generating code.

AI has made it incredibly easy to build things that would've taken teams of engineers a few years ago. But it's also made it easy to create the appearance of engineering work without much engineering thinking.
