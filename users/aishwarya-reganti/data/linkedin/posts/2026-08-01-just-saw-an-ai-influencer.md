Just saw an AI influencer take our CC/CD philosophy and the agency-control tradeoff, rename it into a botched term and framework, add the much needed AI slop, and teach it as their own.

The things people do to create content and make it sound like it came from experience and ofc, the audacity to openly copy IP without crediting the people who've spent years building, testing, and writing about them through real experience is something else. 

🫠 But I guess we’ll see more of this as AI makes it easier to repackage other people’s thinking. It adds just enough slop to make the copying harder to call out precisely, even when it’s painfully obvious to any human reading it. 

The need to build thought leadership and be seen as an expert has become so pressing that people will do almost anything to repackage others ideas as their own. After all, it’s a much easier route than taking the time to build something, develop real intuition, and form original opinions through experience. So maybe I'll stop being a sour grape and take the imitation as flattery.

⛳ Anyway, the CC/CD philosophy itself is simple. but it's one mindset shift can help you build far more impactful and reliable systems.

When you’re building agents for large scale production use cases, start simple. 
CC/CD stands for continuous calibration & continuous development

Graduate to more complex architectures over multiple iterations. Increase autonomy slowly, guided by reference data, context & evals, instead of building the most autonomous system you can from day one.

You gradually increase agency and reduce control over time. Every new component that increases autonomy should be introduced carefully, tested rigorously through evals, and vetted before you trust it.
