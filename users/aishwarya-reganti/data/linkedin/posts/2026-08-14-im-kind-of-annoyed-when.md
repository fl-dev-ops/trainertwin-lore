🌶️ 😅 I'm kind of annoyed when people casually throw “reimagine workflows with AI.” For most products, the hard part is figuring out how much you can reimagine *without hurting adoption*

And if you've been building AI systems over the past year that actually need to work at scale, you'd probably agree that one of the hardest parts is almost always UI/UX.

More so when your users aren't technical, already have established ways of working, and suddenly you're asking them to change all of it.

Take a healthcare example:

A doctor talks to a patient, writes or updates a note, reviews their history, decides on the next action, maybe suggests a prescription, and documents everything in the systems they already use.

A lot of that process is manual today, and AI can clearly make it faster.

But now imagine adding AI into that workflow.

Should it be a chat interface? A text box? A separate copilot? A set of buttons and suggested actions? In many cases, the best answer is probably none of those.

Ideally, the AI should feel almost invisible. It should connect to the tools they already use, understand enough context to help at the right moment, and change as little of their existing workflow as possible.

Because every new interface is another thing the user has to learn.

And regardless of how good your reimagined workflow is, if you ask people to completely change how they work from day one, adoption gets much harder.

This is the tradeoff I think we should actually be talking about:
- How much do you reimagine the workflow, versus how much do you preserve to make adoption easier?
⛳ Too little change, and you're probably just adding AI on top of an old process.
⛳ Too much change, and you've built something theoretically better that nobody wants to use.

The best enterprise AI products I've seen find the balance (the golden zone, I'd like to call it). They rethink the parts that actually need to change, while making the transition feel surprisingly boring for the end user.

Sometimes great AI UX isn't about making the AI more visible. It's about making the user think about the AI less. That's been our motto at LevelUp Labs!
