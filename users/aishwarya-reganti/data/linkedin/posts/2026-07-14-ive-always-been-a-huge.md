I’ve always been a huge fan of Naval Ravikant’s idea that you shouldn’t sell something you don’t genuinely like or feel obsessed with.

When you deeply believe in the power of what you’re building, sales shouldn’t feel like a task. It should feel enjoyable. You should genuinely want to tell people about it because you believe they’re missing out on something valuable.

That’s been our philosophy while building every course we’ve launched on Maven.

AI education is notorious for teaching people how to build an agent in 30 minutes and put it online. But when you don’t understand the code, the system design, or the decisions your agent is making, you’re pretty much standing on quicksand.

What’s much harder to find is the thinking behind building strong systems, the expertise that comes from doing this work in practice, and the opportunity to have real, two way interactions with a community and instructors working at the frontier.

This field is moving so quickly that many of the most important decisions come down to tradeoffs. Those tradeoffs often aren’t documented anywhere. You only learn them by building, making decisions, seeing what breaks, and talking to people doing the work right now.

That’s why in any of our marketing content or just in general when we talk Kiriti Badam and I always been far more obsessed with explaining our mindset and helping people build that mindset than listing terms such as LLMs, RAG, fine tuning,  MCPs, context engineering, and everything else.

In one of our recent marketing emails, I wrote about what it means to be "the modern AI builder", which I also posted about on LinkedIn, link below, and how we’re building educational experiences around that idea.

The fact that someone connected with it enough to join and leave such a thoughtful note means the world to me!

I’m really looking forward to welcoming more like minded people into our next cohort, which starts in about two weeks! Super excited
