A&E is a great place to learn and observe human life. Spent twelve hours in A&E yesterday from just after lunch to 2:30 am Sunday morning, this post is not about me, I’m fine still refired not retired just slowed down a bit from the experience. People watching passed the time - footballers, from soccer, Gaelic football rugby, with various injuries from broken ankles to one young man who performed a somersault on the field and hurt his back, to the police arriving with accident victims, elderly people who had fallen, one gentleman with a broken hip, to Mums and Dads with young children, even two friends with their son, who I learned this morning is fine. There were two bonuses I will treasure.

Firstly the resilience of the staff, under severe pressure to cope, how the emanated kindness a smile, a kind word, so beautiful to witness. The doctors stressed dealing with emegencies to people like me demanding “what about me?”

Then an exceptional bonus the man who approaches and says you don’t recognise me Colly, he turned out to be Derek Fallis one of my best customers from the 80’s thank you Derek of the kind words you had to say about my sales skills and approach when I served him as owner of Dobbins Inn Carrickfergus in the 80s. Hope your feeling better Derek, and Burns Night went OK with an alternative host.

What can I say about A&E i witnessed an understaffed system coping not complaining, the privilege having conversations with people as we waited and waited and the experience of practising a resilent mindset.
