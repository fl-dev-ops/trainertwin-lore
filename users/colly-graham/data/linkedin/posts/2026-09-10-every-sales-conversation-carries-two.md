Every sales conversation carries two conversations at once. There is the one happening out loud, the questions asked and the answers given. Then there is the one happening underneath, shaped entirely by intent. A salesperson who walks in wanting to close will ask different questions than one who walks in wanting to understand. Buyers feel that difference before they can name it. They sense whether they are being served or being sold to, and that sense decides how much truth they are willing to share in return.
 
Intent shapes discovery long before a single word is spoken. If the goal is a signature, questions become tools for extraction, and the conversation narrows toward whatever justifies the close. If the goal is genuine understanding, questions open outward, and the buyer starts trusting the process enough to reveal what actually matters to them, their real constraints, their real ambitions, the challenges they have not said out loud yet. I have watched this play out across eleven countries and thousands of conversations. The salespeople who consistently earn trust are rarely the sharpest talkers. They are the ones whose intent was never in question.
 
This matters because intent cannot be faked for long. Buyers are not naive, and most have sat through enough conversations to recognise a script wearing the costume of curiosity. The fix is not a better close or a cleverer question. The fix is deciding, before the call, what you actually want from the person in front of you. Clarity of intent changes tone, changes pace, changes which questions feel natural to ask. It also tends to produce better outcomes, because a conversation built on genuine understanding gives both sides something worth acting on.
