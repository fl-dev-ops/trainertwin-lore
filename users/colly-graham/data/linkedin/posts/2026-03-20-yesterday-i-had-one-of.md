Yesterday I had one of those moments that quietly stays with you.
I was listening to a panel discussion and one of the speakers quoted something I had taught many years ago in a sales training workshop. They explained how they had applied that idea in their work and how it had served them well over time.
I have to admit, it touched me more than I expected.
When you spend decades teaching, coaching, and sharing ideas, you rarely see where those ideas travel. Most of the time you plant seeds and move on to the next room, the next team, the next conversation.
So hearing someone repeat a lesson that had stayed with them — and knowing it had made a difference — was deeply meaningful.
It reminded me of something I’ve come to understand over the years.
Every person who steps onto a stage carries a story that no one else can replicate.
For me, that story is built from a lifetime in sales, thousands of conversations with customers and teams, and an enduring curiosity about how the human mind works — particularly how confidence, belief, and decision-making shape performance.
None of that makes me better than anyone else.
But it does mean that the perspective I bring is uniquely my own.
And moments like yesterday remind me why I still care so deeply about this work.
Because if something you share today helps someone perform better, think differently, or believe in themselves a little more tomorrow — that is a legacy worth building.
After all these years, the fire to keep learning, teaching, and contributing is still very much alive.
