Everyone says you need to learn 'prompt engineering' to survive the AI wave. They are entirely wrong. If you want to stay relevant in 2026, you need to master problem framing instead. Here is why:
Two years ago, I was terrified of AI! 
Today, I use it as a 'junior teammate' to do the work of three people in half the time.
Here are the three mindset shifts I had to make:
1. From "Competitor" to "Intern"
Old mindset: AI is coming for my job. I need to outwork it.
New mindset: AI is my wildly eager, slightly naive junior assistant.
The Shift: I stopped trying to beat it and started delegating to it. I use AI to conquer the blank page, build outlines, and do heavy research. I never let it do the final polish. It builds the frame; I paint the house.
2. From "Knowing the Answers" to "Asking the Questions"
Old mindset: My value comes from having all the right answers in my head.
New mindset: My value comes from having the context to ask the right questions.
The Shift: When information is infinite and instantly generated, knowing facts is cheap. Problem-framing is the new superpower. The better the context you feed the machine, the better the output. Your lived experience is the ultimate prompt.
3. From "Creator" to "Curator"
Old mindset: I am paid for the volume of content/code/work I create.
New mindset: I am paid for my taste, judgment, and empathy.
The Shift: AI can generate hundreds of ideas in ten seconds. The human job is no longer just generation; it's curation. You have to be the editor who looks at those hundred ideas and says, "This is the one that will actually resonate with another human being."
AI won't replace you. A person using AI with the right mindset will.
