The world of sales has changed beyond recognition.

In 1969 an 70s, sales was gritty and unrelenting. A worn suit, calls from phone boxes, a pitch over a pint, and afternoons in grey offices. Survival built on grit as much as charm.

By 1980, as a sales manager, the game was pressure and performance. Quotas marked in red pen, phones ringing, typewriters clattering — success measured only by the month’s numbers.

In the 1990s, as a sales director, the world got bigger. Open-plan offices, mobile phones, spreadsheets, and overseas trips. Growth was everything, judged by market share not handshakes.

The 2000s brought technology. Email, mobiles, and early CRMs replaced paper diaries and Rolodexes. Pipelines, dashboards, and conference calls defined the rhythm.

In the 2010s, sales went digital. Social media, automation, and analytics reshaped prospecting. Buyers arrived informed — value and customer experience became the differentiator.

Now in the 2020s, AI and neuroscience are at the heart of sales. Data predicts, chatbots qualify, and algorithms score leads. Success depends on trust, empathy, and blending artificial with human intelligence.

From grit to growth, from data to AI — the world of sales keeps evolving.

👉 How do you see the next decade of sales unfolding?
