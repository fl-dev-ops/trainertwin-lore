We deliver real value.”
Every buyer has heard that sentence a thousand times, and almost none of them believe it. After fifty-six years of frontline selling across eleven countries, I have come to a simple conclusion. Buyers are rarely persuaded by what a salesperson tells them. They are persuaded by what they hear themselves say. The moment you declare value, the buyer’s brain starts looking for reasons to doubt you, however sincere you are.
In this week’s Brain Based Newsletter, I explore why value is discovered, not declared. The research is clear. People remember what they work out for themselves, they value what they help to build, and they resist conclusions that are pressed upon them. I walk through how the BRAIN Selling System turns each stage of a sales conversation into discovery rather than persuasion, and why this matters even more now that inboxes are full of confident, machine-written claims that all sound the same.
If you lead a sales team, or you sit across the table from buyers yourself, this one is worth eight minutes of your time. There are three practical things you can try in your very next conversation. Have a read, and then tell me in the comments: where are you still declaring value when you could be asking a better question instead?
