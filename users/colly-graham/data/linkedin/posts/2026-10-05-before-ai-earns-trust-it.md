BEFORE AI EARNS TRUST, IT MUST PROVE VALUE AND IDENTIFY ITSELF
AI is becoming increasingly capable of researching potential customers, drafting communication, recommending actions and arranging meetings.But capability does not automatically create value—or trust.Sales leaders should ask two questions before automating any part of prospecting.
WHAT COMMERCIALLY MEANINGFUL RESULT IMPROVED?
Sending more messages is activity, not evidence.Measure what matters:• Relevant customer responses• Qualified conversations• Opportunities progressing• Research and preparation time saved• Accuracy of customer information• Customer trust and experienceInclude the hidden costs: checking, correcting and managing AI output.
DOES EVERYONE KNOW WHEN AI IS ACTING AND WHO REMAINS ACCOUNTABLE?
A customer should not have to guess whether an AI agent is researching them, contacting them or arranging a meeting.Sales leaders must define:• What AI may research• What it may draft• What requires human approval• When its involvement should be disclosed• Who verifies its claims• Who owns the outcomeAI should never become a hiding place for human responsibility.The future of ethical AI prospecting will not be determined solely by what the technology can do.It will be determined by whether it creates measurable value, respects the customer and keeps a human accountable.Before AI earns trust, it must prove value and identify itself.What rules has your organisation established for AI-assisted prospecting?
