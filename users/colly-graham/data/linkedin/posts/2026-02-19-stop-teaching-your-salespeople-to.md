Stop teaching your salespeople to "handle" objections. Here's why it's costing you sales.
After 56 years in frontline sales across 12 countries, I've watched companies pour thousands into objection-handling scripts and role-play sessions. The result? Salespeople who sound like they're reading from a courtroom defence brief. When a prospect senses they're being "handled," trust evaporates — and so does the sale. Objection handling is a reactive skill built on the wrong foundation.

The real problem isn't the objection. It's that something earlier in the conversation broke down. A need wasn't fully uncovered. A value wasn't clearly connected. A concern was glossed over to get to the close. The objection is just the symptom. Train your people to go back upstream — to ask better questions, listen more deeply, and earn the right to the next step before the objection ever surfaces.

What you should teach instead is objection prevention — and it starts with curiosity, not cleverness. Teach your salespeople to slow down, to notice what the prospect is really telling them, and to make the prospect feel so genuinely understood that resistance simply doesn't build. The best salespeople I've ever seen don't answer objections brilliantly. They rarely hear them at all.
