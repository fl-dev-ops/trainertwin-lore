Ever walked into the meeting that mattered too much, only to feel your mind go quiet at the worst moment? The palms are damp. The words that came so easily in practice will not arrive. That is not a character flaw. It is how the brain behaves under pressure, and it is the subject of the newest Brain Based Newsletter.
The issue follows the Yerkes–Dodson curve, first published in 1908, down into the chemistry underneath it. When the stakes rise, the amygdala treats a big customer like a threat. Cortisol climbs. The prefrontal cortex, the part that listens and reads the other person, quietly goes offline. Amy Arnsten's research at Yale shows how fast that can happen. The result is a salesperson who stops listening and starts rehearsing the next thing to say.
The good news is that this is a trainable skill, not a fixed trait. The newsletter sets out five practical ways to regulate pressure rather than remove it, so the thinking brain stays switched on when it matters most.
