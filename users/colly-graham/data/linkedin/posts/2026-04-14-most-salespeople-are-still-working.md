Most salespeople are still working from an outdated idea — that a sale ends in a moment. A close. A decision. A line in the sand.
 But the human brain doesn’t experience it that way.
From a neuroscience perspective, the brain is not looking for a “closing point.” It is looking for safety in motion. The moment a conversation feels like pressure, the brain resists. The moment it feels like progress, it leans in. What we call a sale is not an event — it is a sequence of micro-decisions where trust is either reinforced… or quietly withdrawn. And that changes everything about how we lead conversations, present ideas, and guide next steps.
In this week’s Brain Based Sales Newsletter, I explore a simple but powerful shift: the sale doesn’t close — it continues. You’ll see why the most effective salespeople don’t push for decisions… they create momentum. They reduce uncertainty. They make the next step feel natural, not forced. Because when the brain feels safe to move forward, decisions take care of themselves.
