If I’ve learned anything after years on LinkedIn, it’s this: most objections aren’t commercial — they’re neurological.
We were all taught to “handle” objections. Scripts. Reframes. Feel–felt–found. And yet the harder you push back, the more the resistance quietly grows. That’s not a skills issue. It’s reasistence. When someone feels their position is being challenged, their brain defends it. An objection isn’t a problem to overcome — it’s a signal that the buyer doesn’t yet feel safe enough, clear enough, or confident enough to move forward.
Think about the common ones we see every day:
“It’s too expensive.” Often not about price — it’s about perceived value or trust.
“We need to think about it.” Usually a comfort gap, not a calendar issue.
“We’re happy with our current supplier.” That’s status quo bias in action.
“I need to discuss with colleagues.” Sometimes genuine. Sometimes a polite exit.
The shift? Stop countering. Start calming. Cushion the concern. Ask about it. Listen properly. Then meet it at the level it’s actually operating. When people feel heard, they lean in. When they feel managed, they lean away. 
On LinkedIn — and in sales — the professionals who win aren’t the ones who argue best. They’re the ones who understand how the brain really works.
