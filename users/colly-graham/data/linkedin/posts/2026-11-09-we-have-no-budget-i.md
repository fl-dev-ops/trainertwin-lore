“We have no budget.”
I have heard those four words in boardrooms across eleven countries. They rarely mean what they say.
Most organisations find money for the things they believe in. Budget follows conviction. When a buyer says there is no budget, they are often telling you something else. They have not yet seen enough value to move money from somewhere else.
That leaves one honest question to ask yourself. Have you shown enough value to justify the spend?
If the answer is no, the problem sits with you, not the buyer. Price only ever feels high in relation to the value the buyer can see.
Before you leave that conversation, ask yourself three things. Do you understand what the problem is costing them today? Have they put a number on it in their own words? Can they see clearly what changes when the problem is solved?
When the cost of doing nothing becomes larger than the cost of acting, budget has a way of appearing.
This is not about pushing harder. It is about listening more carefully. Relationships come first, and value is built through curiosity, good questions and genuine care for the outcome.
The next time you hear “we have no budget,” resist the urge to discount. Go back into the conversation. Find out what matters most to them and what standing still is costing them.
The objection is rarely about money. It is about value not yet seen.
What do you hear behind “we have no budget”?
