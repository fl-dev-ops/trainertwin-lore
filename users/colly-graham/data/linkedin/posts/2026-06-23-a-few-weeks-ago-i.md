A few weeks ago I wrote about the salesperson who feels everything. The fear before the call. The disappointment after the loss. So many of you replied, and one question kept coming back. What about the person across the table?

It is the right question, and it is the whole of my new piece. Because you are never the only nervous system in the room. The buyer is afraid too. Afraid of a costly mistake, of looking foolish, of being talked into something they will regret. Their crossed arms are not rudeness. They are protection. And here is the part that changed how I sell. Your calm is catching, and so is your fear. The steadiest person in the room sets the tone for everyone in it. That is not a soft idea. It is the most practical thing you carry into a meeting.

In this week's Winning Mindset Newsletter I show you how to read the room without mind-reading, how to settle a buyer who has gone quiet, and why compassion is a selling skill rather than a weakness. It builds straight on the last article, so the two read as a pair. Have a read, then tell me in the comments. The next time a buyer goes silent and you feel the pull to fill the space, what would change if you settled yourself first?
