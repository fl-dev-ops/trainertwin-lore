After more than fifty years in sales, I have come to the conclusion that most sales training starts in the wrong place.

We train salespeople to understand products, services, presentations and processes. All important. But none of those are where buying decisions are made.

Buying decisions are made inside the human brain.

Long before a prospect compares specifications or signs an order, their brain is answering a series of questions. Do I trust this person? Do they understand my situation? Is this relevant to me? Is there enough value to justify change? Is the risk acceptable?

If those questions remain unanswered, the sale rarely moves forward, regardless of how good the presentation is.

This is why I became interested in neuroscience. Not because salespeople need to become neuroscientists, but because understanding how people think, feel and decide helps us have better conversations.

The most successful salespeople I have worked with do not push harder than everyone else. They create trust, ask thoughtful questions, listen carefully and help buyers think clearly about their choices.

Perhaps the future of sales is not about becoming more persuasive. Perhaps it is about becoming more useful.
