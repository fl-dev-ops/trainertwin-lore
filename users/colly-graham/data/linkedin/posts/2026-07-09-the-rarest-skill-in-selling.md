The rarest skill in selling is not asking. It is what you do after you ask. In fifty-six years of selling I have watched sellers deliver a superb question and then waste the answer. They were rehearsing their next clever line while the buyer was still speaking. Buyers always notice. The question opened a door, and the seller walked straight past it.
The latest edition of my newsletter, The Seller Who Listens, Wins, is about walking through that door. I share how to listen at three depths, because facts tell you the situation, feelings tell you the stakes, and silences tell you the truth. I explain why the three-second pause draws out the answer the buyer did not rehearse. And I show how playing back the buyer's exact words builds trust faster than any claim you could make. When the pressure rises and objections come, listening is the move most sellers skip. It is also the one that does the real work.
Being truly heard is one of the rarest experiences in business, and the seller who provides it is never forgotten. Machines can ask questions. Attention is human. The full edition is an eight minute read, with two techniques you can use within the hour at no cost. Read it, choose your two, and tell me in the comments which ones you picked. The seller who asks, leads. The seller who listens, wins.
