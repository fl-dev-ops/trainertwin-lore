Six eras of selling. One truth that never changed.

When I started in sales there were no laptops, no CRM systems, and certainly no AI. Everything lived in a notebook. Appointments were made by telephone. If you wanted to understand a company, you asked someone who knew them — or you went to the library. Since then I’ve watched the profession move through the product era, the relationship era, the solution era, the consultative era, the data-driven era, and now the AI era.

Yet after fifty-six years in sales and three decades training organisations, one observation stands out above everything else: technology has transformed how we sell, but it has not changed how the human brain decides. Before logic analyses a proposal, the brain asks something much more fundamental — Is this safe? Do I trust this person? Does this matter to me?

Which is why the future of high-performance selling will not belong to those who simply master the latest tools. It will belong to those who understand the human decision process behind every sale. Because in every era — from notebooks to artificial intelligence — the sale was never truly won by the product. It was always won inside the human mind.
