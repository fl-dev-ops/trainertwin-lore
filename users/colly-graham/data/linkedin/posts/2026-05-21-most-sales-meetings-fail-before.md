Most sales meetings fail before the solution is ever discussed.

I have been training sales professionals for thirty years across twelve countries. The single most common mistake I see is not poor technique. It is the wrong sequence. The salesperson arrives prepared, confident and ready to present. The buyer's brain is still asking a much simpler question: "Am I safe here?" Until that question is answered, nothing else lands. The salesperson is presenting value while the buyer is still deciding whether to trust the person in front of them. And that gap — between where the salesperson is and where the buyer's brain actually is — is where most sales conversations quietly fall apart. Polite interest in the room. Nothing afterwards. No movement. No decision. No meaningful next step.

In my latest Brain Based Sale newsletter I walk through the BRAIN framework in full — the five-stage conversation structure that follows how the brain actually becomes ready to decide. Background creates safety. Results and Challenges surface real friction. Ambitions move the buyer towards a better future. Impact makes the cost of doing nothing visible. Next Steps create commitment without pressure. Each stage matters. The sequence matters even more. Whether you lead a sales team, coach salespeople, or sell into complex business environments, this is a question worth sitting with: are your conversations giving buyers information, or helping them become ready to decide? Those are not the same thing. The full article is in the first comment — it is an eight-minute read and it will change the way you think about every sales conversation you have from here.
